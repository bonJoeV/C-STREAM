    Session 9: Catholic Scientists¶

    LESSON AT A GLANCE¶

    Field Reference
    Grade / schedule / rotation / unit 5-6 / Bi-Weekly / A / Research
    Time 1 meeting of 45 minutes
    Objective / why / big idea I can cite a supported scientist claim, compare two model-data samples and revise a clear evidence poster with limits. Source learning includes inquiry, not biography recall alone.
    Domains / Catholic connection C, T, A; faith/reason and honest sourcing, not a predetermined conflict rebuttal.
    Local standards CST-C1: historical/model-data claim and limit; CST-T3: provenance/data interpretation; CST-A3: revised evidence message. Official benchmarks VERIFICATION REQUIRED.
    Technology Requirement None; complete printed/board packet; optional approved source viewing only.
    Difficulty / entry Developing; teach source author/title/date and paraphrase.
    Prep / cleanup Light: first 15 min, repeat 10 min; cleanup 4 min. Kit: Mendel Source Study.

    BEFORE CLASS / MATERIALS¶

    Review contact/accommodations; tool teams ⅘/7/9. Copy the three complete cards and the two model-data rows below onto one reference sheet/team. These facts were checked October 4, 2026. No unsourced quotations, world-first claims, open-web search or broader biography homework. Draw claim/source/limit poster columns and a sample/R count/W count/total table. Each pupil owns their counts, comparison and poster even when reading together.

    Item Allocation 10 15 20 25
    Paper poster/evidence sheets 2/student 20 30 40 50
    Pencil 1/student 10 15 20 25
    Paper source/data packet; ruler; marker 1 each/team 4 5 7 9
    Board; clock; roster 1 each/teacher 1 1 1 1

    Paper consumable; tools reusable.

    VOCABULARY / TEACHER BACKGROUND¶

    Provenance: origin. Paraphrase: own wording preserving meaning. Claim: statement needing support. Limit: what source does not establish. Common misconception: religious identity verifies every scientific claim. If asked, "Did faith cause his discovery?" These cards show his role/work, not establish a causal account of his private motives.

    Card A - verified heritage/history: Adapted from the original Mendel heritage card. Masaryk University's Mendel Museum identifies Mendel as a member and abbot of the Augustinian order, his pea experiments, and preserved original objects/work on plant hybrids. A religious role and scientific work can coexist; this record does not establish private motives or prove a faith claim. Its historical facts are separate from our invented classroom data. Card B - archive: Villanova Library, Rediscovering Gregor Mendel lists holdings of Mendel's plant-hybridization paper and monastery archive materials. A holdings list establishes preserved sources, not every quoted belief. Card C - faith: Fides et Ratio, opening affirms faith/reason in pursuit of truth; this is Church teaching, not pea data. All cards are paraphrases, not attributed quotations.

    Complete model dataset and inquiry key¶

    Invented classroom labels, not Mendel's measurements or observations of real plants. R means round and W wrinkled in a paper pea-trait model:

    Sample A: R R R R R R R R W W
Sample B: R R R R R R R W W W

    Question: "Do these two equal-sized samples have identical category counts?" Each student tallies directly from the labels: A has 8 R / 2 W / 10 total; B has 7 R / 3 W / 10 total. A has one more R and one fewer W. Combined counts 15 R / 5 W / 20 total give a 3:1 count ratio (divide both counts by 5). This arithmetic describes only the invented dataset; it is not an inheritance experiment, a guaranteed offspring ratio or evidence about human traits. The individual poster distinguishes museum fact, classroom model-data result and an unanswered question about real plant observations.

    EXACT LESSON SEQUENCE¶

    1. 0-4 (4 min): Ask "Which source supports which kind of claim?"
    2. 4-11 (7 min): Read the short heritage card and source cards; model history versus invented data, one tally and the sample-comparison question.
    3. 11-25 (14 min): Each counts both rows, records R/W/total and compares equal sample sizes; records one museum fact/source and one limit. Make a poster with original pea symbols and separate source/model-result panels.
    4. 25-35 (10 min): Partners check counts against labels, not memory; each explains 15/5 and its 3:1 ratio. Ask "Where did that result come from?" Revise a misleading caption while retaining the original; name a question the model cannot answer.
    5. 35-41 (6 min): Individual A/B counts, one numerical comparison, supported historical claim/source/date/limit and revised caption; distinguish model arithmetic from historical or biological evidence.
    6. 41-45 (4 min): Save posters/notes, cap/count tools, reuse scraps and tidy.

    QUESTIONS TO ASK STUDENTS / WHAT SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE¶

    "Which result did you count yourself? What real records would compare plants honestly? Does our model prove inheritance? Does a catalog prove a quotation?" Each has both correct 10-item tallies, a supported A/B comparison, a sourced historical claim, provenance/date, model/history limit and reader-driven revision. The combined count ratio must be checked against 15/5, not recalled as biography. 1 unsupported; 2 prompted; 3 independent; 4 distinguishes source types and explains a further observation needed; NE if missing.

    IF THINGS GO WRONG / SAFETY¶

    Source page offline -> supplied verified packet; do not invent fresh claims. Count mismatch -> mark each R/W once and compare the printed labels; keep the correction. Too much text -> one historical fact and one model-data result. Missing markers -> pencil. No plants, food, biological sampling, personal-trait or belief interviews, copied images/quotes, public media or browsing without school policy. Never infer a pupil's genes or Mendel's private faith motives. Stop/report unsafe data or device use.

    SUPPORT / CHALLENGE / INDOOR FALLBACK¶

    Grade 5/support: read cards aloud, point/tally each of ten labels, use a table and comparison stem; scribing permitted. Grade 6/challenge: check 15/5 as 75%/25% of the 20 labels and explain why model proportions do not establish real inheritance; distinguish a museum record from a catalog referring to the same work. No simplistic "always supported" history. Indoor source study.

    CLEANUP / FAMILY NEWSLETTER¶

    Save claim/source records and changes, count tools, log unanswered questions. Explored: Mendel, sources and model-data inquiry. Did: counted two invented samples and revised an evidence poster. Learned: historical facts and our model results have different sources/limits. Catholic connection: faith/reason and honest evidence. Ask: "What did you count yourself, and what came from the source?" No routine homework or home trait survey.

    Teacher artifact pack (release checklist)¶

    • Student material and expected evidence (print/no-print): Give/board-copy Cards A-C and both invented rows with sample / R / W / total, then poster boxes historical claim + source/date / model-data result / limit / revised caption. Exact counts: A 8R/2W/10; B 7R/3W/10; combined 15R/5W/20 = 3:1. Evidence must separate counted model labels from museum/archive/Church claims.
    • Worked example and finished example: Work Sample A by crossing each label once. Finished poster states Mendel was an Augustinian abbot who worked on pea hybrids—Mendel Museum, checked 2026-10-04; our invented rows total 15R/5W; and this is not Mendel’s measurement or proof of inheritance/private motive, with a misleading caption retained and corrected.
    • Board setup and visual example: Set SOURCE/PROVENANCE | SUPPORTED CLAIM | DOES NOT ESTABLISH beside the A/B tally table and original caption -> reader question -> revised caption. The crossed Sample A tally is the visual example; label data INVENTED CLASSROOM MODEL.
    • Questions/misconceptions: Ask “What did you count?” (paper labels), “Does 3:1 prove inheritance?” (no), “Does a holdings list prove a quotation?” (no), “Does religious identity verify science?” (no). Accept source-specific paraphrases only.

    Exact supplies¶

    • Kit contents and Kit label: Exact Mendel Source Study kit: two paper poster/evidence sheets and pencil per pupil; source/data packet, ruler and marker per team; teacher board/clock/roster. Kit label: G5-6 YA S09 — MENDEL SOURCE STUDY — CARDS A-C + A/B DATA. Reset by counting packets/tools, capping markers and filing original/revised posters. Mark last tallied symbol/source box on unfinished work and resume from supplied packet, not open-web homework.
    • Annotated exemplar, Common error interpretation, and reteach: Annotate Mendel proved 3:1 in our rows with provenance error: rows are invented; box 15/5 arithmetic, underline museum-supported fact and circle the limitation. Common error interpretation: this is a provenance error, not evidence that the pupil cannot tally. Reteach by color-free crossing/tally, physically sorting history/model/Church cards, then rewrite one claim with source.

    SAFETY¶

    • Safety classification, supervision, SDS/product gate, and Print/accessibility check: Severity low, likelihood rare; no plants, food, samples or belief interviews. Adult ratio 1:25. SDS/product gate: HOLD — school-approved marker (CONTROL-ROW commercial mixture). The exact product/manufacturer is not specified and no approved marker row exists; see the Safety Data Sheet Register, use its CONTROL-ROW, and do not issue or substitute a marker until the school safety lead approves the exact product and current SDS. Print/accessibility check: print source cards at 14-point/high contrast with URLs/titles intact, read aloud, use tactile tally marks and allow oral/scribed poster text.
