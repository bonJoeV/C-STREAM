    Session 12: Plants & Growth¶

    Lesson at a glance¶

    Field Teacher information
    Grade / unit / title Grades 3-4 / Life inquiry / Plants & Growth
    Time One 40-minute meeting, including cleanup; real germination takes days
    Domains Catholic truth/stewardship, life science, investigation, measurement
    Big idea A living seed needs suitable conditions to germinate; observing takes time.
    Student objective "I can observe a seed, plan a one-change test, and compare labeled practice data."
    Why Gardeners and scientists separate predictions from observations and plan reliable care.
    Catholic connection John 12:24 uses a seed image to teach about Jesus' self-giving; it is a theological metaphor, not a claim that a seed must die to germinate. Care for living things and tell the truth about evidence.
    Local standards Local, not official: CST-C1 (distinguish metaphor/evidence); CST-S1 (observations); CST-S2 (one variable/controls); CST-M1 (length/count comparisons).
    Technology None; photos optional, not required
    Difficulty / prerequisites Moderate; compare whole millimeters and identify one changed condition. No Year A/Year B prior sequence required.
    Prep / cleanup 15 minutes for dry kit/data; 5 minutes cleanup. Optional actual sprout samples need days of advance growth, not concealed in prep estimate.

    Before class¶

    1. Check school seed/legume allergy guidance; substitute paper seed drawings if needed. No seed tasting or sending loose seeds to younger children.
    2. Pair pupils, final trio if needed. Give each team two clear cups labeled moist and dry, two folded paper towels, and four untreated bean seeds (two per cup).
    3. Board the practice table below, labeled SIMULATED DATA; NOT OUR OBSERVATIONS. Draw lines 4 mm and 9 mm long for ruler practice.
    4. Decide whether a regular teacher has accepted daily care for a real optional test. Write caretaker name and check dates. If no caretaker, leave seeds dry and assess the plan/data today; do not start an unattended living experiment.

    Exact supplies¶

    Tables budget ceil(students / 2) kits. A final trio gives 5/7/10/12 active teams at 10/15/20/25 pupils; unused eighth/thirteenth kit is spare. The water budget includes those spare cups and is a maximum.

    • Per student: pencil, evidence half-sheet.
    • Per team: 4 untreated bean seeds, 2 clear plastic cups, 2 paper towels, 2 labels, 1 ruler, 2 recording sheets.
    • Class: jug with 100 mL maximum water; 1 teacher measuring spoon; 1 tray/bin to hold all cups; board/timer.
    • Teacher only: water dispensing, optional live sample and care calendar. No soil, fertilizers or grow lights needed.
    Students Teams Seeds Cups / towels / labels Rulers Team paper Slips / pencils
    10 5 20 10 each 5 10 sheets 10 each
    15 8 32 16 each 8 16 sheets 15 each
    20 10 40 20 each 10 20 sheets 20 each
    25 13 52 26 each 13 26 sheets 25 each

    Use 5 mL water per moist cup initially only if care is arranged: 25/40/50/65 mL for the four class sizes, from the 100 mL supply. Damp, not pooled. Cups/rulers/bin reusable; seeds/towels/labels/paper consumable.

    Vocabulary, background, misconception and answer¶

    Seed: contains a living embryo and stored resources. Germinate: begin growth from a viable seed. Variable: condition intentionally changed. Control conditions: things kept the same. Prediction: expected result, not data.

    Beans need water, oxygen and suitable temperature for germination; they can germinate on a damp towel without soil/burial. Many germinating seeds use stored food before leaves develop; growing green plants need light and nutrients. Needs vary by species; do not claim every seed has identical light needs.

    Misconception: "The seed dies or must be buried to sprout." A viable embryo remains alive while growing. If asked: "Will it grow today?" Answer: "Not visibly during our class. Today we observe, plan and practice measuring; actual changes must be checked over days."

    Practice data: two seeds per condition¶

    Condition / seed Day 0 root length (mm) Day 3 (mm) Day 5 (mm)
    Moist / 1 0 4 9
    Moist / 2 0 3 8
    Dry / 1 0 0 0
    Dry / 2 0 0 0

    Invented instructional values, not guaranteed bean growth or observed evidence. They illustrate comparison; two seeds do not prove a universal rule. Keep seed type, cup/towel, location, temperature and observation time the same; vary added water only. Do not test several variables at once.

    SAFETY¶

    No eating seeds, rubbing eyes, handling mold or using chemicals. Teacher pours water. Keep cups open to air; no tight bags around faces. Wash hands after contact; wipe spills. Adult discards moldy samples without student handling. No outdoor requirement or classroom heat equipment.

    Numbered sequence: exactly 40 minutes¶

    1. 0-5: Observe. Short original prayer: "God, help us care for living things and speak truthfully." Show a dry bean. "What can you actually see? What are you guessing is inside?"
    2. 5-10: Explain life and metaphor. Draw embryo/stored food/coat. Clarify that an alive seed grows; John 12:24 is not a botany rule. "Could a dead embryo grow?"
    3. 10-17: Plan a test. Each team states question: "How does added water affect these seeds?" Write prediction; circle changed condition and list two controls. Ask: "Why use the same kind of seed in both cups?"
    4. 17-23: Set up model. Place towels and two seeds in each labeled cup. If care is arranged, teacher adds 5 mL to moist cup; otherwise all remain dry and are labeled planned setup only. Every child sketches both conditions. No growth result claimed.
    5. 23-29: Read practice evidence. Teacher labels simulated table. Pupils compare day 5 moist and dry counts/lengths, and measure the drawn 4/9 mm lines. "Did our class grow these roots? What would real observations need?"
    6. 29-34: Individual evidence. Child records one observation, the variable/two controls, and a correct whole-number comparison: 9 mm versus 0 mm, difference 9 mm. Grade 4 also compares the two moist seeds (1 mm difference) and names a reason more seeds could help.
    7. 34-35: Check success. Teacher asks two children: "What do we know today, and what remains a prediction?"
    8. 35-40: Cleanup. Store any cared-for cups in adult-approved spot with caretaker/date card; otherwise return dry seeds to teacher storage. Collect rulers, wipe cups/desks and wash hands. Collect slips.

    Success, troubleshooting, support, challenge and indoor fallback¶

    Success: each child distinguishes observation from prediction/simulated data, identifies water as one variable and two kept-same conditions, and reads/compares whole-mm values. Growth itself is not today's assessment. Score 1 unsupported growth claim; 2 identifies water with prompts; 3 observation/variable/controls/comparison correct; 4 adds replication or species/model limitation.

    • No seed supplies or allergy restriction: sketch two labeled cups with seed drawings; the same timed planning/data work remains. Record direct seed observation as unavailable.
    • No care between bi-weekly meetings: use dry planning setup and supplied simulated data; no failed forgotten plants or required home care.
    • No sprout appears later: log "no visible root"; check water/temperature/viability without inventing a result.
    • Support / Grade 3: oral explanations, pre-drawn cups, adult ruler placement and tally/whole-number comparison.
    • Challenge / Grade 4: propose more seeds per condition and a consistent check schedule; no multi-variable "challenge" that ruins the fair test.
    • Indoor alternative / winter: the primary plan is entirely indoor and includes data now. No spring weather or greenhouse needed.

    Family snippet¶

    We observed seeds, planned a fair water test and compared clearly labeled practice data. We learned that germination is growth of a living embryo, not a resurrection experiment. Our Catholic connection was honest inquiry and care for life. Ask: "Which condition changed, and which stayed the same?" Optional at home: notice a growing plant with an adult. No planting supplies, daily log or homework required.

    Teacher artifact pack (release checklist)¶

    This pack makes today's direct observations, the fair-test plan, and the invented multi-day practice values impossible to confuse.

    • Student material (observation/fair-test slip, print or board-copy): Use these exact prompts: I directly observe this dry seed detail: ___; I infer/predict ___; Question: How does added water affect these seeds?; Changed condition: ___; Kept the same (name two): ___ and ___; My prediction: If ___, then ___; Cup sketch: MOIST / DRY; Was water actually added today? yes—teacher 5 mL / no—planned setup only; Practice Day 5: moist seed 1 ___ mm; dry seed 1 ___ mm; difference ___ mm; These numbers are OUR OBSERVATIONS / SIMULATED; What we know today: ___; What still needs days of evidence: ___. The team recording sheet adds rows for cup, seed ½, Day 0/⅗ and a caretaker/date box. If not printing, pupils copy the prompts under headings SEE TODAY / FAIR TEST / SIMULATED TABLE / CARE DECISION.
    • Evidence and answer guidance: Accept observable properties such as color, shape, size or seed coat without claiming to see a living embryo directly. The sole changed condition is added water; acceptable controls include same bean type, number of seeds, cup, towel, location, temperature and observation times. From the supplied practice table, accept Day 5 moist seed 1 9 mm, dry seed 1 0 mm, difference 9 mm; Grade 4 may also state moist seeds differ by 1 mm. Every use of these values must say simulated/not class-grown. Accept we know the seed's visible features and our plan; germination remains a prediction. Reject the seed died, soil is required to begin germination, or a claimed growth result during this 40-minute lesson.
    • Worked example: Model Changed—5 mL water; same—bean type, two seeds, towel, cup location and check time; prediction—the moist seeds may germinate before the dry seeds. Work the comparison 9 mm - 0 mm = 9 mm while pointing to the SIMULATED DATA; NOT OUR OBSERVATIONS heading. The finished example is a slip containing one observation, one prediction, water as the only variable, at least two controls, labeled moist/dry sketches, 9/0/9 with units and simulation label, plus the actual care decision. Visible germination and a final causal result are N/A today; they cannot be substituted with the practice table.
    • Board setup and visual example: Under a document camera or on a tray, show one intact dry bean and a teacher drawing of coat, embryo and stored resources; label the inner parts as background knowledge, not what pupils can see through the coat. Display two open cups labeled MOIST and DRY joined by a bracket same except added water. Across the board write the full practice table beneath a boxed heading INVENTED INSTRUCTIONAL VALUES—NOT GUARANTEED GROWTH. Add a care chart team | caretaker | check dates | actual observation only if an adult has accepted daily care.
    • Discussion with acceptable response: Ask, What can you see and what are you inferring is inside? Accept visible coat/shape versus background knowledge of embryo/stored resources. Ask, Why use the same kind and number of seeds? Accept: to make water the intended comparison and reduce another difference. Ask, Did our class grow a 9 mm root? Accept: no, 9 mm is invented practice data. Ask, Must a seed die or be buried to germinate? Accept: no; a viable living embryo can begin growth with suitable water, oxygen and temperature, including on a damp towel. Ask, Does two seeds prove a rule for every species? Accept: no; more replication and species-specific evidence would be needed. Keep John 12:24 identified as a theological metaphor, not a botany mechanism.
    • Kit contents, kit label and reset: Per team: 4 untreated bean seeds, 2 clear open plastic cups, 2 folded paper towels, 2 labels, 1 ruler and 2 recording sheets; per pupil: one evidence half-sheet and pencil. Teacher controls the jug (100 mL class maximum), one measuring spoon, tray/bin and any live sample. Kit label on the cups: S12 TEAM __ | MOIST | 2 SEEDS | DATE __ and S12 TEAM __ | DRY | 2 SEEDS | DATE __; on the bin: C-STREAM | G3-4 | S12 | CARETAKER ___ | NEXT CHECK ___. If care is approved, the teacher alone adds exactly 5 mL to each moist cup, places all cups open to air in the accepted location and signs the calendar. If no caretaker is named, add no water, mark both PLANNED SETUP ONLY, return dry seeds to teacher storage, and complete cleanup and reset: wipe cups/desks, collect rulers and require handwashing.
    • Unfinished work: Circle observation / prediction / variable / controls / cup sketch / practice comparison / simulation label / care decision. Keep cups dry unless the signed care gate is already met. Store the slip with the team plan and resume at school; do not send seeds home, assign family watering or infer growth between meetings. Later absence/no root is recorded honestly as no visible root, not converted into success or failure without review.
    • Annotated exemplar, common error reading and reteach: Underline only the direct observation, box changed—water, bracket each control, double-box 9 mm / 0 mm / 9 mm difference, stamp SIMULATED, and circle the caretaker/no-care decision. Our root grew 9 mm signals a source-label error; changing water and light signals an unfair-test plan; calling a guess an observation signals evidence-language confusion. Reteach by sorting cards into see / predict / supplied practice data, then use two cup cards and the stem We changed ___; kept ___ and ___ the same; the table shows ___; today we observed ___.
    • Safety classification: Severity is moderate because seed allergy, ingestion, mold or spilled water can cause harm; likelihood is low after the school allergy check, teacher-only water and adult care plan. Adult ratio is 1:3 maximum: one adult for each active team of two, or final trio, during seed distribution/setup; the teacher alone pours and later disposes of moldy material. No tasting, eye rubbing, student mold handling, fertilizers, chemicals, soil, heat equipment or sealed bags near faces. Wash hands and wipe spills. Untreated food-grade/classroom seeds and water generally have no SDS, but school allergy guidance and supplier labeling apply; any treated seed would be excluded rather than introduced.
    • Print/accessibility check: Use 14-point black-and-white text, high-contrast cup labels, whole-mm number boxes and a practice table whose simulated status is written on every page, not conveyed by color alone. Read the table heading aloud, provide enlarged/tactile cup diagrams, ruler support, oral answers and a scribe. For allergy or touch avoidance, substitute paper seed drawings and mark direct seed handling/observation unavailable without lowering the reasoning criterion.
    • Technology artifact: None applies; this lesson has no technology artifact.
