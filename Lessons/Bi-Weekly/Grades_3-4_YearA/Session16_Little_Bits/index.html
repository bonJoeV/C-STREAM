    Session 16: Little Bits¶

    Lesson at a glance¶

    Grades 3-4; Bi-Weekly A; Circuits; one 40-minute meeting. Objective: assemble/test a documented button-light chain and revise its readable classroom signal label. Why: input-output tools require actual operation, not guessed sensor behavior. Catholic/CST-C2 real turns/access label; CST-T1 input/output/troubleshooting; CST-E1 criterion/limits; CST-E2 physical/label retest. Official alignment VERIFICATION REQUIRED. Technology None: no software, but approved physical modules/power are mandatory. Developing. Prep 20 after matching instructions/inspection; approval/loan extra; cleanup 5.

    Before class and exact supplies¶

    Select the actual model's documented power/button/enclosed-light project. Record manufacturer/model/manual page, module IDs, age guidance, approved supply and b = fitted cells per station (or documented enclosed rechargeable supply). Adult checks protection/holder/access, assembles/test release/press twice and leaves diagram/setup card. No assumed battery type/count, sensors or motors. Failure -> paper pathway/report, not invented wiring. Do not mix with Snap/AA parts. Existing Snap routes remain unchanged in classroom guide.

    Teams <=3 (⅘/7/9), one complete station/team: one each approved power, button and light module, tray/diagram/cardstock label/marker. Per pupil paper/pencil. Teacher board/timer/demo from kit. Fitted cells = station count x b, adult-only; record exact local allocation before teaching.

    Allocation 10 15 20 25
    Stations; each module; trays; diagrams; labels; markers (1/team each) 4 5 7 9
    Paper; pencils (1/pupil each) 10 15 20 25

    Vocabulary/background/SAFETY¶

    Power = energy source; input = button action; output = light; signal chain = documented module order. Misconception: any magnetic chain is safe. If asked "Can it turn on in the dark?" Only a matching verified sensor project could do that; not today's button path. Adult isolates supply before module changes/checks before power. No mains, bare cells, free wiring, loose magnets, blades or take-home hardware. Warm/smelly/leaking/damaged -> stop, adult isolate, OUT OF SERVICE/report. Dry indoor stations, no water or battery access by pupils.

    Meeting 1: exactly 40 minutes¶

    1. 0-4: "Who needs a clear classroom signal?" Optional inclusion prayer.
    2. 4-10: Show approved diagram, isolation, button states and stop rules.
    3. 10-22: Each contributes a connection while isolated; adult checks/powers. Every pupil operates release/press twice, predicts/records actual outputs.
    4. 22-30: Design word/symbol label; peer reads at 1 m, revise/retest. Adult manages one documented disconnected-path fault/repair while isolated; retest, never make a live short.
    5. 30-35: Each explains input/output, physical repair observation and before/after readability; log individual operation separately.
    6. 35-40: Adult isolates/counts modules/cells, stores dry; save paper only.

    Success/access/troubleshooting¶

    Meets: real contribution/two-cycle test, input-output explanation and tested label revision. Grade 3/support: traced diagram/oral evidence/motor assistance. Grade 4/challenge: justify access tradeoff, no undocumented modules. Missing/fewer kits: two waves with half stations inside work windows only after teacher verifies inspection/turn capacity; otherwise paper and missing operation record. Paper at same times uses button/light cards: reasoning/design only, electrical operation not observed. No screen label waives electrical gates.

    Family: We tested a documented input-output tool and its label. Ask, "What did the button change?" Describe paper model if hardware was not released.

    Teacher artifact pack (release checklist)¶

    Prepare one Student material (button-light test and label card) per pupil, or copy these fields exactly to the board: Station/model ID: ____; Draw arrows: POWER -> BUTTON -> LIGHT; When released, I predict ____; When pressed, I predict ____; My cycle 1: released ____ / pressed ____; My cycle 2: released ____ / pressed ____; Disconnected-path symptom I observed: ____; After the adult isolated and repaired it: ____; First signal label (word + symbol): ____; Peer read it from 1 m as: ____; I changed ____ because ____. Responses may be drawn, pointed, dictated or scribed, but only the pupil's own two-cycle hardware operation satisfies the operation field.

    • Evidence and answer guidance: The only acceptable physical chain is the exact manufacturer-documented, model-matched power-button-enclosed-light path on the station card. Expected button states are recorded from the actual released and pressed behavior; normally the released button leaves the light off and pressing it turns the light on, but the verified manual and observed device govern. Accept "button is the input; light is the output; power supplies energy." Accept a readable signal such as QUIET with a closed-mouth symbol or HELP with a raised-hand symbol when a peer correctly interprets it at 1 m. Evidence must include two pupil-operated cycles, observation of the adult- managed isolated fault/repair and a specific readability revision. Do not accept claims about darkness sensing, undocumented modules, guessed output or another pupil's button presses.
    • Worked example: With power isolated, align the three approved modules to the printed diagram; the adult checks the connection and powers the station. Model release/press twice and record the actual light state. Isolate power, create the documented disconnected-path fault, ask pupils to predict no complete path/no intended light response, reconnect while isolated and retest. Show a thin HELP label that a peer misreads from 1 m, then finish it with large block letters and a bold raised-hand outline. The finished example is that posted test card with its revised cardstock signal. If an approved station is unavailable, use module cards for reasoning and mark contribution, two cycles, fault and retest N/A - electrical operation not observed.
    • Board setup and visual example: Place the verified manufacturer diagram upright behind the tray and arrange large POWER, INPUT: BUTTON and OUTPUT: LIGHT cards left to right with arrows. Measure a 1 m viewing position on the floor and have the peer stand there without adding tape or another floor product. Divide the board into ISOLATE -> FOLLOW DIAGRAM -> ADULT CHECK -> POWER -> RELEASE/PRESS x2 -> ISOLATE BEFORE CHANGE and a second row FIRST LABEL | PEER'S READING | REVISION.
    • Discussion with acceptable response: Ask, "What action supplies information to this system?" Accept pressing/releasing the button. Ask, "What observable result is the output?" Accept the enclosed light's state. Ask, "Why must power be isolated before a module changes?" Accept reducing electrical/battery damage risk and allowing an adult to inspect the path. Ask, "Can this chain turn on because the room is dark?" Accept no; there is no verified light sensor in today's button project. Ask, "Does a magnetic fit make every combination safe?" Accept no; only the matching documented modules, supply and order are released. Correct the misconceptions that the button creates energy, every sensor is automatic and any magnetically connected part is approved.
    • Kit contents, kit label and reset: Each released tray must contain one model-matched power module with approved fitted supply, one button module, one enclosed-light module, the actual manual/page or setup card, one sorting tray, one cardstock label, one marker, three pencils and three test sheets. The adult records module IDs and b fitted cells/supply type before class. Kit label on the tray: S16 BUTTON-LIGHT - MODEL ____ - b=____ - ADULT POWER/ISOLATION - NO EXTRA PARTS. At cleanup and reset, the adult isolates power, counts all three modules and the enclosed/fitted supply, inspects for heat, odor, leakage, cracks or loose parts, and stores the dry kit; pupils return markers and submit only paper/cardstock. Tag any suspect component OUT OF SERVICE and report it.
    • Unfinished work: On the pupil card mark the last safe stage: diagram / contribution while isolated / cycle 1 / cycle 2 / fault observation / first label / 1 m peer test / revision. Keep incomplete hardware inside the isolated labeled tray for an approved school wave; never send modules home. If the station fails inspection or turn capacity, finish the paper design and record electrical operation not observed.
    • Annotated exemplar, common error reading and reteach: Annotate the exemplar with arrows between module names, a box around the button as input, a box around the light as output, two checkmarks beside the repeated states, a broken arrow at the disconnected-path symptom and a 1 m bracket beside the enlarged label. A light that does not match the verified behavior is a common error signal: pupils stop; the adult isolates power and compares module IDs, orientation and connections with the manual. Never add cells, wires or modules to "make it work." If the circuit works but the label is misunderstood, reteach communication rather than circuitry: My peer read ____; I enlarged/changed ____ so the signal means ____.
    • Safety classification: Severity serious because damaged cells/supplies, small magnets or incompatible electrical parts can cause harm; likelihood is low only after model verification, inspection and adult isolation. Adult ratio is 1:3 maximum: assign one adult to each active station/team of no more than three. Pupils do not handle cells, power supplies or damaged components. No mains, bare cells, free wire, loose magnets, blades, water, live reconfiguration or take-home hardware. SDS applicability is conditional: retain the LittleBits manufacturer battery/product safety documentation and any school-required SDS. Enter the exact manufacturer/product for the used marker category in the Safety Data Sheet Register, record SDS applicability and school approval, and keep the marker on HOLD until resolved. For leaking, hot, swollen or damaged cells/components, isolate the area and follow manufacturer documentation rather than handling or cleaning with pupils.
    • Print/accessibility check: Use 16-point sans-serif black-and-white type, high-contrast module pictures with printed names, thick directional arrows, large state checkboxes and a word-plus-symbol label. Read the manual excerpt and prompts aloud; offer tactile matching while power is isolated, pointing, oral response, scribing, a seated station and motor assistance. Do not rely on light color alone: record on/off and allow a peer or adult to state the observed output.
    • Technology artifact: No software or external service applies. The LittleBits modules are commercial hardware and require the manufacturer safety documentation; actual approved physical modules are mandatory for electrical-operation evidence. Paper or unplugged work is not executed-device evidence, and module operation remains deferred/not observed until an individual supervised turn is witnessed.
