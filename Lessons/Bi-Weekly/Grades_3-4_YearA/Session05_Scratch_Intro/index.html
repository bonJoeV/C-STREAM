    Session 5: Scratch Intro¶

    Lesson at a glance¶

    Grades 3-4; Bi-Weekly A; Computing; one 40-minute meeting. Objective: edit/run a finite loop, predict four movements and explain a correction. Why: execution makes repeated instructions observable. Catholic/CST-C2 accessible turns/respectful message; CST-T1 actual edit/run/stop; CST-T2 loop/debug; CST-M3 four repeats/whole-number turn total. Official alignment VERIFICATION REQUIRED. Technology Required; novice. Prep 20 after Scratch 3 works, installation extra; cleanup 5.

    Before class and exact supplies¶

    Guest/offline Scratch; build starter, run twice, Stop, local .sb3 save/reopen. Leave sample/save location. Teams <=3 (⅘/7/9): computer/four counters. Per pupil paper/pencil; teacher board/timer, demo uses kit. No accounts/projector purchase. Failed preflight -> paper trace and missing operation report.

    Allocation 10 15 20 25
    Computers (1/team) 4 5 7 9
    Counters (4/team) 16 20 28 36
    Paper; pencils (1/pupil each) 10 15 20 25

    Starter, vocabulary and background¶

    On one sprite:

    when green flag clicked
go to x: (0) y: (0)
point in direction (90)
repeat (4)
  move (20) steps
  turn clockwise (90) degrees
  wait (1) seconds
end
say [Welcome!] for (2) seconds

    Stage = display; sprite = character; loop = repeated enclosed blocks; event = trigger. Misconception: a block below the loop repeats. If asked "Why not forever?" Finite repeat permits an exact prediction and clear end. Scratch steps are screen coordinates, not physical centimeters.

    SAFETY/privacy¶

    Dry seated devices/counters; no public accounts/media, passwords or downloaded assets. Counters off bodies/away from mouthing pupils. Stop/report damaged gear. God-as-programmer is not a literal scientific model. Indoor primary.

    Meeting 1: exactly 40 minutes¶

    1. 0-4: "Which action happens four times?" Optional inclusive-work prayer.
    2. 4-10: Tour stage/sprite/Code; model starter and Stop.
    3. 10-21: Every child edits/runs in a three-minute turn; peers trace with four counters and record four moves/turns.
    4. 21-29: Change repeat to 3, predict/run, then restore 4 and retest; locate misplaced block or document correct operation.
    5. 29-35: Each explains inside/outside loop and actual result; roster logs individual edit/run/stop. Four 90-degree turns total 360, no ratios.
    6. 35-40: Stop/save/reopen, count counters, return devices, file dated trace.

    Success/access/troubleshooting¶

    Meets: four-repeat prediction, correction/retest and observed operation/ fair turn. Grade 3/support: copied stack/token trace/scribing. Grade 4/challenge: compare ¾ repeats and total turns. Sprite drifts: reset position/direction, check block enclosure, rerun. Half stations: two waves of three-minute turns within work blocks; fewer means reschedule operation. No device: same times token/block cards, CT/counting only, Scratch operation not observed. Early finish: new message, not unbounded program.

    Family: We executed and checked a finite dance loop. Ask, "What repeated?" Optional: trace four turns verbally; no home device.

    Teacher artifact pack (release checklist)¶

    Release these artifacts with the prebuilt finite-loop starter. Do not replace the four-repeat investigation with a forever loop or an open-ended animation.

    • Student material (prediction/debug sheet): Include a miniature empty block stack and these exact prompts: Draw a bracket around the blocks inside repeat (4); Before running, I predict the sprite will move ___ times, turn ___ times, and turn ___ degrees total; four numbered rows move ___ steps / turn ___ degrees / facing or position sketch; The say block runs ___ time(s); When repeat changed to 3, I predicted ___ moves and ___ degrees; I observed ___; I corrected/restored ___; I personally EDITED ___ / RAN ___ / STOPPED ___; File reopened? YES / NO / PAPER PATH. For no-print, pupils draw four boxes and copy the ten prompts from a projected-free board model.
    • Evidence and answer guidance: The baseline answer is four 20-step moves, four clockwise 90-degree turns, 360 degrees total, and the say Welcome! block once because it sits below the loop. The comparison answer is three moves, three turns, and 270 degrees when repeat is temporarily 3, followed by a restored 4. Accept a correction involving repeat count, loop enclosure, starting x/y, starting direction, or a misplaced block when it matches an actual run. Scratch steps are not centimeters. Teacher observation, not a partner report, establishes each pupil's edit/run/stop evidence.
    • Worked example: Place four counters in a square. Touch one counter for each predicted move 20 / turn 90, then run the starter. Change 4 to 3 and document the missing fourth side/270-degree turn; restore 4, rerun, save locally, and reopen. The finished example brackets the three loop blocks, completes all four rows, distinguishes the once-only say block, records the 3-versus-4 comparison, and names a correction or confirms correct operation after checking. On paper fallback, .sb3 reopened and operation marks are N/A--Scratch unavailable; loop reasoning is still completed.
    • Board setup and visual example: On the teacher screen show the entire starter with a thick removable border around move/turn/wait only; place four numbered counters beneath it. Board table: repeat | moves | 90-degree turns | total degrees | observed endpoint, with rows 4 and 3. Beside it, post INSIDE LOOP = REPEATS | BELOW LOOP = ONCE | GREEN FLAG = START | STOP = END RUN.
    • Discussion with acceptable response: Ask Which blocks repeat and how do you know? Accept move, turn, and wait because they are enclosed. Why does Welcome appear once? It is below/outside the repeat. Why restore repeat 4? The required finished program and prediction use four iterations. Why not use forever? A finite loop has a countable outcome and clear stop. Correct claims that every lower block repeats, that four 90-degree turns equal 90 degrees total, or that dragging blocks without running proves execution.
    • Kit contents, kit label and reset: Each station has one compatible computer with the tested Scratch 3 starter and known local save folder, four large plastic counters, and one station card; issue paper/pencil per pupil. Kit label: G3-4 YA S05 | SCRATCH STATION | 4 COUNTERS | LOCAL SB3--NO LOGIN. Cleanup and reset: return the visible starter to repeat 4, x=0/y=0/direction=90, stop all scripts, save and reopen the local .sb3, close Scratch as directed by school procedure, count four counters, wipe only with approved dry method, and log a missing file, damaged key, or station that did not reopen.
    • Unfinished work: On the pupil sheet circle paper trace / edit turn / run / repeat 3 comparison / restore 4 / save / reopen and note the last visible repeat value and sprite position. Schedule unfinished device evidence during a later supervised school turn. Do not require a Scratch account or home computer, and do not mark device operation from a peer's run.
    • Annotated exemplar, common error reading and reteach: Underline the repeat count, draw a box around the enclosed blocks, box 4 moves / 4 turns / 360 degrees, and circle the observed correction. Three drawn iterations beside repeat 4 indicates counting/trace mismatch; four moves but only one turn suggests the turn block was outside the loop; a shifted endpoint with correct loop may indicate missing start-position/direction blocks. Reteach by physically nesting printed block cards inside a loop frame, touch-counting four counters, and completing Inside the loop, ___ repeats ___ times; below it, ___ runs once before another run.
    • Safety classification: Severity low and likelihood low for seated, dry device use. Adult ratio is 1:25 maximum, with computer teams of three or fewer and timed individual turns. Keep counters away from mouths and bodies, drinks away from devices, cords outside walkways, and stop/report damaged equipment. SDS is not applicable to computers, paper, pencils, or intact plastic counters in normal use; use only school-approved device cleaning products according to their SDS and never during pupil operation.
    • Print/accessibility check: Print block outlines in black-and-white at 125% or larger, use at least 14-point text, number the four trace rows, and pair icons with words; do not rely on color alone. Read each block in execution order. Allow enlarged counters, keyboard/mouse assistance, switch-compatible access already approved by the school, pointing, oral prediction, dictation, scribing, or a seated code-reader role while preserving an actual individual operation turn when technology is available.
    • Device compliance: Required pathway is the tested school Scratch 3 installation or guest/offline editor with local .sb3 save. Disable/avoid pupil accounts, cloud saving, public sharing, comments, names, passwords, camera/microphone, downloaded sprites, and external media. If the preflight fails, use counters/block cards and label digital edit/run/stop not observed; paper or unplugged work is not executed-device evidence, and Scratch operation remains deferred/not observed until an individual edit, run, and stop are witnessed. Do not represent the paper path as Scratch operation.
