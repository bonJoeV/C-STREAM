    Session 13: Easter Tech¶

    Lesson at a glance¶

    Grades 3-4; Bi-Weekly A; Faith communication; one 40-minute meeting. Objective: edit/present a two-slide Easter message, revise for a peer and explain private sharing. Why: digital revision/display can clarify a religious message without treating it as scientific proof. CST-C1 belief/symbol/evidence distinction; CST-T1 actual digital operation; CST-T3 privacy/credit; CST-A3 audience critique. Official alignment VERIFICATION REQUIRED. Technology Required; novice workflow. Prep 20 after software works; installation extra; cleanup 5.

    Before class and exact supplies¶

    Approved offline LibreOffice Impress: blank presentation, Slide > New Slide, Insert > Text Box, Slide Show > Start from First Slide, Escape, local File > Save As .odp, reopen. Pretest two-slide sample, leave save location. Equivalent school app only after teacher leaves tested exact commands; otherwise paper pathway. Teams <=3 (⅘/7/9): computer/team; two paper sheets/ pencil per pupil; teacher board/timer/demo from device. Teacher paraphrase on board: Christians celebrate Jesus' Resurrection at Easter; empty-tomb image communicates hope. No inherited Scripture quotation needed.

    Allocation 10 15 20 25
    Computers (1/team) 4 5 7 9
    Paper (2/pupil) 20 30 40 50
    Pencils (1/pupil) 10 15 20 25

    Vocabulary/background/SAFETY¶

    Slide = display page; audience = reader; symbol = meaningful image; revision = deliberate improvement. Slide 1 states Easter belief, slide 2 original hopeful message with simple drawn shape. Misconception: butterfly proves Resurrection. If asked "Is metamorphosis death?" No; organism develops through living stages. Dry devices, no names/photos/voices, accounts, external art downloads/public uploads or automatic delivery. Stop/report damaged gear; indoor primary.

    Meeting 1: exactly 40 minutes¶

    1. 0-4: Optional Easter prayer; "What message can our design communicate?"
    2. 4-10: Demo two slides/text/present/Escape; clarify belief versus model.
    3. 10-21: Every pupil edits/presents three-minute turn; peers storyboard and record actual software action.
    4. 21-29: Peer states message without coaching; record feedback, revise word/layout, present again.
    5. 29-35: Each child explains religious meaning, actual edit/test revision and safe sharing choice; roster marks operation independently.
    6. 35-40: Save/reopen, exit/return, collect dated storyboards.

    Success/access/troubleshooting¶

    Meets: accurate message distinction, observed operation, critique-backed revision/private plan. Grade 3/support: two text stems/dictation/motor assistance. Grade 4/challenge: space/readability tradeoff, no invented quotation. Text missing: check selected slide/box. No device/app: same times two paper slides/peer test; art/faith reasoning only, digital operation not observed. Half stations permit two work-window waves; fewer requires later operation. Early finish: new reader, not multimedia expansion.

    Family: We revised private Easter messages. Ask, "Which symbol conveyed hope?" Optional conversation, no home device or public sharing.

    Teacher artifact pack (release checklist)¶

    Prepare one Student material (Easter message planning/review slip) per pupil, or copy these prompts exactly to the board for a no-print response: Slide 1 belief: ____; Slide 2 hopeful message in my own words: ____; My simple symbol: ____ because ____; Software action I performed: ____; My partner said the message meant: ____; Partner suggestion: ____; I changed ____ to ____ because ____; This symbol communicates ____ but does not prove ____; I will keep the file private by ____. Accept writing, drawing, pointing, dictation or a scribed response; a pupil still must perform the observed software turn to earn digital operation evidence.

    • Evidence and answer guidance: Slide 1 may say that Christians celebrate Jesus' Resurrection at Easter. Slide 2 must be an original message such as "Hope can grow after a hard time," paired with a pupil-made cross, sunrise, empty-tomb outline or other simple meaningful shape. Accept revisions to wording, size, spacing, contrast or symbol placement when the pupil connects the change to peer understanding. Accept "a symbol helps communicate belief or hope; it is not a scientific test or proof." A safe sharing answer is local save, teacher- approved classroom display or showing the file privately on the device. Do not accept an invented Scripture quotation, a downloaded image, a public post or a partner's operation as the pupil's evidence.
    • Worked example: Model a first draft with Slide 1, "Christians celebrate Jesus' Resurrection at Easter," and Slide 2, "Easter gives Christians hope," beside a small sunrise. Read the cramped draft from the back, record the feedback "The second line is hard to read," enlarge the words and move the sunrise, present again, save locally and reopen the .odp. The finished example is that two-slide file displayed beside its completed review slip. On the paper pathway, display two revised paper slides; mark digital editing, presenting and reopening N/A - not observed, rather than treating paper as equivalent.
    • Board setup and visual example: Project the approved blank presentation and place a paper before/after pair beside it as the visual example. Divide the board into BELIEF | ORIGINAL MESSAGE + SYMBOL | PEER'S PARAPHRASE | REVISION | PRIVATE SAVE; add beneath it A symbol communicates meaning. It does not scientifically prove a belief.
    • Discussion with acceptable response: Ask, "How could a reader tell this is an Easter message?" Accept the stated Christian belief, an explained Easter symbol or hopeful wording. Ask, "What did the peer's paraphrase reveal?" Accept that the intended meaning was clear or identify the word/layout that confused the reader. Ask, "Does a butterfly or metamorphosis prove the Resurrection?" Accept no: metamorphosis is development through living stages and the butterfly is a symbol. Ask, "Does saving a file send it to everyone?" Accept no: a local file remains on the approved device/location unless an authorized adult deliberately shares it. Correct the misconceptions that religious meaning is scientific evidence and that presenting or saving automatically publishes work.
    • Kit contents, kit label and reset: At each team place one tested compatible computer open to offline Impress (or the preapproved equivalent), three pencils, six plain sheets and one card. Kit label: S13 EASTER TECH - LOCAL FILES ONLY - NO NAMES / DOWNLOADS / UPLOADS. Cleanup and reset by confirming each file saves and reopens in the assigned local folder, closing the presentation, returning to the blank two-slide starter, collecting dated slips/storyboards and noting each pupil's edit/present/reopen status. Do not erase an incomplete file needed for a scheduled school turn.
    • Unfinished work: On the slip check the last completed stage: two messages / first presentation / peer paraphrase / revision / second presentation / save-reopen. Save a clearly dated local draft without a pupil's full name and attach the slip for the next school session. If no device turn occurred, record digital operation not observed; do not assign uploading or home-device work.
    • Annotated exemplar, common error reading and reteach: Annotate the model with a box around the Christian belief, an arrow from symbol to its intended meaning, a bracket around the peer's exact feedback, a star beside the visible revision and a lock beside the local-save choice. A peer who cannot state the message indicates a common error of audience clarity; calling belief proof indicates a claim-type error; hunting for menu commands indicates an operation-sequence error. Reteach audience clarity with the stem I want my reader to understand ____; if belief is called proof, use The symbol communicates ____; it did not test ____; if the pupil cannot locate commands, rehearse New Slide -> Text Box -> Slide Show -> Escape -> Save As on paper cards before one supported software attempt.
    • Safety classification: Severity low and likelihood low with dry, inspected devices. Adult ratio is 1:3 maximum: one adult supervising teams of no more than three; provide direct motor help without taking over the assessed turn. Stop and report damaged cords or devices. SDS review is not applicable because no chemical product is used.
    • Print/accessibility check: Use at least 16-point sans-serif prompts, strong black-and-white contrast, generous response space and symbol-plus-word headings. Read every prompt aloud; offer a private paper copy, pencil grip, pointing, dictation or scribing. Do not require color alone to distinguish the two slides or require public reading of a faith response.
    • Device compliance: Use only the pretested offline LibreOffice Impress workflow on the approved school computer; local save/reopen is required, with no pupil account or cloud storage. No email, cloud drive, external image search, automatic delivery, names, photos, voice recording or public upload. The paper pathway preserves faith/art/audience reasoning but cannot satisfy the required observed digital operation. Paper or unplugged work is not executed-device evidence; Impress operation remains deferred/not observed until the individual edit, present, save, and reopen turn is witnessed.
