    Week 34: Year-End STREAM Expo¶

    Lesson at a glance¶

    Grades 3-4; Weekly A; Portfolio; one 40-minute meeting, not an extended event. Objective: support a learning claim with two dated artifacts, demonstrate reasoning individually and revise an explanation for a peer. Why: an expo should show what changed, not certify attendance as mastery. Catholic/CST-C3: stewardship of gifts/work; CST-E3: evidence/tradeoff/limit; CST-A3: display critique/revision. Official alignment VERIFICATION REQUIRED. Technology None; developing. Prep 15 minutes; cleanup 5 included.

    Before class and exact supplies¶

    Per pupil: portfolio, two paper sheets (evidence caption/fresh demonstration), pencil. Teams <=3 (⅘/7/9) share ruler. Teacher board/timer; choose safe paper artifacts, not live circuits/robots. Missing work: fresh tower folding/measuring task, labeled today's evidence, never invented history. Board: earlier date/result | later date/result | fresh explanation | limit. Tools reusable; evidence retained. No certificate/treat supplies required.

    Allocation 10 15 20 25
    Portfolios; pencils (1/pupil each) 10 15 20 25
    Paper (2/pupil) 20 30 40 50
    Rulers (1/team) 4 5 7 9

    Vocabulary/background/SAFETY¶

    Evidence = actual record; growth = supported change; demonstration = individual showing; limit = what record cannot establish. Misconception: a photo proves each child programmed. If asked "Can I say mastered all STREAM?" Not without individual evidence across those skills; local mastery requires two dated observations on separate occasions, including changed context/explanation. No live hardware, food, balloons or public private photos; report missing artifacts. Indoor desk displays, no weather dependency.

    Meeting 1: exactly 40 minutes¶

    1. 0-4: Optional gratitude prayer. "Which records show a changed decision?"
    2. 4-9: Model qualified claim: actual dates/results; separate paper/real operation, prediction/measurement and faith reflection.
    3. 9-20: Select two artifacts, prepare caption and independently re-trace code or explain/recreate a design. Without work, fold/test tower ten seconds, measure cm, change base/retest; label fresh evidence only.
    4. 20-29: Parallel teams: two minutes per child to show evidence/demonstrate; peers ask a specific question. At 25, nine simultaneous groups, not 25 serial two-minute speeches.
    5. 29-35: Each revises caption, names supported skill/limit and stewardship action; teacher collects individual explanation and missing-evidence flags.
    6. 35-40: File dated captions, count rulers, return portfolios, clear desks. Optional recognition says "participated/explored," not certified mastery.

    Success/access/troubleshooting¶

    Meets: evidence-referenced claim, fresh individual reasoning and critique-backed caption; growth not credited without two genuine records. Grade 3/support: pointing/dictation/caption stems. Grade 4/challenge: defend a tradeoff and what transfer evidence would be needed. Missing earlier work: report growth not evidenced; today's task still assesses reasoning. Overlong speech: use prompt card/task-result-limit within two minutes. Early finish: answer new peer question.

    Family: Our Expo used evidence and individual explanations. Ask, "What can your records show, and what can't they show?" Optional: listen to one explanation.

    Teacher artifact pack¶

    • Student material — expo evidence caption and fresh-demonstration sheet (print/no-print): Side A fields are name/date, My specific learning claim: I improved at ___, Earlier artifact title/date ___, earlier result/decision ___, Later artifact title/date ___, later result/decision ___, What changed in my reasoning/design ___, and These records cannot show ___. Side B fields are Fresh individual demonstration chosen: retrace/explain/recreate OR today's paper tower, what I did without teammate help ___, result/measurement ___, reason for my decision ___, peer's exact question ___, my answer ___, and caption revision after critique ___. Final prompt: I used my gifts responsibly by ___. With no printer, pupils fold two plain sheets into these labeled sections copied from the board. Do not use an attendance, applause, photo, or group product as individual mastery evidence.
    • Expected evidence and acceptable answers: A growth claim requires two genuine, dated artifacts with a described change; accept changes in measurement, plan, explanation, code trace, design choice, or treatment of a tradeoff. The fresh demonstration must include each pupil's own reasoning, not my team did it. A valid limit names something the records cannot establish, such as transfer to a new task, independent programming, every STREAM strand, or consistent mastery over time. Missing earlier work means growth is not evidenced, but today's measured tower or explanation can still provide current reasoning evidence. Stewardship answers may address careful tool use, truthful reporting, listening, useful critique, or improving work rather than wasting materials.
    • Worked and finished example: Use invented artifacts, never a pupil's: Claim: I improved at using measurements to revise a structure. Earlier: W12, tower sketch, 18 cm; narrow base tipped. Later: W20, bridge record, 24 cm span; I widened supports after measuring. Change: I began using the result to choose the next design. Fresh demonstration: I folded a paper tower with a 6 cm base; it stood 10 seconds and measured 21 cm. I chose the wider base for stability. Peer question: Would it stand with a 4 cm base? Revision: My records support that I used measurements to improve these two paper structures; they do not prove every future design will work. Annotate both dates/results, the independent decision, peer-driven revision, and qualified limit.
    • Visual/board setup: Before class post four columns: EARLIER DATE/RESULT | LATER DATE/RESULT | FRESH EXPLANATION | LIMIT. Add a two-minute presentation path: 1 claim (20 s) -> 2 artifacts (40 s) -> demonstrate/ explain (40 s) -> peer question and response (20 s). Beside it make a supports / does not yet support T-chart and a private teacher checklist with two genuine dates, individual reasoning, revision, and limit. Number team display spaces; portfolios stay flat at desks rather than forming a public wall.
    • Discussion questions, answers and misconceptions: Ask Why are two dates stronger than one for a growth claim? (they can show a change over time); Can a group photo prove each person can explain the design? (no, it shows presence/product, not individual reasoning); What can today's tower show if old work is missing? (current planning, measurement, testing, and explanation, but not growth); What makes peer critique useful? (a specific question reveals a missing reason, tradeoff, or limit and leads to revision); Does participation mean mastery? (no; recognition may say participated/explored); and How can a truthful limit show stewardship? (it respects evidence and avoids exaggerating one's work). Correct I mastered STREAM by asking which specific skill, records, independent demonstration, and new-context evidence would support it.
    • Kit contents. Kit label: and Cleanup/Reset: Prepare one pupil portfolio plus exactly 2 plain paper sheets and 1 pencil per pupil; each team station receives 1 metric ruler. Label station rulers W34 EXPO | TEAM CODE | 1 METRIC RULER; label the collection crate C-STREAM G3-4 YRA W34 | PORTFOLIOS--PRIVATE | CLASS CODE and the blank-sheet folder W34 CAPTION + DEMONSTRATION | 2 SHEETS PER PUPIL. Reset by checking every portfolio and new dated caption against the class roster, placing papers inside the correct portfolio without displaying private information, counting 1 ruler per station, returning unused clean paper, and recycling only teacher-confirmed scraps. Do not place live circuits, robots, food, balloons, or certificates in the kit.
    • Unfinished work: On the teacher checklist mark exactly one next action: find second dated artifact, complete fresh demonstration, answer peer question, or write qualified limit. File all work in the pupil's portfolio and schedule a brief school-time conference or supervised fresh task. If an earlier artifact cannot be found, record growth not evidenced--current reasoning assessed today; never invent a date/result, require home completion, or withhold participation recognition.
    • Annotated exemplar, Common error interpretation, and Reteach: On the example, box the two dates, underline the result from each, arrow the changed decision, star the independent demonstration, and bracket the limiting clause. Two artifacts with no described change indicate a comparison/explanation gap; a polished group artifact with no individual reasoning indicates attribution is unresolved; I got better without dates/results is an unsupported claim, not proof of no learning. Reteach with a four-card sort: claim, record, reason, limit; pupil matches each sentence from the example, then orally rebuilds a caption from one of their own records. If no records exist, use the paper-tower result and label it only current evidence.
    • Safety classification: Severity low; likelihood unlikely after controls. Possible harms are a paper edge, dropped ruler, or crowded movement; seated parallel stations and desk-flat portfolios reduce those risks. Adult ratio: 1:25 maximum for seated stations of no more than 3; the adult monitors aisle clearance, ruler use, and portfolio handling while circulating. Add a school-approved adult where an individual supervision/access plan requires it. No live hardware, mains electricity, food, balloons, climbing, or elevated displays; keep aisles clear and report a missing portfolio immediately. SDS applicability: not applicable because only paper, pencils, portfolios, and rulers are used.
    • Print accessibility: Print at 100% in black and white, at least 12-point sans serif, bold section headings, no color-only distinctions, and generous writing space; offer 16-18-point copies, oral presentation, scribing, pointing to artifacts, seated demonstration, and extra processing time without extending the team's two-minute rotation. Check one sample for legible date/result lines and ensure the copied-paper version includes the cannot show field.
    • Technology compliance: No technology artifact applies.
