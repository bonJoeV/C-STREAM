    Weeks 14-15: Light Science & Advent¶

    Lesson at a glance¶

    Grades 3-4; Weekly A; Light; two 40-minute meetings. Objective: record reflected/refracted light observations and revise a transmitting Advent composition after a same-distance test. Why: optics helps explain tools/windows; purposeful art communicates hope. Catholic/CST-C1: distinguish measured light from religious symbolism; CST-S1: observations; CST-S3: light-path model; CST-A2: purposeful composition; CST-A3: critique/revision. Official alignment VERIFICATION REQUIRED. Technology None, but physical flashlights required; introductory. Prep 15 minutes plus adult battery check; cleanup 5 per meeting.

    Before class and exact supplies¶

    Teams <=3 (⅘/7/9). Each kit: enclosed flashlight, acrylic mirror, uncut clear plastic cup, tray, three tissue squares, cardstock sheet, glue stick. Per pupil: two recording sheets/pencil. Teacher board/timer and 50 mL water per team, poured by adult only in meeting 1; demo uses issued kit. Class: one graduated plastic pouring cup and one roll of towels for drying/spills. Check secured batteries/no cracked mirrors; pretest mirror aiming onto desk paper and pencil appearance through water. Adult cuts a window in cardstock before meeting 2; keep room normally lit. Dry kits before storage.

    Vocabulary/background/SAFETY¶

    Reflection = light changes direction at a surface; refraction = change of direction between materials; transmission = passage through; composition = arrangement conveying meaning. Misconception: pencil is actually bent. If asked "Is spiritual light measurable?" No; Advent light is a religious symbol, not a lux reading. No flames, glass, lasers, sun-viewing, loose cells or fully dark rotations. Beam onto desks, never eyes. Adult handles batteries/ water; stop/dry spills and report damaged kit. Indoor flashlight tests do not depend on daylight. No unverified liturgical/saint claims needed.

    Meeting 1: exactly 40 minutes¶

    1. 0-4: "How can light change direction?" Optional prayer for careful inquiry.
    2. 4-10: Model desk mirror target, water-pencil observation and safe beam.
    3. 10-22: Every pupil aims mirror once and draws source/mirror/target arrows. Adult pours water; each draws pencil dry then viewed through water.
    4. 22-30: Repeat from a second viewing angle; compare appearance and explain that a picture models light, not a real bent pencil.
    5. 30-35: Individual reflection/refraction observations and symbol-versus- measurement distinction; "Which evidence showed a change?"
    6. 35-40: Adult empties cups; dry/count/store, collect dated sheets.

    Meeting 2: exactly 40 minutes¶

    1. 0-4: Review observations; Advent preparation/hope is the faith application.
    2. 4-9: Show tissue/window frame; flashlight always 20 cm behind frame.
    3. 9-21: Design three-panel composition using tissue; each pupil explains one placement/color choice communicating hope, not just decoration.
    4. 21-30: Peer identifies intended shape at 1 m; record correct/not yet, revise one overlap or outline and retest at same light/view distance.
    5. 30-35: Each draws path through tissue, records two results and explains critique-backed visual choice and religious/scientific distinction.
    6. 35-40: Switch off/count equipment, save paper art, cap glue/clear desks.

    Success/access/troubleshooting¶

    Meets: two accurate optical observations/path, explicit symbolic distinction and test-backed visual revision. Grade 3/support: trace arrows, oral explanation, precut window. Grade 4/challenge: explain overlapping layers/readability tradeoff. No visible reflection: reset angle onto closer paper, not eyes. No water: reflection/transmission only; refraction not observed. Missing flashlight: share between neighboring teams within work windows; never infer operation from watching. No home hardware.

    Family: We investigated light and revised Advent art. Ask, "Which part was science and which was a symbol?" Optional: notice a safe light source.

    Teacher artifact pack¶

    • Student materials (two recording sheets, printable/no-print): Meeting 1 fields: Name/date/team; draw flashlight -> mirror -> desk target with arrows; When I changed the mirror angle, the light spot ___; draw pencil before water and viewed through water; From angle 1 it appeared ___; from angle 2 it appeared ___; check reflection / refraction; The pencil itself did/did not bend; Science observation ___; Advent “light” as a symbol can mean ___ and is/is not a measurement. Meeting 2 fields: sketch three-panel composition; Intended hopeful shape/message ___; Flashlight stayed 20 cm behind; Viewer stayed 1 m away; Test 1: identified / not yet; peer said ___; I changed overlap / outline / placement by ___; Test 2 at same distances: identified / not yet; draw a light-path arrow through tissue; Revision evidence ___. No-print: pupils divide each plain sheet into the labeled boxes above.
    • Expected evidence/answers: Reflection evidence describes the desk spot changing location when mirror angle changes and shows an incoming and outgoing arrow. Refraction evidence says the pencil appeared bent, shifted, or broken at the water boundary and may change with viewing angle; the pencil did not physically bend. Transmission evidence shows light passing through tissue, often dimmer or color-filtered. Accept Advent light as a symbol of hope, preparation, guidance, or Christ when consistent with local teaching, while requiring not physically measured here. For the art test, either result is acceptable if distances are recorded and the revision responds to peer identification; do not claim a brighter color without an observation.
    • Worked example and Finished example: Science sheet: mirror arrows form a change in direction toward a paper target; note Tilting the top back moved the spot closer to me. Water drawings show a straight dry pencil and an apparently shifted submerged section from two angles; annotation: appearance due to refraction—not a bent pencil. Finished composition: three tissue panels form a candle silhouette. At 20 cm/1 m the peer first says rectangle; pupil removes one overlapping layer around the flame and adds a dark cardstock outline; peer then identifies candle. Annotate fixed distances as fair-test controls, the removed overlap as revision, and hope as symbolic meaning rather than optical evidence.
    • Visual/board setup: Meeting 1 board has three diagrams: reflection with incident/outgoing arrows, a side-view air/water boundary with a bent light path, and a crossed-out drawing of a physically bent pencil. Use a chart surface/setup | what light did | what we observed. Meeting 2 shows a ruler line flashlight --20 cm-- frame --1 m-- viewer, a two-row before/after identification chart, and separate headings SCIENCE: measurable light behavior and ADVENT: religious symbol/meaning.
    • Discussion questions, answers, misconceptions: Does the mirror make light? (no, it redirects flashlight light); Why can the pencil look displaced? (light changes direction between water, cup, and air); Did the water permanently bend it? (no); Why hold both test distances constant? (so the composition change, not distance, explains the comparison); Do two tissue layers transmit exactly like one? (not necessarily; observe rather than assume); Is spiritual light measured by our flashlight test? (no). Correct reflection is the same as refraction by identifying surface bounce versus passage between materials, and correct dark room is required by using the normally lit safe setup.
    • Kit contents. Kit label: and Cleanup/Reset: Team kit contains exactly 1 enclosed flashlight; 1 acrylic mirror; 1 uncut clear plastic cup; 1 sorting tray; 3 tissue squares; 1 adult-windowed cardstock sheet; 1 glue stick. Label C-STREAM G3-4 | W14-15 LIGHT | FLASHLIGHT 1 MIRROR 1 CUP 1 TRAY 1; label the dry art envelope 3 TISSUE + 1 WINDOW FRAME. Teacher station: graduated plastic cup; water supply; towel roll. Per pupil: 2 recording sheets; pencil. Meeting 1 reset: adult empties cups, dries cup/mirror/tray fully, switches off and counts flashlight, checks battery door, and replaces wet paper. Meeting 2 reset: switch off flashlight, cap glue, retain named art flat, discard only unusable tissue scraps, and return dry optical components; quarantine cracked or leaking items.
    • Unfinished work: After meeting 1, mark mirror observation, water observation, or symbol distinction as pending and store the dated sheet in W14 SCIENCE PENDING; never send the flashlight/mirror/water task home. After meeting 2, mark composition built, test 1, revision, or same-distance retest and store art flat with its sheet in W15 ART PENDING. Resume only the unchecked observation/test at school; if water was unavailable, explicitly record refraction not observed.
    • Annotated exemplar, Common error interpretation, and Reteach: A ray drawn straight through the mirror is reflection path not yet represented; have the pupil trace the visible desk spot and redraw an arrow away from the mirror. The pencil bent becomes The pencil appeared bent; inspect it after removal as counterevidence. If test 2 uses a closer viewer, label comparison invalid—distance changed and repeat at 1 m. Reteach reflection with one flashlight/mirror/target under adult control, refraction with teacher-held cup from two seated viewpoints, and transmission by comparing one versus two tissue layers without introducing brightness numbers.
    • Safety classification: Severity moderate; likelihood unlikely for beam-to-eye exposure or a cracked component; water spills, minor glue contact, and trips have low severity / possible likelihood. Enclosed flashlights aimed only at desk paper, acrylic mirrors, adult water handling, normally lit rooms, immediate spill drying, and pre-use inspection reduce residual risk to low / unlikely. Adult ratio: 1:18 maximum whenever flashlights or water are in use; classes of 20-25 require a second supervising adult or separately supervised investigation waves so every beam remains observable. The adult alone pours/empties water and handles batteries. The used commercial product category is classroom glue stick. Enter the exact manufacturer and product in the Safety Data Sheet Register, and keep it on HOLD until school approval and SDS applicability are resolved; remove a leaking battery device without pupil handling.
    • Print/accessibility check — black-and-white, and technology: Use 12-point minimum text, thick arrows, patterned as well as color labels, and separate observation/interpretation boxes. Offer large-print diagrams, tactile arrow pieces, high-contrast target paper, verbal description, scribing, and a seated viewing position that does not require head movement near the cup. Check a grayscale copy for visible water boundary, arrowheads, and 20 cm/1 m labels. No technology artifact applies.
