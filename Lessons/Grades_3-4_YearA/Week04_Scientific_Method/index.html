    Week 4: Scientific Method¶

    Lesson at a glance¶

    Grades 3-4; Weekly A; Inquiry; one 40-minute meeting. Objective: predict, record three roll distances at each of two ramp heights and explain a fair test. Why/big idea: repeated evidence beats a lucky result. Catholic/CST-C1: report unexpected results truthfully; CST-S1: supported claim; CST-S2: one change/two controls; CST-M1: six readings with cm units. Official alignment VERIFICATION REQUIRED. Technology None; developing, ruler use supported. Prep 15 minutes; cleanup 5 included.

    Before class and exact supplies¶

    Balanced teams <=3: ⅘/7/9. Each kit: smooth 30 cm cardboard ramp, two thin closed books, ball too large to swallow, ruler, catch tray, recording sheet. Per pupil: evidence sheet/pencil. Teacher board/timer; demo uses issued kit. Pretest one- and two-book heights with a clear 60 cm desk lane/tray; shorten ramp if balls escape. Board six trial boxes. Reuse kit; retain records.

    Allocation 10 15 20 25
    Kits; ramps; balls; rulers; trays; team sheets (1/team each) 4 5 7 9
    Books (2/team) 8 10 14 18
    Evidence sheets; pencils (1/pupil each) 10 15 20 25

    Vocabulary/background¶

    Hypothesis = reasoned testable prediction; variable = changeable feature; trial = repeated test; evidence = recorded result. Measure ramp end to stopped ball; keep ball, lane, release mark and no-push method fixed. Investigations can revisit steps, not follow one rigid recipe. Misconception: higher always means farther. If asked “Why did repeats differ?” Small release/surface differences matter; record spread. Prayer does not guarantee a desired result.

    SAFETY¶

    Low desk only; no throws, hot liquids, chemical hook, tasting or heavy loads. Teacher retrieves floor escapes; stop/report unsafe setups. Indoor primary. The former alternate experiments are not retained.

    Meeting 1: exactly 40 minutes¶

    1. 0-4: “Does ramp height affect distance?” Each predicts with a reason; optional original prayer for honest inquiry.
    2. 4-9: Demonstrate release, catch tray and ruler zero. “What changes? What two conditions must stay the same?”
    3. 9-20: One-book height, three trials; rotate releaser/measurer/recorder.
    4. 20-29: Two-book height, three trials; same lane/ball/release. Circle shortest/longest at each height, no required mean.
    5. 29-35: Each child records six readings, change/two controls and a claim using data. Ask, “Which reading supports or weakens your prediction?”
    6. 35-40: Count tools/balls/books, store ramps, clear trays, collect dated evidence and name one possible next question.

    Success/access/troubleshooting¶

    Meets: six cm readings, one change/two controls, evidence-based claim; prediction need not be right. Grade 3/support: marked ruler zero, dictated records. Grade 4/challenge: compare whole-number ranges and overlapping results. Sliding books: reset, label earlier trial invalid, repeat. Short lane: use equal shorter lanes and label capped distances. Missing balls: shared kit with pupil turns; record missing turns explicitly, never invent results. Protect evidence/ cleanup when late.

    Family: We repeated a ramp test and reported honest data. Ask, “What stayed the same?” Optional: discuss a testable question, no home experiment.

    Teacher artifact pack¶

    Student material: investigation sheet to print or copy¶

    Use one full page per pupil, or copy this exact compact table and prompts to the board for notebook paper:

    Name: ___ Date: ___ Team: ___

    Question: How does ramp height (one book/two books) affect roll distance?

    Ramp height Prediction Trial 1 Trial 2 Trial 3 Shortest Longest
    1 book ___ ___ cm ___ cm ___ cm ___ cm ___ cm
    2 books ___ ___ cm ___ cm ___ cm ___ cm ___ cm

    Changed variable: ___

    Kept the same (name two): 1. ___ 2. ___

    Claim: The ___ ramp usually rolled farther/shorter/about the same.

    Evidence: ___ cm compared with ___ cm; an unexpected/overlapping result was ___.

    Next testable question: ___

    Expected evidence: accept any honestly recorded whole-centimeter results. A complete ramp record has six readings with units, ramp height as the changed variable, and two valid controls from ball, ramp, lane/surface, release mark, no-push release, or measuring method. A claim may support or reject the prediction and may say results overlap. Do not require a mean or treat the highest single reading as proof.

    Worked and annotated example¶

    Post this sample only--class results will vary:

    Ramp height Prediction Trial 1 Trial 2 Trial 3 Shortest Longest
    1 book shorter 38 cm 41 cm 39 cm 38 cm 41 cm
    2 books farther 61 cm 58 cm 60 cm 58 cm 61 cm

    Changed: number of books under the same ramp. Kept the same: ball and release mark/no push. Claim: In these trials, the two-book ramp rolled farther because every two-book result (58-61 cm) exceeded every one-book result (38-41 cm). Next question: Does the same pattern occur on a carpeted lane?

    Circle all six units, bracket each three-trial set as repeats, underline the one change/two controls, and box the qualified phrase in these trials. If actual sets overlap, a finished acceptable claim is The results do not show a clear difference because both heights produced ___ cm; the expected answer is not automatically “higher goes farther.” The completed data/claim sheet is the finished artifact; a separate product is N/A.

    Visual example, Board setup, and discussion guide¶

    Draw the two-row/six-reading table before class. Beside it, show a side-view ramp with same release line, ruler zero at the ramp end, arrow to the stopped ball, and columns CHANGE 1 | KEEP SAME 2+ | REPEAT 3. Post release--do not push, the objective, role rotation, and record unexpected results.

    • “Why run three trials?” Accept that repeats reveal variation and reduce reliance on one lucky/unusual roll; repeats do not guarantee identical data.
    • “Where does measurement begin and end?” Accept from the ramp end/marked zero to the stopped ball using the same method each time.
    • “If one two-book result is shorter than a one-book result, should we erase it?” Accept no; verify the method, mark a known invalid trial only when a specific error occurred, otherwise retain it as evidence.
    • “What makes this fair?” Accept one changed height and fixed ball, ramp, lane, release mark, no-push method, and measurement method. “We all took turns” is fair collaboration but not a controlled variable.
    • “Does higher always mean farther?” Accept only a claim limited to the recorded trials; other surfaces, balls, or heights require tests.
    • “Why report evidence that weakens a prediction?” Accept truthful data helps others understand and repeat the investigation.

    Kit contents, Kit label:, Cleanup, Reset, and Unfinished work¶

    Each team tray contains exactly 1 smooth 30 cm cardboard ramp, 2 thin closed books, 1 large ball, 1 metric ruler, 1 catch tray, 1 team recording sheet. Issue 1 individual investigation sheet and 1 pencil per pupil. Label it C-STREAM G3-4 | W04 RAMP TEST | TEAM CODE | 1 RAMP / 2 BOOKS / 1 BALL / 1 RULER / 1 CATCH TRAY. At reset, place the ball in the catch tray, stack both books, lay the ruler and ramp flat, and check all six reusable pieces for damage. Remove floor escapes only after motion stops; retain all dated records.

    If time is called, finish the current trial safely, draw a line through every unrun cell, and write NEXT: trial __ at __-book height rather than copying a peer's number. Store the sheet with that team's tray for an in-school completion or assess only the evidence actually collected; no home rolling is assigned.

    Error interpretation and reteaching¶

    Annotate a unitless 42 with 42 what? add cm; six identical invented-looking entries with show me the recorded/repeated rolls--evidence not yet verified; higher is better with compare your six numbers and qualify the claim; and a pushed-ball trial with invalid method--state reason and repeat if time. Variation of a few centimeters is not automatically poor work. Reteach one teacher-modeled roll: pupil identifies ramp height as change, points to two picture controls, aligns ruler zero, reads one whole-centimeter distance, and uses The ___ ramp rolled ___ cm; this [supports/does not support] ___. Then collect only the missing matched trial rather than restarting all data.

    Safety classification, Print/accessibility check, and technology¶

    Safety classification: Severity low; likelihood unlikely after controls. Likely harms are a minor finger pinch at a book/ramp or a trip if a ball escapes; with desk-height lanes, catch trays, and teacher retrieval. Adult ratio: 1:25 maximum (one adult to no more than 25 pupils/nine ramp teams) while circulating and keeping all lanes visible; pupils remain at desks and never chase balls. Stop a sliding-book setup before continuing. SDS review is not applicable to cardboard, books, plastic ball, ruler, paper, pencil, and tray used as intended.

    Print the table in at least 12-point dark type with wide cells and units already shown; do not encode height or results by color alone. Offer a large-print sheet, raised/marked release line, high-contrast ball, partner reading, speech, scribing, or pointing. Check at 100% that all six data cells and cm labels are legible. No technology artifact applies.
