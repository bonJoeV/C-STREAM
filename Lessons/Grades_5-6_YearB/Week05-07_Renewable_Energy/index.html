    Weeks 5-7: Renewable Energy Lab¶

    LESSON AT A GLANCE¶

    Field Teacher reference
    Grade / unit / title Grades 5-6 / Energy B / Renewable Energy Lab
    Time Three meetings of 45 minutes; 135 total
    Domains C, S, E, M
    Big idea Renewable sources involve tradeoffs; a model's performance is not electrical efficiency.
    Student objective I can compare sources, test a rotor modification fairly, and defend a resource-aware design choice.
    Why Communities make energy choices under cost, land, reliability, and environmental constraints.
    Catholic connection Stewardship considers neighbors and future generations; Laudato Si' source. No one energy policy is inferred from a pinwheel.
    Local standards / evidence CST-S2: individual variables/control statement; CST-E2: before/after model tests; CST-M2: each student's table/graph; CST-C3: individual stewardship tradeoff.
    Official benchmarks VERIFICATION REQUIRED; four local program codes.
    Technology Recommended: primary hand-fanned rotor path uses no electrical device; guarded tabletop fan optional for more repeatable tests. This models wind-to-motion, not electricity generation.
    Difficulty / prerequisites Moderate; count rotations, keep time, compare repeated measurements. No circuit assembly or energy-efficiency calculations required.
    Prep / cleanup First kit 25 minutes; repeat 10 minutes; cleanup 4 minutes/meeting included.

    BEFORE CLASS¶

    1. Make ⅘/7/9 teams of at most three. Adult inspects commercial classroom pinwheels: securely enclosed axle ends, no exposed sharp pins or detachable small tips.
    2. Mark one rotor blade so a return to the same position counts one full rotation. Each team uses the same rotor before and after modification.
    3. Make one paper hand fan/team (folded cardstock). Mark 30 cm distance between fan and rotor; use seated work and a predictable fanning pace, acknowledging imperfect airflow control.
    4. Optional adult-operated guarded fan: stable stand, 75 cm rotor distance, low setting, no extension cords through walkways. Never insert materials into its guard.
    5. Copy source cards below. Prepare a fictional community brief: "Supply useful energy year-round while considering cost, neighbors, habitats, and winter conditions." No outside research/homework.

    Data/graph sheet 1/student/unit 10 15 20 25

    Cardstock fan; source-card sheet 1 each/team 4 5 7 9

    No heat lamps, water turbines near electricity, unprotected wiring, or bare LEDs. Optional solar electricity demonstration requires a separate adult-reviewed manufacturer kit and specified supply/load; it is not part of this timed lesson or required purchases.

    VOCABULARY AND SOURCE CARDS¶

    Energy: capacity to cause change/do work; units include joules and kilowatt-hours. Power: rate of energy transfer; watts. Renewable: replenished on human-use timescales. Rotor: rotating blade assembly. Performance: measured result of a test. Tradeoff: gain in one criterion with a cost in another.

    Source Model fact card Tradeoff to consider
    Solar Photovoltaic panels transform light into electricity; output depends on conditions. Darkness/clouds, land/materials, storage or other supply
    Wind Moving air turns a rotor; a generator can transform motion into electricity. Variable wind, siting/habitat, maintenance
    Hydro Moving water drives turbines and generators. Ecosystems, available flow, effects of dams
    Fossil fuels Chemical energy is transferred through combustion. Finite fuels, emissions, transport
    Nuclear Nuclear reactions release energy; uranium fuel is generally classified nonrenewable. Waste, construction, fuel and safety systems

    These basic descriptions are models, not site-specific feasibility evidence. Renewable does not mean impact-free. Nuclear fuel did not form by the same process as fossil fuels.

    TEACHER BACKGROUND¶

    Our fan input ultimately uses muscular energy (or purchased electricity for the optional fan), not a demonstrated renewable electricity source. Counting turns per ten seconds compares rotor response under specified conditions. It does not measure joules, watts, voltage, or efficiency; efficiency would require comparable energy input/output measurements.

    Common misconception: fastest rotor is always the best generator. Useful output, loads, durability, and conditions also matter.

    If asked, "Did we make free electricity?" Answer: No. We observed wind-to-motion transfer in a model; no generator or energy meter is used.

    EXACT LESSON SEQUENCE¶

    Meeting 1 - sources and test planning¶

    1. 0-5 (5 min): Read stewardship/community brief; ask who an energy decision affects.
    2. 5-12 (7 min): Sort the five source cards; distinguish energy from power and renewable from impact-free.
    3. 12-22 (10 min): Teams make a comparison chart; each student chooses two sources and a different tradeoff for each.
    4. 22-32 (10 min): Demonstrate count of full rotor turns in 10 seconds. Plan same rotor, fan distance, operator/pace, interval, and room location.
    5. 32-38 (6 min): Each writes change variable (tabs), measured response (turns/10 s), and two controls; practice counting.
    6. 38-41 (3 min): Individual exit: one energy/power distinction and one source tradeoff.
    7. 41-45 (4 min): Store kits.

    Meeting 2 - compare and redesign¶

    1. 0-5 (5 min): Review controls and honest unsuccessful tests.
    2. 5-12 (7 min): Measure 30 cm fan distance and run one unrecorded practice to coordinate counters.
    3. 12-22 (10 min): Run three baseline trials, 10 seconds each; record every count. Fanner remains the same for comparison; swap roles only after that comparison block.
    4. 22-32 (10 min): Attach four equal 2 x 4 cm paper tabs with matched tape pieces, clear of axle. Run three modified trials under same conditions; adult checks secure tabs.
    5. 32-38 (6 min): Each graphs or compares baseline/modified counts; retain zero turns. Identify airflow inconsistency as a limitation.
    6. 38-41 (3 min): Individual conclusion cites two counts and one uncertainty; no "percent efficiency" claims.
    7. 41-45 (4 min): Remove tabs and inventory kits.

    Meeting 3 - communicate a design recommendation¶

    1. 0-5 (5 min): Revisit community brief; rotor evidence alone cannot choose a town's energy supply.
    2. 5-12 (7 min): Explain a two-criterion decision table: reliability and effects on people/resources; list information still needed.
    3. 12-22 (10 min): Teams sketch a revised rotor or a source combination; clearly separate tested result from proposed untested features.
    4. 22-32 (10 min): Neighboring-team exchanges: explain data, ask one tradeoff question, revise recommendation. Nine teams use paired exchanges, not long public talks.
    5. 32-38 (6 min): Each student annotates recommendation with evidence, limitation, and one fairness/stewardship concern.
    6. 38-41 (3 min): Individual exit: why rotor turns are not electrical efficiency and one needed real-world measurement.
    7. 41-45 (4 min): Clean up, retain evidence.

    QUESTIONS TO ASK STUDENTS¶

    What stayed the same? Where did the model's input energy originate? Why can a zero count be valuable? What could winter change about a solar proposal? Whose needs would a cheap-but-unreliable choice ignore?

    WHAT SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE¶

    Each student names two controls, records six counts, compares using actual numbers, and explains one uncertainty and stewardship tradeoff. Grade 5 compares ranges/largest counts. Grade 6 calculates means and explains why a three-trial difference does not establish general superiority.

    IF THINGS GO WRONG¶

    Rotor sticks: stop, adult checks axle; do not force/bend sharp fasteners. Too fast to count: lower airflow or move hand fan farther and repeat both conditions. No rotor: use fictional counts baseline 8/9/7; tabs 6/8/7, labeled simulated; objective shifts to data interpretation, not building/testing a physical rotor. No electric fan: primary hand-fan route remains complete.

    SAFETY¶

    No exposed pins, projectiles, heat lamps, fire, mains wiring experiments, batteries, or water/electrical combinations. Adult operates optional guarded fan. Keep paper/tape out of guards, secure rotor/tabs, and stop when a part loosens. Student models are not installable generators.

    SUPPORT / CHALLENGE - GRADES 5 AND 6¶

    Grade 5: large marked blade, partner counter, table with trial numbers. Grade 5 challenge: compare count ranges. Grade 6: mean worked example and tradeoff matrix; challenge explain a better airflow control and why voltage alone would still not measure efficiency. Provide seated fans and alternative roles.

    INDOOR FALLBACK¶

    All primary testing is indoors, independent of sun, snow, or temperature. Outdoor weather is discussed as a design constraint, not required exposure.

    CLEANUP¶

    Stop optional fan, count pinwheels, remove loose tabs, recycle scraps, store fan away from paper and retain individual tables.

    FAMILY NEWSLETTER¶

    Explored: energy sources and wind-model performance. Did: tested a rotor before/after and compared evidence. Learned: performance and efficiency are different. Catholic connection: wise care for resources and neighbors. Ask: "What tradeoff would your community need to investigate?" No routine homework or home electrical construction.

    TEACHER ARTIFACTS¶

    Exact supplies and class-size allocation / required lesson-specific packet¶

    • Prompt/evidence: Supply source cards and a six-row table (three baseline/three four-tab trials, turns/10 s). Students name tab variable, two controls, all six counts, comparison/uncertainty, and community tradeoff. Rotor counts are performance, never watts/efficiency.
    • Worked/finished example: Baseline 8/9/7, tabs 6/8/7; mean 8 versus 7, “modified was lower here; hand airflow limits inference.” Finished graph/recommendation separates tested rotor result from untested energy-source choice.
    • Board setup / expected discussion responses: Chart trial|baseline|tabs, controls (same rotor, 30 cm, fanner/pace, 10 s, location), and performance ≠ electrical efficiency. Correct “fastest is best generator,” “renewable impact-free,” and “free electricity.”
    • Kit contents / Kit label / reset / unfinished: Per team: one enclosed-axle pinwheel, cardstock hand fan, source-card sheet, A4/letter tab sheet, ruler, blunt scissors, marker, and 0.25 m masking tape; per student: one data/graph sheet, pencil, and journal; class: one timer and, if selected, one guarded tabletop fan. Kit label: RENEWABLE ENERGY—W5-7—30 cm HAND/75 cm GUARDED FAN—10 s—team ___. Adult stops and stores the guarded fan, removes tabs, counts the pinwheel and tools, and files raw tables. Mark the last completed trial; resume both comparable conditions if setup changed.
    • Annotated exemplar / common error interpretation / reteach: Box six counts/units, circle comparison, star uncertainty. Missing zero=recording error; unequal distance=invalid comparison; percent efficiency=construct error. Re-run synchronized practice or use labeled simulated counts.
    • Devices/external-service compliance and deferred evidence: The recommended hardware is the adult-operated guarded tabletop fan on low, with the rotor at 75 cm. Evidence is three actually run 10-second baseline trials and three actually run modified-rotor trials using that fan. If it is unavailable, defer guarded-fan setup/operation and its six observed rotation counts; hand-fan results and paper reasoning do not count as guarded-fan runtime/device evidence.
    • Safety severity and likelihood / Adult supervision ratio / Print/accessibility check: Hand route severity low/possible; guarded tabletop fan moderate/unlikely, adult-operated with direct sightline. Adult supervision ratio: one adult/class, teams ≤3. No heat, fire, batteries, experimental wiring, or water near electricity. Print a large marked blade/table in high contrast; provide seated hand-fanning, partner counter, or scribe.
    • SDS/product gate: HOLD — masking tape. The exact masking-tape product/manufacturer is not specified. The Safety Data Sheet Register TAPE row is on HOLD. Do not issue or substitute masking tape until the school safety lead approves the exact product and its current SDS.
    • SDS/product gate: HOLD — school-approved marker. The exact marker product/manufacturer is not specified, and the Safety Data Sheet Register CONTROL-ROW does not approve a marker product. Do not issue or substitute any marker until the school safety lead approves the exact product and its current SDS.
