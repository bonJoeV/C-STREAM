    Weeks 14-17: Marine Science Lab¶

    Lesson at a glance¶

    Grades 3-4; Weekly B; Marine systems; four 40-minute meetings. Objective: label ocean-light zones, explain a food-to-eater model and improve a collection tool using equal ten-token tests. Why: ocean stewardship needs system knowledge, evidence and honest limits. Catholic/CST-C3 creation care; CST-S3 zone/feeding models; CST-E1 criteria/limits; CST-E2 actual retest; CST-M2 labeled before/after count display. Official alignment VERIFICATION REQUIRED. Technology None; developing. Prep 20 minutes; cleanup 5 each. No voyage legends, unverified statistics, soap/oil/cocoa experiments or universal energy-transfer percentages.

    Before class / supplies¶

    Teams <=3 (⅘/7/9). Per unit kit: three cardstock sheets (zone strip/tool), ruler, tray, plastic spoon, 40 cm tape, twenty 3 cm paper debris squares (ten per test). Per pupil: four record sheets/pencil. Teacher board/timer; adult pours 100 mL fresh water per test, 200 mL/team for two tests, in meeting 4 only; demo from issued kit. Class: one graduated plastic pouring cup and one roll of paper towels for drying/spills. Pretest spoon/card scoop and paper pickup within 30 seconds, use a fresh ten-square set each test; soaking/sinking is recorded, not replaced silently. Reuse tools, retain charts, dispose wet paper after drying.

    Copy these ready cards to board, no separate research required: NOAA light zones, retrieved October 4, 2026: sunlight 0-200 m; twilight 200-1,000 m; below 1,000 m no sunlight. Deeper subdivisions: midnight 1,000-4,000 m, abyss 4,000-6,000 m, hadal 6,000 m and deeper. Boundaries are teaching divisions, not equal-width physical layers. Draw a not-to-scale strip. Food-model cards: hypothetical photosynthetic producer P; small consumer Q eats P; larger R eats Q; decomposer D breaks down remains. These stipulated relationships teach arrow direction, not unverified species diets.

    SAFETY¶

    Background, vocabulary, and troubleshooting:

    Zone = depth/light region; producer = makes food; consumer = eats organisms; decomposer = breaks down dead matter; model = limited representation. Misconception: removing one card proves all organisms will die. If asked "Are all producers sun-powered?" No; some use chemical energy; this simplified model uses photosynthesis. Religious creation care motivates responsibility, not predicted cleanup success. No actual pollution, animals, chemicals, glass, tasting or stagnant water. Adult pours/empties, dry spills immediately, stop/report unsafe setups. Indoor paper/water work, no winter outing or unseen home research.

    Meeting 1: exactly 40 minutes¶

    1. 0-4: Wonder/care question: "What changes with depth?"
    2. 4-10: Read NOAA cards, model five labeled depth bands, not stacked liquids.
    3. 10-22: Each labels five zones/depth bounds on not-to-scale strip and marks which regions permit sunlight; team compares cards.
    4. 22-30: Peer checks boundaries/model scale; revise one misleading spacing or caption. "Does equal paper spacing mean equal ocean depth?"
    5. 30-35: Individual three light regions, five labels and scale limitation.
    6. 35-40: Save dated strips/count/clear.

    Meeting 2: exactly 40 minutes¶

    1. 0-4: Recall light/producer relationship.
    2. 4-9: Model P -> Q -> R, arrows food-to-eater; D receives remains.
    3. 9-21: Each draws stipulated feeding web, labels roles/arrows and states what is hypothetical; add light as energy source, not an organism.
    4. 21-30: Remove Q card on paper; identify represented links lost and an uncertain possible effect, not a guaranteed cascade.
    5. 30-35: Each explains arrow/role and a model limitation; "What source would we need to name real species?"
    6. 35-40: File diagrams/count tools/clear.

    Meeting 3: exactly 40 minutes¶

    1. 0-4: "How can collecting debris help people and creation?"
    2. 4-9: Criterion >=8 of ten paper squares collected in 30 seconds; limit one spoon/card tool/40 cm tape, no claim about dissolved contaminants.
    3. 9-21: Design two scoop shapes and build dry prototype from cardstock.
    4. 21-30: Dry-tray ten-square practice; every pupil operates/records, choose one change for wet test.
    5. 30-35: Individual criterion/constraint, practice count and design reason.
    6. 35-40: Save tools/remaining squares, count/clear.

    Meeting 4: exactly 40 minutes¶

    1. 0-4: Retrieve dry-test plan; distinguish paper debris from real marine waste.
    2. 4-9: Adult water setup/safety; fixed ten squares, 30 seconds, spoon/tool.
    3. 9-21: Test version 1, record collected 0-10 and sinking; rotate operator/ counter/recorder. Revise one scoop edge, not water volume.
    4. 21-30: Adult empties/resets with fresh 100 mL, fresh ten-square set, same setup/time; retest. Each draws two bars and makes whole-number comparison.
    5. 30-35: Individual results/limit and stewardship choice; "Does clear water mean safe drinking water?" No.
    6. 35-40: Adult empties clean water, collect wet paper, dry trays/count/store.

    What success looks like¶

    Meets: zone/food models with limits, criterion and real repeated counts/chart. Grade 3/support: read-aloud depth labels/0-10 graph. Grade 4/challenge: zone scale error and collection-versus-contamination tradeoff. Sinking paper: record limitation, no replacement during a timed test. Water not approved: same ten-token dry tray/time tests, clearly dry debris model, no flotation evidence. No tray: dry desk boundary, same criterion. Early finish: explain what a real ocean trial would need. Creation-care source: paraphrased Laudato Si'.

    Family: We studied marine models and retested debris collection. Ask, "What did your model leave out?" Optional: discuss reducing waste.

    Teacher artifact pack¶

    • Four-part student packet (print/no-print): (1) Ocean light strip fields for five zone names, depth bounds, sunlight/twilight/no sunlight, and not to scale because ___; (2) Feeding model with light -> P -> Q -> R, arrows labeled food to eater, D/remains, and hypothetical limitation; (3) Tool plan with two scoop sketches, criterion >=8/10 in 30 s, 40 cm tape limit, and dry-practice count; (4) Wet test with 100 mL, 10 fresh squares, V1 collected/sank, one edge changed, V2 collected/sank, two bars, and real-ocean limitation. With no printer, pupils copy these exact headings across four record sheets.
    • Answer/evidence guidance: Zone key: sunlight 0-200 m, twilight 200-1,000 m, midnight 1,000-4,000 m, abyss 4,000-6,000 m, hadal 6,000 m and deeper; below 1,000 m has no sunlight. Feeding arrows run P to Q to R, with D receiving remains; removing Q directly removes the represented P-to-Q and Q-to-R links but does not prove all organisms die. Collection counts are 0-10; accept improvement, no change, or decline when honestly charted under equal water/time/token conditions.
    • Finished worked example: Show a not-to-scale zone strip with correct boundaries; a P -> Q -> R diagram labeled hypothetical; and scoop data dry practice 6/10; wet V1 5 collected, 2 sank; change: fold a 1 cm front lip; wet V2 8 collected, 1 sank. The finished bars reach 5 and 8, and the claim reads The lip met our paper-token criterion in V2; this does not show removal of dissolved pollution or safe drinking water.
    • Board/visual setup: Post the NOAA-derived depth card and a deliberately not-to-scale vertical strip, then the feeding-arrow convention. For meetings 3-4, display a ten-box token array, 30-second timer chart, 100 mL each test, before/after bar axes 0-10, and a wet-area boundary labeled ADULT POURS/EMPTIES.
    • Discussion questions/misconceptions: “Does equal paper height mean equal ocean depth?” (no), “Which way does the feeding arrow point?” (from food to eater), “What can we conclude after removing Q?” (represented links are lost; broader effects uncertain), “Does clear tray water become drinkable?” (no), and “Why use fresh ten-square sets?” (so soaking from test 1 does not change test 2). Correct the claims that every producer uses sunlight or that a paper scoop models all pollution.
    • Exact kit, label, reset: Per team: 3 cardstock sheets, 1 ruler, 1 sorting tray, 1 plastic spoon, 40 cm tape, 20 paper debris squares in bags V1 and V2; per pupil: 4 record sheets, 1 pencil; class: 1 plastic graduated cup, <=200 mL fresh water per team, 1 towel roll. Label W14-17 | assigned team ID | DRY KIT and separate wet bags V1 10/V2 10. Adult drains water; spread wet paper for disposal after drying, wash/dry cup per school routine, dry tray/spoon fully, return ruler, preserve tools/diagrams, and replenish only counted dry sets.
    • Unfinished work: Keep dry diagrams and prototype in the team kit with a stage card zones, feeding, dry plan, or wet test. If wet testing cannot finish, discard/dry used tokens, record the interruption, and begin later with fresh 100 mL and a complete labeled ten-square set; never carry standing water between meetings.
    • Annotated error interpretation/reteach: Mark sunlight zone 0-1,000 m with the boundary card; reverse arrows shows eater as food—reverse; replacing sunk squares mid-test denominator changed; V1 5/V2 8 but bar at 9 graph mismatch; a missed criterion engineering evidence, not failure of stewardship. Reteach each strand separately: order zone cards, physically pass a “food” card along arrows, then count ten dry squares into a frame before one timed demonstration.
    • Safety and supervision: Severity moderate because spilled water can cause slips or contact nearby electrical items; likelihood unlikely with shallow trays, 100 mL pours, and immediate drying. Adult ratio is 1 adult to no more than 9 teams/25 pupils, with the adult alone pouring/emptying and all wet work visible. No tasting, animals, actual waste, glass, stagnant storage, or outlets in the wet zone. The masking tape is a commercial tape article; record the exact manufacturer/product in Safety_Data_Sheet_Register.csv. Keep that product ON HOLD until school approval and SDS applicability are resolved.
    • Print accessibility: Use 14-point minimum, textured/patterned zone bands with written depths, arrowheads large enough to trace, and 0-10 graph cells at least 8 mm high. Read source cards aloud; offer large-print cards, tactile zone strips/tokens, a scribe, or dry-only handling while retaining individual interpretation. Confirm monochrome printing preserves zone boundaries and that no meaning relies on blue shading.

    • Finished example: Finished example: the packet contains correct zone boundaries, a labeled hypothetical P -> Q -> R feeding model, dry practice 6/10, wet V1 5 collected, 2 sank, a 1 cm front-lip change, and wet V2 8 collected, 1 sank.
    • Board setup: Board setup: post the depth card and not-to-scale strip, feeding-arrow convention, ten-token array, 30-second timer, 100 mL each test, 0-10 before/after axes, and adult wet-work boundary.
    • Kit label: Kit label: mark each dry kit W14-17, the school-assigned team identifier, and DRY KIT; mark the two separate wet-token bags V1 10 and V2 10.
    • Annotated exemplar: Annotated exemplar: mark the zone boundaries and not-to-scale warning, food-to-eater arrows, equal 100 mL and ten-token conditions, front-lip change, bars at 5 and 8, and the real-ocean limitation.
