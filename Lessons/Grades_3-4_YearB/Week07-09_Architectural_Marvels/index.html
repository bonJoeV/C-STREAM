    Weeks 7-9: Architectural Marvels¶

    Lesson at a glance¶

    Grades 3-4; Weekly B; Structures; three 40-minute meetings. Objective: draw three labeled views, build a sacred gathering-space model and revise stability/access using two tests. Why: architecture combines purposeful form with support and users' needs. Catholic/CST-C2: welcoming gathering space; CST-E1 criteria/limits; CST-E2 retest; CST-A1 three-view model; CST-A2 purposeful light/shape. Official alignment VERIFICATION REQUIRED. Technology None; developing. Prep 15; cleanup 5 each. No claims about every cathedral builder's motives or current famous-building completion dates.

    Before class / supplies¶

    Teams <=3 (⅘/7/9). Per unit kit: 20 x 20 cm cardboard base, three cardstock sheets, ruler, blunt scissors, 80 cm tape. Per pupil: three paper sheets/ pencil. Teacher board/timer; draw simple plan/front/side of a gathering room with columns, beam and window. Demo rolled card column under folded card roof; no stock-photo purchase needed. Teacher checks recycled edges; stored on bases.

    SAFETY¶

    Background, vocabulary, and troubleshooting:

    Column = upright support; beam = crossing support; plan = top view; arch = curved structural form needing supports. Paper arch is not automatically a stone arch. Misconception: symmetry guarantees stability/beauty. If asked "Must every church look this way?" No; our model applies a welcoming purpose, not a universal sacred-building rule. Desk model 30 cm height cap; no heavy loads, shaking tables, fans, sharp tools or real shelter use. Scissors seated; report loose/damaged model. Indoor primary, stored dry.

    Meeting 1: exactly 40 minutes¶

    1. 0-4: "How can a gathering space welcome people?" Optional original prayer.
    2. 4-10: Model plan/front/side, label column/beam/window; distinguish support from visual purpose.
    3. 10-22: Each draws three views; include entrance >=4 cm wide, two supports and purposeful window; team agrees design.
    4. 22-30: Fold/roll sample column/roof, ten-second free-standing test; revise one confusing view from peer comments.
    5. 30-35: Each labels three views/criterion/limit and welcome choice.
    6. 35-40: Save views/samples, count tools, clear.

    Meeting 2: exactly 40 minutes¶

    1. 0-4: Retrieve plan/30 cm cap, stock limit.
    2. 4-9: Model same flat base and hands-off ten-second stability test.
    3. 9-21: Build card model, each contributes support/window/entrance.
    4. 21-30: Test standing twice and entrance width; record pass/not yet/cm, propose one support revision.
    5. 30-35: Each annotates actual model against views and test result.
    6. 35-40: Store bases safely, count scissors/rulers, clear scraps.

    Meeting 3: exactly 40 minutes¶

    1. 0-4: "Which recorded test suggests the change?"
    2. 4-9: Model support change without adding unbudgeted stock.
    3. 9-21: Revise/retest twice ten seconds, same base; check >=4 cm entrance.
    4. 21-30: Neighbor interprets model views; every pupil explains one purposeful light/form decision, not a beauty contest.
    5. 30-35: Individual before/after results, updated views and access limit: model entrance is not a building-code certification.
    6. 35-40: Count/store or flatten models; archive dated views.

    What success looks like¶

    Meets: three understandable views, criteria/limits, actual retest and purposeful welcome/form explanation. Grade 3/support: view frames/precut parts/dictation. Grade 4/challenge: structural-versus-visual tradeoff. Collapse: record failure, widen supports/retest, no hidden extra tape. No cardboard: folded cardstock base, state changed material. Early finish: compare views to actual built model.

    Family: We joined sacred-space design, support tests and visual purpose. Ask, "Which feature welcomes a visitor?" Optional: notice a window in a picture.

    Teacher artifact pack¶

    • Student architecture packet (print/no-print): Use three sheets labeled PLAN (top), FRONT, and SIDE, each with Name/team/date, column, beam, window/light purpose, entrance width ___ cm, and leader-line labels. Add a test card with Height <=30 cm, Hands-off stability trial 1/2: stood 10 s?, Entrance >=4 cm? actual ___ cm, Revision, Retest 1/2, and How this choice welcomes a user. With no printer, pupils fold three plain sheets into these exact frames and copy the test card on the back.
    • Evidence/answer guidance: All three views must correspond sufficiently for a peer to find the entrance, two supports, and window. Accept any sacred gathering-space form; no particular church style is required. Passing evidence is two hands-off 10-second stands and an entrance measurement at least 4 cm after revision. A failed stand is acceptable evidence when accurately recorded and used to justify a support change.
    • Completed worked example: Show a model with a 20 x 20 cm base, two 5 cm rolled columns, a folded beam, a 5 cm entrance, and a high side window. Record before: 6 s, 4 s; entrance 5 cm; change: widen both column feet by folding 1 cm tabs; after: 10 s, 10 s; entrance still 5 cm. Annotate matching features across the three views and label the window “high opening directs light toward gathering area—visual purpose, not structural support.”
    • Visual/board/chart setup: Draw aligned plan/front/side boxes with projection lines between the entrance and columns. Post gauges for 30 cm MAX, 4 cm MIN, and a two-row before/after stability chart. Keep a separate SUPPORT / VISUAL PURPOSE / ACCESS sorting area so a feature can serve more than one purpose only when explained.
    • Discussion with acceptable answers: Ask “Can a symmetrical model still fall?” (yes, weak joints/narrow bases can fail), “How do we know the opening meets our model criterion?” (measure its narrowest clear width), “Does 4 cm certify a real building?” (no), and “Must a sacred space look like our example?” (no). Correct the idea that decoration or symmetry alone proves stability.
    • Kit contents, labels, reset: Per team: 1 cardboard base 20 x 20 cm, 3 cardstock sheets, 1 metric ruler, 1 blunt scissors, 80 cm masking tape; per pupil: 3 plain-paper sheets, 1 pencil. Label base underside and storage bay W07-09 | assigned team ID | SACRED-SPACE MODEL; label tool pouch 1 RULER + 1 BLUNT SCISSORS. Return counted tools, wind remaining tape on a card marked with approximate remainder, keep models upright on their assigned bases, and flatten only models the teacher has marked archive complete.
    • Unfinished work: Store the packet beneath the matching model base with a flag views, first test, revision, or retest. Photographing is not a substitute. At the next meeting, resume the flagged stage with the existing tape allowance; do not add stock to make up for interrupted time.
    • Annotated exemplar/error/reteach: Mark views whose entrance shifts sides views do not yet agree; a hand-held “pass” test invalid—hands-off required; a 3.5 cm opening model criterion not yet met; a collapsed roof evidence about support, not student ability. Reteach with three view frames around one simple box, then rebuild one column with a broader foot and repeat a timed hands-off check.
    • Safety and supervision: Severity minor to moderate (scissor cut, sharp recycled edge, or falling desk model); likelihood unlikely after edge inspection and seated cutting. Adult ratio: 1 adult to no more than 9 teams/25 pupils, with all cutting visible and adult handling any non-blunt trimming. Models remain below 30 cm and carry no loads. The masking tape is a commercial tape article; record the exact manufacturer/product in Safety_Data_Sheet_Register.csv. Keep that product ON HOLD until school approval and SDS applicability are resolved.
    • Print accessibility: Use 14-point labels, high-contrast view borders, arrows with text, and raised/tactile dots if needed to distinguish entrance/support/window. Supply precut or precreased parts, adapted scissors, a ruler with tactile marks, oral labeling, or scribing. Test grayscale printing at 100%; verify projection lines do not merge and provide an enlarged single-view page when three panels are visually crowded.
    • Student material: Student material: provide plan, front, and side sacred-space sheets plus the test card for height, two hands-off stability trials, entrance width, revision, retest, and welcoming purpose.
    • Finished example: Finished example: the 20 x 20 cm sacred-space model has two rolled columns, a folded beam, a 5 cm entrance, a high side window, and recorded stability improving from 6/4 seconds to 10/10 seconds after wider column feet.
    • Visual example: Visual example: draw aligned plan/front/side views of the same entrance, columns, beam, and window, with projection lines and separate SUPPORT, VISUAL PURPOSE, and ACCESS labels.
    • Kit label: Kit label: mark the base underside and storage bay W07-09, the school-assigned team identifier, and SACRED-SPACE MODEL; mark the tool pouch 1 RULER + 1 BLUNT SCISSORS.
    • Common error: Common error: if the entrance moves between views or a hand-held model is called stable, annotate views do not agree or hands-off test required before one support revision and retest.
