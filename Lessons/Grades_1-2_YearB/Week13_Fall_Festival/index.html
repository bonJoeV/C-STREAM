    Fall Festival¶

    Lesson at a glance¶

    Field Teacher plan
    Grade / track / unit Grades 1-2 / Weekly B / Reflection
    Native time / scope 1 meeting(s), 30 minutes each including cleanup / OPTIONAL
    Domains / big idea A, M / A festival can display evidence and interpret an anonymous class count.
    Student objective / why "I can clarify a work sample and compare learning-choice data." A specific explanation is more meaningful than free-play stations.
    Catholic connection Give a specific respectful comment to each partner; gratitude is welcomed but not scored as mastery. This is a classroom application, not a historical quotation or official academic standard.
    Local standards CST-A3: artistic communication, critique and revision; CST-M2: represent and interpret data/patterns. Only the evidence below supports these local claims.
    Technology requirement None. No digital device or home access needed.
    Difficulty / prep / cleanup Guided; Light, 10-15 minutes; final 4 minutes of every meeting.

    Before class¶

    1. Assign teams of at most three: 4 / 5 / 7 / 9 kits for 10 / 15 / 20 / 25 pupils. Keep work at tables; distribute materials, not roaming station rotations.
    2. Prepare two fall records per child; draw observe/build/code preference columns. If records are absent, mark fresh work clearly.
    3. Put two plain-paper sheets per child per meeting and crayons at tables. Draw this record on the board: plan or prediction / actual result / reason or revision. Children copy boxes/icons, not paragraphs. Teacher dates and saves each child's record between meetings.
    4. Read the SAFETY section, pretest the actual task with one issued kit and put a roster beside the timer. Demonstrate orally with objects; read every direction aloud. No independent reading, Year A completion or family donation is a prerequisite.

Plain paper includes one work/model page and one individual evidence page per meeting. Supplies are counted before issue and after return; the teacher demonstrates with an issued kit, not an unlisted extra.

    Vocabulary and teacher background¶

    Evidence = support; feedback = useful comment; preference = a choice, not a skill score.

    An anonymous preference display does not measure competence. Comparable records support a change claim only when the child explains it.

    Common misconception: Every remembered project proves mastery.

    If asked: "Who wins the festival?" Nobody is ranked; each child communicates one specific example.

    SAFETY¶

    Table galleries only; no food awards, device replays, public names or crowding. Keep aisles/exits clear. Stop for damaged materials; do not improvise a hazardous replacement. No student handles loose batteries or teacher cutting tools.

    Meeting 1: Fall evidence festival - 30 minutes¶

    1. 0-3: Show an example claim and ask what record supports it.
    2. 3-7: Model a two-sample visual explanation and one specific helpful comment.
    3. 7-20: Each child makes a visual explanation of a changed feature, receives peer feedback and revises. Place one anonymous card in a favorite-experience column; count at tables.
    4. 20-26: Each child explains one change and compares two graph counts; distinguish preference from achievement.
    5. 26-30: Store private portfolios, count cards and clear gallery tables. Collect each child's dated evidence, count kit items, return tools and clear tables. Cleanup is part of the period, not time added afterward.

    Questions to ask¶

    • What changed in the work you shared?
    • Does the graph show skill or preference?

    Individual evidence and success¶

    Grade 1: Point to an actual changed feature and compare two counts.

    Grade 2: Explain a visual revision and interpret the preference graph without calling it a mastery score.

    Teacher visits three table teams during each of the first three four-minute blocks of practice (7-19), listening to or observing each member briefly; use the remaining practice minute and 20-26 for unfinished checks and comparison. At 25, this is nine table visits, not 25 presentations. Where a meeting specifies waves, use its supervised wave/check schedule instead of simultaneous table visits. Children keep separate records and partners may not answer for each other. Score each stated criterion not yet / prompted / independent; record absent/unobserved evidence as such. A group product, attendance, attractive art or a certificate is not individual mastery. OPTIONAL reflection does not replace missed core evidence.

    Support and challenge¶

    Support / nonreader access: Two pictured examples and a quiet paired audience. Adult scribing, pointing, tactile/large pieces and a seated partner-directed action can show understanding; do not score motor speed.

    Challenge: Explain why two unlike tasks cannot show measured growth. Do not add an unprepared activity or extend the native period.

    If things go wrong / indoor alternative¶

    No portfolio: make a first and revised observation drawing today and label both fresh; annual progress remains unassessed.

    Indoor: The complete primary lesson is indoors at tables; Minnesota weather, internet access and daylight are not prerequisites.

    Family copy - optional, no routine homework¶

    Our optional festival shared specific examples and interpreted a class preference graph. Ask: "What changed in the work you shared?" Optional: ask for one explained revision. No purchase, donation, device, home teaching or required take-home project.

    Sources and claim boundaries¶

    These are local program competencies, not official Minnesota/Archdiocesan benchmark numbers. See local standards and the source register. Catholic care, dignity and honest-inquiry applications follow that register; no unsupported scientist/saint biography, Scripture quotation or devotional history is required to execute this lesson. No external historical or official numeric claim is made.

    Error interpretation and reteaching¶

    • Common error interpretation: Compare the pupil's plan, action, and actual result. A mismatch means the prediction, sequence, condition, measurement, or explanation needs another check; it does not establish low ability, poor effort, or weak faith. Specifically guard against: Every remembered project proves mastery.
    • Reteaching option within existing time: Use the lesson's own fallback/support, not a new activity: No portfolio: make a first and revised observation drawing today and label both fresh; annual progress remains unassessed. Then apply **Support / nonreader access: Two pictured examples and a quiet paired audience. Adult scribing, pointing, tactile/large pieces and a seated partner-directed action can show understanding; do not score motor speed. ** Re-model only 3-7: Model a two-sample visual explanation and one specific helpful comment., let the pupil repeat one smallest safe step, and collect a new independent result in a different color or on a fresh line so the first evidence remains visible.

    Safety, accessibility, and technology release checks¶

    • Safety risk classification: Severity low; likelihood unlikely when screened materials stay at the assigned table and the written controls are followed. The credible harm is minor and first-aid-level. Primary hazard: crowding, blocked egress, or exposure of pupil-identifying work Existing control: Table galleries only; no food awards, device replays, public names or crowding. Keep aisles/exits clear. Stop for damaged materials; do not improvise a hazardous replacement. No student handles loose batteries or teacher cutting tools. Stop immediately for injury, ingestion, overheating, breakage, spill, unexpected allergy/sensitivity, unsafe movement, or loss of the defined work area; follow school reporting rather than improvising.
    • Adult supervision ratio: Keep one responsible adult actively supervising each simultaneously active class area, with pupils in three. The adult must be able to see every active team and personally controls teacher-only cutting, calibration, product dispensing, and damaged-item isolation. If the school ratio or available adults cannot support every active area, reduce the number of simultaneous teams/waves; do not reduce supervision.
    • SDS/product gate: HOLD - exact product approval required. The issued kit specifies these commercial product categories: whiteboard marker. A category name is not approval. Before issue, add or complete the applicable row in the Safety Data Sheet register with exact product and manufacturer, current SDS revision/location, reviewer/date, storage/disposal, and approval status. Keep this product pathway on HOLD and do not substitute until the school safety lead approves that exact product.
    • Print/accessibility check: Print one test slip at 100% in black and white. Confirm at least 14-point body text, high contrast without color-only meaning, unclipped labels, and enough space for a large drawing or adult scribing. Read every prompt aloud and permit the lesson's stated picture cues, pointing, speech, tactile/large materials, seated role, extra response space, and scribing without lowering "I can clarify a work sample and compare learning-choice data." A specific explanation is more meaningful than free-play stations.
