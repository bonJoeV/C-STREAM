    Weeks 29-31: Growing Things¶

    Lesson at a glance¶

    Field Teacher information
    Grade / unit / title Grades 1-2 / Growth / Growing Things
    Time / domains Three 30-minute meetings at roughly weekly intervals / C, S, M
    Big idea Repeated observations show changes; unequal growth alone does not explain causes.
    Student objective "I can observe a seedling, measure the same way, and describe what changed."
    Why Growers use records and responsible care, not guesses or tallest-plant contests.
    Catholic connection Care for plants/resources, honestly report no growth too; CCC 2415. The Sower is a faith parable, not an experimental explanation.
    Standards Local CST-C3: care/resource choice; CST-S1: dated observation/question; CST-S3: seed/seedling model; CST-M1: height comparison; CST-M2: dated record/class display. Official benchmarks: VERIFICATION REQUIRED.
    Technology None primary path. A teacher photo of changes is optional, not required.
    Difficulty / prep / cleanup Moderate; 20 minutes first setup plus 2-3 minutes school-day care check; 5 minutes cleanup each meeting.

    Before class¶

    1. Check school seed/potting-mix allergy guidance. Select one untreated bean or radish variety; read packet depth/water guidance. No fertilizer, pesticides or edible harvest in class.
    2. Prestart two teacher backup pots 7-10 days earlier. Draw three dated backup observations if live growing conditions are unavailable; clearly identify teacher/picture data.
    3. Teacher makes drainage holes before class. Put pots in leakproof trays by a safe bright indoor window, not on a freezing sill/heater. No electrical grow light required.
    4. Mark a height baseline at the soil surface. Assign a named adult to check moisture on school days and before weekends; water when needed, not automatically daily. Keep care at school, including school breaks or arrange approved adult care.
    5. Make a three-column record: date / drawing / height. Leave "no shoot yet" distinct from a missing measurement.

    Supplies¶

    • Per student: one folded record sheet, pencil/crayon, individual roster check.
    • Per team of 2-3: one pot with drainage, two seeds, about 200 mL commercial potting mix, tray space, ruler or equal cubes; measuring tool reused.
    • Class: one small water jug, scoop, spill cloth, handwashing access, board display.
    • Teacher-only: seed packet, hole tool, backup pots, plant care log; optional plant pictures.

    Vocabulary / teacher background¶

    Seed = contains a young plant and stored food; germinate = begin growing; seedling = young plant; nutrient = substance needed for growth; data = recorded observations. Many seeds need water, oxygen and suitable temperature to germinate. Seedlings need light for making food, plus water/air/nutrients and support. Soil is a useful growing medium, not plant food or a universal requirement; plants can grow without soil under appropriate conditions. This is a longitudinal observation, not a controlled experiment proving needs. Misconception: an unsprouted seed means its caretaker was bad or lacked faith. If asked "Why did mine not grow?" Seed viability, temperature, water and other factors may matter; our records do not isolate the cause.

    Meeting 1: plant and predict (30 minutes)¶

    1. 0-4: Show seed/seedling; state objective and care boundary. Optional original prayer asks for careful stewardship.
    2. 4-8: Explain seed food versus seedling needs; demonstrate packet-based planting depth and soil-surface ruler baseline.
    3. 8-18: Teams scoop mix, plant two seeds, lightly water according to packet and label pot/date. Each child draws what is visible and predicts a future change orally.
    4. 18-22: Make a care log and place pots in trays; choose an adult checker. Explain that not every seed will sprout.
    5. 22-25: Individual evidence: point to seed/seedling, name one care action and one observation.
    6. 25-30: Cleanup/handwashing.

    Meeting 2: observe and measure (30 minutes)¶

    1. 0-4: Retrieve pots; compare to first dated drawing, not another team's tallest plant.
    2. 4-8: Teacher models height from soil surface to top of shoot, same tool each time. Grade 1 may use equal cubes; Grade 2 nearest whole cm. Do not include pot height.
    3. 8-19: Each child measures or points to the baseline and records one observation; record "no visible shoot" truthfully. Compare own two dates. Teacher checks individuals while partners work.
    4. 19-23: Inspect moisture with teacher and update care log; discuss what we know versus what we cannot yet explain.
    5. 23-25: Optional Sower paraphrase (Matthew 13:3-9) and care reflection: plant growth is not a score for anyone's faith.
    6. 25-30: Cleanup/handwashing.

    Meeting 3: communicate changes (30 minutes)¶

    1. 0-4: Recall measurement baseline and previous record.
    2. 4-13: Final measurement/drawing using same units; each student compares two dated observations.
    3. 13-19: Display one current height per team on class chart; separate "no shoot" from "not recorded." Grade 2 explains why different heights do not identify one cause.
    4. 19-23: Pairs present change/no change and a question. Keep plants at school unless a family chooses a free take-home plant; provide teacher care instead where needed.
    5. 23-25: Individual exit: show two dated observations and one responsible care decision.
    6. 25-30: Cleanup/handwashing.

    Questions / success¶

    What changed since that date? Where does the ruler start? What if a seed does not sprout? Does our evidence prove why plants differ? - Grade 1: two dated drawings, correct baseline demonstration, longer/shorter/same comparison and one care action, all oral/drawn. - Grade 2: at least two dated measures in the same units and explanation of change; distinguish observation from an untested cause. - Score not yet / prompted / independent for observation, measurement, comparison and care; never grade germination success or home participation.

    If things go wrong / indoor fallback¶

    No sprouts: retain genuine record and measure backup plant; do not relabel teacher data as student's. Mold: teacher removes affected pot sealed, cleans tray per school policy, supplies pictures. No bright window/no live growing allowed: use the three dated teacher drawings and equal-length paper plant models; assess reading/comparing observations, not live plant care mastery. Indoor work remains available all Minnesota winter; no outdoor planting required.

    SAFETY / support / challenge¶

    No tasting seeds/plants/mix, unknown soil, spraying faces, standing water near electronics, student puncturing or glass pots. Wash hands. Teacher handles spill/mold and packet safety. Support: prefilled pots, picture steps, accessible table, adult scribing, thick equal blocks; nonreaders point/draw/tell. Challenge: Grade 2 use differences between two heights or propose a future fair test without claiming this one proves it.

    Cleanup / family / sources¶

    Return tools, wipe trays/tables, put labeled pots safely back, wash hands and store records; adult checks care outside meetings. Family snippet: We kept dated plant records and compared change honestly, including no growth. Our Catholic connection was stewardship. Ask: "What did your evidence show, and what are you still wondering?" Optional: notice a plant or picture at home; no seed purchase, watering homework or device required. CCC 2415; optional Matthew 13 and 1 Corinthians 3:6 references use school-approved Bible (wording VERIFICATION REQUIRED). Seed-specific timing/depth must be verified against the selected packet; no universal sprouting deadline is promised.

    Error interpretation and reteaching¶

    • Common error interpretation: Compare the pupil's plan, action, and actual result. A mismatch means the prediction, sequence, condition, measurement, or explanation needs another check; it does not establish low ability, poor effort, or weak faith. Specifically guard against: an unsprouted seed means its caretaker was bad or lacked faith.
    • Reteaching option within existing time: Use the lesson's own fallback/support, not a new activity: No sprouts: retain genuine record and measure backup plant; do not relabel teacher data as student's. Mold: teacher removes affected pot sealed, cleans tray per school policy, supplies pictures. Then apply Support: prefilled pots, picture steps, accessible table, adult scribing, thick equal blocks; nonreaders point/draw/tell. Do not include pot height., let the pupil repeat one smallest safe step, and collect a new independent result in a different color or on a fresh line so the first evidence remains visible.

    Safety, accessibility, and technology release checks¶

    • Safety risk classification: Severity moderate; likelihood unlikely when the written controls and active supervision are used, and possible if those controls are skipped. A foreseeable incident could require first aid or equipment isolation. Primary hazard: spill, slip, allergy/sensitivity, or contact with an unapproved sample Existing control: No tasting seeds/plants/mix, unknown soil, spraying faces, standing water near electronics, student puncturing or glass pots. Wash hands. Teacher handles spill/mold and packet safety. Stop immediately for injury, ingestion, overheating, breakage, spill, unexpected allergy/sensitivity, unsafe movement, or loss of the defined work area; follow school reporting rather than improvising.
    • Adult supervision ratio: Keep one responsible adult actively supervising each simultaneously active class area, with pupils in 2-3. The adult must be able to see every active team and personally controls teacher-only cutting, calibration, product dispensing, and damaged-item isolation. If the school ratio or available adults cannot support every active area, reduce the number of simultaneous teams/waves; do not reduce supervision.
    • SDS/product gate: HOLD - exact product approval required. The issued kit specifies these commercial product categories: potting mix. A category name is not approval. Before issue, add or complete the applicable row in the Safety Data Sheet register with exact product and manufacturer, current SDS revision/location, reviewer/date, storage/disposal, and approval status. Keep this product pathway on HOLD and do not substitute until the school safety lead approves that exact product.
    • Print/accessibility check: Print one test slip at 100% in black and white. Confirm at least 14-point body text, high contrast without color-only meaning, unclipped labels, and enough space for a large drawing or adult scribing. Read every prompt aloud and permit the lesson's stated picture cues, pointing, speech, tactile/large materials, seated role, extra response space, and scribing without lowering "I can observe a seedling, measure the same way, and describe what changed."
