# Service-oriented STEM rubric

Select this when the lesson explicitly teaches a reasoned design/service
decision. Do not require every science investigation to be a deployed service
project. **October 2026.** C is Catholic identity; R is the explicit faith
teaching/reflection. A classroom prototype is not evidence of real-world impact.

## Source and age conditions

Use the [local Catholic standards](../Review/Local_Standards.md) and the verified
source record. A primary source for the social-teaching themes is the
[USCCB themes overview](https://www.usccb.org/beliefs-and-teachings/what-we-believe/catholic-social-teaching/seven-themes-of-catholic-social-teaching).
**VERIFICATION REQUIRED** for exact lesson paraphrases/quotations, source
sections, diocesan requirements and current approval. This is a local classroom
rubric, not an official USCCB/Archdiocesan scoring instrument.

Keep beneficiaries respectfully described. Do not simulate disability, use real
student medical profiles or deploy medical/assistive/structural/water-purification
prototypes without qualified adult review. Ordinary academic inquiry and
individual work are not less valuable or less Catholic than group outreach.

| Explicit objective/criterion | 1 Beginning | 2 Developing | 3 Meets Expectation | 4 Extends Learning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Identify a user need | Names a solution without a relevant need | Names user/need but link is incomplete | Names a concrete user need and shows how a design choice responds | Compares two choices using user constraints/feedback |
| Apply taught dignity/service principle | Proposed choice conflicts with the taught concern | Names the principle but cannot yet connect the choice | Explains how one choice respects the taught principle | Explains a tradeoff and a way to address an unintended exclusion |
| Explain stewardship | Does not yet identify a resource consequence | Names a resource but not the effect of the choice | Explains a specific reuse/waste choice and consequence | Compares alternatives using material/cost or test evidence |
| Distinguish prototype from impact | Claims a model has solved a real problem without evidence | Recognizes it is a model but cannot state the limit | Explains what was tested and what remains unproven for real use | Suggests a safe next test or appropriate adult consultation |

Use only selected objectives. Fill in the exact grade-appropriate success
condition; K can point to a saved material and explain whom a design helps.
Grades 5-6 can reason about two alternatives and limitations. **NE** sits
outside the scale. Access accommodations do not lower scores.

Ask: "Who might use it?" "What need are you addressing?" "What did the test
actually show?" "Which choice avoids waste?" "Who could be left out?"
Assess understanding of taught content, not motivation, charitable donations,
family resources, prayer practice or interior belief.

Service possibilities preserved from the earlier guide: classroom models of
accessible tools, stewardship systems, environmental observations and helpful
information designs. Partners and actual deployment are optional confirmed
extensions, never prerequisites to classroom mastery.
