# Week 16: Astronomy & the Star

## Lesson at a glance

Grades 3-4; Weekly A; Astronomy; **one 40-minute meeting**.
**Objective:** construct/measure a not-to-scale sky model, show a pattern and
distinguish observation from interpretation. **Why:** models reveal relationships
but cannot identify the Christmas star. Catholic/CST-C1: honest claim limits,
Magi as religious narrative; CST-S3: model/limit; CST-A1: two labeled views;
CST-M1: three cm positions/comparison. Official alignment **VERIFICATION
REQUIRED**. Technology **None**; introductory. Prep 10; cleanup 5 included.

## Before class and exact supplies

Teams <=3 (4/5/7/9); kit ruler and three cardstock markers labeled X/Y/Z.
Per pupil: two paper sheets/pencil. Teacher board/timer, draw side view positions
10/20/30 cm and front-view star dots. No star chart, biography or astronomical
distance claim needed. Demo from issued kit. Cards/rulers reusable, records saved.

## Vocabulary/background/SAFETY

Star = self-luminous astronomical object; planet = orbiting body, visible here
by reflected light; constellation = recognized sky pattern; model = selective
representation. Our Sun is a star. A flat pattern does not show actual star
distances. **Misconception:** neighboring dots mean neighboring stars in space.
**If asked "Was the Christmas star a comet?"** This lesson does not establish
its physical identity. The Magi narrative is religious interpretation, not our
experiment. No inherited firsts/saint labels or speculative travel distances.
Paper desk work; no Sun observation, laser or required night outing. Stop/report
damaged rulers; indoor primary works through Minnesota winter.

## Meeting 1: exactly 40 minutes

1. **0-4:** "Can a drawing tell us everything about the sky?" Optional prayer
   for wonder and truthful inquiry; paraphrase Magi seeking/worshiping.
2. **4-10:** Draw three front-view dots, then side view; label not to scale.
3. **10-22:** Every pupil places/measures markers at 10/20/30 cm and draws
   side/front views. Rotate tools; compare near/far and whole-number differences.
4. **22-30:** Move Y to 25 cm, redraw side view; discuss how similar-looking
   sky patterns can hide different depths. "What did the model omit?"
5. **30-35:** Each child submits three measurements, labeled model limit and two
   statements: one observable in model, one religious interpretation.
6. **35-40:** Count cards/rulers, save dated views, clear desks.

## Success/access/troubleshooting

Meets: three cm readings, two intelligible views, one model limitation and
correct claim distinction (C1/S3/A1/M1). Grade 3/support: marked positions,
pointing/dictation. Grade 4/challenge: explain how perspective confounds a
distance guess; no light-year calculation. Unclear front view: teacher draws
projected dots rather than asking pupils to crouch at desk edges. Missing ruler:
shared ruler turns, record positions actually checked. Early finish: propose
what real astronomical evidence would be needed.

**Family:** We compared model views and honest limits. Ask, "Does a sky pattern
show depth?" Optional: look at a sky picture, no outdoor/night homework.
