# Week 16: Astronomy & the Star

## Lesson at a glance

Grades 3-4; Weekly A; Astronomy; **one 40-minute meeting**.
**Objective:** construct/measure a not-to-scale sky model, show a pattern and
distinguish observation from interpretation. **Why:** models reveal relationships
but cannot identify the Christmas star. Catholic/CST-C1: honest claim limits,
Magi as religious narrative; CST-S3: model/limit; CST-A1: two labeled views;
CST-M1: three cm positions/comparison. Official alignment **VERIFICATION
REQUIRED**. Technology **None**; introductory. Prep 10; cleanup 5 included.

## Before class and exact supplies

Teams <=3 (4/5/7/9); kit ruler and three cardstock markers labeled X/Y/Z.
Per pupil: two paper sheets/pencil. Teacher board/timer, draw side view positions
10/20/30 cm and front-view star dots. No star chart, biography or astronomical
distance claim needed. Demo from issued kit. Cards/rulers reusable, records saved.

## Vocabulary/background/SAFETY

Star = self-luminous astronomical object; planet = orbiting body, visible here
by reflected light; constellation = recognized sky pattern; model = selective
representation. Our Sun is a star. A flat pattern does not show actual star
distances. **Misconception:** neighboring dots mean neighboring stars in space.
**If asked "Was the Christmas star a comet?"** This lesson does not establish
its physical identity. The Magi narrative is religious interpretation, not our
experiment. No inherited firsts/saint labels or speculative travel distances.
Paper desk work; no Sun observation, laser or required night outing. Stop/report
damaged rulers; indoor primary works through Minnesota winter.

## Meeting 1: exactly 40 minutes

1. **0-4:** "Can a drawing tell us everything about the sky?" Optional prayer
   for wonder and truthful inquiry; paraphrase Magi seeking/worshiping.
2. **4-10:** Draw three front-view dots, then side view; label not to scale.
3. **10-22:** Every pupil places/measures markers at 10/20/30 cm and draws
   side/front views. Rotate tools; compare near/far and whole-number differences.
4. **22-30:** Move Y to 25 cm, redraw side view; discuss how similar-looking
   sky patterns can hide different depths. "What did the model omit?"
5. **30-35:** Each child submits three measurements, labeled model limit and two
   statements: one observable in model, one religious interpretation.
6. **35-40:** Count cards/rulers, save dated views, clear desks.

## Success/access/troubleshooting

Meets: three cm readings, two intelligible views, one model limitation and
correct claim distinction (C1/S3/A1/M1). Grade 3/support: marked positions,
pointing/dictation. Grade 4/challenge: explain how perspective confounds a
distance guess; no light-year calculation. Unclear front view: teacher draws
projected dots rather than asking pupils to crouch at desk edges. Missing ruler:
shared ruler turns, record positions actually checked. Early finish: propose
what real astronomical evidence would be needed.

**Family:** We compared model views and honest limits. Ask, "Does a sky pattern
show depth?" Optional: look at a sky picture, no outdoor/night homework.

## Teacher artifact pack

- **Student material — model record (two pages or two sides, printable/no-print):** `Name/date/team`; table `Marker X ___ cm | Y ___ cm | Z ___ cm`; `Difference X to Y ___ cm; Y to Z ___ cm`; draw and label `SIDE VIEW—NOT TO SCALE` with observer end and three depths; draw `FRONT/SKY VIEW—NOT TO SCALE` with the projected X/Y/Z pattern; `After moving Y to 25 cm, the side view changed by ___ and the front pattern ___`; `One thing directly observable in our model: ___`; `One model limitation: ___`; `A religious interpretation from the Magi narrative: ___`; `The model can/cannot establish the physical identity of the Christmas star because ___.` No-print: reproduce this exact table and the two labeled view boxes on the board.
- **Expected evidence and acceptable answers:** Initial positions are X = 10 cm, Y = 20 cm, Z = 30 cm; whole-number differences are 10 cm and 10 cm. After Y moves to 25 cm, it is 15 cm from X and 5 cm from Z. Accept observations such as `the cards are at different measured depths` or `the front dots can form a pattern`; accept limitations including `not to scale`, `paper markers are not stars`, `the desk model omits true sizes/motion/brightness/large distances`, and `one front view hides depth`. Accept religious interpretation that the Magi understood a star as a sign and sought/worshiped Jesus, while requiring that this model cannot identify the event as a comet, planet, conjunction, or particular star.
- **Worked finished example:** The side view places X/10, Y/20, Z/30 along one ruler line and labels `observer here`; the front view shows three nearby dots forming a shallow V. After Y moves to 25, the side label changes and the pupil writes `Y moved 5 cm farther from the observer; a similar front pattern still does not tell the three depths.` Observable statement: `Z is 20 cm farther than X in this model.` Interpretation: `In the religious narrative, the star is understood as a sign guiding the Magi.` Limit: `These cardstock positions are not astronomical distances and do not identify the Christmas star.` Annotate measurement, projection, interpretation, and limit in four distinct brackets.
- **Board/visual setup:** Draw a horizontal `SIDE VIEW` baseline with an eye symbol at 0 and ticks at 10, 20, 25, 30 cm; above it draw a separate `FRONT/SKY VIEW` box with X/Y/Z dots. Connect each marker to its projected dot using dashed guide lines and write `same-looking neighbors may have different depths`. Create a T-chart `OBSERVATION/MODEL MEASUREMENT` versus `INTERPRETATION/BELIEF`, plus a box `MODEL OMITS: scale, real distance, size, motion, brightness`.
- **Discussion questions, acceptable answers, misconceptions:** `Are dots close together on paper necessarily close in space?` (`no`); `What did the ruler establish?` (`card positions in centimeters in this model only`); `What changed when Y moved?` (`its modeled depth and numerical differences`); `Why label not to scale?` (`centimeters do not represent real astronomical distances or sizes`); `What evidence would astronomers need for a physical identification?` (`repeated observations, positions over time, spectra/brightness or historical records, depending on the claim`); `What belongs to religious interpretation here?` (`the meaning of the sign in the Magi narrative`). Correct `constellation means stars are physically grouped` and `our model proves what the Christmas star was` explicitly.
- **Kit contents. Kit label: and Cleanup/Reset:** Each team kit has `1 metric ruler; 3 reusable cardstock markers labeled X, Y, Z`. Label it `C-STREAM G3-4 | W16 SKY DEPTH MODEL | X Y Z + 1 RULER`. Per pupil: `2 plain sheets; 1 pencil`. Reset by counting X/Y/Z as a matched set, erasing removable position marks from the work mat if used, returning the ruler unbent, flattening cards, and storing dated student views separately from reusable markers; report a missing label rather than substituting an unlabeled card.
- **Unfinished work:** Mark the latest completed stage: `three positions measured`, `side view`, `front view`, `Y moved/recalculated`, or `claim distinction`. Store both dated sheets in `W16 UNFINISHED`; reset the physical markers after recording the actual positions. On return, reconstruct from the pupil's recorded measurements and finish the missing representation or statement at school—no night-sky observation or home ruler task.
- **Annotated exemplar, Common error interpretation, and Reteach:** If a pupil writes X/Y/Z as 10/20/30 but draws all three at one side-view point, annotate `measurements correct; representation does not yet show depth`. If the pupil says `Y is 25 cm from Z`, use the ruler/number line to show `30 - 25 = 5`. If the observation says `The star guided the Magi`, move it to interpretation and replace the observation with a model fact. Reteach using only X at 10 and Z at 30: measure, draw side view, hide depth in a front-view sketch, then add Y once the distinction is clear.
- **Safety classification: Severity low; likelihood unlikely** after controls. Desk-based paper/ruler work has possible low-severity for paper cuts, ruler poking, or dropped materials; with seated use, intact rulers, and no crouching at desk edges, residual risk is **low / unlikely**. **Adult ratio: 1:25 maximum** for this seated paper model, with active room supervision and direct marker-positioning assistance when needed. No Sun viewing, outdoor/night requirement, laser, chemical, battery, or adhesive is used. **SDS is not applicable.**
- **Print/accessibility and technology:** Print at 100% with 12-point minimum type, bold X/Y/Z labels, high-contrast solid versus dashed lines, and text labels in addition to spatial position. Offer a full-page large-print version, tactile markers, raised ruler/talking measurement support already available at school, premarked 10/20/25/30 cm positions, oral description, pointing, or scribing. Verify on a black-and-white sample that side/front views and `NOT TO SCALE` remain unmistakable. No technology artifact applies.
