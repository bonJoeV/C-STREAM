# Week 18: New Year SMART Goals

## Lesson at a glance

Grades 3-4; Weekly A; Reflection; **one 40-minute meeting**.
**Objective:** write one achievable classroom goal, perform two practice checks
and use counts to choose a next action. **Why/big idea:** a tracker informs
practice, not promises success. Catholic/CST-C3: stewardship of effort/materials;
CST-M2: two labeled trial records; CST-M3: count/compare achieved criteria.
Official alignment **VERIFICATION REQUIRED**. Technology **None**;
introductory. Prep 10 minutes; cleanup 5 included. No prayer/holiness score.

## Before class and exact supplies

Per pupil: two sheets/pencil. Teams <=3 (4/5/7/9) share ruler. Board SMART:
Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound. Draw a 2 cm line example.
Practice goal: "By minute 30 I will label a drawing with a title, cm unit and
measured line length: three checked features." Teacher board/timer, demo uses
issued paper. Evidence requires no calendars/printed template.

## Vocabulary/background/SAFETY

Specific = clear action; measurable = observable count; achievable = realistic;
tracker = dated evidence record. SMART is a planning tool, not a guarantee.
**Misconception:** many goals ensure more growth. **If asked "Does missing a
goal mean God is disappointed?"** A practice result tells us what support/
next action to try; it does not measure a person's worth or faith.
Desk paper/rulers; no speed pressure, private faith/family disclosure or
compulsory daily tracking. Report broken tools; indoor primary.

## Meeting 1: exactly 40 minutes

1. **0-4:** Optional prayer for patient growth. "How would we know 'get better'
   happened?" Explain one practice goal, not three private goals.
2. **4-10:** Model SMART and tracker `trial/time | features 0-3 | next action`.
3. **10-20:** Each writes goal/reason/two actions; draws and labels first measured
   line. Peer checks title/unit/value; pupil records actual 0-3 count.
4. **20-30:** Choose action from missing feature, draw different length line,
   check three features again and compare counts. Deadline is this window.
5. **30-35:** Each child explains goal, two actual checks, count difference and next
   action; "Which action responded to your evidence?"
6. **35-40:** Date/file tracker, count rulers, clear desks; teacher notes who
   needs reteaching. Optional next-class check uses tracker during opening,
   not hidden additional required instruction.

## Success/access/troubleshooting

Meets: one SMART goal, two real 0-3 records and evidence-based next action with
responsible work choice. Grade 3/support: copy goal, oral/dictated reason.
Grade 4/challenge: explain why one successful attempt does not establish durable
skill and propose a later check. Unrealistic goal: shorten to today's three
features, not five projects. No change in counts: explain maintenance or missing
support; do not invent improvement. Early finish: change line length, same rubric.

**Family:** We practiced one measurable goal and compared checks. Ask, "What
did your tracker change about your next step?" Optional conversation only.

## Teacher artifact pack

- **Student material — goal-and-check sheet (print or copy exactly):** `Name ___  Date ___`;
  `By minute 30, I will ___`; `I will know because I will check these three
  visible features: 1 ___ 2 ___ 3 ___`; `This matters for my classroom work
  because ___`; `Action before check 1 ___`; then a table with columns
  `check | line length in cm | title? Y/N | cm unit? Y/N | measured value? Y/N
  | total 0-3 | next action`. Supply rows `1`, `2`, and `later check (optional)`.
  End with `My count changed from ___ to ___`; `The evidence says my next action
  is ___`; `A score does not tell a person's worth because ___`. The no-print
  version is the same headings on the board; pupils divide plain paper into the
  goal box and three-row table.
- **Expected evidence and acceptable answers:** The goal must name one action,
  the minute-30 deadline and three observable checks. The assigned practice
  goal may be copied. Each of the first two rows must record a real, different
  line length, three Y/N observations and the matching total. Accept a decrease,
  no change or increase if accurately recorded. The next action must address
  evidence: for example, `add cm after the number`, `write the title before I
  measure`, `repeat later to see whether 3/3 lasts`, or `ask for help reading the
  ruler`. Do not accept a promised `3/3`, invented improvement or `try harder`
  without a named action.
- **Worked and finished example:** `By minute 30, I will label a drawing with a
  title, cm unit, and measured line value.` Check 1: `6 cm; title Y; unit N;
  value Y; 2/3`; annotation: `The missing unit, not the learner, is the error.`
  Next action: `write "cm" beside every measurement`. Check 2: `9 cm; Y/Y/Y;
  3/3`. Finished conclusion: `My count rose 2 to 3 after I added the unit. I
  will do a later check because one 3/3 does not prove the habit will last.`
  Box the two actual counts, underline the action tied to the missing feature
  and circle the cautious later-check statement.
- **Visual example and Board setup:** Make four panels before class: (1) SMART with a one-line
  meaning for each letter; (2) the exact practice goal and a labeled 2 cm model;
  (3) the empty check table with `total = number of Y answers, 0-3`; (4)
  `Evidence -> smallest useful next action`, with arrows from `missing title`,
  `missing cm`, `missing value`, and `3/3 once` to the four acceptable actions
  above. Keep individual scores off the public board.
- **Discussion guidance:** Ask `Is "get better at math" measurable?` Accept
  `no, it has no visible action/count/deadline`; `Does a lower second count mean
  failure?` Accept `no, it may show a recording mistake, a harder attempt or
  support needed`; `Why change only the missing feature?` Accept `the evidence
  identifies a specific next step`; and `What can a 3/3 prove?` Accept `that
  this attempt met three checks, not guaranteed future success`. Correct the
  misconceptions that SMART means guaranteed, more goals are better, or a
  tracker measures goodness, faith or worth.
- **Kit contents. Kit label: and Cleanup/Reset:** Pack one envelope per class with exactly two plain
  sheets and one pencil per pupil, plus one metric ruler per team (10 pupils:
  20/10/4; 15: 30/15/5; 20: 40/20/7; 25: 50/25/9). Label it exactly
  `C-STREAM G3-4 | W18 SMART GOAL CHECKS | PAPER/PENCIL/RULER | RETURN RULERS`.
  At reset, count rulers and usable pencils, remove broken items for repair,
  recycle only blank offcuts, and file each dated sheet; leave no pupil goal on
  the board.
- **Unfinished work:** At minute 35, mark the last completed row and have the
  pupil write one precise restart cue, such as `measure check 2, then score its
  unit`. File it in the `W18 - TO RESUME` pocket for an opening check next class.
  Record missing second-check evidence as `not yet observed`; do not estimate
  the count, send it home as required work or convert it into a behavior mark.
- **Annotated exemplar, Common error interpretation, and Reteach:** `3/3 -> 3/3` can mean maintained
  performance, not no learning; `2/3 -> 2/3` may show that the chosen action did
  not address the missing feature; totals that disagree with Y/N marks are a
  counting/recording error; identical claimed line lengths require checking
  whether the second trial was actually new. Reteach with the 2 cm board model:
  point to title/unit/value, score it together, deliberately erase `cm`, choose
  `restore the unit`, and run one new line check. Use `I have evidence for ___;
  I do not yet have evidence for ___; next I will ___`.
- **Safety classification: Severity low; likelihood unlikely** when
  pupils remain seated and rulers stay flat. **Adult ratio: 1:25 maximum** for
  this seated paper-and-ruler lesson, with teams of no more than three and
  normal line of sight; provide closer 1:1 support if an individual plan
  requires it. Stop for splintered rulers, exposed pencil points used unsafely
  or throwing. No chemical product is used, so an SDS is **not applicable**.
- **Print/accessibility check — black-and-white:** Use black text and rules on white paper, 14-point
  minimum type (18-point large-print copy), no color-only marks, left-aligned
  prompts and at least 1.5 cm writing rows. Read every heading aloud; permit
  dictation, scribing, pointing to Y/N, or an oral explanation while the adult
  records exact words. Test one copy at 100% so the table does not clip; the
  board version must retain every field rather than shortening the task.
- **Technology compliance:** No technology artifact applies.
