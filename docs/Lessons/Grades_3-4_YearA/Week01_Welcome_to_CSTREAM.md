# Week 1: Welcome to C-STREAM

## Lesson at a glance

Grades 3-4; Weekly A; Foundations; **one 40-minute meeting**.
**Objective:** I can record two observations, ask one testable question and
demonstrate a fair turn. **Why:** curious questions and respectful cooperation
make shared investigations possible. C-STREAM joins Catholic identity, science,
tools, religion, engineering, arts and mathematics; this introduction assesses
observation and dignity, not mastery of every domain. Catholic connection:
honest reporting and including another learner put faith into action.
CST-C1 truthful observation versus prediction; CST-C2 fair access; CST-S1 detail
and question. Official alignment **VERIFICATION REQUIRED**. Technology **None**;
introductory, no prior course assumed. Prep 10 minutes; cleanup 5 included.

## Before class and exact supplies

Balanced teams <=3: 4/5/7/9. Fold one demonstration paper tent. Board:
`I see / I predict / I wonder`, objective and acronym chart; no bought handout

Per pupil: evidence sheet/pencil. Teacher board/timer; demo from issued supplies.

## Vocabulary and background

Observation = noticed evidence; prediction = expected result; question =
something to investigate; collaboration = working with fair access.
Science can revisit questions, not follow one compulsory recipe.
**Misconception:** a guess about strength is an observation; point to what was
actually seen. **If asked “Can faith answer how far this bends?”** We measure
the model; faith guides truthful, respectful work. Faith/reason paraphrase:
[Fides et Ratio opening](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_jp-ii_enc_14091998_fides-et-ratio.html).
No inherited biography recital or unattributed quotation.

## SAFETY

Desk paper only; no loads, launches, food or sharp tools. Rulers stay flat.
Stop damaged-material work and notify the teacher. Indoor primary, no weather
requirement.

## Meeting 1: exactly 40 minutes

1. **0-5:** Optional original prayer: “God, help us notice carefully and include
   one another.” Explain acronym with a tool, question and drawing example.
2. **5-10:** Show tent. “What can you see? What needs a test?” Model `two folds`
   as observation and `it will stand` as prediction.
3. **10-20:** Teams fold standing paper/card forms. Every child folds and measures
   whole-centimeter height; rotate roles.
4. **20-28:** Compare forms. Each records two visible details and one testable
   question. “What would stay the same?” This does not prove all card stronger.
5. **28-35:** Each child shows observation/question/fair-turn action. Partners
   propose one lab norm; teacher records individual evidence.
6. **35-40:** Return rulers, flatten/save models, collect dated sheets, clear
   tables. Agree to report failures honestly and share tools.

## Success, access and troubleshooting

Meets: two verifiable details, investigable question and observed fair turn
(C1/C2/S1). Partial = developing; missing = not yet evidenced; excitement not
assessed. Grade 3/support: stems, scribing, large folds. Grade 4/challenge:
identify a confounded comparison and propose two controls. No card: compare
paper shapes, labeling the change. Falling model: record rather than erase.
Early finish: refine question, no loads. All nine desks work simultaneously.

**Family:** We noticed details, asked questions and shared tools. Ask, “What was
an observation rather than a guess?” Optional: notice an everyday tool. No
homework, purchase or device needed.

## Teacher artifact pack

### Student material: evidence card to print or copy

Use one half-sheet per pupil. Print in black and white or copy these exact fields
onto the board for notebook paper:

`Name: ___  Date: ___  Team: ___`

1. `Sketch my standing form. Height: ___ cm.`
2. `Observation 1 (something visible/measurable): ___`
3. `Observation 2 (a different visible/measurable detail): ___`
4. `Prediction (what I expected before comparing): ___`
5. `Testable question: How does ___ affect ___?`
6. `My fair turn: I [folded / measured / recorded / passed the tool] when ___.`

**Evidence and answer guidance:** a labeled drawing or dictated/scribed response is
valid when both observations can be checked on the paper form, the question
names something that could be changed and observed, and the teacher or partner
can confirm one specific fair-turn action. Accept “7 cm tall,” “has two folds,”
or “stands without a hand.” Do not accept “strong,” “best,” or “will not fall”
as an observation unless a defined test produced that evidence.

### Worked example, finished example, and annotated exemplar

Display a simple paper tent beside this completed card:

> Height: **7 cm**. Observation 1: **The form has two straight creases.**
> Observation 2: **It stands without my hand touching it.** Prediction:
> **The cardstock form will stay standing longer than the paper form.**
> Testable question: **How does the number of folds affect the form's standing
> height?** Fair turn: **I measured after Ana folded, then passed the ruler to
> Malik.**

Underline “7 cm” and “two straight creases” as checkable evidence; circle the
change (`number of folds`) and result (`standing height`) in the question; box
the named tool-sharing action. Mark the cardstock sentence **prediction, not
observation** because no comparison is reported. A separate polished product is
N/A: the finished artifact is the standing form plus the individual evidence
card.

### Visual setup and discussion guide

Before class, draw three columns headed `I SEE (evidence) | I PREDICT | I
WONDER (testable)`. Add a ruler sketch with `0 cm` aligned to the desk, the
C-STREAM acronym, the objective, and a role rotation box:
`fold -> measure -> record/share`. Post the success check: `2 observations + 1
testable question + 1 confirmed fair turn`.

- “Which statements belong under **I see**?” Accept shape, fold count, measured
  height, or whether it stands now. Redirect future claims such as “it will be
  stronger” to **I predict**.
- “What makes a question testable here?” Accept changing one available feature,
  such as folds, paper/cardstock, or base width, and observing height or standing
  time. “Why is paper cool?” is interesting but not yet measurable as written.
- “Does a taller form prove it is stronger?” Accept **no**; height was measured,
  but strength needs a defined, safe test. Do not add loads in this lesson.
- “What does a fair turn look like?” Accept handing over the ruler, inviting a
  peer to fold, listening while another records, or waiting for an assigned
  role. Correct the misconception that finishing fastest proves collaboration.
- “Why record a form that falls?” Accept that an honest failed result helps
  improve the next design; falling is evidence, not misbehavior.

### Kit contents, Kit label:, Cleanup, Reset, and Unfinished work

Prepare each team kit with exactly `2 plain-paper sheets, 1 metric ruler, 1
cardstock card`; issue `1 pencil and 1 evidence card per pupil`. Label each kit
`C-STREAM G3-4 | W01 PAPER FORMS | TEAM CODE | 2 PAPER / 1 RULER / 1 CARD`.
At reset, pupils flatten reusable models into the portfolio, place the ruler and
unused cardstock in the kit, recycle only teacher-approved scraps, and report a
missing or damaged ruler/card. The teacher verifies the four listed team items
before shelving.

If time ends, write `NEXT: observation 2 / question / fair-turn check` on the
card, date it, and store it with the flattened model in the class portfolio.
The pupil finishes the missing observation from the saved model during the next
school check-in; no home work or reconstruction from memory is required.

### Error interpretation and reteaching

Annotate `It is strong` with `prediction--what did you see or measure?`;
annotate `How tall is it?` with `measurable, but add the change: How does ___
affect height?`; annotate a blank fair-turn field with `collaboration not yet
observed`, not `uncooperative`. Reteach with the displayed tent: pupil points to
one feature, measures from desk to top, sorts three teacher statements into
see/predict/wonder, then completes the frames `I observed ___` and `How does ___
affect ___?`. Provide a new 60-second partner role rotation to collect the
missing collaboration evidence.

### Safety classification, Print/accessibility check, and technology

**Safety classification: Severity low; likelihood unlikely** after controls.
Credible harm is a minor paper cut or a ruler/card edge striking a nearby pupil;
with flat rulers,
seated work, and teams of no more than three. **Adult ratio: 1:25 maximum** (one
classroom adult to no more than 25 pupils/nine paper-form teams) while
circulating; stop the class if that adult cannot maintain sight of all tables.
SDS review is **not applicable** to plain paper, cardstock, graphite pencils, or
a standard metric ruler used as directed; review local documentation if any
adhesive, coating, or other product is substituted.

Print at 100% with at least 12-point type, dark text on white, no color-only
meaning, generous writing lines, and a large-print copy available. Read every
prompt aloud and permit speech, pointing, drawing, or scribing; tactilely trace
folds and ruler marks when useful. No technology artifact applies.
