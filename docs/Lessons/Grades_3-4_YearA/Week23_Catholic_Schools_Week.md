# Week 23: Catholic Schools Week STREAM

## Lesson at a glance

Grades 3-4; Weekly A; Faith and evidence; **one 40-minute meeting**.
**Objective:** support one heritage fact with its source, explain four groups
of three with a diagram and revise the explanation using a reader's feedback.
**Why:** Catholic education joins truthful source use, mathematical clarity and
knowledge used in service. CST-C1 history/classroom-evidence distinction;
CST-T3 source/credit; CST-A3 visual explanation/critique/revision; CST-M3
whole-number grouping and justified total. Official alignment **VERIFICATION
REQUIRED**. Technology **None**; introductory, count to twelve with support.
Prep 15 minutes first use, 10 with prepared cards; cleanup 5 included.

## Before class and exact supplies

Use the Agnesi card in
[verified Catholic scientific heritage](../../Resources/Catholic_Scientists_Heritage.md#maria-gaetana-agnesi-mathematics-clarity-and-service).
Prepare two half-sheet cards per team <=3 (4/5/7/9), from one ordinary sheet:

1. **Heritage card:** original read-aloud from that resource:
   "Agnesi wrote a mathematics book intended to make ideas easier to learn.
   Later she devoted herself to caring for people in need. Clear explanations
   and service are valuable uses of knowledge."
   Source: [University of St Andrews MacTutor biography](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Agnesi/),
   scholarly institutional **secondary history**, directly checked October 4,
   2026. This is our teacher paraphrase, not an attributed Agnesi quotation.
2. **Classroom task card:** "A classroom has four trays with three markers in
   each. Show a new learner how to find the total." Draw four boxes with three
   marks each. This is an **original classroom example**, not a quotation or
   numerical example taken from Agnesi's book.

Each pupil receives two sheets/pencil; each team shares two markers/card pair.
Teacher board/timer; demo uses issued paper. Reuse cards. No printer requirement:
write these two cards once on the board and read aloud if copies are unavailable.

## Vocabulary/background/SAFETY

Source = where a historical claim comes from; group = equal collection;
worked example = shown reasoning; revision = change that clarifies meaning.
Four groups of three total twelve: **3+3+3+3=12**. Multiplication notation is
optional; the diagram/reasoning is the evidence.
**Misconception:** an attractive diagram proves correct arithmetic.
**If asked "Was Agnesi the first woman professor?"** That claim is not taught;
the scholarly source distinguishes an appointment from actual teaching.
No inferred personal motivation or invented saint/faith quotation.
Paper work stays indoors. Report damaged markers; no real marker-stock total
is inferred.

## Meeting 1: exactly 40 minutes

1. **0-4:** "How can clear knowledge serve a learner?" Optional original prayer
   for truthful, generous work. Read the short Agnesi card.
2. **4-10:** Identify institution/source type; separate heritage fact, classroom
   example and faith application. Model counting one group, not the full answer.
3. **10-22:** Each child cites one supported fact, draws four groups of three
   and explains the total with repeated addition or counting. Give the
   diagram a purposeful title/labels so another learner can follow it.
4. **22-30:** Peer names the groups and explains the total without coaching.
   Record an actual question/comment; correct any arithmetic and revise one
   visual feature or explanatory sentence. Retest with the same four groups.
5. **30-35:** Each child shows a sourced fact, correct twelve-object diagram/
   reasoning and feedback-backed before/after explanation. Ask, "Which fact
   came from history, and which result did you work out?"
6. **35-40:** Cap/count tools, retain dated diagrams/cards, clear desks.

## Success/access/troubleshooting

Meets: supported fact/citation, four equal groups with total twelve and
justified calculation, specific communication revision and source distinction.
Biography recall alone is insufficient. Grade 3/support: four drawn boxes,
counting marks/read-aloud/scribing. Grade 4/challenge: compare three groups of
four with four groups of three and explain the same total; no ratios/decimals.
Missing cards: use the complete board text above. Wrong total: recount each
group and correct the written reasoning, not just decoration. Unclear diagram:
revise labels/spacing from the actual reader comment. Early finish: a different
reader explains the same example. No required home research.

**Family:** We used a sourced heritage example, then explained mathematics and
revised for a learner. Ask, "What made your explanation clearer?" Optional:
explain the four groups verbally; no purchase or homework.

## Teacher artifact pack

- **Student material — source-and-explanation sheet (print/no-print):** Use two sides with
  these exact fields: `Name/date`; `One fact from the heritage card ___`;
  `Source institution ___`; `source type: primary / secondary history /
  classroom example`; `This source supports the fact because ___`; title
  `Four trays with three markers each`; four preprinted or pupil-drawn boxes;
  `objects in each group ___`; `number of groups ___`; `repeated addition
  ___`; `total ___`; `How I know ___`; `Reader's exact question/comment ___`;
  `My before explanation ___`; `One change I made ___`; `My after explanation
  ___`; `On retest, the reader said ___`; `History supplied ___; I worked out
  ___`. With no printer, put the complete heritage and task card text already
  given above on the board and have pupils fold plain paper into `source`,
  `math`, `feedback`, and `revision` quarters.
- **Expected evidence and acceptable answers:** The supported fact may be that Agnesi
  wrote a mathematics book intended to make ideas easier to learn or later
  cared for people in need. Accept `University of St Andrews MacTutor
  biography` as the source and `secondary history` as its type. The mathematics
  must show four equal groups of three and a justified total: `3+3+3+3=12`,
  counting all twelve once, or optional `4 x 3 = 12`. A valid revision answers
  an actual reader comment by improving a title, group labels, spacing, arrow,
  equation or sentence. Do not accept an invented Agnesi quotation, a claim
  that the classroom marker problem came from her book, an unsupported
  `first woman professor` claim, or decoration alone as revision.
- **Worked and finished example:** Fact: `Agnesi wrote a mathematics book
  intended to make ideas easier to learn.` Source: `University of St Andrews
  MacTutor biography - secondary history.` Draw four separately outlined trays,
  each with exactly three dots, label `3 in each tray`, and write
  `3+3+3+3=12 markers`. Before sentence: `There are 12.` Reader comment:
  `I do not know why.` Revision: `I added the three markers in each of the four
  trays: 3+3+3+3=12.` Retest response: `I can match each 3 to a tray.` Annotate
  the citation as historical evidence, bracket the four equal groups as
  mathematical evidence, and arrow the reader comment to the changed sentence.
- **Board/chart setup:** Create three columns headed `SOURCE SAYS`, `WE WORK
  OUT`, and `CLEARNESS/SERVICE CONNECTION`. Place the heritage fact and full
  citation only in the first, an empty four-box model and repeated-addition
  frame in the second, and `reader comment -> revision -> retest` in the third.
  Add `teacher paraphrase - not an Agnesi quotation` beneath the card and the
  success list: sourced fact, 4 equal groups, justified 12, feedback-backed
  change.
- **Discussion guidance:** Ask `Which statement comes from history?` Accept
  either card fact with its source; `Which fact did we calculate?` Accept
  `four groups of three total twelve`; `Can a beautiful picture fix 3+3+3+3=11?`
  Accept `no, recount/correct the arithmetic`; `Why record a reader's words?`
  Accept `they identify what needs clarification`; and `How can clarity serve
  someone?` Accept `it helps another learner understand`, without attributing
  private motives or measuring holiness. Correct the misconceptions that a
  source automatically supports every claim, the original classroom problem
  is historical, or peer approval proves arithmetic.
- **Kit contents. Kit label: and Cleanup/Reset:** Per team pack two heritage/task cards and two
  markers; issue one ruler-free pupil set of two plain sheets and one pencil
  per child. Class totals remain cards/markers 8/8, 10/10, 14/14 or 18/18 and
  pupil paper/pencils 20/10, 30/15, 40/20 or 50/25. Label the reusable card
  envelope exactly `C-STREAM G3-4 | W23 AGNESI SOURCE + 4x3 TASK | 2 CARDS/TEAM`.
  Label the work folder `W23 SOURCED MATH EXPLANATIONS`. Cap and count markers,
  return both cards per team, keep the citation attached, and file dated
  before/after work; discard no card into pupil papers.
- **Unfinished work:** Circle the first missing requirement and add a restart
  note such as `recount tray 3` or `ask reader to explain revised labels`.
  Store the sheet with its team's cards for the next opening opportunity.
  Record `revision/retest not yet observed` when time expires; do not fabricate
  feedback, require home research or ask families to verify the history.
- **Annotated exemplar, Common error interpretation, and Reteach:** Unequal groups indicate representation,
  not handwriting, difficulty; a correct `12` with no grouping/reasoning is
  incomplete evidence; `4+4+4=12` is a different three-groups-of-four model
  unless the diagram and comparison explicitly justify it; a citation beside
  an unrelated claim does not support that claim. Reteach by placing three
  marker marks in each of four drawn boxes, touching each group while saying
  `3, 6, 9, 12`, matching each box to one `+3`, then asking a reader which label
  helped. Use `The source supports ___; my diagram supports ___`.
- **Safety classification: Severity low; likelihood unlikely; SDS gate:** for
  seated paper and marker work. **Adult ratio: 1:25 maximum** with teams of no
  more than three and normal line of sight; use closer support when an
  individual plan requires it. Stop use of cracked or strongly odorous markers,
  cap markers when idle, and prevent mouthing or marking skin. Under normal
  use, the used commercial product category is classroom markers. Enter the
  exact manufacturer and product in the [Safety Data Sheet
  Register](../../Review/Safety_Data_Sheet_Register.csv), and keep it on
  **HOLD** until school approval and SDS applicability are resolved.
- **Print/accessibility check — black-and-white:** Print the source card and sheet in black on white at
  14-point minimum (18-point large-print), use solid box outlines and text
  labels rather than color, and leave at least 2 cm for each explanation.
  Read the card/citation aloud and allow oral dictation, scribing, manipulable
  marker caps for counting, or pointing to source-type choices. Verify at 100%
  scale that the URL/source line and all four boxes are legible; the board
  route must reproduce the source wording exactly.
- **Technology compliance:** No technology artifact applies.
  The teacher uses the already verified approved source text; any local copy of
  the linked biography remains a teacher preparation resource governed by
  school source-access and copyright procedures.
