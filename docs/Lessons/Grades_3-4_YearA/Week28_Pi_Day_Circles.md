# Week 28: Pi Day - Circles & Circumference

## Lesson at a glance

Grades 3-4; Weekly A; Mathematics; **one 40-minute meeting**.
**Objective:** measure two circles, compare around with three across-lengths and
explain a remainder/measurement limit. **Why/big idea:** repeatable relationships
support mathematical reasoning. Catholic/CST-C1: truthful approximation versus
faith wonder; CST-M1: around/across measurements; CST-M3: three equal lengths
plus remainder. Official alignment **VERIFICATION REQUIRED**. Technology
**None**; developing, no required ratios/calculators/decimal division.
Prep 10; cleanup 5 included.

## Before class and exact supplies

Teams <=3 (4/5/7/9). Kit: two smooth plastic lids/plates of different sizes,
ruler, four **50 cm string pieces**. Per pupil: paper/pencil. Teacher board/timer;
demo uses issued kit, ensure string reaches around both circles; select circles
under 15 cm diameter. Write table `circle | across cm | around cm | three
across compared with around`. Tools/string reusable; paper saved.

| Allocation | 10 | 15 | 20 | 25 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Rulers (1/team) | 4 | 5 | 7 | 9 |
| Circles (2/team) | 8 | 10 | 14 | 18 |
| String pieces (4/team) | 16 | 20 | 28 | 36 |
| Paper; pencils (1/pupil each) | 10 | 15 | 20 | 25 |

## Vocabulary/background/SAFETY

Diameter = across through center; circumference = around; approximation =
close measurement. Pi is finite, between 3 and 4; its decimal expansion does not
terminate/repeat periodically. **Misconception:** endless digits mean infinite
size. **If asked "Did we prove exact pi?"** No; string/ruler readings approximate
an ideal circle relationship. Faith wonder is not proof of theology.
No sharp can lids, neck loops, compasses, treats or robot-circle commands.
Desk string only; stop/report sharp edges. Indoor primary.

## Meeting 1: exactly 40 minutes

1. **0-4:** "Is around closer to one, two or three across-lengths?" Predict;
   optional prayer of wonder without treating pi as God's measurement.
2. **4-10:** Model across through center, wrap string without stretching, then
   measure whole cm from zero.
3. **10-22:** Rotate measuring/recording; each pupil measures at least one
   around/across pair; team records both circles.
4. **22-30:** Lay three equal diameter-length portions along circumference;
   mark small remaining length. Remeasure one suspicious reading.
5. **30-35:** Each child records two pairs, demonstrates three diameters plus remainder
   and states approximation versus finite pi. "What could create error?"
6. **35-40:** Count/coil short strings, stack circles/rulers, collect dated table.

## Success/access/troubleshooting

Meets: two measurement pairs with units, three-length comparison and honest
limit. Grade 3/support: teacher center mark, premarked equal portions, oral
comparison. Grade 4/challenge: compare repeat readings and explain error, no
required division. Around shorter than three across: check center/stretch/zero,
record corrected measurement without hiding original. Missing circles: trace
two teacher-selected circular bases, same task; do not use arbitrary oval.
Early finish: explain why same relation does not mean same circumference.

**Family:** We compared around/across and learned pi is finite. Ask, "What was
left after three across-lengths?" Optional: notice a circle, no measurement task.
