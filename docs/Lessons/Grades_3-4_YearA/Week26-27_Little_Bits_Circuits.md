# Weeks 26-27: Snap Circuits - Build, Test & Design

## Lesson at a glance

| Field | Teacher information |
|---|---|
| Grade / unit | Grades 3-4 / Circuits |
| Time | **Two 40-minute meetings**, including 5-minute cleanup in each |
| Domains | Catholic inclusive design, science systems, technology tools, engineering |
| Big idea | A switch controls a light by opening/closing a complete conducting path. |
| Student objective | "I can trace and test a switched light, then improve its classroom label using evidence." |
| Why | Safe troubleshooting and clear signals help people use shared tools. |
| Catholic connection | Each person has dignity regardless of a successful build. Give everyone a real turn and design a label that includes readers with different needs; cooperation is not proof that people function like circuit parts. |
| Local standards | **Local, not official:** CST-C2 (access decision), CST-S3 (return-path model), CST-T1 (safe test/repair), CST-E1 (criteria/constraints), CST-E2 (before/after redesign). |
| Technology | **None:** no computer, app or programming. Approved electrical hardware is required for actual circuit evidence; paper does not demonstrate electrical operation. |
| Prerequisites | No previous electronics required; teacher introduces open/closed now. |
| Prep / cleanup | First inspection 30 minutes; repeat prep 15 minutes; missing instructions/approval extra. Cleanup 5 minutes per meeting included. |

## Vocabulary, background and safety

**Circuit:** conducting path including a return to the source. **Open/closed:**
interrupted/complete path. **Input/output:** switch action/light response.
**Criterion:** tested success requirement. **Constraint:** a limit.
A battery supplies energy; current is charge flow, not energy "used up" by light.

**Misconception:** any arrangement of snapped parts is safe.
**If asked:** "Why won't it light?" Isolate power; compare with the approved
diagram and ask the teacher to check contacts/cells. Never bypass protection.

**SAFETY:** adult isolates the source before any snap change and checks before
reconnecting. No pupil battery access, loose coin cells, household power, shorts,
extra loads, live faults, fan blades, soldering or take-home hardware.
Warm/smelly/leaking/damaged -> stop, tell adult, OUT OF SERVICE.
Follow the guide, including the protected-AA or paper alternative when gated.

## Meeting 1: exactly 40 minutes

1. **0-4: Purpose.** Prayer: "God, help us use our gifts with care and include one another." Explain the real-turn rule and distinguish human dignity from circuit success.
2. **4-8: Model.** Teacher traces the approved full path, demonstrates OFF/ON, isolation and adult inspection. Do not distribute unused parts.
3. **8-10: Predict.** Each child draws the return path and predicts OFF/ON outputs.
4. **10-22: Wave A.** Teams follow the guide's isolated build, inspection, individual two-cycle switch tests and one teacher-managed open-path repair/retest. Waiting teams label diagrams and plan an ON/OFF label using words and symbols, not color alone.
5. **22-23: Reset.** Adult isolates power and transfers/resets station kits.
6. **23-35: Wave B / feedback.** Remaining teams receive the same hardware routine. Finished teams explain their repair and peer-check labels; at smaller enrollment, use this time for retests and individual checks. Ask each child: "Where is the gap? What evidence showed the repair worked?"
7. **35-40: Cleanup.** Isolate power, count/return parts and cells, store kits and save sheets/labels. Record physical evidence not yet collected.

## Meeting 2: exactly 40 minutes

1. **0-4: Retrieve.** Review each child's diagram; model correct OFF/ON behavior again without assuming meeting 1 mastery.
2. **4-8: Define.** User brief: "A classmate needs to tell when this classroom model is ON or OFF." Criteria: two correct OFF/ON cycles and a peer can read the label from **1 m**. Constraints: supplied grid, original approved circuit, no extra electrical parts.
3. **8-10: Plan.** Each child predicts one readability obstacle and proposes a label change.
4. **10-22: Wave A.** Rebuild/check/test using the guide. Test label at 1 m, record pass/not yet, revise one visual feature and retest. Change paper layout only, not the approved circuit.
5. **22-23: Reset.** Adult isolates power and transfers station kits.
6. **23-35: Wave B / evidence.** Remaining teams run the same routine. Finished teams record before/after results. Each child explains one criterion, constraint, observed revision and inclusion choice; not a medical/emergency device.
7. **35-40: Cleanup.** Isolate/count/store and collect individual evidence; paper labels only go home.

## Success, support and trouble

Every child contributes to the isolated team build, operates the switch,
records two cycles, explains the full path/repair and cites a before/after label
test. Team success alone does not certify each child's operation.
Score **1** participation only; **2** path/test with prompts; **3** independent
path/state explanation and test-backed revision; **4** additionally explains
a design limitation and justifies an access tradeoff.

- **Grade 3/support:** traced diagram, large labels, oral/dictated records and
  motor assistance. Every child still predicts and operates with support.
- **Grade 4/challenge:** compare two possible open-path faults on paper;
  justify a word/symbol choice. No live shorts or undocumented extra branches.
- **25 pupils:** nine teams, five stations, two 12-minute waves; no four-pupil
  team. Inspection or access overruns mean deferred evidence, not rushed approval.
- **Missing/failed kit:** isolate/report and use a checked reserve. Otherwise
  use paper and schedule the missing physical turn.

## Teacher artifact pack

- **Student material — two-meeting individual evidence sheet (print/no-print):** Fields are
  `Name/team/date`; `approved project number/title ___`; `teacher-copied
  component IDs/counts ___`; a supplied-grid diagram with arrows and blanks
  `source -> ___ -> switch -> ___ -> lamp -> return to source`; `I predict:
  switch OFF, lamp ___; switch ON, lamp ___`; two meeting-1 rows `cycle |
  switch state | observed lamp state | match?`; `teacher-managed open point
  ___`; `evidence after repair ___`; `label before ___`; `predicted readability
  obstacle ___`; two meeting-2 rows `1 m reader result before/after: pass/not
  yet | reader words`; `paper feature changed ___`; `criterion met ___`;
  `constraint honored ___`; `inclusion choice ___`; `limitation ___`; and
  pupil/adult initials for the child's actual switch turn. For no printing,
  copy these headings onto one sheet used both meetings; pupils trace the
  approved diagram rather than drawing an invented circuit.
- **Expected evidence and acceptable answers:** OFF must open the approved path and the
  lamp should be off; ON must complete the path and the lamp should light in
  two observed cycles. The path explanation must include a return to the
  source. A repair claim needs the isolated open point, adult-approved
  correction and successful retest. The label succeeds when a peer at 1 m
  correctly identifies ON/OFF; acceptable changes include larger words,
  stronger contrast, more spacing or a distinct word-plus-symbol combination,
  never color alone. Accept `not yet` or `electrical operation not observed`
  when truthful. Do not infer operation from team success or paper reasoning.
- **Worked and finished example:** Trace `battery source -> conductor -> slide
  switch -> conductor -> lamp -> conductor -> battery return`. Prediction:
  `OFF/open = lamp off; ON/closed = lamp on`. Observations: two cycles both
  match. Teacher isolates power and opens one approved connection; learner
  points to the gap; adult-approved reconnection produces light on retest.
  Before label: small `ON` in pale marker; 1 m reader says `I cannot tell which
  word it is`, so the learner makes `ON` and `OFF` large black words with
  different outlined switch-position symbols; retest passes. Annotate the
  return path, the observed rather than predicted states, the adult-managed
  repair and the feedback-linked paper change. Limitation: `This classroom
  model is not an emergency or medical indicator.`
- **Board setup and Visual example:** Post only the selected manufacturer's project
  diagram and its transcribed component inventory, plus a large closed-loop
  trace. Add `ISOLATE -> ADULT CHECK -> CONNECT -> TEST`, an OFF/ON prediction
  and observation chart, the 1 m floor/desk marker, criteria/constraints, and a
  nine-team/two-wave turn chart. Mark each role so every pupil receives a switch
  turn; waiting work is diagram/label evidence, not handling spare parts.
- **Discussion guidance:** Ask `Why is one wire from source to lamp not enough?`
  Accept `current needs a complete return path`; `What evidence locates an open
  point?` Accept `the approved path is interrupted and the lamp returns after
  the isolated repair`; `Why isolate before changing a snap?` Accept `to avoid
  changing a powered circuit`; `Why not use color alone?` Accept `some readers
  may not distinguish it and words/symbols add access`; and `Does a failed lamp
  mean a pupil failed?` Accept `no, it is a system result that directs safe
  checking`. Correct the misconceptions that any snapped arrangement is safe,
  the lamp consumes current, or a team test proves every child's operation.
- **Kit inventory, labels and reset:** For each active station issue one
  preflight-approved OHM-135 kit grid, one tray, the selected project diagram
  and **only** the component IDs/counts printed on that approved diagram.
  Before class, the teacher must copy those IDs/counts onto a station inventory
  card; without that completed card, the hardware route does not open. Also
  issue one cardstock label, ruler and shared marker per team, and one evidence
  sheet/pencil per pupil. Label trays exactly `C-STREAM G3-4 | W26-27 SNAP
  SWITCHED LIGHT | STATION CODE | PROJECT CODE | INVENTORY CARD REQUIRED`; label
  paper storage `W26-27 EVIDENCE + LABELS`. Adult reset is: switch off, isolate
  the source, reconcile every part to the card, inspect snaps/grid/cells, cap
  marker, stack diagram/ruler/label, record missing/damaged parts, and place
  cells in approved dry storage. Never substitute an unlisted component.
- **Unfinished work:** Circle the next evidence field and write a safe restart
  cue (`needs individual cycle 2` or `retest revised label at 1 m`). Store
  sheets/labels by team; hardware remains at school and is fully reset rather
  than preserved as an unfinished live build. If time or access prevents a
  physical turn, record `electrical operation not yet observed` and schedule a
  supervised school turn; no take-home completion or paper-to-hardware credit.
- **Annotated exemplar, Common error interpretation, and Reteach:** Lamp always off may indicate an open
  path, wrong placement, poor contact, depleted cells or failed lamp; it does
  not authorize trial-and-error additions. Lamp on in the predicted OFF state
  suggests the switch was bypassed or the state/path was misread; adult
  isolates immediately. A correct diagram with no observed cycles is reasoning
  evidence only. A readable label with no before/after reader result lacks
  redesign evidence. Reteach de-energized: finger-trace every approved component
  out and back, use an `OPEN` paper gap and `CLOSED` bridge, predict states,
  then permit one adult-inspected two-cycle test. For labels, conduct one
  word/symbol 1 m test and change exactly the feature named by the reader.
- **Safety classification: Severity moderate; likelihood rare** under the
  required controls. **SDS gate:** the marker and battery conditions below
  remain subject to the stated register/manufacturer controls. **Adult ratio: 1:15 maximum at active
  powered stations** (one trained adult for no more than five stations with
  three pupils each); with nine teams, use the stated waves rather than
  energizing nine stations at once. The adult controls cells, isolation, fault
  insertion, inspection and reconnection. Stop and quarantine any warm,
  odorous, leaking, cracked or damaged item; no shorts, household power, coin
  cells, extra loads, soldering or pupil battery access. Snap parts/cells are
  articles. The used commercial product category is the shared classroom
  marker. Enter the exact
  manufacturer and product in the [Safety Data Sheet
  Register](../../Review/Safety_Data_Sheet_Register.csv), and keep it on
  **HOLD** until school approval and SDS applicability are resolved. For
  leakage or damage, the school's battery plan and manufacturer SDS/safety
  information are applicable to trained adults; pupils move away and do not
  clean it.
- **Print accessibility:** Use 14-point minimum text and 18-point large-print
  copies, thick black circuit lines, arrowheads plus words, high-contrast
  ON/OFF labels and no color-only state. Provide a tactile/off-power path trace,
  oral prediction, scribing or supported switch access while retaining each
  pupil's evidence. Test the sheet and project diagram at 100%; component IDs,
  return arrow, pass/not-yet boxes and adult/pupil initials must remain legible.
  Place the 1 m marker where mobility access and sight lines are clear.
- **Technology and school compliance:** No computing technology artifact applies.
  The electrical hardware route is school-only and may run only after the OLP
  guide gates, exact model/project approval, age/power check, kit inspection,
  inventory card and trained-adult supervision are confirmed. When any gate
  fails, use the protected approved alternative or paper route and label the
  evidence limits exactly as stated above. Paper work does not count as executed
  hardware evidence; execution remains deferred and **not observed** until a
  teacher witnesses the pupil's switch operation on the approved Snap Circuits
  OHM-135 hardware.
