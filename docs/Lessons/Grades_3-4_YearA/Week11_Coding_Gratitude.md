# Week 11: Coding Gratitude

## Lesson at a glance

Grades 3-4; Weekly A; Gratitude; **one 40-minute meeting**.
**Objective:** edit/run two ordered Scratch messages and revise for a reader.
**Why:** actual execution exposes order/timing errors. Catholic/CST-C2:
respectful thanks without private disclosure; CST-T1 actual edit/run/stop;
CST-T2 sequence/debug; CST-A3 critique/revised message. Official alignment
**VERIFICATION REQUIRED**. Technology **Required** for executed code;
introductory, no Scratch prerequisite. Prep 20 minutes after software works;
installation extra. Cleanup 5 included.

## Before class and exact supplies

Scratch 3 guest/offline, no accounts. Preflight blank project: assemble below,
run twice, Stop, save local `.sb3`, reopen. Leave working sample/save location
with substitute. Failed preflight means paper path/report missing operation.
Balanced teams <=3 (4/5/7/9), computer/team; paper/pencil per pupil. Teacher
board/timer, demo from issued device; devices reusable, paper retained.

| Allocation | 10 | 15 | 20 | 25 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Computers (1/team) | 4 | 5 | 7 | 9 |
| Paper; pencils (1/pupil each) | 10 | 15 | 20 | 25 |

## Starter, vocabulary and background

One sprite, Code tab; connect blocks:

```text
when green flag clicked
say [Thank you, classroom helpers.] for (2) seconds
say [You keep our space ready.] for (2) seconds
```

Event = trigger; sequence = ordered actions; sprite = programmed character;
debug = fix mismatch. **Misconception:** timed message remains after Stop.
**If asked “Need broadcasts?”** Not for one sprite's sequence; learn a small
working program first. Human moral judgment is not an automatic coded rule.

## SAFETY / privacy

Dry seated devices; no names/photos/voices, passwords, downloads or public
delivery. Stop/report damaged leads or access failures. Fictional/classroom
thanks allowed; feelings/prayer not graded. Indoor primary.

## Meeting 1: exactly 40 minutes

1. **0-4:** “How can thanks name a helpful action?” Optional original prayer
   for respectful communication.
2. **4-10:** Demo starter/flag/Stop. “Which message appears first?”
3. **10-21:** Each pupil has three-minute edit/run turn: replace message, predict
   order, run/record. Peers plan/read for clarity.
4. **21-29:** Peer reads messages, gives specific feedback; change text/duration
   and rerun. “Was there enough reading time?”
5. **29-35:** Individual two-step trace and critique-backed change; roster logs
   actual edit/run/stop separately.
6. **35-40:** Stop/save/reopen check, return devices, collect dated paper.

## Success/access/troubleshooting

Meets: correct trigger/trace, observed operation and specific message revision.
Grade 3/support: copy starter/dictation/motor assistance. Grade 4/challenge: add
one-second wait and predict timing. Not running: correct sprite, hat and block
attachment; rerun. Fewer devices: two waves of three-minute turns within work
windows; below half required stations use paper/reschedule, not rushed turns.
No device/internet: paper event/two-message cards at same times; CT/art only,
**Scratch operation not observed**. No home completion.

**Family:** We executed and revised gratitude messages. Ask, “What triggered
the sequence?” Optional: say a thanks, no home coding.

## Teacher artifact pack

- **Student material — code-and-reader check (one sheet, printable/no-print):** `Name ___  Date ___  Device/team ___`; draw or copy the five blocks in order; `Before running, I predict message 1 will be ___ and message 2 will be ___`; `I clicked ___ to start`; check `I personally edited / ran / stopped`; `Observed order: first ___, then ___`; `Reader feedback: The words ___ / the reading time ___`; `My exact revision: I changed ___ to ___`; `After rerunning, I observed ___`; `One privacy check: my project contains no real names, photos, voices, passwords, or private story details.` If no printer, pupils fold paper into `Trace`, `Run evidence`, and `Revision` columns and copy these prompts from the board.
- **Evidence and answer guidance:** Accept the green flag as the trigger, the two `say for seconds` blocks in top-to-bottom order, and a specific revision to wording or duration that was actually rerun. Suitable gratitude text names a non-private helpful action, such as `Thank you, classroom helpers` and `You keep our space ready`. Reader feedback may say `2 seconds was enough`, `I needed more time`, or identify unclear wording. A paper-only trace demonstrates sequence reasoning but does **not** satisfy the required `personally edited/ran/stopped` operation; mark those boxes `not observed—school device follow-up`.
- **Worked finished example:** Initial code says `Thank you, helpers` for 2 seconds, then `You help` for 2 seconds. The pupil predicts the first message correctly, runs it, and records `My partner did not know which help I meant.` Revision: `Thank you, library helpers` for 3 seconds, then `You return books so readers can find them` for 4 seconds. After rerun: `Both messages appeared in order and my partner finished reading them.` Annotate the hat block `trigger`, number the message blocks `1` and `2`, box the changed action words, and circle the longer durations as the critique-backed change.
- **Screen/board setup:** Project the exact starter stack with arrows labeled `event -> action 1 -> action 2`. Add a three-row trace table: `green flag | message visible | seconds`; a `RUN / OBSERVE / REVISE / RERUN` loop; and privacy icons crossed out for name, camera, microphone, and upload. Keep the roster operation columns visible only to the teacher: `edit`, `run`, `stop`, `reopen`.
- **Discussion prompts and acceptable answers:** `What makes the second message wait?` (`the first timed say block must finish because the connected blocks run in sequence`); `What happens if the lower block is disconnected?` (`it will not run from the green flag`); `Is Stop the same as erasing code?` (`no, it ends the run`); `How can thanks be specific without being private?` (`name a role and helpful action, not a child's name or personal story`); `Why rerun after editing?` (`to see whether the program now matches the intended order and reading time`). Correct `the sprite remembers the last timed message after Stop` by rerunning and observing that a timed bubble ends; correct `watching a partner proves I can operate Scratch` by scheduling the pupil's own turn.
- **Kit contents. Kit label: and Cleanup/Reset:** Each team station has `1 compatible school-managed computer with Scratch 3 offline/approved guest access; power adapter if site practice requires it; 1 plain-paper sheet and pencil per pupil`. Label the cart slot and paper envelope `C-STREAM G3-4 | W11 GRATITUDE CODE | DEVICE NUMBER | LOCAL SAVE ONLY`. Cleanup and reset: click Stop, save only to the approved local class folder using the teacher's non-identifying naming scheme, close Scratch, delete no files unless school retention policy directs it, sign out of any accidentally opened account, wipe no screen with classroom chemicals, connect the assigned charger, and reconcile device/charger numbers.
- **Unfinished work:** On the sheet check one status: `trace only`, `edited—not yet run`, `first run complete—revision not rerun`, or `complete`. Save an unfinished `.sb3` only in the approved local class folder and attach its teacher-assigned code (not a pupil name) to the dated sheet. Schedule the missing individual operation on a school device; do not send accounts, files, or coding homework home.
- **Annotated exemplar, Common error interpretation, and Reteach:** If both bubbles appear but the second is too fast, annotate `sequence correct; reader timing not yet effective` and change only the seconds before rerunning. If nothing happens, check in order: selected sprite, green-flag hat present, blocks snapped together, then actual flag click. If the messages appear reversed, number the observed order and physically reorder the blocks. Reteach with printed block cards: pupil touches `flag`, `say 1`, `say 2`, predicts the screen, then reconstructs and runs the same three-block stack in Scratch.
- **Safety/risk and SDS:** Overall **moderate severity / unlikely likelihood** because a damaged electrical lead or liquid near powered equipment could cause injury; ordinary seated use also has **low severity / possible likelihood** ergonomic or trip hazards. With dry tables, inspected school equipment, routed leads, seated turns, and immediate stop/report procedures, residual risk is **low / unlikely**. **Adult ratio: 1:18 maximum** while powered stations are active so leads, device turns, and stop/report responses remain visible; classes of 20-25 require a second supervising adult or two separately supervised device waves. An adult must disconnect damaged equipment and provide closer support where a pupil cannot safely manage hardware. **SDS: not applicable because screen cleaner and every other chemical product are prohibited during this lesson;** any site cleaning occurs separately under school procedure.
- **Print/accessibility:** Use 12-point or larger sans-serif text, grayscale-safe block outlines, block names printed inside shapes, numbered sequence cues, and checkboxes that do not rely on color. Provide large-print block cards, keyboard/mouse accessibility already approved by the school, a peer who may position hardware while the pupil dictates choices, and read-aloud/scribing options; each pupil must still make the edit/run/stop decisions. Test one black-and-white page and verify the block-stack connectors and privacy symbols remain distinct.
- **Device compliance:** Required operation occurs only on school-managed compatible computers using installed Scratch 3 offline or a locally approved guest configuration. Use no pupil account, email, cloud save, public share, extension download, camera, microphone, real name, photo, voice, or analytics-enabled third-party add-on. Confirm Scratch/version, local-save permissions, content filtering, accessibility settings, and the approved storage/retention location before class. Paper tracing is the documented contingency, but it does not count as executed device evidence. The operational objective remains deferred and **not observed** until a teacher witnesses the pupil's school-device run.
