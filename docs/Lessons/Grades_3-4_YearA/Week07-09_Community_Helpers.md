# Weeks 7-9: Engineering Design: Community Helpers

## Lesson at a glance

Grades 3-4; Weekly A; Service design; **three 40-minute meetings**.
**Objective:** define a visitor's need, test two sign versions and recommend a
revision using evidence. **Why:** community helpers seek feedback rather than
assume another person's needs. Catholic/CST-C2: dignity/access decision;
CST-E1: criteria/limits; CST-E2: actual retest; CST-E3: qualified recommendation;
CST-A3: critique/revised communication. Official alignment **VERIFICATION
REQUIRED**. Technology **None**; developing. Prep 15 minutes; cleanup 5 per
meeting. Classroom prototype, not certified emergency signage or proven benefit.

## Before class and exact supplies

Board brief: “A visitor needs to find the book-return tray from **1 m** away.
Use word AND symbol, not color alone.” Select a non-emergency desk destination.
Balanced teams <=3: 4/5/7/9; neighboring peer users, three-team exchange when odd.
Per team: two cardstock sheets, ruler, two markers, **40 cm tape/unit**.
Per pupil: three evidence sheets/pencil. Teacher board/timer; demo from issued
card, mark 1 m using ruler lengths. No hallway tour/printing.

## Vocabulary/background/SAFETY

User = person using design; prototype = trial version; feedback = actual
response; criterion = tested success. **Misconception:** designer liking proves
usefulness. **If asked “Will everyone read it?”** Peer sample cannot prove
universal access; more users are needed before installation.
No filters, body-tested aids, shelters or improvised lights. Paper/tape only;
no skin tape or private hardship/disability disclosures. Stop/report damaged
tools. Indoor primary works in winter.

## Meeting 1: exactly 40 minutes

1. **0-5:** Brief/prayer for service optional. “What do we know, not assume?”
2. **5-10:** Criterion: identify destination/direction at 1 m within five
   seconds; constraint: two cards/40 cm tape.
3. **10-22:** Each asks peer “Which word/symbol helps?” Record response, sketch
   two alternatives; agree on one.
4. **22-30:** Paper mock-up; exchange and record destination/direction correct
   or not yet, not “looks nice.”
5. **30-35:** Each states need, criterion, constraint, actual comment.
6. **35-40:** Cap/count/store; save dated plans.

## Meeting 2: exactly 40 minutes

1. **0-4:** Retrieve feedback without assuming mastery.
2. **4-9:** Model large word/symbol; equal distance/time in tests.
3. **9-21:** Card version 1; every child contributes a purposeful feature.
4. **21-30:** Peer tests twice; record results/confusion and plan one change.
5. **30-35:** Individual feature-purpose diagram and two test results.
6. **35-40:** Save sign/plans, count tools; no real installation.

## Meeting 3: exactly 40 minutes

1. **0-4:** “Which recorded confusion will our revision address?”
2. **4-9:** Critique example: “Arrow unclear at 1 m,” not “bad artist.”
3. **9-20:** Revise on card 2; repeat two equal tests with a neighboring team.
4. **20-29:** Parallel paired presentations; each explains results/tradeoff;
   three-team exchange rotates listeners within this window.
5. **29-35:** Individual before/after comparison, actual comment and sample
   limitation; teacher gathers all slips.
6. **35-40:** Count tools/remove test tape/archive. Adult decides display approval.

## Success/access/troubleshooting

Meets: user need/limits, real results, critique-backed revision and qualified
recommendation. Grade 3/support: pictorial arrow starter/scribing. Grade
4/challenge: readability versus space tradeoff. Unclear sign: simplify one
feature/retest. No external partner: label peers as classroom test users.
Late: stop decoration, preserve retest/evidence/cleanup. No community outcomes
claimed from the model.

**Family:** We designed a visitor sign and retested it. Ask, “What did a user
actually misunderstand?” Optional: discuss a public sign.

5. `One evidence-based change for version 2: ___ because ___.`

**Meeting 3 -- version 2/recommendation**

1. `Changed feature: ___; confusion it addresses: ___.`
2. `Retest 1: destination ___; direction ___; seconds ___; comment ___.`
3. `Retest 2: destination ___; direction ___; seconds ___; comment ___.`
4. `Before/after result: version 1 ___; version 2 ___.`
5. `Recommendation: [use V2 / revise again / collect more evidence] because
___.`
6. `Tradeoff: ___  Sample limitation: We tested only ___.`

**Expected evidence:** accept `not yet` as an honest result. A complete visitor-
sign record identifies the user need and criterion/constraints, records two
actual 1 m/five-second tests for each cardstock version, connects one revision
to observed confusion, and qualifies the recommendation. Spelling/art quality
is not the criterion if intended words and symbols are interpretable; “everyone
will understand” is not supported by two peer users.

### Worked example, finished example, and annotated exemplar

Display two sample cards and this record:

> Need: **A first-time classroom visitor needs to find the book-return tray
> from 1 m away.** Version 1 said **BOOK RETURN** in small letters with a thin
> sideways arrow. Tests: User A named the destination in **4 s** but pointed
> left; User B said **“I cannot tell which way the arrow points”** in **5 s**.
> Version 2 used larger dark words, an outlined book symbol, and one bold arrow
> pointing right. Equal retests: both users named the destination and right
> direction in **3 s**. Recommendation: **Use V2 for another classroom trial
> because 2/2 retests met the criterion, but do not claim universal access.**
> Tradeoff: the larger arrow left less room for words. Sample limitation: **two
> classmates, not unfamiliar visitors or all access needs.**

Draw arrows from each V1 confusion to the V2 feature intended to address it;
box the equal 1 m/five-second conditions and underline the qualified `2/2`
recommendation. Annotate `looks nicer` as preference, not test evidence. This
two-card before/after set plus all three individual records is the finished
classroom artifact; installation or certified wayfinding is **N/A and requires
adult approval outside this lesson**.

### Visual/board/chart setup and discussion

Keep the fixed brief visible all three meetings: `Find BOOK-RETURN TRAY | 1 m |
5 s | word + symbol | not color alone | 2 cards / 40 cm tape`. Meeting 1 adds
`KNOWN | ASSUMED | ASKED USER` and two thumbnail boxes. Meeting 2 adds a chart
`User | destination | direction | seconds | exact comment | change`. Meeting 3
adds paired columns `V1 evidence | V2 equal retest | recommendation | tradeoff |
sample limit`. Mark a floor/desk test line exactly 1 m from the sign with ruler
lengths; use the same position both versions.

- “What do we know instead of assume?” Accept the stated destination, distance,
  time, materials, and actual peer comments; a visitor's reading level or access
  need is unknown unless respectfully provided.
- “Does the designer liking the sign prove it works?” Accept no; the user must
  identify destination/direction under the criterion.
- “Why use a word and symbol rather than color alone?” Accept that redundant
  cues support people who cannot distinguish/see the chosen color or do not
  interpret it the same way.
- “What feedback is actionable?” Accept exact confusion such as “arrow direction
  unclear at 1 m,” not insults such as “bad art.”
- “Can two successful peers prove everyone can use it?” Accept no; the small,
  familiar sample limits the recommendation and more varied users are needed.
- “How is revising service rather than failure?” Accept listening to evidence,
  respecting the user, and improving access within constraints.
- “May we ask a peer to disclose a disability?” Accept no; test the sign without
  requesting private information or making a child represent a group.

### Kit contents, Kit label:, Cleanup, Reset, and Unfinished work

Each team kit contains exactly `2 cardstock sheets, 1 metric ruler, 2 markers,
40 cm masking tape`; issue `plain paper for the meeting plan, 1 pencil, and 3
evidence half-sheets per pupil across the unit`. Label it `C-STREAM G3-4 |
W07-09 VISITOR SIGN | TEAM CODE | 2 CARD / 1 RULER / 2 MARKERS / 40 cm TAPE`.
At reset, cap both markers, lay the ruler flat, roll only clean reusable tape or
discard used tape as directed, store V1 and V2 flat with dated records, remove
all temporary test tape, and report dry markers or damaged tools. Teacher checks
that no sign remains installed and initials the reusable-tool count.

If unfinished, mark the exact phase `NEXT: user response / sketch choice / V1
test __ / V2 change / retest __ / recommendation`, store both versions flat in
the team portfolio, and resume with the same criterion at school. Do not send
work home, invent a missing peer comment, substitute a decoration for a retest,
or display an unapproved sign.

### Error interpretation and reteaching

Annotate `looks good` with `preference--record destination, direction, seconds,
and comment`; `2/2 means accessible to everyone` with `sample too small/familiar`;
a V2 tested at a closer distance with `unequal retest`; and a changed symbol
unrelated to the recorded arrow confusion with `revision does not address the
evidence yet`. An incorrect user response is design evidence, not a low score
for the tester. Reteach using the exemplar: pupil matches each comment to a
feature, chooses one change, tests two mini signs from the same 1 m mark, and
completes `User ___; therefore we changed ___; the equal retest showed ___;
we still do not know ___`.

### Safety classification, Print/accessibility check, and technology

**Safety classification: Severity low; likelihood unlikely** after controls.
Marker/tape misuse, a ruler edge, or loose floor tape could cause minor
skin/eye irritation or a trip; with capped markers, tape kept off skin, and teacher-controlled
test marks. **Adult ratio: 1:25 maximum** (one adult to no more than 25
pupils/nine sign teams), with no more than one scheduled tester per team moving
to its marked position; pause movement when sight lines are blocked. The used
commercial product categories are classroom markers and masking tape. Enter
the exact manufacturer and product for each in the [Safety Data Sheet
Register](../../Review/Safety_Data_Sheet_Register.csv), and keep each product
on **HOLD** until school approval and SDS applicability are resolved.

Print records at 100% in at least 12-point dark type with generous comment boxes
and no color-only fields. Provide large-print/high-contrast prompts, read them
aloud, and accept speech, pointing, drawing, or scribing. For the sign itself,
require strong figure/background contrast, large plain lettering, one clear
directional arrow, and both word and symbol; do not simulate disability or
claim certified accessibility from peer testing.

No technology artifact applies.
