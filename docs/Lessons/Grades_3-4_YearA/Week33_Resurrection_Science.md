# Week 33: Resurrection & New Life Science

## Lesson at a glance

Grades 3-4; Weekly A; Life science; **one 40-minute meeting**.
**Objective:** observe a seed, order four living butterfly stages and explain why
development is not biological Resurrection. **Why:** accurate biology and
religious symbolism can coexist without confusing evidence.
CST-C1 observation/faith distinction; CST-S1 seed detail; CST-S3 living stages/
generation link; CST-A1 labeled diagram. Official alignment **VERIFICATION
REQUIRED**. Technology **None**; introductory. Prep 10; cleanup 5 included.

## Before class and exact supplies

Teams <=3 (4/5/7/9). Each: four large drawn cards egg/larva/pupa/adult, two
untreated dry bean seeds, plastic magnifier. Per pupil: two paper sheets/pencil.
Teacher board/timer; allergy/handling check; demo uses issued set. Copy stage
information from [Monarch Joint Venture](https://www.monarchjointventure.org/monarch-biology/life-cycle):
living immature stages develop to adults; adults reproduce, beginning another
generation. No invented timing, live specimens, planting or hidden caretaker.

## Vocabulary/background/SAFETY

Embryo = young plant in viable seed; dormancy = reduced activity; metamorphosis
= developmental change; reproduction = new generation.
**Misconception:** pupa/seed dies to transform. Viable embryos/pupae are alive;
dead embryos do not germinate. **If asked "Does every seed grow?"** No; viability
and conditions matter, and appearance alone does not test viability.
Easter celebrates Jesus' Resurrection; growth is a metaphor of hope, not proof.
No tasting, broken seeds, animal handling/releases or outdoor collection.
Count seeds away from mouthing pupils; wash hands, report loss/allergy concern.
Photo/drawn-card seed observation substitutes if allergy gate fails, labeled.

## Meeting 1: exactly 40 minutes

1. **0-4:** "Can something alive look still?" Optional prayer of Easter hope.
2. **4-10:** Model observed seed surface versus inferred embryo; read four stages.
3. **10-22:** Each observes seed with lens, draws two visible details, then
   orders stage cards and explains living pupa.
4. **22-30:** Draw arrows through stages and adult-to-new-eggs reproduction.
   Peer checks labels; revise one unclear arrow/detail.
5. **30-35:** Each identifies pupa/embryo as living, generation link, actual
   observed detail and separate Easter metaphor. "What did we not test?"
6. **35-40:** Count seeds/cards/lenses, file dated diagrams, wash/clear.

## Success/access/troubleshooting

Meets: visible detail, ordered living stages/generation link, labeled visual
revision and science/faith distinction. Grade 3/support: pictured stages,
dictation. Grade 4/challenge: explain observation cannot prove a seed viable.
Wrong order: compare source card and revise; never call a pupa dead. Missing
lenses: individual turns with shared lens or unaided observation. Early finish:
identify what longer-term evidence a growth investigation would need.
Indoor primary works without spring specimens.

**Family:** We studied living development and Easter symbolism. Ask, "Why is
a pupa not dead?" Optional: notice a plant picture, no planting homework.
