# Weeks 29-31: Environmental STREAM

## Lesson at a glance

Grades 3-4; Weekly A; Stewardship; **three 40-minute meetings**.
**Objective:** observe clean sample materials, make a sorting system, compare
two ten-card tests and distinguish modeled from actual waste reduction.
**Why:** creation care needs usable routines and honest data.
Catholic/CST-C3: reduce/reuse choice; CST-S1: observations; CST-E1: sorting
criterion/limits; CST-E2: retest; CST-M2: two labeled results.
Official alignment **VERIFICATION REQUIRED**. Technology **None**; developing.
Prep 15; cleanup 5 each. Creation-care teaching paraphrases
[Laudato Si' opening](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/encyclicals/documents/papa-francesco_20150524_enciclica-laudato-si.html);
no inherited quotation or measured-savings claim.

## Before class and exact supplies

Teams <=3 (4/5/7/9). Per team/unit: two cardstock sign sheets, ten teacher-drawn
large scenario cards (five clean unused paper, five clean reusable containers),
two markers, tray, 40 cm tape. Cards are **classroom practice categories**, not
municipal recycling rules. Per pupil: three evidence sheets/pencil. Teacher
board/timer; draw card key, demo from issued set. No trash/waste handling or
unattended building audit. Cards/tools reusable; signs/tape consumable.

## Vocabulary/background/SAFETY

Reduce = use less; reuse = use again; recycling = material processing under
local rules; impact = measured effect. **Misconception:** correct model sorting
proves less landfill waste. **If asked "Can this go in our real bin?"** Ask the
school's responsible adult/local rule, not this practice card.
Dry cards only; no compost, sockets, plumbing, real pollutants, stagnant water
or claims about family waste. Stop/report damaged supplies. Indoor primary.

## Meeting 1: exactly 40 minutes

1. **0-4:** "How can we care for shared resources without guessing results?"
2. **4-10:** Model observation versus inference on a clean paper/card sample.
3. **10-22:** Each sorts/notices card features, records two observations and
   labels a personal reduce/reuse option without disclosing home circumstances.
4. **22-30:** Baseline ten-card sort with neighboring peer, no coaching; tally
   correct placements using key. Identify confusing categories.
5. **30-35:** Individual baseline count, observation and possible criterion:
   at least eight correct of ten in two minutes.
6. **35-40:** Count cards/tools, save records, clear.

## Meeting 2: exactly 40 minutes

1. **0-4:** Retrieve baseline; choose confusion to address.
2. **4-9:** State limits two signs/40 cm tape; keep ten cards and two-minute test.
3. **9-21:** Build word/symbol sorting signs, every pupil authors one feature.
4. **21-30:** Neighbor tests ten cards, record count; revise one confusing
   feature and repeat identical-length test with shuffled cards.
5. **30-35:** Each records before/after, criterion and change rationale.
6. **35-40:** Count/save signs/cards; no real bin installation.

## Meeting 3: exactly 40 minutes

1. **0-4:** "What result would justify recommending the routine?"
2. **4-9:** Model a two-bar chart 0-10, title and labels.
3. **9-21:** Run final ten-card test with new neighbor; individual bar chart
   compares baseline/final counts, not fabricated percentages.
4. **21-30:** Parallel peer explanation: evidence, reuse choice, model limit;
   revise recommendation for audience.
5. **30-35:** Each explains two bars and what adult observation would be needed
   to measure actual waste reduction.
6. **35-40:** Archive dated data, count/save kit, remove tape.

## Success/access/troubleshooting

Meets: observations, criterion/limit, actual repeated counts and chart/impact
distinction. Grade 3/support: 0-10 template/oral evidence. Grade 4/challenge:
compare changes and explain new-user sampling. Wrong sort: note confusion,
revise sign, not key mid-test. No physical cards: draw ten on scrap paper; do not
handle trash. Early finish: test different card order under same conditions.

**Family:** We tested a creation-care sorting model. Ask, "What did the data
measure, and what didn't it measure?" Optional: discuss one reuse habit.
