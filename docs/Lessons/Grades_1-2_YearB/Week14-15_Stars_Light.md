## Before class

1. Assign teams of at most three: **4 / 5 / 7 / 9** kits for **10 / 15 / 20 / 25** pupils. Keep work at tables; distribute materials, not roaming station rotations.
2. Draw six-dot MODEL cards. Adult fits two AA cells inside each of three screw-closed, current-protected packs; these fitted cells are included in each complete pack allocation. Use manufacturer-rated 3-V current-limited modules, not bare LEDs. Connect positive-to-positive and negative-to-negative with two leads, switch off/on and pretest all three complete kits. Place three dry stations in one sightline; do not issue unmatched parts.
3. Put two plain-paper sheets per child per meeting and crayons at tables. Draw this record on the board: **represented pattern / predicted output / actual path-switch result**. Children copy boxes/icons, not paragraphs. Teacher dates and saves each child's record between meetings.
4. Read the SAFETY section, pretest the actual task with one issued kit and put a roster beside the timer. Demonstrate orally with objects; read every direction aloud. No independent reading, Year A completion or family donation is a prerequisite.

## Vocabulary and teacher background

Source = makes light; reflect = redirects light; circuit = complete electrical path; switch = opens/closes the path.

The Sun is a star; a paper dot does not represent its true size/distance. A protected lamp circuit supplies an observable switch input/light output; a broken path does not power the lamp.

**Common misconception:** Paper stars emit light, or touching any two wires to a battery is safe.

**If asked:** "Does a lamp prove the Christmas story?" No; it shows an electrical output, not a religious or historical conclusion.

## SAFETY

No mains power, bare cells, button cells, student cell replacement, short circuits, bare LEDs or hot parts. Adult stops/disconnects a warm/damaged kit. Students only join the two preidentified terminals while switch is OFF, then use the switch. Never connect the pack terminals directly together. Keep aisles/exits clear. Stop for damaged materials; do not improvise a hazardous replacement. No student handles loose batteries or teacher cutting tools.

## Meeting 1: Represent star light - 30 minutes

1. **0-3:** Distinguish a light-producing star from paper that reflects classroom light.
2. **3-7:** Model counting six dots and choosing a clear arrangement that communicates a hope idea without calling it sky data.
3. **7-20:** Each child represents the six-dot model on cardstock, counts once and chooses intentional contrast/placement. Partner checks two position details; author revises.
4. **20-26:** Each child explains the count, purposeful choice and size/distance model limit.
5. **26-30:** Switch all packs OFF; adult disconnects/stores packs and leads separately from dry art, counting three packs/modules and six leads. Collect each child's dated evidence, count kit items, return tools and clear tables. Cleanup is part of the period, not time added afterward.

## Meeting 2: Closed path, real output - 30 minutes

1. **0-3:** Show the protected pack/lamp and predict OFF/ON output; repeat the no-short-circuit rule.
2. **3-7:** Adult demonstrates the two marked connections with switch OFF, then ON/OFF. Open one lamp connection with power OFF, then test ON briefly and switch OFF.
3. **7-20:** Two four-minute waves for 10/15 pupils or three for 20/25, three teams per wave. Each child gets about 40 seconds to join/rejoin one designated lamp lead with power OFF, switch ON/OFF and report light/no-light. Waiting teams trace large path drawings. Adult monitors all three stations.
4. **20-26:** Each child points to the complete path, gives two actual switch/path outcomes and distinguishes drawing evidence from physical operation. Teacher checks during waves; any unfinished turns remain unassessed.
5. **26-30:** Switch all packs OFF; adult disconnects/stores packs and leads separately from dry art, counting three packs/modules and six leads. Collect each child's dated evidence, count kit items, return tools and clear tables. Cleanup is part of the period, not time added afterward.

## Questions to ask

- What happened when the electrical path was opened?
- Which object makes light and which reflects it?

## Individual evidence and success

**Grade 1:** Count the pattern and individually switch a complete protected path ON/OFF, identifying output.

**Grade 2:** Explain the open-versus-closed path result, safe connection procedure and star-model limitation; justify an artistic contrast choice.

Teacher visits three table teams during each of the first three four-minute blocks of practice (7-19), listening to or observing each member briefly; use the remaining practice minute and 20-26 for unfinished checks and comparison. At 25, this is nine table visits, **not 25 presentations**. Where a meeting specifies waves, use its supervised wave/check schedule instead of simultaneous table visits. Children keep separate records and partners may not answer for each other. Score each stated criterion **not yet / prompted / independent**; record absent/unobserved evidence as such. A group product, attendance, attractive art or a certificate is not individual mastery. Paper tracing is model evidence only, not physical circuit mastery. No guaranteed school holding is asserted.

## Support and challenge

**Support / nonreader access:** Large color-coded terminal labels and adult stabilization; child directs and operates the switch. Adult scribing, pointing, tactile/large pieces and a seated partner-directed action can show understanding; do not score motor speed. Record who actually operated hardware/software: a partner's operation does not establish the child's independent operation.

**Challenge:** Explain why a correct drawing cannot prove the physical lamp actually worked. Do not add an unprepared activity or extend the native period.

## If things go wrong / indoor alternative

Only one/two complete kits: use fewer waves/turns and record missing operation evidence; never substitute bare battery wiring. No safe matched kits: trace a paper path and compare source/reflection, recording electrical operation unassessed. Lamp dark: switch OFF, adult checks marked terminals and manufacturer polarity; no random rewiring.

**Indoor:** The complete primary lesson is indoors at tables; Minnesota weather, internet access and daylight are not prerequisites.

## Family copy - optional, no routine homework

We represented star light and, where complete safe kits were available, tested real open/closed lamp paths. Ask: "What happened when the electrical path was opened?" Optional: identify an ordinary lamp switch without opening or rewiring anything. No purchase, donation, device, home teaching or required take-home project.
