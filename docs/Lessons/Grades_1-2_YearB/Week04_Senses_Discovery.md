# Week 4: Senses and Discovery

## Lesson at a glance
| Field | Teacher information |
|---|---|
| Grade / unit / title | Grades 1-2 / Observation / Senses and Discovery |
| Time / domains | One 30-minute meeting / C, S, A |
| Big idea | Different safe tools/senses reveal different details; everyone can contribute evidence. |
| Student objective | "I can describe two details and compare two objects using a safe way to observe." |
| Why | Observations support comparisons without requiring every child to use every sense. |

## Before class
1. Screen clean large textured objects: cloth, smooth plastic lid and corrugated cardboard. Remove scents, food, feathers, spices and unknown plant materials.
2. Teacher seals two identical rice shakers permanently, checks leakage and never opens them in class. Alternative: teacher claps two gentle sound patterns; children can observe motion instead of listening.
3. Put texture examples and two lids of visibly different sizes at each table. Draw a two-box observation sheet on the board; folding plain paper suffices.
4. Offer look, safe optional touch, or picture/tactile replica; never surprise-touch or blindfold a child. Show quiet space and opt-out choices.

## Supplies
- **Per student:** one folded sheet, crayon.
- **Per team of 2-3:** three large clean texture examples, two smooth lids; optional plastic magnifier.
- **Whole class:** two permanently sealed shakers, timer, sorting mat, handwashing.
- **Teacher-only:** shaker sealing/checking, roster, object screening; no taste/smell supplies.

## Vocabulary / background
Observe = notice safely; texture = surface feel or appearance; compare = describe what is alike/different; evidence = noticed detail; sort = group by a property.
Sight/hearing/touch are options, not requirements. Scientists use instruments when direct senses are unavailable or unsafe. Tasting an unknown substance is never a scientific observation method for this class.
**Misconception:** a guessing game answer matters more than evidence. "Bumpy ridges" is better evidence than "I think it is a shell."
**If asked "Why aren't we tasting?"** Taste can expose allergens/unsafe substances; today's objectives need no food.

## Exact numbered sequence (30 minutes)
1. **0-4:** State objective, access choices and safe touch rule; optional original prayer thanks God for each person's contributions.
2. **4-8:** Teacher models two details on cardboard using looking and optional touch; contrast observation with guess.
3. **8-17:** Teams examine texture examples; each child draws/points/describes two details. Partner compares two objects using rough/smooth, size or visible ridges. Teacher checks individuals.
4. **17-21:** Teacher demonstrates quiet sealed shaker sounds or gentle claps with visible movement. Children compare patterns by hearing **or** seeing; no requirement to hear.
5. **21-26:** Each child sorts two objects by one stated property and explains/draws evidence. Grade 2 sorts same objects a second way. Partners name a method that includes someone who chooses not to touch/hear.
6. **26-30:** Cleanup and handwashing.

## Questions / success
What did you notice rather than guess? Which observation method is safe for you? Can the same objects be grouped another way?
- **Grade 1:** oral/pointed/drawn two details, one alike/different comparison and one grouping reason; choose an accessible method.
- **Grade 2:** two precise details, two defensible grouping rules and an explanation that a different method can still supply evidence.
- Teacher marks **not yet / prompted / independent** for details, sorting and inclusion; never scores use of all senses.

## If things go wrong / indoor fallback
Leaking shaker: remove immediately and use claps/visible motion. Sensory overload: quiet visual/tactile table by choice. Missing objects: use large teacher drawings with visible texture marks; distinguish pictured observations from touch evidence. Entire lesson is indoors and no-device ready.

## SAFETY / support / challenge
No tasting, smell jars, blindfolds, reaching into unknown bags, loud shaking near ears, loose beans/rice or forced touch. Teacher seals and counts shakers. Respect sensory and physical access without implying anyone is less blessed.
Support: two picture choices, sentence starter "I notice...", oral response, larger objects and adult scribing. Challenge: Grade 2 justify two classifications or ask what a magnifier might reveal.

## Cleanup / family / sources
Return/count objects, wipe washable surfaces per school practice, recycle scraps and wash hands.
**Family snippet:** We compared details using safe accessible observation methods. Our Catholic connection was including every observer. Ask: "What detail helped you sort an object?" Optional: describe a familiar object or picture together; no tasting or purchase. No routine homework.
