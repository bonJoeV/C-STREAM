## Before class / supplies
1. Test a 40-50 cm rigid cardboard ramp with one and two thin books, maximum support height 10 cm. Tape securely in a shallow tray/table lane with end catch box; runout cannot enter an aisle.
2. Mark fixed starting point and ramp-end line. Use same toy car, ramp, lane and release method for both heights. Do not change cars or landing surfaces during this test.
3. Prepare two-column low/high recording paper; teacher makes a shared example.
- **Per student:** half-sheet, crayon, personal prediction.
- **Per team of 2-3:** one car, ramp, two books, tape, measuring strip of equal 2 cm marks or equal cubes, catch box.
- **Class:** timer, class chart; reusable kits.
- **Teacher-only:** ramp preflight, height check, roster. No small loose balls or launched loads.

| Students | Teams / complete ramp kits | Individual sheets |
|---|---:|---:|
| 10 | 4 | 10 |
| 15 | 5 | 15 |
| 20 | 7 | 20 |

At 25 use nine parallel table lanes only if table area contains each run. Otherwise three supervised lanes with three 4-minute waves; off-lane teams predict and compare teacher example data. Record any student not observed releasing/measuring.

## Vocabulary / background
Gravity = Earth's pull on objects; distance = how far; trial = one test; condition = test setting; fair test = keep important things the same except the planned change.
At these small classroom heights gravity is effectively unchanged. A higher starting position can provide more gravitational potential energy. Runout depends on ramp/surface/car; farther stopping distance does **not** measure speed directly, and results may differ.
**Misconception:** gravity pulls harder from a higher ramp. It does not in this setup.
**If asked "Which car was faster?"** We measured stopping distance, not time/speed; that needs a different test.

## Exact numbered sequence (30 minutes)
1. **0-3:** Explain comparison objective and lane rule; each child predicts low/high/same by pointing.
2. **3-7:** Model no-push release and measuring from ramp end to car's final front position; count equal units, always same reference.
3. **7-10:** Identify what stays the same and changed book height; pairs rehearse roles: releaser, measurer, recorder.
4. **10-22:** Run three low and three high trials per team, swapping roles. Record each result, including surprises; Grade 1 uses marks/cubes, Grade 2 whole cm if ready. Do not force a predicted pattern.
5. **22-26:** Each child points to one result for each condition, compares, and names one controlled feature; ask whether the trials agree.
6. **26-30:** Cleanup.

## Questions / measurable evidence
Did you push? Where does measurement start? What changed? Does one result prove a rule for every car?
- **Grade 1:** release without push, compare one low/high result orally or with drawn lengths, name same car/start.
- **Grade 2:** record at least two trials per condition, compare spread/typical result informally, name one changed and two unchanged features; no required averages.
- Score **not yet / prompted / independent** for release, measurement and evidence-based comparison. Winning a race is not assessed.

## If things go wrong / indoor fallback
Car leaves lane: stop, lower ramp and add catch sides. Car does not roll: inspect wheels/ramp join and retest at same low setting; document change. Inconsistent results are valid; revisit release and surface. No kits: teacher demonstrates while children compare honest shared results, noting direct manipulation not individually assessed. Indoors year-round, no devices needed.

## SAFETY / support / challenge
Ramp below 10 cm; gentle releases only; cars not projectiles. No heavy loads, standing on furniture, chasing or lanes across routes. Teacher approves stable supports.
Support: prebuilt lane, two trials per condition with scaffolded comparison, tactile equal-unit strip, scribing. Challenge: Grade 2 proposes a later different-surface test while explaining why not to combine changes today.

## Cleanup / family / sources
Park/count cars, detach only lesson tape, stack books/ramps, store data.
We tested ramp heights and compared measured stopping distances with honest records. Ask: "What did you keep the same?" Optional: describe a ramp seen in a picture or school; no home experiment required. No routine homework.

### Error interpretation and reteaching

- **Common error interpretation:** Compare the pupil's plan, action, and actual result. A mismatch means the prediction, sequence, condition, measurement, or explanation needs another check; it does not establish low ability, poor effort, or weak faith.
