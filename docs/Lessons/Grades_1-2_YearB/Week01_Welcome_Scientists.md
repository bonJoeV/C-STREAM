## Before class

1. Assign teams of at most three: **4 / 5 / 7 / 9** kits for **10 / 15 / 20 / 25** pupils. Keep work at tables; distribute materials, not roaming station rotations.
2. Screen a detailed leaf picture and smooth block per kit. Draw eye/question/result boxes. Plan an indoor table observation, not an unbounded nature route.
3. Put two plain-paper sheets per child per meeting and crayons at tables. Draw this record on the board: **plan or prediction / actual result / reason or revision**. Children copy boxes/icons, not paragraphs. Teacher dates and saves each child's record between meetings.
4. Read the SAFETY section, pretest the actual task with one issued kit and put a roster beside the timer. Demonstrate orally with objects; read every direction aloud. No independent reading, Year A completion or family donation is a prerequisite.

## Vocabulary and teacher background

Observe = notice; predict = say what may happen; evidence = actual result.

An observation is different from a guess about cause; a classroom block can be turned or rolled to investigate a child's question.

**Common misconception:** Being a scientist means knowing answers rather than asking and checking.

**If asked:** "What if my prediction is wrong?" Keep the result; surprise can improve the next question.

## SAFETY

Dry objects only, no tasting or unknown outdoor specimens. Keep aisles/exits clear. Stop for damaged materials; do not improvise a hazardous replacement. No student handles loose batteries or teacher cutting tools.

## Meeting 1: Scientists begin again - 30 minutes

1. **0-3:** Show leaf and block; model a visible feature and a prediction separately.
2. **3-7:** Demonstrate drawing two details and asking whether changing the block's face changes rolling.
3. **7-20:** Each child closely observes, draws two details, asks an oral question and tries a safe tray turn/roll test. Partner checks the drawing against the object.
4. **20-26:** Each child points to an actual detail/result, states one question and revises a guessed detail that was not observed.
5. **26-30:** Return objects/pictures and save first evidence in private folders. Collect each child's dated evidence, count kit items, return tools and clear tables. Cleanup is part of the period, not time added afterward.

## Questions to ask

- What did you actually notice?
- How could you check your question?

## Individual evidence and success

**Grade 1:** Show two observed details and ask one question.

**Grade 2:** Ask a testable question and distinguish its prediction from an actual result.

Teacher visits three table teams during each of the first three four-minute blocks of practice (7-19), listening to or observing each member briefly; use the remaining practice minute and 20-26 for unfinished checks and comparison. At 25, this is nine table visits, **not 25 presentations**. Where a meeting specifies waves, use its supervised wave/check schedule instead of simultaneous table visits. Children keep separate records and partners may not answer for each other. Score each stated criterion **not yet / prompted / independent**; record absent/unobserved evidence as such. A group product, attendance, attractive art or a certificate is not individual mastery. Use the grade-level criterion, not handwriting, reading speed or religious worth.

## Support and challenge

**Support / nonreader access:** Two object choices, eye/question icons and oral dictation. Adult scribing, pointing, tactile/large pieces and a seated partner-directed action can show understanding; do not score motor speed. Record who actually operated hardware/software: a partner's operation does not establish the child's independent operation.

**Challenge:** Explain what evidence would answer a cause question. Do not add an unprepared activity or extend the native period.

## If things go wrong / indoor alternative

No leaf image: use another dry classroom object. Vague question: offer turn/compare/roll choices without supplying its result.

**Indoor:** The complete primary lesson is indoors at tables; Minnesota weather, internet access and daylight are not prerequisites.

## Family copy - optional, no routine homework

We began Year B with detailed observation and honest inquiry. Ask: "What did you actually notice?" Optional: notice two features of a familiar indoor object. No purchase, donation, device, home teaching or required take-home project.
