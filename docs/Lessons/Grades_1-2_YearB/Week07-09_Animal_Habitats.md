## Before class

1. Assign teams of at most three: **4 / 5 / 7 / 9** kits for **10 / 15 / 20 / 25** pupils. Keep work at tables; distribute materials, not roaming station rotations.
2. Draw/read the supplied reference on each card: "A tree squirrel uses seeds/nuts as food, drinks water, uses a nest/cavity for shelter, and moves through trees/ground." Label this a simplified general model, not a species care guide. Precut materials and draw a four-need checklist.
3. Put two plain-paper sheets per child per meeting and crayons at tables. Draw this record on the board: **need / reference-supported feature / visible-or-missing check**. Children copy boxes/icons, not paragraphs. Teacher dates and saves each child's record between meetings.
4. Read the SAFETY section, pretest the actual task with one issued kit and put a roster beside the timer. Demonstrate orally with objects; read every direction aloud. No independent reading, Year A completion or family donation is a prerequisite.

## Vocabulary and teacher background

Habitat = place meeting needs; shelter = protected place; model = representation; evidence = reference detail.

A nest is not the whole habitat. No live animal, internet research or seed/food prop is needed; squirrels and habitats vary.

**Common misconception:** Every animal lives in a house like ours, or a cardboard habitat can keep an animal alive.

**If asked:** "Can we catch a squirrel?" No; we observe/model and leave wildlife alone.

## SAFETY

No live animals, droppings, food, feathers or outdoor collection; dry screened card only. Keep aisles/exits clear. Stop for damaged materials; do not improvise a hazardous replacement. No student handles loose batteries or teacher cutting tools.

## Meeting 1: Read and represent needs - 30 minutes

1. **0-3:** Read the reference aloud with four icons; distinguish the animal from its habitat.
2. **3-7:** Model one need-and-place connection, not a finished required model.
3. **7-20:** Each child draws four need icons from the reference and a possible layout. Partner checks that each need appears; teacher reads/scribes labels.
4. **20-26:** Each child points to a reference-supported connection and explains why shelter alone is insufficient.
5. **26-30:** Store dry models between meetings; return unused card/tape and keep individual diagrams. Collect each child's dated evidence, count kit items, return tools and clear tables. Cleanup is part of the period, not time added afterward.

## Meeting 2: Build an explanatory model - 30 minutes

1. **0-3:** Retrieve individual plans and state the four-need criterion.
2. **3-7:** Show a flat or folded shelter model and how arrows/icons can show food/water/space without real props.
3. **7-20:** Teams build a habitat on the cardboard square using four cardstock pieces and eight tape strips. Each child creates/directs one feature and checks all four need icons.
4. **20-26:** Each child draws the model and records a four-icon checklist: visible/missing; revise one missing or misleading feature.
5. **26-30:** Store dry models between meetings; return unused card/tape and keep individual diagrams. Collect each child's dated evidence, count kit items, return tools and clear tables. Cleanup is part of the period, not time added afterward.

## Meeting 3: Check and communicate - 30 minutes

1. **0-3:** Recall the reference and distinguish recorded model features from living-animal observations.
2. **3-7:** Model a critique: "I can see shelter; where does the animal get water?"
3. **7-20:** Teams exchange a reference/checklist critique and revise. Each child represents the completed needs and interprets the before/after checklist counts out of four.
4. **20-26:** Each child explains two need-feature links, a corrected feature and one model limit; name a wildlife-care action.
5. **26-30:** Store dry models between meetings; return unused card/tape and keep individual diagrams. Collect each child's dated evidence, count kit items, return tools and clear tables. Cleanup is part of the period, not time added afterward.

## Questions to ask

- How does your model meet this animal's needs?
- What can the cardboard model not do?

## Individual evidence and success

**Grade 1:** Show food, water and shelter connections and count checklist features.

**Grade 2:** Explain all four connections, compare before/after coverage and name a model limit without inventing species facts.

Teacher visits three table teams during each of the first three four-minute blocks of practice (7-19), listening to or observing each member briefly; use the remaining practice minute and 20-26 for unfinished checks and comparison. At 25, this is nine table visits, **not 25 presentations**. Where a meeting specifies waves, use its supervised wave/check schedule instead of simultaneous table visits. Children keep separate records and partners may not answer for each other. Score each stated criterion **not yet / prompted / independent**; record absent/unobserved evidence as such. A group product, attendance, attractive art or a certificate is not individual mastery. Use the grade-level criterion, not handwriting, reading speed or religious worth.

## Support and challenge

**Support / nonreader access:** Use four large need icons, teacher narration and partner placement directed by the child. Adult scribing, pointing, tactile/large pieces and a seated partner-directed action can show understanding; do not score motor speed.

**Challenge:** Explain why another animal would require different features; do not invent a care plan. Do not add an unprepared activity or extend the native period.

## If things go wrong / indoor alternative

No reference pictures: draw icons while reading the supplied general description. Short build time: use a flat diagram model; science evidence stays the same and no structural mastery is claimed.

**Indoor:** The complete primary lesson is indoors at tables; Minnesota weather, internet access and daylight are not prerequisites.

## Family copy - optional, no routine homework

We modeled animal habitat needs and checked explanatory features against a read-aloud reference. Ask: "How does your model meet this animal's needs?" Optional: discuss a habitat in a familiar picture without touching wildlife. No purchase, donation, device, home teaching or required take-home project.
