## Before class

1. Assign teams of at most three: **4 / 5 / 7 / 9** kits for **10 / 15 / 20 / 25** pupils. Keep work at tables; distribute materials, not roaming station rotations.
2. Draw dot quantities 1-5 twice and two copies of 6-10 on 20 cards. Set Grade 1 target within 10, Grade 2 within 20 if secure. Draw AB and ABB pattern strips; no reading or random die required.
3. Put two plain-paper sheets per child per meeting and crayons at tables. Draw this record on the board: **two quantities / combined total / pattern unit**. Children copy boxes/icons, not paragraphs. Teacher dates and saves each child's record between meetings.
4. Read the SAFETY section, pretest the actual task with one issued kit and put a roster beside the timer. Demonstrate orally with objects; read every direction aloud. No independent reading, Year A completion or family donation is a prerequisite.

## Vocabulary and teacher background

Sum = combined amount; compare = more/less/same; unit = repeating part.

Counters show an operation strategy. A correct answer without a demonstrated strategy is not the full evidence.

**Common misconception:** Fastest answer means best reasoning.

**If asked:** "May I use counters?" Yes; explain how they support your count.

## SAFETY

Large counters only; none in mouths, no competition or floor scattering. Keep aisles/exits clear. Stop for damaged materials; do not improvise a hazardous replacement. No student handles loose batteries or teacher cutting tools.

## Meeting 1: Combine, record, repair - 30 minutes

1. **0-3:** Invite a shared-turn game and set grade-specific number bounds.
2. **3-7:** Model drawing two dot cards, combining counters, recording a two-column picture result and fixing an AB mistake.
3. **7-20:** Each child takes repeated turns: choose two cards, combine within the grade bound, explain a count strategy and record two sums. Partners compare results; each makes/repairs a pattern.
4. **20-26:** Each child demonstrates one sum strategy, interprets larger/smaller recorded totals and identifies the pattern unit/next two elements.
5. **26-30:** Count 20 cards/counters, retain each child's result strip and clear tables. Collect each child's dated evidence, count kit items, return tools and clear tables. Cleanup is part of the period, not time added afterward.

## Questions to ask

- How did you find the combined total?
- Which part of the pattern repeats?

## Individual evidence and success

**Grade 1:** Combine within 10 using counters, compare two totals and extend AB by two elements.

**Grade 2:** Use a demonstrated within-20 strategy, explain why one total is larger and repair an ABB/AAB pattern.

Teacher visits three table teams during each of the first three four-minute blocks of practice (7-19), listening to or observing each member briefly; use the remaining practice minute and 20-26 for unfinished checks and comparison. At 25, this is nine table visits, **not 25 presentations**. Where a meeting specifies waves, use its supervised wave/check schedule instead of simultaneous table visits. Children keep separate records and partners may not answer for each other. Score each stated criterion **not yet / prompted / independent**; record absent/unobserved evidence as such. A group product, attendance, attractive art or a certificate is not individual mastery. Use the grade-level criterion, not handwriting, reading speed or religious worth.

## Support and challenge

**Support / nonreader access:** Use quantities 1-3 and a ten-frame drawn on allocated paper; record scaffolded number bounds. Adult scribing, pointing, tactile/large pieces and a seated partner-directed action can show understanding; do not score motor speed.

**Challenge:** Find a different pair with the same total and justify it. Do not add an unprepared activity or extend the native period.

## If things go wrong / indoor alternative

20 not secure: use within 10, recording prompted/scaffolded evidence rather than assume mastery. Cards missing: teacher/children draw dots on the allocated paper.

**Indoor:** The complete primary lesson is indoors at tables; Minnesota weather, internet access and daylight are not prerequisites.

## Family copy - optional, no routine homework

We used visible quantities and explained strategies in a cooperative math game. Ask: "How did you find the combined total?" Optional: combine two small familiar groups verbally; no timed drills. No purchase, donation, device, home teaching or required take-home project.

### Error interpretation and reteaching

- **Common error interpretation:** Compare the pupil's plan, action, and actual result. A mismatch means the prediction, sequence, condition, measurement, or explanation needs another check; it does not establish low ability, poor effort, or weak faith. Specifically guard against: **Fastest answer means best reasoning.**
- **Reteaching option within existing time:** Use the lesson's own fallback/support, not a new activity: **20 not secure: use within 10, recording prompted/scaffolded evidence rather than assume mastery. Cards missing: teacher/children draw dots on the allocated paper.** Then apply **Support / nonreader access: Use quantities 1-3 and a ten-frame drawn on allocated paper; record scaffolded number bounds. Adult scribing, pointing, tactile/large pieces and a seated partner-directed action can show understanding; do not score motor speed. ** Re-model only **3-7: Model drawing two dot cards, combining counters, recording a two-column picture result and fixing an AB mistake.**, let the pupil repeat one smallest safe step, and collect a new independent result in a different color or on a fresh line so the first evidence remains visible.
