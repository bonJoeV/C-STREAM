# Session 11: Lenten Engineering

## Lesson at a glance

Grades 3-4; Bi-Weekly A; Service design; **one 40-minute meeting**.
**Objective:** make a ruler-parking mat for the classroom, test retrieval/return
with a peer, revise and hand over a usable mat to the teacher.
**Why:** Lenten service can include an actual small useful action, not only a
promised community project. Catholic/CST-C2 listen/serve respectfully;
CST-E1 user criterion; CST-E2 real retest; CST-E3 recommendation/limit.
Official alignment **VERIFICATION REQUIRED**. Technology **None**; introductory.
Prep 10; cleanup 5. Classroom usefulness tested, no claims of wider social impact.

## Before class and exact supplies

Teacher approves current classroom need: shared rulers get misplaced.
Teams <=3 (4/5/7/9): one cardstock mat, real ruler, two markers.
Each pupil evidence sheet/pencil. Teacher board/timer/demo from kit.
If no actual need/approval, call it fictional prototype, record **service
handover not completed**, not fabricated benefit. No external partner needed.

| Allocation | 10 | 15 | 20 | 25 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Mats; rulers (1/team each) | 4 | 5 | 7 | 9 |
| Markers (2/team) | 8 | 10 | 14 | 18 |
| Paper; pencils (1/pupil each) | 10 | 15 | 20 | 25 |

## Vocabulary/background/SAFETY

Need = stated difficulty; service = useful action; criterion = tested success;
handover = actual transfer. **Misconception:** a plan is completed service.
**If asked "Will this fix all lost tools?"** Two peer tests cannot prove that;
teacher use over time would be further evidence. Lent's prayer/charity
application is not a grade for sacrifices or family donations.
Desk paper/ruler only, no medical/food/disability simulations/private hardships.
Stop/report damaged tools. Indoor primary.

## Meeting 1: exactly 40 minutes

1. **0-4:** Explain approved need; "What did the user actually request?"
2. **4-9:** Criterion peer returns ruler to outline and identifies it twice
   within ten seconds; limit one mat/word AND symbol.
3. **9-21:** Each sketches feature; team outlines ruler/labels parking location.
4. **21-30:** Neighbor retrieves/returns twice, record correct cycles 0-2;
   revise confusing label/outline and repeat twice.
5. **30-35:** Each child explains need, before/after and limit; teacher accepts
   approved mats for actual classroom use, logging received/not yet.
6. **35-40:** Use mats to return real rulers, cap/count/clear, file dated evidence.

## Success/access/troubleshooting

Meets: stated need, actual repeated user tests, justified revision and qualified
recommendation; service completion separately requires teacher handover.
Grade 3/support: traced outline/dictation. Grade 4/challenge: contrast/location
tradeoff and longer-term effectiveness measure. Confusion: change label not
coach user. No ruler: printed/drawn tool model, function/service shifted and
physical retrieval not observed. Early finish: new tester, same criterion.

**Family:** We tested and handed over a small classroom service tool. Ask,
"What did the user test show?" Describe prototype only if handover was not made.
## Teacher artifact pack (release checklist)

Artifacts must document a real user test and keep prototype completion separate
from actual classroom handover.

- **Student material (engineering evidence page, print/no-print):** Print the exact
  prompts `The approved classroom need is ___`; `Our criterion: a peer can
  identify, retrieve and return the ruler to the mat twice, each cycle within
  10 seconds, without coaching`; `My sketched feature is ___ because ___`.
  Add a test table with rows **Test 1A, Test 1B, Test 2A, Test 2B** and columns
  `identified correct place?`, `returned inside outline?`, `time <=10 s?`,
  `prompted?`. Follow with `The tester was confused by ___`;
  `We changed ___`; `The retest showed ___`; `The mat uses this word ___ and
  this symbol ___`; `Recommendation/limit: ___`; and teacher-only
  `HANDOVER: received / not yet / fictional prototype—no approved need`.
  No printer is needed: draw the four-row table on the board for pupils to copy
  onto plain paper. Timing, results and explanations may be spoken to a scribe.
- **Evidence and answer guidance:** Meeting evidence names the
  misplaced shared-ruler need, records two pre-revision retrieval/return
  cycles, identifies an observed confusion, justifies a visible revision, and
  records two uncoached retest cycles against the ten-second criterion.
  Accept changes such as darkening the ruler outline, moving `RULER PARKING`
  above it, or adding a simple ruler symbol because a tester searched the wrong
  area. A sound recommendation is `Use this mat at the ruler shelf and check
  after a week`; a sound limit is `Two peers do not prove no ruler will be
  lost.` Service completion requires the teacher's **received** mark. If the
  need was not approved, accept design/testing as a **fictional prototype** but
  not claims of classroom benefit or handover.
- **Worked example:** Demonstrate a plain cardstock
  ruler outline labeled only `TOOLS`; have a peer hesitate or choose the wrong
  place; record the uncoached result; revise to `RULER PARKING` plus a ruler
  symbol; and run the same two-cycle criterion again.
  The finished example is a durable, legible mat with one outline, one word label and one symbol,
  matched to individual before/after test records and a qualified
  recommendation. The handover field is **N/A—not completed** only when no
  current need was approved; never pre-check “received.”
- **Board setup and visual example:** Display the real ruler beside a
  sample mat, an analog/digital classroom timer visible only to the recorder,
  and a before/after feature sketch. On the board write
  `NEED -> CRITERION -> BUILD -> USER TEST -> OBSERVED CONFUSION -> CHANGE ->
  SAME RETEST -> HANDOVER`. Add a neutral test script:
  `Please find the ruler, take it, and return it where you think it belongs.`
  Keep a teacher handover chart with team numbers and only
  `received / revise / fictional`; do not post pupil performance.
- **Discussion with acceptable response:** Ask, `What did the user do, rather
  than what did the designer hope?` Accept an observed retrieval, return,
  timing or confusion. Ask, `Why must the tester receive no hints?` Accept:
  hints test the coach, not whether the mat communicates independently. Ask,
  `When does the prototype become completed service?` Accept: after approval
  and actual teacher handover/use, not at the drawing stage. Ask,
  `Will two successful cycles prevent every future loss?` Accept: no; longer
  classroom use is needed. Correct the misconceptions that decoration alone is
  engineering evidence or that Lenten service is measured by family donations
  or disclosed sacrifices.
- **Kit contents, kit label and reset:** Per team: one cardstock mat, one real
  metric ruler and exactly two classroom markers; per pupil: one evidence sheet
  and pencil. Teacher supplies board, timer and the approved-need/handover
  decision. Kit label on the back of each mat:
  `C-STREAM | G3-4 | S11 | TEAM __ | RULER PARKING | TESTED __/__/__`.
  Cleanup and reset by capping/counting two markers, placing the ruler on its outline,
  pairing each retained mat with its four test rows, and moving only
  teacher-accepted mats to the approved classroom location. Store
  `revise/not yet` and fictional prototypes separately so they cannot imply
  completed service.
- **Unfinished work:** Circle
  `need / criterion / build / Test 1 / revision / Test 2 / recommendation /
  handover` and record the last completed cycle and whether it was prompted.
  Return the real ruler to classroom storage; hold the mat and evidence page
  together for the next supervised test. No take-home service, solicitation,
  donation or private hardship interview is assigned.
- **Annotated exemplar, common error reading and reteach:** Underline the approved
  need and measurable criterion, box all four result rows, arrow the tester's
  confusion to the exact changed feature, and circle the handover status and
  limitation. A fast return after the designer points is **prompted**, not a
  passing independent trial; a changed decoration unrelated to observed
  confusion lacks revision evidence; `received` without teacher confirmation
  is a handover-record error. Reteach with the neutral script, hide the
  designer's hands, run one ten-second cycle, and complete
  `The user did ___; was confused by ___; so we changed ___.`
- **Safety classification:** Severity **low** and likelihood
  **low** for desk-based cardstock, rulers and markers. Adult ratio is **1:3 maximum**,
  one circulating adult for each working team of no more than three pupils, and
  keep walking retrieval routes clear. Stop/report splintered rulers or leaking
  markers. Do not simulate medical, food, disability or private financial
  hardship and do not grade personal Lenten sacrifices. The used commercial
  product category is non-aerosol classroom marker. Enter the exact
  manufacturer/product in the [Safety Data Sheet Register](../../../Review/Safety_Data_Sheet_Register.csv),
  record whether an SDS applies and the school approval decision, and keep the
  markers on **HOLD** until that review is resolved. Use only an approved
  product after the hold is cleared.
- **Print/accessibility check:** Use 14-point black-and-white fields, a thick
  ruler silhouette, one high-contrast word plus a recognizable symbol, and
  yes/no result boxes that do not depend on color alone. Read the criterion and
  neutral script aloud. Permit seated tester/designer/recorder roles, oral or
  dictated evidence, adaptive grips and a scribe; do not interpret motor speed
  as design understanding.
- **Technology artifact:** None applies; this lesson has no technology artifact.
