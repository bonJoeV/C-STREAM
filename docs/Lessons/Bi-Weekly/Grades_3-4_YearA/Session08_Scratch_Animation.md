# Session 8: Scratch Animation

## Lesson at a glance

Grades 3-4; Bi-Weekly A; Computing art; **one 40-minute meeting**.
**Objective:** edit/run a coordinated two-sprite story, trace three messages
and revise dialogue from feedback. **Why:** actual events can coordinate
characters more reliably than guessed timing. Catholic/CST-C2 helping without
stereotypes; CST-T1 operation; CST-T2 event/broadcast trace; CST-A3 narrative
critique. Official alignment **VERIFICATION REQUIRED**. Technology **Required**;
novice starter provided, Session 5 helpful not assumed. Prep 20 after Scratch
works, installation extra; cleanup 5.

## Before class and exact supplies

Guest/offline Scratch 3; add two library sprites A/B and one backdrop. Assemble
below; flag twice/Stop/save `.sb3`/reopen; leave sample/save location.
Teams <=3 (4/5/7/9): computer/team. Each pupil two paper sheets/pencil.
Teacher board/timer/demo from issued device. Original fictional helping story,
not invented saint/Bible dialogue presented as history.

| Allocation | 10 | 15 | 20 | 25 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Computers (1/team) | 4 | 5 | 7 | 9 |
| Paper (2/pupil) | 20 | 30 | 40 | 50 |
| Pencils (1/pupil) | 10 | 15 | 20 | 25 |

## Starter, vocabulary and background

```text
A: when green flag clicked
   go to x: (-100) y: (0)
   say [May I have help finding the book tray?] for (2) seconds
   broadcast [reply] and wait
   say [Thank you for showing me.] for (2) seconds

B: when green flag clicked
   go to x: (100) y: (0)
   say []
B: when I receive [reply]
   say [I can show you the tray.] for (2) seconds
```

Broadcast = event message; wait = finish receiving scripts before continuing;
dialogue = conversation; revision = purposeful change.
**Misconception:** changing B's flag script makes it answer after A.
**If asked "Can we add scenes?"** Not required; first verify A/B/A order.

## SAFETY/privacy

Dry seated devices, no public accounts/photos/voices/private names. Stop/report
damaged leads. Indoor primary; invented story labeled fiction, not religious
proof.

## Meeting 1: exactly 40 minutes

1. **0-4:** "How can a story model respectful help?" Optional original prayer.
2. **4-10:** Demo sprites/broadcast/Stop; predict A/B/A order.
3. **10-21:** Each pupil edits/runs three-minute turn; peers storyboard three
   messages and record actual order.
4. **21-29:** Reader identifies problem/help/thanks; revise one line then rerun
   twice, checking coordinated order.
5. **29-35:** Each child traces trigger/reply/return, explains feedback revision;
   log actual edit/run/stop separately.
6. **35-40:** Stop/save/reopen, return devices, collect dated storyboards.

## Success/access/troubleshooting

Meets: correct three-event trace, observed operation and story revision.
Grade 3/support: copied scripts/read-aloud/dictation. Grade 4/challenge:
explain broadcast versus broadcast-and-wait before changing anything.
No reply: match message spelling/selected sprite, inspect receive hat, rerun.
Half devices: two waves of three-minute turns in work windows; fewer means
reschedule. No device: paper A/B/reply cards at same times; narrative/CT only,
**animation execution not observed**. Early finish: clearer dialogue, no new
unverified multimedia workflow.

**Family:** We coordinated a fictional helping story. Ask, "What triggered the
reply?" Optional: tell a three-part story, no home coding.
## Teacher artifact pack (release checklist)

Release only after the starter file, local save route and the following evidence
materials have been checked on the issued devices.

- **Student material (storyboard/trace sheet, print or plain paper):** Use
  three large frames headed `1—A asks`, `2—B helps`, `3—A thanks`. Beneath them
  print these exact fields: `My predicted order: ___`; `A sends message ___
  using broadcast / broadcast and wait`; `B starts because ___`; `My observed
  order after Run 1: ___`; `I personally edited ___, clicked the green flag
  ___ time(s), and clicked Stop ___ time(s)`; `Reader feedback: ___`;
  `I changed ___ to ___ because ___`; `Observed order after revision, Run 1:
  ___; Run 2: ___`. Add checkboxes **device execution observed** and
  **paper role-play only**. If copies are unavailable, pupils fold one sheet
  into three frames and copy the fields from the board. Dialogue may be typed,
  handwritten, dictated or demonstrated with A/B cards.
- **Evidence and answer guidance:** Require the actual sequence
  **A asks -> B replies -> A thanks**, identification that B's
  `when I receive [reply]` hat responds to A's matching message, one
  personally made dialogue edit, and two successful reruns after feedback.
  Accept a revision that improves clarity, courtesy or specificity, such as
  changing `Help me` to `May I have help finding the book tray?`. Accept
  `broadcast [reply] and wait` as the reason A's thanks follows B's completed
  reply. Do not count a correctly ordered paper enactment as Scratch execution,
  a renamed message that does not match both scripts, or a peer operating
  during the child's recorded turn.
- **Worked example:** Demonstrate the supplied starter unchanged:
  predict A/B/A, click the flag, point to `broadcast [reply] and wait`, collect
  feedback `Say which tray`, revise B to `I can show you the blue book tray`,
  and rerun twice. The finished example `.sb3` opens locally, contains two sprites and
  one backdrop, produces A/B/A twice from the green flag, and matches a
  completed storyboard with the before/after line. For a no-device pupil,
  `.sb3`, flag/Stop actions and execution evidence are **N/A**; the completed
  artifact is a three-card event trace labeled **paper simulation**.
- **Board setup and visual example:** On the demo screen enlarge the exact
  A scripts and B receive script; use arrows
  `green flag -> A ask -> broadcast reply and wait -> B receives reply ->
  A thanks`. That enlarged script pair is the visual example. Post separate sprite cards so children can see that scripts belong
  to particular sprites. The board has columns
  `Prediction | Run 1 actual | Feedback | Revision | Rerun 1 | Rerun 2`.
  Above the story write **ORIGINAL FICTION—NOT A HISTORICAL SAINT OR BIBLE
  QUOTATION**. Keep the local filename pattern
  `S08-Team__-HelpingStory.sb3` visible.
- **Discussion with acceptable response:** Ask, `What event starts B's
  reply?` Accept: B receives the message named `reply`, sent by A. Ask,
  `Why use “and wait” here?` Accept: A pauses until scripts triggered by that
  broadcast finish, so thanks follows the help. Ask, `What made the revision
  more respectful?` Accept answers naming a polite request, specific help, or
  removal of a stereotype. Ask, `Will moving B's green-flag script below A's
  code guarantee it speaks second?` Correct answer: no; script position does
  not schedule separate green-flag stacks. Watch for the misconceptions that
  elapsed seconds are the only coordination method or that a planned order is
  evidence of a run.
- **Kit contents, kit label and reset:** Per team: one compatible school computer
  with tested offline/guest Scratch 3, starter containing sprites A/B and one
  backdrop, and a known local save folder; per pupil: two sheets and pencil.
  Kit label: `C-STREAM | G3-4 | S08 | DEVICE __ | OFFLINE LOCAL FILES ONLY`.
  Cleanup and reset by clicking Stop, saving with the team filename, closing Scratch,
  reopening that `.sb3`, running once to confirm A/B/A, closing without upload,
  and returning charger/device according to school procedure. Collect dated
  storyboards and clear any pupil names inadvertently entered in dialogue.
- **Unfinished work:** The teacher circles the resume point
  `starter opens / A sends / B receives / A returns / first run / feedback /
  revision / two reruns / save-reopen`. Record the last observed order and
  local filename. Resume only on a school device; no child is asked to create
  an account, upload the project or finish at home.
- **Annotated exemplar, common error reading and reteach:** In the exemplar, underline
  the three displayed lines in A/B/A order, draw a box around the identical
  `reply` names, put a check beside each personally observed flag/Stop action,
  and circle the changed words plus reader reason. If B never speaks, check
  sprite ownership, receive hat and exact message selection; if B speaks too
  early, check whether its dialogue remains under the green flag; if A thanks
  before B finishes, distinguish `broadcast` from `broadcast and wait`.
  Reteach with pupils physically holding A, `reply`, and B cards, then rebuild
  only the receive/broadcast connection and complete one prediction-run-check
  cycle before revising prose.
- **Safety classification:** Severity is **low** and likelihood **low** for
  normal seated use, rising if damaged power leads or online sharing are
  allowed. Adult ratio is **1:3 maximum**, one adult circulating for each active team of up to three,
  with school device supervision rules taking precedence if stricter. Keep
  liquids away; stop and report damaged leads. No public account, cloud share,
  camera, microphone, recorded voice, photograph, private name or personal
  story detail. SDS is **not applicable** to software/paper; follow the
  school's equipment documentation for batteries and cleaning products rather
  than adding them to student kits.
- **Print/accessibility check:** Storyboard frames use 14-point or
  larger black-and-white type, thick directional arrows, numbered order and text labels rather
  than color alone. Read scripts aloud, permit role-play before typing, offer
  keyboard navigation, a scribe or dictated dialogue, and rotate editor,
  runner and trace-reader roles while still observing each child's edit/run.
- **Device compliance:** Offline or guest-mode Scratch 3 and local `.sb3`
  storage are required. Preflight launching, saving and reopening on the
  district-approved device image; use no extensions, external assets, logins,
  publishing, analytics submission or student identifiers. A paper route can
  meet narrative/event-tracing evidence only and must state
  **animation execution not observed**. Paper or unplugged work is not
  executed-device evidence; Scratch operation remains deferred/not observed
  until the individual edit, run, and stop are witnessed.
