# Session 3: Bridge Engineering

## Lesson at a glance

Grades 3-4; Bi-Weekly A; Structures; **one 40-minute meeting**.
**Objective:** build an 8 cm span, compare two capped loads and justify an overlap
change. **Why:** access structures need fair tests, not unlimited loading.
Catholic/CST-C2 access/user; CST-E1 criteria/limits; CST-E2 actual retest;
CST-M1 span/count comparison. Official alignment **VERIFICATION REQUIRED**.
Technology **None**; developing. Prep 15 with stock, loans extra; cleanup 5.

## Before class and exact supplies

Teams <=3 (4/5/7/9). Each kit: 20 inspected KEVA planks, two flat closed books,
ten identical plastic counters, tray, ruler. Each pupil paper/pencil.
Teacher board/timer/demo from kit; pretest stable low supports with **8 cm gap**.
Books don't move during tests; save record, dismantle. Rectangular blocks may
replace KEVA for all; label substitution, do not mix material claims.

## Vocabulary/background/SAFETY

Span = gap; deck = crossing surface; overlap = shared contact; load = objects.
Loose unjoined planks aren't rigid triangular trusses; demonstrate stable
overlap rather than promise all triangles strong.
**Misconception:** ten held proves maximum ten. **If asked "More weights?"**
No; record "at least ten." Bridge is a model, never for people/pets.
Low trays only, no heavy loads/throwing/hands underneath. Stop/reset sliding
books and report damaged planks. Indoor primary.

## Meeting 1: exactly 40 minutes

1. **0-4:** "Who benefits from safe access?" Optional service prayer; criteria.
2. **4-9:** Demo center loading, one counter/three-second hold; last successful
   count (fails at 7 -> record 6).
3. **9-21:** Each sketches; build version 1, check 8 cm gap, rotate roles.
4. **21-30:** Test/cap at ten, record; change overlap only, same gap/stock/counters,
   retest. All nine desks test simultaneously.
5. **30-35:** Each child records both counts, whole-number difference, one control/
   change reason and access need.
6. **35-40:** Remove loads, count 20 planks/ten counters, return books/tools.

## Success/access/troubleshooting

Meets: measured span, two actual counts, criterion/control and evidence-based
revision. Grade 3/support: overlap starter/tally/dictation. Grade 4/challenge:
repeat at cap, explain lower bound. Collapse: record zero, improve support
contact. All hold ten: compare design limits, not unsupported strength ranking.
Missing supports: neighboring shared tray, alternate within test block; record
missing physical turns. Early finish: new equal test, no longer unsafe span.

**Family:** We tested plank bridges fairly. Ask, "What does 'at least ten'
mean?" Optional: discuss who a bridge serves.
## Teacher artifact pack (release checklist)

Use this pack to document the capped center-load comparison; it must not invite
loading beyond ten counters or changing more than overlap.

- **Student material (individual bridge-test card):** Print or copy these prompts exactly:
  `The bridge user/access need is ___`; `Measured gap: ___ cm`; `Sketch Version
  1 and shade each overlap`; `TEST 1 last successful load: ___ counters for 3
  seconds`; `Failure occurred at ___ / No failure by cap 10`; `For Version 2 I
  changed only overlap by ___`; `TEST 2 last successful load: ___ counters for
  3 seconds`; `Difference (Test 2 - Test 1): ___`; `I kept ___, ___, and ___
  the same`; `My evidence supports ___`. A no-print pupil folds plain paper
  into `Version 1`, `Version 2`, and `Conclusion` sections and copies the ten
  numbered prompts from the board.
- **Evidence and answer guidance:** Require an 8 cm measured gap, two
  real capped load counts, an overlap-only revision, a signed whole-number
  difference, and at least one control. Accept books, 20-plank stock, counter
  type, center placement, and three-second hold as controls. If the deck fails
  while adding counter 7, the last successful load is 6. If ten remain for
  three seconds, accept only `at least 10 under this test`, not `maximum 10`,
  `unbreakable`, or a real-bridge strength claim. Zero is acceptable evidence
  when no counter is held.
- **Worked example:** Demonstrate an 8 cm gap and Version 1
  holding 6 before failure at 7. Increase shared plank contact without moving
  books or adding planks; Version 2 holds all 10. Complete `10 - 6 = +4` and
  `Longer overlap improved this model in our capped test; it held at least 10`.
  The finished example includes shaded overlaps, both actual counts, difference,
  controls, and limited claim. N/A is justified only for `failure occurred at`
  when the bridge reaches the cap; Test 2 itself cannot be N/A and should be
  marked `not completed` if time expires.
- **Board setup and visual example:** Put two closed books and a sorting tray
  on a low table, place an 8 cm ruler reference between inner book edges, and
  highlight overlap on two demo planks; that pair is the visual example of a
  short versus long overlap. Post `CENTER ONLY | ONE COUNTER AT A
  TIME | HOLD 3 s | STOP AT 10`. Board columns are `version | overlap sketch |
  gap | last successful | first failed | fixed conditions | claim`.
- **Discussion with acceptable response:** Ask `Why record 6 when counter 7 causes collapse?`
  Six is the last load that met the full three-second test. Ask `What can we
  conclude when ten hold?` At least ten held under these conditions; the
  maximum is unknown. Ask `Would moving the books and changing overlap be a
  fair comparison?` No, two variables changed. Correct the misconceptions that
  counters strengthen the bridge, that one successful trial proves the
  strongest design, or that loose planks automatically form a truss.
- **Kit contents, kit label and reset:** Load each tray with 20 inspected KEVA planks,
  10 identical plastic counters, one metric ruler, and a `CENTER / 3 SECONDS /
  CAP 10` test card; place two flat closed books beside it and issue paper/
  pencils individually. Kit label: `G3-4 YA S03 | 20 PLANKS | 10 COUNTERS | 1 RULER
  | 2 BOOKS`. Cleanup and reset after testing: remove every load before touching the deck, count
  20/10/1/2, stack planks flat, nest counters in the tray, return books, and
  quarantine splintered planks or sliding/damaged supports.
- **Unfinished work:** Circle the next stage--`8 cm check / Version
  1 test / overlap change / Version 2 test / difference / claim`--and write the
  last successful count. Store the pupil card with the team's dismantled-kit
  label, not an assembled bridge. On resumption, rebuild and remeasure; do not
  carry forward an unverified load count or describe an untested design as
  stronger.
- **Annotated exemplar, common error reading and reteach:** Underline `8 cm` and fixed
  conditions, box both last-successful counts, hatch the two overlap areas,
  and circle `at least`. A count of 7 after failure at 7 reveals confusion
  between attempted and successful load; two different gaps invalidate the
  comparison; `+4` paired with 8 then 6 shows a subtraction/sign error.
  Reteach with a three-counter mini-sequence, verbalizing `held for three /
  next failed / record last held`, then reset the ruler to exactly 8 cm and
  rehearse one-change language.
- **Safety classification:** Severity **moderate** (falling blocks/counters or pinched
  fingers) and likelihood **low** on low trays with capped plastic loads.
  Adult ratio is **1:25 maximum**, organized in teams of three
  or fewer; the adult must scan all teams during loading. Keep hands out from
  under decks, books flat and stationary, loads light, and models off floors
  and bodies; no throwing or tests with people/pets. SDS is not applicable to
  intact unfinished wood planks, plastic counters, books, paper, or pencils;
  remove damaged/splintered materials and check documentation for any coated
  substitute block or cleaning chemical.
- **Print/accessibility check:** Use 14-point black-and-white text, 0-10 tally boxes, a bold
  8 cm field, and patterns rather than color alone for overlap. Provide a
  tactile/large-print ruler, read counts and instructions aloud, and accept
  tally marks, a labeled sketch, oral calculation, dictation, or scribing.
  A pupil may be recorder/timekeeper rather than reaching under or over a deck.
- **Technology artifact:** None applies; this lesson has no technology artifact.
