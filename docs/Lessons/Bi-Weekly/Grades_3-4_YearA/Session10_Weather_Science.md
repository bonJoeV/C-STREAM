# Session 10: Weather Science

## Lesson at a glance

Grades 3-4; Bi-Weekly A; Weather; **one 40-minute meeting**.
**Objective:** read two water depths with units, explain a water-cycle model
and state a tool/forecast limit. **Why:** weather-aware care for neighbors needs
accurate readings, not a guaranteed prediction.
Catholic/CST-C2 accessible safety message; CST-S1 observed versus simulated;
CST-S3 water-cycle model; CST-T1 ruler/tool troubleshooting; CST-M1 mm readings.
Official alignment **VERIFICATION REQUIRED**. Technology **None**;
introductory. Prep 15; cleanup 5 included.

## Before class and exact supplies

Teams <=3 (4/5/7/9): straight-sided flat-bottom **uncut** clear cup/tray/ruler.
Each pupil two sheets/pencil. Teacher board/timer, one plastic pouring cup and
**100 mL water/team maximum**, one towel roll for drying/spills. Check water-line zero at inside bottom; pretest
two depths under **20 mm**, measure actual, not assumed from volume.
Board simulated rain Mon 0/Tue 4/Wed 2 mm, labeled practice.
Reuse/dry kit; save paper, no outside placement required.

## Vocabulary/background/SAFETY

Precipitation = water falling from atmosphere; evaporation = liquid to gas;
condensation = gas to liquid; gauge = measuring tool.
**Misconception:** this cup reads snow depth or guarantees rain tomorrow.
**If asked "Is today's practice rain real?"** No, unless a named actual source/
time/location is recorded. A placed straight-sided collector is needed for
rainfall measurement; our indoor cup tests depth only.
No glass/cuts/pins/latex, tasting or outside exposure. Adult pours/empties,
dry spills, stop/report damaged cup. Indoor primary.

## Meeting 1: exactly 40 minutes

1. **0-4:** "How can weather information help a neighbor?" Optional care prayer.
2. **4-10:** Model eye-level ruler zero/depth and water-cycle arrows.
3. **10-22:** Adult pours two successive depths; each reads/records both mm
   values, rotating ruler turns. Compare readings and correct a zero error.
4. **22-30:** Each draws evaporation/condensation/precipitation/collection and
   compares practice data; create a clear weather-safety message for a peer.
5. **30-35:** Individual readings/units, cycle relation, model limit and reader
   interpretation; practice total 6 mm, not actual forecast.
6. **35-40:** Adult empties, pupils dry/count/store, save dated evidence.

## Success/access/troubleshooting

Meets: two actual depths/units, accurate cycle model, tool limit and inclusive
message. Grade 3/support: ruler zero marked/arrow stems/scribing.
Grade 4/challenge: reading precision and observation versus forecast.
Uneven cup: use a checked straight-sided replacement or diagram readings,
physical measurement not observed. No water: supplied depth drawings/
simulation labeled, not actual cup test. Early finish: compare two readings
and explain why three practice days do not establish climate.

**Family:** We practiced depth measurement and weather models. Ask, "Which
data were simulated?" Optional: discuss an adult's safe-weather plan.
## Teacher artifact pack (release checklist)

Use the artifact pack to distinguish measured cup depth, invented practice data,
water-cycle explanation and forecasting limits.

- **Student material (measurement/cycle sheet, copy-ready or no-print):** Include a cup
  sketch beside `Reading 1: water line ___ mm; ruler zero was at ___; my eye
  was level yes/not yet`. Repeat for `Reading 2`. Add
  `Reading 2 is ___ mm greater/less than Reading 1`; four arrow boxes
  `evaporation -> condensation -> precipitation -> collection`;
  `Mon 0, Tue 4, Wed 2 mm are REAL / SIMULATED`; `Practice total ___ mm`;
  `This cup test can tell ___ but cannot tell ___`; and
  `My weather-care message to ___ is ___`. The teacher writes the two actual,
  pretested class depths on a private key rather than printing assumed values.
  With no copier, pupils divide paper into **MEASURED TODAY**, **SIMULATED
  PRACTICE**, **MODEL/LIMIT**, and **MESSAGE** and copy each prompt.
- **Evidence and answer guidance:** Accept each child's two observed whole-mm values
  when they match the teacher's actual pretest within the ruler's readable
  precision, with **mm**, zero aligned to the inside bottom, and eye-level
  reading. Accept the correct numerical comparison for those two class values.
  The practice series must be labeled simulated and totaled
  **0 + 4 + 2 = 6 mm**; it is not today's rainfall or a forecast. Accept a
  cycle showing liquid water evaporating, water vapor condensing, water falling
  as precipitation and collecting. A valid limit says the indoor cup test
  reads water depth only and does not measure snow, prove a forecast or
  establish climate. A no-water/diagram route is marked **physical depth
  measurement not observed**.
- **Worked example:** Before class, make a demonstration using a
  different practice depth from either pupil test: ruler zero at the cup's
  inside bottom, eye level at `7 mm`, answer `7 mm—not 7 mL`, then show how to
  compare without calling it rainfall. A finished example contains two dated
  actual readings with units, their correct difference/direction, the four
  ordered cycle labels, `SIMULATED—6 mm total`, one limitation and a reader-
  appropriate safety message such as `To a classmate: ask an adult about the
  weather plan before outdoor recess.` The worked depth is **N/A** as student
  evidence; pupils must read their team's actual water lines.
- **Board setup and visual example:** At the demo station place an uncut
  straight-sided cup in a tray, place a large ruler image with zero at the
  inside bottom, and draw a horizontal eye-level sight line. Post the cycle as
  a circular arrow diagram. Divide the board into
  `ACTUAL CUP TEST—date/time` and
  `SIMULATED PRACTICE—Mon 0 / Tue 4 / Wed 2 mm`; never place the practice
  numbers under “today.” Add a class reading chart with anonymous team rows
  and columns `depth 1 mm | depth 2 mm | difference | zero checked`.
- **Discussion with acceptable response:** Ask, `Why is zero placed at the
  inside bottom?` Accept: depth starts where the water starts; using the rim or
  desk adds an offset. Ask, `Are Mon/Tue/Wed observations from our class?`
  Accept: no, they are invented practice values. Ask, `Can this test tell us
  whether it will rain tomorrow?` Accept: no, it measures poured water depth
  and is not a forecast. Ask, `Where does collected water go next in the
  model?` Accept: it may evaporate again, continuing the cycle. Correct claims
  that mL and mm are interchangeable, that the cup measures snow depth, or that
  three practice days establish climate.
- **Kit contents, kit label and reset:** One tray contains exactly one
  checked straight-sided flat-bottom uncut clear plastic cup and one metric
  ruler; each pupil has two sheets and pencil. The adult station holds one
  plastic graduated pouring cup, no more than **100 mL per team**, and the
  class towel roll. Kit label:
  `C-STREAM | G3-4 | S10 | TEAM __ | DEPTH IN mm | ADULT POUR`.
  Cleanup and reset: the adult pours both levels and empties the cup. Pupils blot cup/tray/ruler,
  wipe the desk, count one cup/one ruler/one tray, and place them inverted in
  the dry bin. The teacher verifies the floor is dry before dismissal and
  retains dated sheets.
- **Unfinished work:** Write the last valid stage on the sheet:
  `ruler alignment / reading 1 / reading 2 / comparison / cycle / simulated
  total / limit / message`. If a spill or damaged cup interrupts a reading,
  record **invalid—retest needed** rather than copying another team. Resume
  only when an adult can repour; cycle and simulation work may continue dry.
- **Annotated exemplar, common error reading and reteach:** On the exemplar,
  underline each number together with `mm`, draw an arrow from ruler zero to
  cup bottom, box the two readings and comparison, and circle **SIMULATED** and
  the forecast limit. A value read from the ruler end/rim indicates a zero
  alignment error; `4 mL` indicates unit confusion; calling Tuesday's 4 mm a
  prediction indicates a data-source error. Reteach with an empty dry cup
  first: align zero, bring a paper “eye” to level, read a drawn line, and use
  the sentence `I read ___ mm; it tells ___; it does not tell ___.` Then offer
  one adult-poured retest.
- **Safety classification:** Severity is **moderate** because
  spilled water can cause a slip and a cracked tool can cut; likelihood is
  **low** with trays, uncut plastic and immediate drying. Adult ratio is
  **1:3 maximum**: one adult pours,
  empties and visually checks each team of up to three pupils; teams may
  rotate if that adult cannot supervise all pours safely. No glass, cutting,
  pins, latex, tasting or outdoor exposure. Stop for a damaged cup and block
  access to a spill until dry. Potable water and intact classroom plastic do
  not require an SDS; apply the school spill procedure and consult any
  manufacturer document for cleaning agents used outside this kit.
- **Print/accessibility check:** Use 14-point black-and-white text, a bold
  millimeter scale, high-contrast water lines, written `REAL/SIMULATED` labels
  and directional arrows with words. Provide a large or tactile ruler guide,
  seated eye-level viewing, partner stabilization without partner answering,
  verbal readings, a scribe and pre-drawn cycle arrows.
- **Technology artifact:** None applies; this lesson has no technology artifact.
