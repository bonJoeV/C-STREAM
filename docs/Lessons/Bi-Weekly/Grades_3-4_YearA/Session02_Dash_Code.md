## Before class

1. Check loan availability; do not assume CSCOE stock. Confirm tablet/model compatibility with [Wonder Workshop Blockly](https://www.makewonder.com/en/apps/blockly/). Exact installed block labels are **VERIFICATION REQUIRED**; test the sequence before pupils arrive.
2. Charge in advance, label two pairs, connect Blockly to Dash. Test **drive forward 20 cm; turn right 90 degrees; drive forward 20 cm**, at the app's low-speed setting, then stop. This is an L, not a square. No sensor blocks required.
3. Mark two **1 m square** floor areas away from doors/stairs, each with start/direction and a soft cardboard boundary. Do not make pupils walk through a maze.
4. Pair pupils, final trio if odd. Assign teams to seven one-minute testing rounds at most (two teams per round); all other work occurs at desks.
5. Board: `predicted path | actual endpoint | one correction`. Draw a 4 by 4 grid for paper work. No printer required.

- **Per student:** pencil and 1 evidence half-sheet.
- **Per team:** 2 paper sheets (grid/record), ruler, paper token, 6 command cards hand-drawn on the sheets.

## Vocabulary and background

**Algorithm:** ordered instructions. **Input:** instruction/start action. **Output:** movement or light/sound. **Debug:** compare prediction to actual and correct. **Relative turn:** right/left from the robot's current facing direction.

Dash does not read the floor tape in this route; tape marks our reference. Blockly programs are distinct from steering it by remote control. Two forward movements separated by one right turn form an L. A whole square needs four sides and turns.

**Misconception:** "It knows where we want it to go." It follows the code using its hardware.
**If asked:** "Why missed the target?" Answer: "Check the start/facing direction, units and turn. Change one setting, then run the same test."

## SAFETY

Low-speed motion only, no races or intentional collisions. Teacher starts/stops demonstration; only scheduled teams enter a testing area. Hands off moving robots; retrieve after stop. Dry floor, no stairs/doorways; sound low. Teacher handles batteries/chargers. Stop if an area is no longer clear.

## Numbered sequence: exactly 40 minutes

1. **0-4: Purpose.** Original prayer: "God, help us use tools responsibly and include each classmate." Ask: "Is steering by hand the same as running saved instructions?"
2. **4-9: Demonstration.** Identify robot, Blockly blocks, start and stop. Run the pretested L. Ask: "Which instruction changed its direction?"
3. **9-14: Paper rehearsal.** On a grid, one forward command means one square. Trace `forward; right turn; forward`; swap executor/reader. Draw expected endpoint. Explain that the grid is a model, not a physical centimeter measurement.
4. **14-27: Build/run slots.** Teams plan code on paper, then use a one-minute robot slot. First child enters forward; second adds right turn/forward; run and measure endpoint difference from the taped target. A trio's third child starts/stops and explains output. Teacher logs observed operations. Waiting teams find a deliberately wrong left-turn card in a paper route.
5. **27-32: One correction.** Teacher chooses a route that missed, checks start and facing, then adjusts one distance/turn. Predict and retest. "What changed? Which part did we keep the same?"
6. **32-35: Individual evidence.** Every child draws the L and identifies the turn, a debug change and its reason. Actual operators additionally identify start/stop and one measured result. Mark **paper only** when appropriate.
7. **35-40: Cleanup/exit.** Stop programs, teacher powers off/stores robots/tablets, pupils gather cards/tokens/rulers and restore lanes. Collect slips. Ask: "What does our paper test fail to test?"

## Low-tech substitute path / indoor alternative

Use the same time windows; replace steps 2/4/5 with teacher/partner token execution on grids. Give a wrong-turn route, identify the first mismatch, replace one instruction and retest. Record **computational-thinking sequence/debug evidence**. Do **not** claim CST-T1 Bluetooth/device operation, robotics or sensor mastery. All pathways are indoors; Minnesota winter has no effect.

## Success, troubleshooting, support and challenge

**Success:** child traces a sequence, identifies the wrong turn and explains a testable correction. Device success is separately observed editing/running/stopping, not merely watching a partner.
Score **1** guess; **2** trace with help; **3** independent trace/debug reason; **4** explains model-versus-device limitations using a measured result.

- **Pairing fails after 2 minutes:** announce paper path and notify regular teacher of missing operation evidence.
- **Wrong turn:** check relative left/right and robot starting direction; no floor-line sensing assumed.
- **Support / Grade 3:** three cards, oral prompts, turn one's own paper token, ruler assistance; no angle calculations required.
- **Challenge / Grade 4:** repeat test and compare whole-number endpoint errors, explaining why traction/units may matter.
- **25 pupils:** thirteen teams, two areas, seven one-minute rounds within the thirteen-minute window; no five-mission race.

## Teacher artifact pack (release checklist)

Assemble these items only after the teacher has run the stated Blockly
sequence on each paired Dash/tablet and confirmed the installed block labels.

- **Student material and no-print equivalent:** Give each pupil a 4-by-4 grid plus
  these exact entries: `Draw Dash's starting arrow`; `Number the commands:
  forward 20 cm / right 90 degrees / forward 20 cm`; `Mark P where you predict
  the endpoint`; `Mark A where Dash actually stopped`; `P-to-A error: ___ cm`;
  `The first mismatch was ___`; `I changed only ___ from ___ to ___`; `On the
  retest Dash ___`; `My evidence is ROBOT OPERATION / PAPER MODEL`; `I
  personally edited ___, ran ___, stopped ___`. Without printing, draw one
  large 4-by-4 grid and these ten numbered prompts; pupils reproduce the route
  with their paper token.
- **Evidence and answer guidance:** Every pupil can show the ordered
  L-route, a current-facing right turn, a predicted endpoint, and a defensible
  correction. Robot operators additionally need a teacher-observed edit,
  run/start or stop action, and whole-centimeter endpoint error. Accept a
  correction to starting direction, one forward distance, or the turn direction
  when it addresses the observed mismatch. Accept `paper model--robot result
  unavailable` on fallback. Do not convert watching, remote steering, or a
  partner's tablet turn into individual operation evidence.
- **Worked example:** Model north-facing start, 20 cm
  forward, right turn, then 20 cm east. Example record: `P at target; A 6 cm
  short on second leg; error 6 cm; changed second forward from 20 to 26 cm;
  kept start and turn; retest closer`. The finished example contains both
  P and A, a ruler measurement, one-variable correction, and retest result.
  On the paper pathway, `actual endpoint`, `error`, and device-operation boxes
  are `N/A--paper model`; the algorithm/debug fields are still required.
- **Board setup and visual example:** In each 1 m square, show start position,
  facing arrow, soft boundary, target, and two perpendicular 20 cm reference
  legs. Post `THE TAPE IS A REFERENCE--DASH DOES NOT SENSE IT`. The visual
  example on the board is one drawn L-route with the start arrow, P, and A
  marked. Use board
  columns `team | predicted | actual | error cm | first mismatch | one change |
  retest`, plus a separate roster with `edit / run / stop` observation marks.
- **Discussion with acceptable response:** `Why is this route an L rather than a square?`
  Two forward legs and one turn do not close four sides. `If the first leg is
  correct but Dash turns left, where is the first mismatch?` The turn command
  or starting orientation. `What can the grid not test?` Wheel traction,
  Bluetooth execution, physical distance, and real starting alignment.
  `Does Dash follow the floor tape?` No; it executes the program from its
  current facing. Address the misconception that intention, tape, or a hidden
  sensor guides it.
- **Kit contents, kit label and reset:** Each team receives two paper sheets, one metric
  ruler, one paper token, and six cards: two `forward`, one `right`, one `left`,
  one `start`, one `stop`. Class equipment is two labeled Dash robots, two
  matching Blockly tablets, two chargers, two soft boundary sets, timer, and
  4 m floor-safe tape. Kit label: `YA-S02 DASH A + TABLET A` and `YA-S02 DASH
  B + TABLET B`; paper bin kit label: `6 CARDS / 1 TOKEN / 1 RULER`. Cleanup and
  reset: the teacher stops
  programs, powers down, matches each robot/tablet/charger, records charge or
  pairing faults, and stores devices; pupils count cards, tokens, and rulers
  and restore clear lanes.
- **Unfinished work:** Check the last verified stage on the sheet:
  `paper trace / code edit / run / endpoint measure / correction / retest`.
  Record the last actual endpoint and whether a device turn was observed.
  Queue the pupil for the next supervised school slot; never require an app,
  account, or robot at home and never fill an absent robot result from another
  team.
- **Annotated exemplar, common error reading and reteach:** On the sample, underline the
  start arrow, box P/A/error, circle the first mismatch, and connect the single
  changed block to the retest. A correct drawing with `robot evidence` but no
  roster mark means operation is unverified. A mirrored L usually indicates
  relative left/right or facing confusion. A diagonal P-to-A measure instead
  of the requested endpoint difference needs ruler support. Reteach by
  rebuilding the
  three-command route with cards, rotating the token to its new facing, then
  narrating `expected ___; observed ___; first mismatch ___; changed ___`.
- **Safety classification:** Severity **moderate** because a moving robot can create
  a trip/collision event; likelihood **low** with bounded low-speed lanes.
  Adult ratio in the active zone is **1 adult:6 pupils maximum** (two teams of
  up to three); all others remain at desks, and the overall class remains at
  **1:25 maximum**. Teacher alone handles chargers and battery concerns.
  Stop for wet flooring, blocked boundaries, stairs/door access, collisions,
  or a child entering a moving lane. The used commercial product category is
  floor-safe tape. Enter the exact manufacturer/product in the [Safety Data
  Sheet Register](../../../Review/Safety_Data_Sheet_Register.csv), record
  whether an SDS applies and the school approval decision, and keep the tape on
  **HOLD** until that review is resolved. Follow manufacturer battery/device
  safety documentation and school procedures for a damaged or leaking battery.
- **Print/accessibility check:** Print the grid at least 5 inches square with
  thick black lines in black-and-white, 14-point labels, open arrowheads, and text labels in
  addition to symbols; do not use color alone as the P/A distinction. Read each
  command aloud and permit tactile cards, a large paper token, ruler assistance,
  pointing, dictation, a scribe, or seated navigator/recorder participation.
- **Device compliance:** The primary lesson requires the two preapproved,
  compatible Dash/Blockly pairs. Use the installed school app without pupil
  login, cloud storage, names, photos, audio, location, public sharing, or new
  downloads. If pairing or compatibility fails after the stated check, use
  the paper route and explicitly report `robot operation not observed`. Paper
  or unplugged work is not executed-device evidence; Dash operation remains
  deferred/not observed until an individual edit, run, and endpoint measurement
  are witnessed.
