# Session 15: Simple Machines

## Lesson at a glance

Grades 3-4; Bi-Weekly A; Machines; **one 40-minute meeting**.
**Objective:** label load/effort/fulcrum, compare two fulcrum positions and
explain a measured arm-length change with qualitative effort.
**Why:** tools trade force/distance/direction; they do not create energy.
Catholic/CST-C3 dignified careful work; CST-S2 fixed load/change; CST-S3 lever
system; CST-A1 labeled diagram; CST-M1 arm lengths.
Official alignment **VERIFICATION REQUIRED**. Technology **None**;
introductory. Prep 15; cleanup 5. Six machines introduced, not six rushed builds.

## Before class and exact supplies

Teams <=3 (4/5/7/9): rigid 30 cm ruler, broad low wooden fulcrum block, paper
cup, five identical plastic counters, **20 cm tape**. Per pupil two paper sheets/
pencil. Teacher board/timer/demo from kit. Tape cup near ruler's zero end;
counter load stays five. Pretest low lift at fulcrum **10 cm then 5 cm** from
load end; pupil presses opposite end gently until cup lifts <=1 cm.
Draw six reference examples: ramp, wedge, screw, lever, wheel/axle, pulley.

## Vocabulary/background/SAFETY

Fulcrum = pivot; load = lifted object; effort = applied force; arm = pivot-to-
load/effort distance. Moving pivot toward load lengthens effort arm, generally
reducing needed effort but changing travel. Felt effort is not calibrated force.
**Misconception:** machine makes free energy. **If asked "How much easier?"**
We can describe feeling, not claim a Newton measurement without a force tool.
Low desk only; no knives/axes, suspended loads, elastic launches, pinches,
heavy weights or swinging ruler. Stop/report broken ruler; indoor primary.

## Meeting 1: exactly 40 minutes

1. **0-4:** "How do good tools help workers?" Optional dignified-work prayer.
2. **4-10:** Show six examples, model lever/lift cap and arm reading.
3. **10-22:** Every pupil tests 10 cm fulcrum, records arms 10/20 cm and
   qualitative effort; rotate roles, same five-counter load.
4. **22-30:** Move fulcrum to 5 cm, repeat; arms 5/25 cm, observe load/effort
   movement. "What changed, and what did we hold constant?"
5. **30-35:** Each child draws labeled two-position diagram, measured arms/control
   and honest effort limit plus tool-care choice.
6. **35-40:** Remove counters/count, untape/store cups/rulers/blocks, file records.

## Success/access/troubleshooting

Meets: four arm lengths, correct labels/control and force-distance explanation
without invented force data. Grade 3/support: premarked positions/diagram/
dictation. Grade 4/challenge: compare arm differences/travel tradeoff, no
mechanical-advantage ratio required. Sliding cup: stop/reset, repeat invalid
trial. No block: teacher supplies stable broad low eraser under ruler, same
positions; if unsafe, diagram only and physical test not observed. Early finish:
identify one different machine's function from reference picture.

**Family:** We tested lever positions and tool tradeoffs. Ask, "Where was the
fulcrum relative to the load?" Optional: identify a safe tool by sight.
## Teacher artifact pack (release checklist)

Give each pupil a **Student material (two-position lever investigation sheet)**, or reproduce these
exact prompts on the board: `Draw the ruler, cup and block`; `Label LOAD,
FULCRUM and EFFORT`; `Control: ____ counters stayed the same`; table columns
`Fulcrum distance from load | Load arm | Effort arm | Effort felt light /
medium / heavy | What I noticed`. Pre-enter rows `10 cm` and `5 cm`, followed by
`Moving the fulcrum toward the load changed ____`; `A lever trades ____ for
____`; `Why I cannot report Newtons: ____`. Permit drawing, oral answers,
dictation or scribing, but every recorded physical trial must be attributable to
the pupil who gently operated it.

- **Evidence and answer guidance:** With the cup at the zero end and effort near
  30 cm, the 10 cm fulcrum position has a 10 cm load arm and approximately 20 cm
  effort arm; the 5 cm position has a 5 cm load arm and approximately 25 cm
  effort arm. The control is the same ruler, cup, five counters, effort end and
  lift cap. Accept `the longer effort arm generally felt easier` and `the effort
  end may travel farther while the load rises a small distance`. Because feeling
  is qualitative, accept light/medium/heavy or "less/more effort," but reject
  invented Newton values or a claim that the lever creates energy. Measurements
  within the ruler/tape placement tolerance are acceptable if the pupil reads and
  explains the actual marks.
- **Worked example:** In view of pupils, tape the cup near zero, add
  exactly five counters, place the broad block at 10 cm and trace both pivot-to-end
  distances with a finger before a gentle lift no higher than 1 cm. Move only the
  block to 5 cm and repeat. Complete a model table with `10 | 10 | 20 | heavier`
  and `5 | 5 | 25 | lighter`.
  The finished example adds two labeled side-view diagrams and the
  sentence, "Moving the fulcrum toward the load lengthened the effort arm, so it
  generally felt easier, but the effort end traveled farther." If no stable broad
  fulcrum is available, the worked diagram remains available but the physical
  test is **N/A - not observed**.
- **Board setup and visual example:** Display the six simple-machine sketches in one
  row, then enlarge only the lever. Mark the ruler's load end, 5 cm, 10 cm and
  effort end with removable labels; use arrows for the downward effort and
  upward load motion. Board headings are `POSITION | LOAD ARM | EFFORT ARM |
  SAME 5-COUNTER LOAD | FELT EFFORT | TRAVEL`, plus `Arm = distance from the
  fulcrum, not distance from zero`.
- **Discussion with acceptable response:** Ask, "What did we hold constant?" Accept the five counters,
  ruler, cup, effort end and low lift. Ask, "Why might the 5 cm position feel
  easier?" Accept that the effort arm is longer relative to the load arm. Ask,
  "Can we say exactly how many Newtons easier?" Accept no, because no calibrated
  force tool was used. Ask, "Did the machine make energy?" Accept no; it changed
  the force-distance relationship or direction. Ask, "Which distance is an arm?"
  Accept pivot-to-load or pivot-to-effort, not ruler-zero-to-end unless zero is
  the pivot. Target misconceptions that "closer fulcrum always means less work,"
  felt effort is precise force data and a lever creates free energy.
- **Kit contents, kit label and reset:** Pack one rigid 30 cm metric ruler, one inspected
  broad low wooden block, one paper cup, exactly five identical counters, one
  precut 20 cm tape strip and three pencils with six plain sheets. Kit label on the tray:
  `S15 LEVER TEST - FIVE COUNTERS - 1 CM MAX LIFT - NO LAUNCHING`. Cleanup and reset by
  counting all five counters into the tray, removing tape without tearing the
  ruler/cup, nesting the cup, checking ruler and block for damage, returning
  pencils and filing individual records. Mark and remove from service any cracked,
  splintered or unstable part.
- **Unfinished work:** Circle the last valid stage: `labels / 10 cm trial / 5 cm
  trial / two diagrams / tradeoff explanation`. Record why a trial stopped
  (`cup slid`, `block unstable`, `time`) and retain the sheet for a supervised
  school retest. Never fill the second row from another team's result or send the
  physical test home.
- **Annotated exemplar, common error reading and reteach:** On the finished model, color is optional:
  use `L` beside the five-counter cup, a triangle and `F` at the pivot, and `E`
  plus a downward arrow at the press point. Brackets from the fulcrum to each end
  carry the actual centimeter values; a box surrounds `five counters stayed
  constant`; a note beside felt effort says `observation, not Newton measurement`.
  Reversed arms are a common error indicating the pupil measured from ruler zero: reteach by having the pupil place
  one finger on the fulcrum and trace outward. A sliding cup or block makes that
  trial invalid: stop, retape/reset and repeat. Use `The fulcrum moved
  from __ to __; the effort arm changed from __ to __; I observed __`.
- **Safety classification:** Severity **moderate** (pinch, splinter or ruler
  launch) and likelihood **low** with the capped setup. Adult ratio is **1:3 maximum**:
  one adult supervises each
  team of no more than three during physical trials. Keep the apparatus on a low
  desk, fingers away from the pivot, use gentle downward pressure and stop at a
  1 cm load lift; prohibit swinging, elastic force, suspended/heavy loads and
  knife/axe examples. The used commercial product category is masking tape.
  Enter the exact manufacturer/product in the [Safety Data Sheet Register](../../../Review/Safety_Data_Sheet_Register.csv),
  record whether an SDS applies and the school approval decision, and keep the
  tape on **HOLD** until that review is resolved. Inspect wooden surfaces and
  follow school first-aid/reporting procedures for damage.
- **Print/accessibility check:** Use a large side-view lever diagram, 16-point
  labels, high black-and-white contrast, wide numeric boxes and patterns/letters rather than color
  alone. Read vocabulary aloud; provide premarked 5 cm and 10 cm positions,
  tactile raised labels, a pencil grip, ruler-reading help, oral response or a
  scribe. Motor assistance may steady the setup, but the pupil should direct or
  make the gentle press when safe.
- **Technology artifact:** None applies; this lesson has no technology artifact.
