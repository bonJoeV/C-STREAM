# Session 16: Water Wonder

| Big idea | Test predictions; an object's shape can change how it behaves in water. |

## Before class

1. Seat 5/7/10/12 teams for 10/15/20/25 children (pairs/final trio). Budget 5/8/10/13 kits, including one reserve for odd enrollment; totals include it. Put one shallow tray at each active team table; mark a 2 cm maximum water depth. Keep reserve water in the jug rather than filling an unused tray.
2. Adult adds 300 mL tap water to each approximately 20-by-15 cm tray, no deeper than 2 cm. Test the same large wooden block and all-metal tablespoon to confirm observable behavior; record actual results, not a guarantee.
3. Cut one 20-by-20 cm foil square per team and keep a second reserve. Teacher tests folding a shallow open boat with turned-up edges; a tightly pressed ball and boat may behave differently, but floating is never promised.
4. Fold each student's half-sheet into two rows with eye and up/down arrows: predicted / observed. Pupils point while adult reads. No worksheet reading or coins needed.

Do not require pennies, corks, caps, tiny rocks, family supplies or a large shared testing tub.

## Vocabulary, background and misconception

**Float:** remain supported at the surface. **Sink:** go below the surface toward the bottom. **Prediction:** what might happen. **Redesign:** change a design and test again. Use hand gestures and pictures.

Water pushes upward on objects. Floating depends on displaced water and the object's overall density/shape, not simply "light floats, heavy sinks." A foil boat can enclose air and displace water; filling with water or pressing flat can change the result. Keep the foil piece and water the same when comparing shapes; this is a limited comparison, not a universal rule.

**Misconception:** "All metal sinks." Metal ships float; shape and enclosed space matter.

**If asked, "Why did our boat sink?"** "Water may have entered, or its shape may not have pushed enough water aside. Let's check the edges and try one change."

## SAFETY

Adult fills/empties trays; never leave water unattended. No drinking, mouth contact, splashing, glass, electronics, sharp foil edges, coins or small test pieces. Teacher folds sharp foil edges inward. Stop testing when spills occur and dry immediately. Use fresh clean water and clean tools. Children handle dry parts with a spoon or adult help; follow school handwashing procedures after water play. Default low-contact pathway uses tools and adult-only wet handling; if a class requires full handwashing, reserve a supervised sink transition after the session rather than claim 25 children wash at one sink in four minutes.

## Numbered sequence: 25 minutes

1. **0-3 (3 min):** Brief stewardship prayer and safety demonstration. Ask "How can we test without wasting water?"
2. **3-6 (3 min):** Model predicting, gently placing one large object, observing and recording up/down. Never drop from a height.
3. **6-12 (6 min):** Each child predicts one block/spoon trial, observes partner/teacher placing it and marks or dictates actual result. Swap roles; the third child gets a full prediction turn.
4. **12-17 (5 min):** Team tests one foil piece as a ball, opens the same piece into a boat, tests again and changes one edge if needed. Reserve foil only for tears; if replaced, record that material also changed. Teacher begins individual prediction/result/change checks.
5. **17-21 (4 min):** Finish remaining checks during paired retelling: child reports one prediction versus actual result and identifies a foil change or suggests a retest. Teacher records evidence without a 25-child serial exit.
6. **21-25 (4 min):** Pupils return dry tools/records; adult collects water into pitcher, empties at sink, wipes trays/tables. Do not rush a heavy water-carrying trip; keep pupils seated while adult collects.

## Evidence and what success looks like

Record prediction/result, two-row representation, and identified redesign as independent / prompted / not yet. Success is an honest report and reasoned retest, not necessarily a floating boat. Child may point, dictate or arrange picture arrows instead of drawing/writing.

## Questions, support, challenge, troubleshooting, indoor fallback

- "Did your observation match your prediction? What changed in the foil? What stayed the same?"
- **Support:** choose one of two large objects; adult performs wet handling; use picture response and a pre-folded boat.
- **Challenge:** explain why comparing a torn new piece would not isolate shape.
- **No contrast in foil tests:** honestly report both floating/both sinking; adult demonstrates gently tipping water into an existing boat and names water entry as another changed condition.
- **No trays/water access:** adult demonstration for the whole class using one tray, with simultaneous individual picture predictions; no claims that each child tested directly.
- **Minnesota indoor fallback:** all work is indoors. No lake, snow or tap outside is needed.

## Optional family snippet

We tested predictions and changed a foil shape. Ask: "What did the water test show, and what did you change?" We learned that heavy/light alone does not explain floating. No bathtub task, homework or supplies required.
