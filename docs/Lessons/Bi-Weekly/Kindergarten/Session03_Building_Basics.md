## Vocabulary, background and SAFETY

Base = bottom supports; stable = unaided standing; retest = repeat the same
check; compare = identify a difference. Criterion is ten seconds, not tallest.
Misconception: a fall means the child failed. It is information about this model.
If asked about height, use the same marked ruler next to both attempts; no
untaught centimeter reading is required. Seated low structures; no throwing,
shaking, tall competition, splintered pieces or standing on furniture.

## Exact sequence: 25 minutes

1. **0-4:** Name careful sharing and the ten-second unaided criterion.
2. **4-8:** Count six blocks, demonstrate a low base and common measuring reference.
3. **8-17:** Partners test, change only base arrangement and retest with the same
   six blocks/time. Swap builder/tester turns; begin individual checks.
4. **17-21:** Child counts the selected blocks, points to a tested change,
   compares stability and above/below the same 10 cm reference. Save two results.
5. **21-25:** Return/count all 20 blocks, retain evidence, collect rulers and clear.

## Success, access, troubleshooting and family copy

Record count/common comparison and actual redesign result, not participation.
Ask "What changed? What stayed the same?"
Support: adult-started three-block structure and pointing; record reduced task.
Challenge: repeat to check consistency. Both fall: try flat pieces on a broader
base; preserve earlier results. Few kits: groups <=3 with full turns, same test.
No safe blocks: defer construction evidence rather than call a drawing a tested tower.
All work indoors. **Family:** We compared two fair tower tests.
Ask, "What did your base change do?" No homework or purchase.

## Teacher artifact block

**Student material (print/no-print):** No-print half-sheet, crayon, six large blocks, sorting tray, and marked metric ruler; adult may record a child’s spoken comparison.

**Evidence and answer guidance:** Record the selected block count, the base change, the ten-second result, and the comparison with the same 10 cm reference separately. A fall is information, not automatic failure.

**Worked example:** Count six blocks, build a broad base, time ten seconds, change only the base arrangement, and retest with the same six blocks.

**Finished example:** Two dated attempts show the same six-block tower, the changed base, and “stood” or “fell” with a child-pointed above/below 10 cm comparison.

**Visual example:** Draw a narrow-base and broad-base six-block tower beside a ten-second timer mark and the ruler’s 10 cm reference.

**Board setup:** Make four boxes: “same blocks,” “base changed,” “test result,” and “10 cm comparison.”

**Expected discussion responses:** “The base changed”; “The blocks stayed the same”; “It stood for ten seconds”; “The fall tells us about this model.”

**Kit contents and label:** Twenty large blocks, one sorting tray, and one ruler marked at 10 cm; label the kit “six-block fair tower test.”

**Cleanup/reset:** Count all 20 blocks, return rulers and trays, retain the two results, and leave structures low and tables clear.

**Unfinished work:** Keep the first result and dictate the changed base or outcome; do not replace a missing test with a claim that the tower was stable.

**Common misconceptions:** Tallest is not the criterion; a fall is not failure; centimeter reading beyond the marked reference is not required.

**Annotated exemplar:** “6 blocks” (count noted); “base wider” (change circled); “stood 10 s” (result); “below 10 cm” (comparison).

**Common error interpretation:** If several conditions changed, record the comparison as not yet fair and repeat with only the base changed.

**Reteaching option:** Start with three blocks, demonstrate a low broad base, and rehearse “same blocks, one base change, same timer.”

**Safety risk classification:** Severity: low; likelihood: unlikely. Seated construction only; no throwing, shaking, tall competition, splintered pieces, or standing on furniture.

**Adult supervision ratio:** One teacher supervises low seated structures and times tests; adult handles any unsafe or splintered piece.

**SDS/product gate:** SDS not applicable: no chemical product is used; use inspected classroom blocks, trays, rulers, paper, and shared crayons only.

**Print/accessibility check:** No printing is required; use large block models, the marked 10 cm reference, pointing, and scribing.
