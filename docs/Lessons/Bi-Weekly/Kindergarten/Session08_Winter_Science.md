## Before class

1. Freeze plain tap water in standard ice trays. No toys, salt, sugar, dyes or food experiment.
2. Make 5/7/10/12 teams for 10/15/20/25 children (pairs/final trio). Budget 5/8/10/13 kits, including one reserve for odd enrollment; totals include it. Put one transparent 240 mL cup on a tray per team. Place one cube in each active cup just before class; no handling by pupils.
3. Start one teacher demonstration cube 10-15 minutes early at room temperature so there is likely some visible meltwater. Label "started earlier." Do not claim it melted during student observation.
4. Fold individual paper into two boxes: draw an eye above BEFORE and AFTER, spoken aloud. Prepare teacher sketches of an ice cube and a puddle as a clearly labeled model if no freezer is available.

## Vocabulary and background

**Solid:** keeps its own shape, like the cube. **Liquid:** flows and takes the container's shape. **Melt:** change from solid to liquid. **Evidence:** what we can point to that supports our account.

Ice receives energy from warmer surroundings and melts. This lesson observes a change; it does not rank melting methods or prove a controlled-variable result. Water may collect from melting and a little from condensation outside the cup; point to liquid **inside** for the intended evidence.

**Misconception:** "Ice disappears." It becomes water; keep it in the cup to see where it went.

**If asked, "Do all winter animals sleep?"** "No. Some migrate, some remain active, and some reduce activity. Different animals have different strategies." Animal adaptation is optional discussion, not this meeting's assessed science target. The former rescue-dog saint story is removed rather than presented as verified history.

## SAFETY

No touching ice against skin, tasting, freezing small toys, hot water, salt experiments or icicle hunting. Adult uses tongs. Use trays on seated tables; stop and wipe spills immediately. No outdoor exposure at -10 F. Keep all cups below table-edge risk and out of mouths. Offer drawings when sensory needs make ice observation uncomfortable.

## Numbered steps: 25 minutes

1. **0-3 (3 min):** Brief prayer for wise care of resources; show the ice cup and ask what pupils notice.
2. **3-6 (3 min):** Model solid/liquid with fresh cube and labeled earlier cup. Explain no touching/tasting and how to point to evidence.
3. **6-12 (6 min):** Teams observe their cup, alternate describing and draw/dictate BEFORE details. Adult points out any water starting to form.
4. **12-17 (5 min):** Reobserve and make AFTER detail. Compare with BEFORE. If little changes, record "little/no visible change" and compare labeled earlier sample. Teacher starts individual solid/liquid/comparison checks.
5. **17-21 (4 min):** Finish remaining checks: each child points to solid and liquid or selects pictures, describes actual change/no change and names one safe action around ice. Teacher marks roster without a 25-child exit line.
6. **21-25 (4 min):** Adult collects cups/water into pitcher; pupils wipe dry trays and return crayons. Adult empties pitcher at sink and stores dry cups/trays.

## Evidence and what success looks like

Record solid/liquid distinction and one before/after comparison as independent / prompted / not yet. A child who truthfully sees no change in their own cup may use the labeled earlier sample to identify melted water. Never require all cubes to melt in 25 minutes or judge drawing beauty.

## Questions, support, challenge, troubleshooting and indoor option

- "Where did the ice go? Can you point to the liquid? Did every cup change equally?"
- **Support:** two large picture choices, oral response, adult sketch from dictated detail; no writing required.
- **Challenge:** predict whether ice kept in a freezer would look the same; identify that a prediction is not today's observation.
- **Cube too large/no visible melting:** use earlier labeled cup; do not increase heat or invent a successful result.
- **No freezer:** compare teacher BEFORE/AFTER sketches, explicitly a model; evidence demonstrates the concept, not a live investigation.
- **25 students:** twelve active trays and one reserve kit; no water station line or whole-class sharing round.
- **Minnesota indoor fallback:** indoor melting is primary. Use a window only if it adds safe evidence; no walk needed.

## Optional family snippet

We observed solid ice and liquid water and used before/after evidence. Ask: "What happened to the ice, and how did you know?" No home freezing activity, homework or donation.

## Teacher artifact block

**Student material (print/no-print):** No-print folded BEFORE/AFTER half-sheet, crayon, and team cup/tray with one adult-placed ice cube; children do not handle ice.

**Evidence and answer guidance:** Record solid/liquid distinction and before/after comparison as independent, prompted, or not yet. “Little/no visible change” is valid.

**Worked example:** Point to the cube as solid, reobserve the cup, point to liquid inside if present, and compare the two labeled boxes.

**Finished example:** Dated page shows a before detail, after detail, and an honest ice-to-liquid or little/no-change statement.

**Visual example:** Draw a cube under BEFORE and liquid inside the cup under AFTER; label an earlier demonstration “started earlier.”

**Board setup:** Use “solid,” “liquid,” and “what changed” columns with a no-touch/no-taste reminder.

**Expected discussion responses:** “The cube keeps its shape”; “Water flows”; “I see liquid inside”; “My cup changed little.”

**Kit contents and label:** Transparent cup, tray, 20 mL cube, two towels, and record sheet; label “adult ice observation.”

**Cleanup/reset:** Adult collects water, pupils wipe dry trays and return crayons, and adult empties the pitcher at the sink.

**Unfinished work:** Preserve before observation and mark after as not observed or little/no change; do not claim all cubes melted.

**Common misconceptions:** Ice becomes water rather than disappearing; an earlier cup is not the current cup’s elapsed-time result.

**Annotated exemplar:** “Cube has shape” and “liquid inside” or “little change” are dated and pointed to.

**Common error interpretation:** If the child points outside the cup, ask where the intended liquid is and record only visible evidence.

**Reteaching option:** Use fresh and earlier labeled cups, then sort solid/liquid picture choices without touching.

**Safety risk classification:** Severity: moderate; likelihood: unlikely after controls. Adult-managed ice/water handling only; no skin contact, tasting, hot water, salt, outdoor exposure, or mouth contact.

**Adult supervision ratio:** Adults transfer/empty cups; teams remain seated while the teacher circulates for checks.

**SDS/product gate:** SDS not applicable: only plain tap water and clean cups/trays are used under the school hygiene plan.

**Print/accessibility check:** No printing; use large eye symbols, oral/tactile picture choices, and scribing.
