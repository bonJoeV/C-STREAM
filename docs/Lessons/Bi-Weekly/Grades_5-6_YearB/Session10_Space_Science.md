## VOCABULARY / TEACHER BACKGROUND

**AU:** Earth-Sun average-distance unit. **Scale:** consistent relationship.
**Payload:** mission instrument/load. **Constraint:** limit.
**Common misconception:** planet marker size shares distance scale.
**If asked, "Is our probe feasible?"** It is a concept; propulsion, thermal
control, communications and review omitted.
Verified paraphrase [NASA Earth facts](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/facts/),
checked October 4, 2026: Earth average Sun distance about 150 million km,
about 1 AU; light travel about eight minutes. Scale 1AU=10cm -> 2AU=20cm;
one model cm =15 million km, marker sizes not scaled.
Fictional mission: observe a target at hypothetical 2AU, budget 6 credits.
Camera card 4 credits gives pictures; temperature sensor 3 gives temperature;
communications 2 required. Camera+comms=6 fits; both instruments+comms=9
doesn't. Criterion: answer selected question with matching instrument and
return information; no guaranteed discovery/current mission claim.

## EXACT LESSON SEQUENCE

1. **0-4 (4 min):** Ask "What would a probe need to answer your question?"
2. **4-11 (7 min):** Model NASA facts versus fictional payload allowances and scale.
3. **11-25 (14 min):** Each places Earth/2AU positions and drafts one mission question/criterion; teams select payload within 6 credits.
4. **25-35 (10 min):** Peer checks scale/sum and asks about missing system; revise diagram or instrument choice, retain before/after.
5. **35-41 (6 min):** Individual correct scale limit, mission criterion/constraint, checked cost and factual versus faith-reflection distinction.
6. **41-45 (4 min):** Save models/plans, count tools/cards, reuse paper and tidy.

## QUESTIONS TO ASK STUDENTS / WHAT SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE

"What is invented? What does camera measure? Can both fit the budget?"
Each has correct distance scale, model limit, matched mission criterion/
constraint and evidence/reflection distinction. 1 unsupported; 2 prompted;
3 independent; 4 system/tradeoff explanation; NE if missing.

## IF THINGS GO WRONG / SAFETY

Too many payloads -> drop one, not invent budget. No ruler -> verified marks.
No NASA internet -> supplied card, no fresh unverifiable discoveries.
No Sun/lens/telescope viewing, rockets/projectiles, night/outdoor homework,
private accounts or claims of real space hardware. Stop/report unsafe substitutions.

## SUPPORT / CHALLENGE / INDOOR FALLBACK

Grade 5/support: labeled positions/cards, sum scaffold, scribing.
Grade 6/challenge: justify communications/measurement tradeoff and unmodeled
mission risk. Indoor tabletop model independent of Minnesota weather.

**Learned:** consistent units and limits. **Catholic connection:** honest wonder.
**Ask:** "Which mission cost was invented?" No routine homework.

## Teacher artifact pack (release checklist)

- **Adult ratio:** 1 adult:25 pupils maximum for the seated scale-model class, with teams capped at three and no optical or launch activity.
- **Student material (print or no-print):** One sheet per pupil with a 0-30 cm strip, labels Sun/Earth/2 AU, `fact or fictional allowance`, mission question, instrument, communications, cost sum, criterion, constraint, and model limit. Board-copy positions/cards when printing fails.
- **Exact evidence/answers:** At `1 AU = 10 cm`, Earth is 10 cm and hypothetical 2 AU target 20 cm from Sun; 1 model cm represents about 15 million km. Camera 4 + communications 2 = 6 fits; camera 4 + temperature 3 + communications 2 = 9 does not. Marker sizes, feasibility, and discovery are not modeled.
- **Worked example / Finished example:** Work camera mission: question requires pictures, camera+communications costs 6/6, criterion is matching pictures returned. Finished non-build example has correct positions, matched instrument/question, cost, NASA fact versus fictional labels, and an unmodeled-system limit; real probe build is **N/A—concept plan only**.
- **Board setup:** Draw Sun 0/Earth 10/target 20 cm; post **VERIFIED FACT | SCALE CALCULATION | FICTIONAL QUESTION | PAYLOAD COST | CRITERION/CONSTRAINT | LIMIT**.
- **Discussion/misconceptions:** Ask “What is invented?” (target/payload credits); “What does camera measure?” (images); “Can both instruments fit?” (no, total 9); “Are marker sizes scaled?” (no). Correct real-mission feasibility and cosmic/theological proof claims.
- **Kit contents / Kit label:** Each pupil: two model/mission sheets and pencil; each team: 30 cm paper strip, payload sheet, ruler, marker, NASA fact sheet. Kit label: **G5-6 YB S10 SPACE MODEL—1 AU=10 cm—BUDGET 6**. Reset Sun/Earth/2 AU labels and three payload cards, return rulers, cap markers, and retain plans.
- **Unfinished work:** Mark last verified position or cost; store strip/cards with plan and resume at school. Never replace a missing calculation with a current-news claim.
- **Annotated exemplar / Common error interpretation / reteach:** Box `10 cm/20 cm` and `4+2=6`, underline question-instrument match, circle model limit. Earth at 20 cm indicates cumulative/scale confusion; 9 accepted as in budget indicates constraint error. Reteach with repeated 10 cm segments and physical cost counters.
- **Safety risk classification—severity/likelihood/ratio/SDS:** Severity **low** (paper/tool misuse), likelihood **unlikely** in seated work. One adult monitors class and checks each team of at most three; prohibit Sun/lens/telescope viewing, launches, projectiles, and night homework. **SDS/product gate: HOLD — school-approved marker.** The exact product and manufacturer are not specified, and no exact approved marker row exists; apply the [`CONTROL-ROW` in the Safety Data Sheet Register](../../../Review/Safety_Data_Sheet_Register.csv). Do not issue or substitute the marker until the school safety lead approves the exact product and current SDS.
- **Print/accessibility:** Print scales at 100%, 14-point high-contrast labels, patterned symbols, and wide arithmetic boxes; allow tactile labels, premarked strips, oral/scribed answers, and seated roles.
