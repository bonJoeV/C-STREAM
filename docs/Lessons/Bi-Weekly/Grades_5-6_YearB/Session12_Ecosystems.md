## BEFORE CLASS / MATERIALS

Review contact/accommodations; seat 4/5/7/9 teams. Copy complete generalized
prairie cards: grass uses sunlight; grasshopper eats grass; rabbit eats grass;
fox eats rabbit; bird eats grasshopper; fungi/bacteria break down dead organic
matter. Sunlight arrow separate label ENERGY; food arrows from food to consumer.
Draw ten equal habitat squares, not a real site map. No species populations
or specific field observations claimed.

## VOCABULARY / TEACHER BACKGROUND

**Producer:** makes food using energy. **Consumer:** obtains food from organisms.
**Decomposer:** breaks down dead organic matter. **Food web:** feeding
relationships; energy flows, matter cycles.
**Common misconception:** energy cycles back unchanged or arrows point to food.
**If asked, "Will fewer plants certainly kill all foxes?"** No; possible
effects depend on alternatives/time; model lacks populations/other foods.
Habitat exercise: 10 equal squares, 4 path/6 planting; widening path by
2 leaves 4 planting. Planting area 6/10 versus 4/10; access benefits may
trade off with habitat area, no real restoration/planting authorization.

## EXACT LESSON SEQUENCE

1. **0-4 (4 min):** Ask "Where does energy enter?" Introduce careful creation care.
2. **4-11 (7 min):** Model food-to-consumer arrow and decomposer/matter distinction.
3. **11-25 (14 min):** Each draws six organism nodes with appropriate form/labels, at least four food arrows; team checks reference.
4. **25-35 (10 min):** Reduce drawn grass area, predict one possible feeding effect without certainty. Compare ten-square path plans, revise model if arrows backwards.
5. **35-41 (6 min):** Individual four correct arrows, possible perturbation/limit, habitat arithmetic and stewardship tradeoff.
6. **41-45 (4 min):** Save models, cap/count tools, reuse paper and tidy.

## QUESTIONS TO ASK STUDENTS / WHAT SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE

"What arrow is energy versus matter? Which alternative foods were omitted?"
Each has correct web/annotated form, bounded prediction and checked 6/10 to
4/10 tradeoff. 1 unsupported; 2 prompted; 3 independent; 4 another model limit;
NE if missing. Not real ecosystem monitoring/restoration.

## IF THINGS GO WRONG / SAFETY

Complex web -> four feeding arrows first. No cards -> board copies.
No grid -> ten equal drawn boxes. No wild specimens, water/soil, cultures,
terrarium, habitat interventions, outdoor exposure or potability claims.
Stop/report unsafe substitutions.

## SUPPORT / CHALLENGE / INDOOR FALLBACK

Grade 5/support: organism outlines, arrows/word bank, scribed explanation.
Grade 6/challenge: distinguish energy loss to thermal pathways from nutrient
cycling and explain uncertain indirect effects. Indoor models only.

**Learned:** arrows and limits. **Catholic connection:** stewardship with access.

## Teacher artifact pack (release checklist)

- **Adult ratio:** 1 adult:25 pupils maximum for the dry paper-model class, with teams capped at three and no live-material collection.
- **Student material (print or no-print):** Give each pupil six labeled organism nodes, arrow key `food -> consumer`, separate sunlight ENERGY arrow, perturbation/limit prompts, and ten habitat squares to compare 6/10 versus 4/10 planting. Board-copy all nodes/grid if no printer.
- **Exact evidence/answers:** Valid arrows include grass -> grasshopper, grass -> rabbit, rabbit -> fox, grasshopper -> bird; sunlight -> grass is energy input; fungi/bacteria decompose dead matter. Widening path by two squares changes planting 6/10 to 4/10. Fewer plants may affect consumers, but extinction/certainty is unsupported.
- **Worked example / Finished example / Visual example:** Work grass -> rabbit by reading “rabbit eats grass”; reverse it, explain it falsely says grass consumes rabbit, and correct. Finished non-build model has six nodes, at least four correct feeding arrows, decomposer/matter distinction, bounded grass-reduction prediction, area arithmetic, access/habitat tradeoff, and model limit. The six-node web with thick directional arrows is the visual example.
- **Board setup:** Post **FOOD -> CONSUMER | SUNLIGHT ENERGY | MATTER/DECOMPOSERS | POSSIBLE CHANGE | MISSING INFORMATION | 6/10 -> 4/10 TRADEOFF**.
- **Discussion/misconceptions:** Ask “Where does energy enter?” (sunlight); “Do arrows point to food?” (no); “Does energy cycle unchanged?” (no, energy flows while matter cycles); “Will fewer plants kill all foxes?” (not certain; alternatives/time/populations omitted).
- **Kit contents / Kit label:** Each pupil: two web/evidence sheets and pencil; each team: complete prairie organism sheet, ten-square grid/reference, ruler, marker. Kit label: **G5-6 YB S12 HABITAT WEB—6 NODES/10 SQUARES—DRY MODEL**. Reset six organism cards/grid, return rulers, cap markers, and retain individual webs.
- **Unfinished work:** Mark last verified arrow and habitat calculation; store at school and resume without collecting organisms or claiming field data.
- **Annotated exemplar / Common error interpretation / reteach:** Box four food arrows, underline possible-language, circle `6/10 -> 4/10` and tradeoff. Reversed arrows indicate relationship-direction confusion; “all foxes die” indicates certainty beyond model. Reteach using “___ is food for ___” sentence cards, then rebuild four arrows.
- **Safety risk classification—severity/likelihood/ratio/SDS:** Severity **low**, likelihood **unlikely** because only dry paper models are used. One adult monitors class and checks every team of at most three; prohibit wild specimens, soil, water, cultures, terraria, outdoor exposure, or intervention. **SDS/product gate: HOLD — school-approved marker.** The exact product and manufacturer are not specified, and no exact approved marker row exists; apply the [`CONTROL-ROW` in the Safety Data Sheet Register](../../../Review/Safety_Data_Sheet_Register.csv). Do not issue or substitute the marker until the school safety lead approves the exact product and current SDS.
- **Print/accessibility check:** Use 14-point high-contrast organism names plus shapes/patterns, thick directional arrows, enlarged ten-square grid, read-aloud, tactile cards, scribing, and seated roles.
