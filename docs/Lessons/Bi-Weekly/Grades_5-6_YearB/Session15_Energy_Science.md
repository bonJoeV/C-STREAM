## BEFORE CLASS / MATERIALS

Check contact/accommodations. Seat 4/5/7/9 teams. Use 30 cm wide cardboard
lever on a low folded-cardboard support in tray, support initial 15 cm from
load end then 10 cm; taped shallow paper pocket holds one 10 g sealed packet.
Adult checks ends/travel stops so load rises at most 3 cm without slipping/
launching and fingers cannot enter pivot. No ball hitting lever, catapult,
chain reaction, bare batteries or heavy mass. Pilot slow hand-down action.

Paper/cardboard/tape consumable/reusable; packets/tools reusable.

## VOCABULARY / TEACHER BACKGROUND

**Lever:** bar pivoting on support. **Fulcrum:** pivot.
**Potential energy:** stored due to configuration/height. **Thermal:** energy
associated with particle motion. **Common misconception:** machine creates energy.
**If asked, "Did we measure efficiency?"** No; height/trial success only, not
input/output energy or force. Hand/body chemical-energy processes supply motion;
lever transfers motion to raising load, plus thermal/sound pathways.
Fictional example heights 1/2/1 cm then 2/2/3 cm, all under cap; compare ranges,
not claimed expected result. 15 versus 10 cm support distances differ 5 cm.

## EXACT LESSON SEQUENCE

1. **0-4 (4 min):** Ask "Where does lifting energy come from?" Introduce honest tool use.
2. **4-11 (7 min):** Model slow press/travel stops, finger boundary and energy arrows.
3. **11-25 (14 min):** Rotate operator/measurer/recorder, adult approves each kit; run three slow lifts at initial fulcrum, record height/cm.
4. **25-35 (10 min):** Adult approves moved support, repeat three lifts with same packet/press limit; compare actual outcomes and draw thermal/sound branches.
5. **35-41 (6 min):** Individual baseline/retest heights, justified support change, energy chain/limit and science-versus-faith reflection.
6. **41-45 (4 min):** Adult retrieves packets, stop/disassemble lever, return tools and save records.

## QUESTIONS TO ASK STUDENTS / WHAT SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE

"What input supplied energy? What stayed the same? Did we measure force?"
Each has six heights/units, supported revision, energy chain including non-useful
pathways and limit. 1 unsupported; 2 prompted; 3 independent;
4 motion/force tradeoff reasoning; NE for untested physical operation.

## IF THINGS GO WRONG / SAFETY

Slipping/fast motion -> stop; adult resets stops/pocket, never flick.
No safe lever -> draw chain and analyze explicitly fictional example, physical
lift deferred. No launches, pinch traps, body weight, sharp sticks, loads above
10 g per kit, high travel, electrical substitutions or theology-as-physics.
Isolate/report unsafe condition.

## SUPPORT / CHALLENGE / INDOOR FALLBACK

Grade 5/support: preassembled kit, arrow cards, height ruler and scribing.
Grade 6/challenge: explain why qualitative force/motion tradeoff is not a
quantitative efficiency finding. Indoor tray task only.

## CLEANUP / FAMILY NEWSLETTER

Count packets/tools, save actual/fictional labels, report damaged supports.
**Explored:** energy/simple machines. **Did:** measured safe lever lifts.
**Learned:** transfer/tradeoffs/limits. **Catholic connection:** truthful
resource care. **Ask:** "Where did energy go besides the raised load?"
No routine homework or home machine/launch.

## Teacher artifact pack (release checklist)

- **Adult ratio:** 1 adult:3 pupils at each active lever tray for approval, observation, and packet handling; without that ratio, use only the fictional paper data.
- **Student material (print or no-print):** Each pupil gets six height rows (three at support 15 cm, three at 10 cm), units, same packet/press checks, range/comparison, energy-chain arrows, thermal/sound branches, and limit. Board-copy the table and fictional `1,2,1 / 2,2,3 cm` only when safe physical kit is unavailable, labeling it fictional.
- **Exact evidence/answers:** Record actual heights; 15 to 10 cm support move is 5 cm. Energy chain: body chemical processes -> hand motion -> lever -> raised 10 g packet, with sound/thermal pathways. Heights alone do not measure force, work, efficiency, or conservation quantitatively.
- **Worked example / Finished example / Visual example:** Using fictional fallback, first range is `2-1=1 cm`, second `3-2=1 cm`; higher values do not prove efficiency. Finished physical example includes six actual cm readings, controlled constants, support revision, comparison, energy chain, and limit; if no safe lever, physical result is **N/A/deferred**. The labeled energy-chain arrows and two three-trial columns are the visual example.
- **Board setup:** Post **FULCRUM 15 cm: trials 1-3 | FULCRUM 10 cm: trials 1-3 | SAME | CHANGED | ENERGY IN/TRANSFER/THERMAL+SOUND | NOT MEASURED**.
- **Discussion/misconceptions:** Ask “Where did lifting energy come from?” (body/hand input); “What stayed same?” (packet, travel/press limit); “Did we measure force/efficiency?” (no); “Does lever create energy?” (no). Correct theology-as-physics or result guarantees.
- **Kit contents / Kit label:** Per team: 30 cm cardboard lever, low support, tray, ruler, taped paper pocket, one adult-placed sealed 10 g packet, and 0.5 m tape; each pupil has evidence/pencil. Kit label: **G5-6 YB S15 SAFE LEVER—10 g—3 cm MAX—NO LAUNCH**. Adult retrieves packet, disassembles/inspects stops/pocket, counts tools, and stores actual/fictional records separately.
- **Unfinished work:** Stop and label last safe trial/support position; preserve data at school. Never complete missing values from the fictional example or send a machine task home.
- **Annotated exemplar / Common error interpretation / reteach:** Box six values/units, underline 5 cm support change, circle energy-loss branches and limit. Missing units is recording error; slipping/rapid motion invalidates that trial and signals kit reset, not pupil failure. Reteach energy arrows and one adult-approved slow lift before any retry.
- **Safety risk classification—severity/likelihood/ratio/SDS:** Severity **moderate** (pinch or launched load), likelihood **unlikely** with shallow pocket, tray/stops, <=3 cm travel, 10 g packet, slow hand action, and finger boundary. One adult directly approves and watches each active team of at most three; adult places/retrieves packets and stops unsafe kits. **SDS/product gate: HOLD — masking tape.** The exact product and manufacturer are not specified. See the [`TAPE` row in the Safety Data Sheet Register](../../../Review/Safety_Data_Sheet_Register.csv). Do not issue or substitute masking tape until the school safety lead approves the exact product and current SDS.
- **Print/accessibility check:** Use 14-point high-contrast tables, cm units in every row, large energy arrows, read-aloud, adapted operator/measurer/recorder roles, scribing, and the fictional-analysis fallback.
