# Session 10: Geology Rocks

## Lesson at a glance

Grades 3-4; Bi-Weekly B; Earth systems; **one 40-minute meeting**.
**Objective:** record two properties/length of a sample, compare formation
references and explain a tentative identification/model limit.
**Why:** geology distinguishes evidence from confident-looking guesses.
Catholic/CST-C1 honest uncertainty/wonder; CST-S1 properties; CST-S3 formation/
multiple paths; CST-A1 labeled sketch; CST-M1 cm lengths.
Official alignment **VERIFICATION REQUIRED**. Technology **None**;
introductory. Prep 15; cleanup 5.

## Before class and exact supplies

Teams <=3 (4/5/7/9): three teacher-inspected **blunt** samples, lens/ruler, three
formation cards. Per pupil two sheets/pencil; the teacher demo uses a separate
inspected sample, lens, metric ruler and the same three formation cards.
Use school source-labeled sample identities only if provenance known; unknowns
stay tentative. Ready formation cards:
igneous from cooling molten rock; sedimentary from deposited material becoming
rock (not all merely layered); metamorphic from existing rock changed by
heat/pressure without melting. Draw cooling/erosion/deposition/change/melting
arrows with alternate paths, not a compulsory cycle order.

## Vocabulary/background/SAFETY

Grain = visible particle; band = stripe; formation = process; tentative =
possible, not certain. Not all metamorphic rocks have bands; appearance alone
doesn't prove origin. **Misconception:** every rock follows identical sequence.
**If asked "Is God literally rock?"** Religious foundation imagery is metaphor,
not mineral classification; no disputed Scripture quotation needed.
No obsidian/sharp fragments, breaking, acids, tasting or unknown outdoor
collecting. Lenses not aimed at Sun. Stop/report rough samples, wash hands.
Indoor primary.

## Meeting 1: exactly 40 minutes

1. **0-4:** "What does a rock actually let us observe?" Optional wonder prayer.
2. **4-10:** Model two visible properties/length, then compare formation cards.
3. **10-22:** Each handles one inspected sample, draws/labels two properties,
   measures length; rotate through other samples.
4. **22-30:** Match possible type from references/provenance, label uncertainty;
   draw two different formation paths and revise from peer check.
5. **30-35:** Each child gives actual properties/cm, supported tentative label and
   formation/model limit. "What further evidence would establish origin?"
6. **35-40:** Count/store samples/lenses/rulers, save dated records, wash/clear.

## Success/access/troubleshooting

Meets: observed detail/measurement, intelligible diagram and justified uncertain
classification. Grade 3/support: pictured property choices/read-aloud/scribing.
Grade 4/challenge: compare lengths and explain why appearance is insufficient.
Unknown identity: record unknown, not force label. No safe samples: teacher
draws three **model** textures with stated hypothetical formation cards; diagram
reasoning only, specimen observation/measurement not observed.
Early finish: additional possible path, no crushing tests.

**Family:** We observed rocks and reported uncertainty. Ask, "Which property
did you see?" Optional: notice a rock picture, no collecting homework.

## Teacher artifact pack (lesson-specific release checklist)

- **Student material (print and no-print):** Prepare one two-sided **Geology Rocks evidence record** per pupil. Side A repeats three rows with `Sample ID ____`, `Property 1 I can see ____`, `Property 2 I can see ____`, `Longest length ____ cm`, and a 7 cm x 7 cm box labeled `Sketch; add arrows to two observed details`. Side B asks `Possible type: igneous / sedimentary / metamorphic / unknown`, `Reference-card evidence ____`, `Why my identification is tentative ____`, `Formation path 1 ____ → ____`, `Formation path 2 ____ → ____`, `One model limit ____`, and `Further evidence that would help ____`. The no-print board version is those exact headings in two columns; pupils fold two plain sheets, copy the headings, and may point/dictate while an adult scribes. Do not add a scratch, acid, breakage, or hardness test.
- **Evidence and answer guidance:** A complete release sample includes three attributable sample IDs, at least two directly visible properties and a centimeter length for the pupil's handled sample, labeled sketches, two non-identical formation paths, and one cautious classification tied to a provided formation card or known school provenance. Accept precise observations such as `black with white speckles`, `rounded grains`, `thin tan bands`, `shiny flat faces`, or `4.8 cm`; accept `unknown` or `possibly sedimentary because deposited material can form visible grains`. Do not accept `old`, `strong`, `came from a volcano`, or `metamorphic because it has stripes` as observations. An origin label is not required when appearance/provenance cannot support it.
- **Worked example and finished example:** Show a completed fictional record for **Sample T (teacher model)**: `gray; white speckles; 5.6 cm`; sketch arrows point to speckles and a coarse-looking grain boundary. Write `possibly igneous--the cooling card could explain interlocking-looking grains; appearance alone cannot establish origin`. Draw `molten material → cools → igneous rock` and `existing rock → heat/pressure without melting → metamorphic rock`; under limit write `the arrows omit time, pressure, and many alternate changes`, and under further evidence write `verified source/provenance or mineral tests performed by a qualified lab`. Label the model **finished example, not the identity key for classroom samples**.
- **Visual example and board setup:** Before pupils enter, place each team's three blunt samples in an ID outline on its tray, with one lens, one metric ruler, and the three formation cards. On the board draw (1) `OBSERVE / INFER / LIMIT`, (2) an enlarged ruler with the sample edge at zero, and (3) a branching formation map using cooling, erosion/deposition, heat/pressure, and melting arrows; deliberately leave it without a single start or compulsory clockwise order. Post `unknown is an honest result` beside the map.
- **Discussion with acceptable response, and misconception:** Ask `Which words describe only what your senses show?` (color, visible grain/band, shape, measured length); `Can bands prove metamorphic origin?` (no--some metamorphic rocks are not banded and other rocks may appear layered/banded); `How are development paths different from a required cycle?` (a rock may take multiple routes and need not pass through every type in one order); `What would make a name less tentative?` (known provenance and additional appropriate evidence); and `Why is "unknown" scientific?` (it reports the evidence limit). Correct the ideas that every rock follows one identical loop, every layered-looking rock is sedimentary, or confidence substitutes for evidence.
- **Kit contents, kit label, cleanup and reset:** Kit label: **S10 GEOLOGY--TEAM __--3 BLUNT SAMPLES / 1 LENS / 1 RULER / 3 FORMATION CARDS**, applied to each team tray. The class kit therefore follows the allocation table: 4/5/7/9 trays for 10/15/20/25 pupils, with 12/15/21/27 inspected samples and cards, 4/5/7/9 lenses and rulers, two plain sheets and one pencil per pupil. At reset, pupils match all three sample IDs to the tray outlines and stack the cards in `igneous/sedimentary/metamorphic` order; the adult checks every sample again for a newly sharp edge, counts lenses/rulers, quarantines damage, and returns records separately. Nothing is brushed, chipped, washed, traded between trays, or taken home.
- **Unfinished work:** Circle the exact stopping code on the record: `O` observation missing, `M` measurement missing, `R` reference comparison missing, or `P` second path missing. Return every sample before storing the dated record in an envelope with its team/sample ID. At the next opportunity, reunite the pupil with the same ID sample; partner observations may be compared but never copied as if personally observed. If hardware is unavailable, attach the teacher's model-texture fallback and mark specimen observation/measurement **not observed**.
- **Annotated exemplar, common error and reteach:** On the release exemplar, annotate brackets for `two visible properties`, `unit and zero alignment`, `evidence-linked tentative language`, `two paths`, and `model limit`. A length without `cm` means unit reporting needs a fresh demonstration; a plausible number measured from the ruler's 1 cm mark requires a zero-alignment demonstration and remeasurement; `this is granite` without provenance means certainty exceeded evidence; a single circular arrow means alternate paths were missed. Use corrective practice with one teacher sample: pupils first say only observations, then place an `I infer` card before any proposed origin, and finally add `because` plus a source-card phrase.
- **Safety classification:** **Severity: low; likelihood: unlikely** after controls. Adult ratio: **1 adult to no more than 12 pupils actively handling samples**; teams remain at assigned trays, only teacher-inspected blunt samples are released, lenses never face the Sun, and pupils stop/report roughness, keep samples away from mouths, and wash hands after reset. No SDS is applicable because this activity uses intact rock articles, plastic lenses/rulers, paper, and pencils--not powders, acids, adhesives, or chemical test products. Any substituted mineral-testing product requires its school approval and SDS review and is outside this lesson.
- **Print/accessibility check:** Use at least 16-point sans-serif prompts, 1.5-line spacing, high-contrast sample IDs repeated in text and simple shapes, non-glossy paper, wide response lines, and formation arrows distinguishable without color. Provide pictured property choices (`speckled`, `grains`, `bands`, `smooth-looking`), a tactile-safe teacher-described option rather than compulsory touch, ruler alignment assistance, oral response/scribing, and enlarged reference cards; never use color alone as the evidence category.
- **Technology disposition:** No technology artifact applies; evidence is the physical samples and paper records.
