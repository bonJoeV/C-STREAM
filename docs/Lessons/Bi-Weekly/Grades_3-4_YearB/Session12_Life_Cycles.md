# Session 12: Life Cycles

## Lesson at a glance

Grades 3-4; Bi-Weekly B; Life systems; **one 40-minute meeting**.
**Objective:** order/compare butterfly and frog stages, identify living pupa
and show a new-generation link with a comparison table.
**Why:** models should distinguish developmental patterns from Easter metaphor.
CST-C1 faith/evidence distinction; CST-S3 living systems; CST-A1 diagrams;
CST-M2 stage/feature comparison. Official alignment **VERIFICATION REQUIRED**.
Technology **None**; introductory. Prep 10; cleanup 5.

## Before class and exact supplies

Teams <=3 (4/5/7/9): eight large drawn cards, two markers.
Four butterfly: egg/larva/pupa/adult, from retrieved
[Monarch Joint Venture](https://www.monarchjointventure.org/monarch-biology/life-cycle).
Four frog teaching stages: egg/tadpole/froglet/adult; **no pupa**, stages not
species timing guarantees. Each pupil two sheets/pencil; teacher board/timer.
Draw adult-to-new-eggs generation link separately; no live animals/care plan.

## Vocabulary/background/SAFETY

Metamorphosis = developmental change; pupa = living insect stage;
generation = offspring group; comparison = similarities/differences.
Butterfly has complete insect metamorphosis, frog not insect egg/larva/pupa/
adult. **Misconception:** pupa is dead. **If asked "Is it Resurrection?"**
No; same organism develops while alive, hope is a religious metaphor.
Paper indoors, no specimens/releases, seeds for tasting, clay allergy or
required observation at home. Stop/report damaged materials.

## Meeting 1: exactly 40 minutes

1. **0-4:** "Can still-looking stages be alive?" Optional original care prayer.
2. **4-10:** Read cards/model living stages and reproduction link.
3. **10-22:** Each orders cards/draws two labeled cycles, rotates arranger/
   explainer/checker; mark pupa only in butterfly.
4. **22-30:** Make table `animal | four teaching stages | pupa yes/no |
   new-generation link`; peer checks/revise mistaken arrow.
5. **30-35:** Each explains living pupa, frog contrast and metaphor boundary;
   "Which arrow represents a new individual rather than growth?"
6. **35-40:** Count eight cards, cap markers, save dated diagrams/table.

## Success/access/troubleshooting

Meets: two ordered diagrams, accurate pupa/no-pupa comparison/generation link
and faith distinction. Grade 3/support: picture sequence/read-aloud/scribing.
Grade 4/challenge: explain stage grouping/time limits, no invented dates.
Frog labeled pupa: compare key/revise, not gloss over. No cards: draw supplied
stages on board, same ordering check. Early finish: compare development versus
reproduction arrows. Indoor primary works in winter.

**Family:** We compared living life cycles. Ask, "Which animal has a pupa?"
Optional: notice a life-cycle picture, no animal care homework.

## Teacher artifact pack (lesson-specific release checklist)

- **Student material (print/no-print):** Give each pupil two plain sheets or print a two-page packet. Page 1 has two four-position diagrams and the exact prompts `Butterfly: ____ → ____ → ____ → ____`, `Frog teaching stages: ____ → ____ → ____ → ____`, `Put a star beside the living pupa`, `Draw a growth/development arrow between stages`, and `Draw the adult → new eggs arrow in a different style; label NEW GENERATION`. Page 2 is a table with columns `animal | four teaching stages | pupa yes/no | new-generation link`, followed by `Both animals ____`, `Only the butterfly ____`, `One way the model simplifies real life ____`, and `The Easter comparison is a metaphor, not biology, because ____`. The no-print version copies this layout from the board; pupils may arrange/point to cards and dictate the table.
- **Evidence and answer guidance:** Butterfly order is `egg → larva/caterpillar → pupa/chrysalis → adult`; frog teaching order is `egg → tadpole → froglet → adult`. `Pupa: butterfly yes; frog no` is required. Accept similarities such as both are living animals, begin a new generation as eggs, develop through ordered stages, and have adults that reproduce. Accept differences such as complete insect metamorphosis includes a pupa, while the simplified frog model includes tadpole/froglet and no pupa. The adult-to-eggs arrow represents reproduction/a new individual or generation, not the adult shrinking or turning back into its own egg. Do not require species-specific timing, claim all frogs have identical visible stages, or accept `the pupa is dead`.
- **Worked example and finished example:** Complete both diagrams with all eight stage names. Star `pupa/chrysalis`, write `The pupa is alive while the same butterfly organism develops`, and make the adult-to-new-eggs arrow dashed and labeled `adult reproduction begins a new generation`. In the table write `butterfly | egg, larva, pupa, adult | yes | adult lays eggs` and `frog | egg, tadpole, froglet, adult | no | adult reproduction leads to eggs`. Finish with `Both change as they develop; only the butterfly has a pupa` and `This four-stage model leaves out timing and many smaller changes`. For the faith boundary write `Resurrection is Christian belief; metamorphosis is development of a living organism, so one may be used as a hope symbol but they are not the same event`.
- **Visual example and board setup:** Make two large lanes headed with a butterfly icon/word and frog icon/word; place four blank card spaces in each lane and a separate `ADULT → NEW EGGS = NEW GENERATION` box below. Beside them post `GROWTH OF SAME ORGANISM` versus `REPRODUCTION/NEW INDIVIDUAL`. Keep the eight large drawn cards shuffled at each team table, two markers capped beside them, and the retrieved Monarch Joint Venture source title visible for the butterfly reference. Use patterns/icons as well as any color coding.
- **Discussion with acceptable response, and misconception:** Ask `How do we know the pupa is living?` (it is a living developmental stage of the same insect even if movement is not obvious); `Is a tadpole a frog pupa?` (no--frog stages are not insect egg/larva/pupa/adult stages); `Which arrow makes a new individual?` (adult to new eggs); `Does an adult turn back into its own egg?` (no); `Why are four cards a model rather than the whole story?` (they group many changes and do not promise exact timing/species detail); and `Is metamorphosis scientific proof of Resurrection?` (no--biology describes living development; an Easter use is metaphor). Explicitly correct the assumptions that still means dead, every animal has a pupa, and every life cycle is the same four-stage loop.
- **Kit contents, kit label, cleanup and reset:** Kit label: **S12 LIFE CYCLES--TEAM __--8 STAGE CARDS / 2 MARKERS**, marked on every envelope. It contains exactly four butterfly cards (`egg`, `larva/caterpillar`, `pupa`, `adult`) and four frog cards (`egg`, `tadpole`, `froglet`, `adult`) plus two markers; issue two paper sheets and one pencil per pupil outside the envelope. Required class quantities remain 32/40/56/72 cards and 8/10/14/18 markers for 10/15/20/25 pupils. Reset by reading each animal's four cards aloud in order, placing butterfly and frog sets in separate labeled pockets, confirming all eight, capping both markers, and storing pupil diagrams separately.
- **Unfinished work:** Leave correctly ordered cards in place long enough to copy their current state, then mark the pupil packet `B?` or `F?` at the first uncertain transition and note `table`, `generation arrow`, or `faith/science statement` if unfinished. Photographing the layout is not needed; the teacher may clip a small stage-order note to the packet. At resumption, provide one animal's four cards only and ask the pupil to justify each adjacent arrow rather than supplying the whole answer. Missing cards trigger the board-drawn fallback, not invented stages.
- **Annotated exemplar, common error and reteach:** Label the exemplar callouts `ordered stages`, `living pupa`, `no frog pupa`, `growth arrow`, `new-generation arrow`, `comparison`, and `model/metaphor limit`. Reversed `adult → pupa` suggests sequence confusion; use corrective practice by narrating which same organism is earlier/later. An arrow `adult → its egg` without reproduction language indicates identity confusion; split same-organism and new-generation arrows. `Frog pupa` indicates overgeneralization from the butterfly pattern; sort by animal before comparing. `Pupa = dead` calls for explicit living-stage language, not an analogy alone.
- **Safety classification:** **Severity: low; likelihood: unlikely** for this seated paper-card activity. Adult ratio: **1 adult to 25 pupils**, provided all remain at indoor desks and damaged cards or leaking markers are removed. There are no live animals, eggs, preserved specimens, releases, tasting seeds, clay, or required home care. **Commercial-product hold:** Used markers. Enter the exact manufacturer/product in [Safety Data Sheet Register](../../../Review/Safety_Data_Sheet_Register.csv). Keep each listed product on **HOLD** until school approval and SDS applicability are resolved; do not infer a brand or claim SDS N/A.
- **Print/accessibility check:** Print stage words at 18-point or larger with one uncluttered, non-stereotyped image per card, high contrast, rounded card corners, and animal names/icons repeated on every card. Arrows must remain distinguishable in grayscale; use solid growth arrows and dashed new-generation arrows with text labels. Offer read-aloud, card manipulation, enlarged/tactile-edged cards, reduced copying, oral comparison, and scribing. Do not test color naming or fine-motor drawing instead of life-cycle reasoning.
- **Technology disposition:** No technology artifact applies; evidence is the listed physical work and paper records.
