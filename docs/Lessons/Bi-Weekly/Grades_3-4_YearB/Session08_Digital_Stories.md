# Session 8: Digital Stories

## Lesson at a glance

Grades 3-4; Bi-Weekly B; Communication art; **one 40-minute meeting**.
**Objective:** create/present beginning/middle/end, revise from a reader's
feedback and explain a safe sharing choice. **Why:** digital stories allow
editable visual communication, not just consuming media.
Catholic/CST-C2 respectful helping theme; CST-T1 actual editing/presenting;
CST-T3 privacy/credit; CST-A3 narrative critique/revision.
Official alignment **VERIFICATION REQUIRED**. Technology **Required**;
novice workflow. Prep 20 after software works; installation extra; cleanup 5.

## Before class and exact supplies

Approved offline LibreOffice Impress: blank, `Slide > New Slide` twice,
`Insert > Text Box`, `Slide Show > Start from First Slide`, Escape, local
`File > Save As` `.odp`, reopen. Pretest/leave three-slide sample and save path.
Equivalent app only with teacher-tested exact commands; otherwise paper.
Teams <=3 (4/5/7/9): computer/team; each pupil two paper sheets/pencil.
Teacher board/timer/demo from issued device. Fictional sample:
"A visitor cannot find the book tray / A classmate listens and points /
They thank each other and return the book." Gospel-value application,
not invented Scripture quotation or saint biography.

## Vocabulary/background/SAFETY

Narrative = ordered story; scene = story part; audience = reader; revision =
meaning-preserving change. **Misconception:** more slides make better story.
**If asked "Can we say this really happened to a saint?"** No; label this original
fiction. Historical retellings need verified source evidence not provided here.
Dry devices, no pupil photos/voices/names, passwords, music downloads, public
uploads or QR links. Stop/report damaged gear. Indoor primary.

## Meeting 1: exactly 40 minutes

1. **0-4:** "How can a story show respect rather than stereotype someone?"
2. **4-10:** Demo three slides/text/present/Escape and fiction label.
3. **10-21:** Every child edits/presents a three-minute turn; peers storyboard
   and record actual beginning/middle/end.
4. **21-29:** Peer names problem/action/resolution without coaching; record
   confusion, revise wording/layout, present again.
5. **29-35:** Each explains structure/feedback revision and private sharing;
   operation roster separate from paper storyboard.
6. **35-40:** Save/reopen, exit/return, collect dated evidence.

## Success/access/troubleshooting

Meets: coherent three-part story, actual operation, critique-backed revision and
privacy choice. Grade 3/support: three sentence/picture frames, scribing/motor
assistance. Grade 4/challenge: pacing/clarity tradeoff. Missing ending: reduce
extra scenes, finish three-part core. No software/device: same times paper comic,
art/narrative only, **digital operation not observed**. Half computers allow
two work-window turn waves; fewer requires later real operation.
Early finish: new reader, not audio/video expansion or homework finishing.

**Family:** We revised a fictional helping story. Ask, "How did the ending
resolve the problem?" Optional: tell a short story without a device.

## Teacher artifact pack (lesson-specific release checklist)

- **Student material (print/no-print):** Print a two-page **Original helping story plan and operation record**. Page 1 has three boxes: `Slide 1 BEGINNING—fictional character, setting, problem; exact sentence: ____`, `Slide 2 MIDDLE—helping action/attempt; exact sentence: ____`, `Slide 3 END—how the stated problem changes/resolves; exact sentence: ____`, and `Label on slide 1: ORIGINAL FICTION`. Page 2 has `My three-minute computer turn: inserted slide / typed / presented / escaped (circle observed actions)`, `Reader retold problem ____ / action ____ / resolution ____`, `Reader confusion: ____`, `Exact wording/layout changed: ____`, `Second presentation result: ____`, and `Private sharing choice: ____`. No-print: fold two sheets into the same three frames and evidence list; paper route is labeled **narrative/art—digital operation not observed**.
- **Evidence and answer guidance:** Accept any respectful fictional helping story with a recognizable problem, one action and related resolution, plus **ORIGINAL FICTION**, an actual reader response and one evidence-based revision. The supplied complete example is acceptable: visitor cannot find book tray / classmate listens and points / they thank each other and return book. Each pupil must have an observed edit and presentation turn for technology evidence. Accept private local sharing such as `show only teacher/class on this offline device`; reject pupil names/photos/voices, stereotypes, invented Scripture quotations or saint biography presented as fact.
- **Worked example and finished example:** Build three offline Impress slides: `ORIGINAL FICTION—A visitor cannot find the classroom book tray.` / `A classmate listens, then points to the tray label.` / `The visitor finds the book, says thank you, and both return it.` First reader says `Who could not find it?`; revise Slide 1 from `Someone` to `A visitor`, present again, and record `reader named visitor/problem/action/ending`. Finished means exactly three coherent slides, fiction label, actual peer feedback/revision, local save/reopen and presentation—not extra transitions or media.
- **Visual example and board setup:** Display the exact novice path `Blank → Slide > New Slide (twice) → Insert > Text Box → Slide Show > Start from First Slide → Escape → File > Save As .odp → reopen`. Beside it post three numbered frames with arrows `problem → helping action → resolution`, then a return arrow `reader confusion → revise words/layout → present again`. Show the safe filename `S8_team##_YYYYMMDD.odp`.
- **Discussion with acceptable response, and misconception:** Ask `How does the ending connect to the opening problem?` (the visitor finds/returns the book), `Why label ORIGINAL FICTION?` (so invention is not mistaken for history/testimony), `What feedback can guide a revision?` (a reader cannot name a character/problem/action/resolution), and `Is more slides better?` (no; a complete clear three-part story is the criterion). Correct stereotypes by changing the character/action, and correct `a real saint said/did this` unless a verified source is supplied.
- **Kit contents, kit label, cleanup and reset:** Kit label: **S8 IMPRESS—TEAM __**. Each tray contains exactly `1 preflighted computer with approved offline LibreOffice Impress and local save path`; each pupil receives `2 plain sheets and 1 pencil`. The teacher station has `1 issued demo device, timer, printed command path, three-slide sample and operation roster`. No headphones, stylus or online asset kit is required. Reset by saving/reopening the numbered `.odp`, removing all personal names, closing Slide Show and Impress, deleting abandoned temporary exports under school retention rules, returning device per charging procedure and filing both paper sheets.
- **Unfinished work:** Save the latest local file using only team number/date and attach a status slip `storyboard complete`, `slide __ missing`, `reader check due`, `revision due`, `second presentation due`, or `reopen not verified`. Preserve paper frames as the content fallback. A completed paper comic meets narrative/art evidence, but missing digital turns remain **not observed** and must be scheduled rather than inferred.
- **Annotated exemplar, common error and reteach:** Call out `fiction label`, `problem`, `listener action`, `resolution`, `reader quote`, `specific revision`, and `private save`. If Slide 2 repeats the problem, use `What did the helper do?`; if Slide 3 introduces a new problem, draw an arrow back to Slide 1 and rewrite it to change the original condition. If text is unreadable, remove excess words and enlarge/contrast type before adding decoration.
- **Safety classification:** **Severity: low; likelihood: unlikely; Adult ratio: 1 adult to 12 pupils at device stations maximum.** Devices stay dry/flat with intact leads, pupils use assigned files only, and screen/turn breaks are built into storyboard roles. Stop/report heat, damage or exposed wiring. **SDS: not applicable** to normal device, paper and pencil use; damaged battery procedures follow school documentation.
- **Print/accessibility check:** Use 18-point prompts, large numbered frames, high-contrast icons plus words, generous line spacing and sentence stems (`A ___ could not ___. / ___ helped by ___. / Then ___ because ___`). Offer read-aloud, dictation/scribe, enlarged print, adapted keyboard or school-approved offline speech-to-text; do not add a voice recording to the story.
- **Device compliance:** Use approved offline LibreOffice Impress (or a teacher-pretested equivalent with documented commands), no accounts or internet. No pupil photos, voices, names, passwords, web images, downloaded music, QR links, public upload, location data or home finishing. Save locally with non-identifying filenames; teacher controls any export and records paper-only work separately from digital operation. Paper/unplugged work is not executed-device evidence; until the approved device or hardware turn is witnessed, operation remains deferred/not observed.
