## Vocabulary, background and safety

**Open/closed:** interrupted/complete conducting path. **Output:** light response.
**Troubleshoot:** isolate a fault, make one approved correction and retest.
The battery supplies energy; current is charge flow. A resistor alone does not
make a light blink. This lesson claims neither blinking nor series/parallel.
Lamp results do not establish LED polarity understanding.

**Misconception:** extra lights can always be attached to the same supply.
**If asked:** "Is our light the Bethlehem star?" No; it is an artistic symbol.
The electrical test cannot reconstruct or prove the religious event.

**SAFETY:** obey the guide's model/age/holder gate. Adult isolates the battery
holder before snap changes and checks before reconnecting. No pupil cell access,
coin cells, household/USB power, live shorts, bare LEDs, extra branches, motors,
fan blades or take-home circuits. Warm/smelly/leaking/damaged -> stop/report,
adult isolates, OUT OF SERVICE. Keep paper beside, not over, the circuit.

## Numbered sequence: exactly 40 minutes

1. **0-4: Audience/meaning.** Prayer: "Jesus, help us share hope with care." Ask how a message can include a reader who cannot distinguish colors.
2. **4-8: Teach from the beginning.** Trace the approved return path; teacher tests OFF/ON and models isolation/inspection.
3. **8-10: Predict.** Each child draws the path and predicts both states; identify a gap in a drawn fault, not a live short.
4. **10-22: Wave A.** Follow the guide's isolated build, inspection, each child's two OFF/ON cycles and one teacher-managed open-path repair/retest. Waiting teams draw a readable Nativity message labeled "light as a symbol of hope."
5. **22-23: Reset.** Adult isolates power and transfers/resets station kits.
6. **23-35: Wave B / revise.** Remaining teams get the identical hardware routine. Finished teams explain the repair, peer-check their scene/message and revise one detail; at smaller enrollment use the window for retesting and individual checks. Ask each child: "Where was the gap? What did the retest show? What change helped your reader?"
7. **35-40: Cleanup.** Isolate/count/store all parts and cells, collect evidence and paper artwork. Note any physical turns still outstanding.

## Success, support and alternatives

Each child contributes to the isolated team build, operates both states twice,
explains the full return path and observed repair, and revises a purposeful
message. Record physical evidence separately as **observed / prompted / not yet**.
Score **1** scene only; **2** prompted path; **3** independent path/repair and
message evidence; **4** explains two possible open-path faults or model limits.

- **Grade 3/support:** labeled diagram, words/symbols not color alone, oral
  record and motor assistance; keep each child's prediction and switch turn.
- **Grade 4/challenge:** compare drawn faults one at a time; no additional loads.
- **25 pupils:** nine pairs/trios, five stations, two 12-minute waves.
  Inspection/access overruns mean deferred physical evidence, not rushed checks.
- **Failed kit:** isolate/report, use a checked reserve or the guide's separate
  verified protected-AA route. Never mix the two systems' supplies.
- **Paper route:** same timed windows using drawn paths/on-off tokens, modeled
  repair and message revision. Record **circuit reasoning/art only; electrical
  assembly/operation not observed**. All work is dry/indoors.

## Family snippet

We predicted and repaired a switched light, then revised a Nativity message.
The light is a religious symbol, not evidence about a miracle. Name the actual
hardware or paper route. **Ask:** "What broke the path, and what showed the
repair worked?" Paper only goes home; no electrical homework or purchases.

## Teacher artifact pack (lesson-specific release checklist)

- **Student material (print/no-print):** Give each pupil two half-sheets. **Circuit evidence:** `Station ____; route: hardware / paper`, `Trace battery holder → switch → lamp → return`, `Switch OFF prediction/output ____/____`, `Switch ON prediction/output ____/____`, `Draw/circle the teacher-created open-path gap`, `One adult-managed correction: ____`, `Retest output: ____`, and `My role: placed approved part / operated switch / traced / explained`. **Nativity reader card:** `Light is a symbol of hope`, `My 8–12 word message: ____`, `Peer could read words and meaning? Y/N`, `Revision: ____`. No-print: pupils copy these labels; paper route is marked **reasoning/art only—electrical assembly/operation not observed**.
- **Evidence and answer guidance:** Hardware mastery requires each child's recorded contribution, two OFF/ON cycles, full return-path explanation, a located open gap, one approved correction and observed retest. Accept `OFF—path interrupted at switch; ON—lamp lit`, or an honest failed output followed by adult isolation/check. Accept messages such as `Jesus brings hope; we share light through loving actions` or `The Nativity reminds us to welcome others with care`; words/symbols must not rely on color alone. The lamp is an artistic symbol, not evidence of the Bethlehem star or a miracle.
- **Worked example and finished example:** On the matching manufacturer project, adult assembles the approved battery-holder/slide-switch/lamp path, demonstrates OFF then ON, isolates the holder, removes/loosens the single setup-card-approved connection, and asks pupils to predict `off`. Adult restores that one connection after inspection; the pupil operates the switch and records `lamp on—repair supported`. The exact snap numbers, connector lengths and cell count are **N/A as universal text** because they must come from the OHM-135 setup card; print the locally verified diagram rather than inventing them. Finish the reader card with `Light reminds us to share Christ's hope with others` and revise any color-only word.
- **Visual example and board setup:** Post the model-matched setup card plus two enlarged schematics: **closed return path—lamp on** and **one open gap—lamp off**. Draw `PREDICT → ADULT ISOLATES → ONE APPROVED CORRECTION → INSPECT → RECONNECT → RETEST`. Beside it place a separate Nativity panel `symbol ≠ scientific proof` and the accessibility check `words/shapes, not color alone`; list Wave A, adult reset, Wave B.
- **Discussion with acceptable response, and misconception:** Ask `What changed between open and closed?` (continuity at one connection/switch), `Why isolate power before snaps change?` (prevent shorts, heat and damage), `What does the retest support?` (that the restored path allowed this lamp to work), and `Is the lamp the Bethlehem star?` (no, it is a designed symbol of hope). Correct `a resistor makes it blink`, `add more lights`, `LED polarity was proved`, and `series/parallel` claims because those are not established by this project.
- **Kit contents, kit label, cleanup and reset:** Kit label: **S7 OHM-135—STATION __—SETUP CARD REV ____**. Each station contains exactly `1 OHM-135 grid, 1 manufacturer-approved battery holder with the guide-specified cells controlled by the adult, 1 slide switch module, 1 lamp module, and the exact numbered snap connectors shown and counted on that station's attached setup card, all in 1 dry tray`. Each team also receives `1 cardstock backing, 1 diagram sheet and 1 shared marker`; each pupil gets `1 evidence half-sheet, 1 message half-sheet and pencil`. Reset: adult switches OFF, isolates holder, counts every numbered part against the foam map/setup card, inspects heat/leak/damage, returns cells to controlled storage as the guide requires, caps marker, and marks `READY` or `OUT OF SERVICE`.
- **Unfinished work:** Adult first isolates and resets all hardware; no partly powered circuit is stored. Mark each pupil `observed / prompted / not yet` and the missing action `second OFF/ON cycle`, `repair retest`, or `path explanation`. Finish paper art separately without implying electrical success. Reschedule identical supervised hardware evidence; never send components home or substitute another child's turn.
- **Annotated exemplar, common error and reteach:** Label `energy source`, `complete return path`, `switch gap`, `lamp output`, `isolated correction`, `observed retest`, and `symbolic message`. If ON gives no light, pupils stop; adult compares one connection at a time to the known-good setup card, then holder/lamp condition—no live shorting. If a pupil points only from battery to lamp, trace the return route with a finger. If the message depends on red/green distinction, add words, outline or pattern.
- **Safety classification:** **Severity: moderate; likelihood: unlikely after controls; Adult ratio: 1 adult to no more than 8 powered OHM-135 stations and no more than 12 pupils in the active wave.** Adult alone accesses cells, isolates power before every snap change and approves reconnection. No coin cells, USB/household power, bare LEDs, motors, fan blades, extra branches, paper over contacts, liquids or take-home builds. Heat, odor, leakage, swelling, exposed conductor or damage means stop, isolate and mark OUT OF SERVICE. **Battery/product documentation and the school's SDS/emergency binder apply to leakage or damaged cells.** **Commercial-product hold:** Used markers. Enter the exact manufacturer/product in [Safety Data Sheet Register](../../../Review/Safety_Data_Sheet_Register.csv). Keep each listed product on **HOLD** until school approval and SDS applicability are resolved; do not infer a brand or claim SDS N/A.
- **Print/accessibility check:** Use 18-point sans-serif text, thick black path lines, standard symbols plus words, patterned OFF/ON states and a tactile raised-loop diagram. Read directions aloud; allow pointing/oral diagnosis, scribing, adapted switch operation or teacher-managed placement while preserving the pupil's prediction, switch turn and explanation where safe.
- **Technology disposition:** Use only the school-owned Snap Circuits STEM Classroom Activity Kits (OHM-135), matching manufacturer guide, approved battery configuration, and teacher-controlled dry storage; no account, internet, or cloud service is required. Paper/unplugged work is not executed-device evidence; until an approved hardware turn is witnessed, electrical assembly/operation remains deferred/not observed.
