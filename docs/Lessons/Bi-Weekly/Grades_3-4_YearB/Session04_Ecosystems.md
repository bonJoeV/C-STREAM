# Session 4: Ecosystems

## Lesson at a glance

Grades 3-4; Bi-Weekly B; Life systems; **one 40-minute meeting**.
**Objective:** build a four-role feeding model, direct food-to-eater arrows and
record which modeled links change when one card is removed.
**Why:** ecological care needs system reasoning, not body-yarn games or
guaranteed catastrophe claims.
Catholic/CST-C3 stewardship choice; CST-S3 roles/model limit;
CST-A1 explanatory drawing; CST-M2 before/after link table.
Official alignment **VERIFICATION REQUIRED**. Technology **None**; introductory.
Prep 10; cleanup 5.

## Before class and exact supplies

Teams <=3 (4/5/7/9): four large **hypothetical** organism cards, five short
20 cm string pieces, two markers. Per pupil two sheets/pencil.
Teacher board/timer; copy stipulated model key:
P photosynthetic producer; Q consumes P; R consumes Q; D decomposes remains
from P/Q/R. Five represented links: P->Q, Q->R, P->D, Q->D, R->D.
These relationships are a prepared model, not unsupported real species diets.
Add sunlight as nonliving energy input, not counted organism/link here.

## Vocabulary/background/SAFETY

Ecosystem = organisms/environment relationships; producer = makes food;
consumer = eats organisms; decomposer = breaks down remains.
**Misconception:** arrows point from predator to prey. **If asked "Does
removing Q prove R dies?"** It removes represented food links; real effects
depend on other food sources/conditions, not shown here.
No yarn on bodies/fingers, tugging, knots, unknown specimens or live animals.
Short desk strings only; report damaged supplies. Indoor primary, no Kateri
ancestry/biography claims needed.

## Meeting 1: exactly 40 minutes

1. **0-4:** "How does caring for habitat require knowing relationships?"
2. **4-10:** Read key; model food-to-eater arrow, sunlight distinction.
3. **10-22:** Each places a card/link, draws five labeled arrows/roles; team
   checks from key and records original five links.
4. **22-30:** Remove Q and attached three links; two remain. Record table
   5 -> 2, discuss uncertain ecological effects, revise a reversed arrow.
5. **30-35:** Each child explains one arrow, roles, link-count change, model limit and
   habitat-care choice without claiming a measured cascade.
6. **35-40:** Count four cards/five strings, cap markers, save dated diagrams.

## Success/access/troubleshooting

Meets: accurate roles/arrows, 5/2 table and honest system limitation.
Grade 3/support: pictured arrows/read-aloud/scribing. Grade 4/challenge:
explain omitted alternate foods, no universal percentages.
Reversed link: follow source-food into eater, compare key. Missing string:
draw arrows, same model objective. Early finish: remove a different card and
count represented links, not predict certain deaths.

**Family:** We modeled ecological links. Ask, "Which way did your arrows point?"
Optional: discuss caring for a habitat from a picture.

## Teacher artifact pack (lesson-specific release checklist)

- **Student material (print/no-print):** Print one **P–Q–R–D feeding-model record** per pupil. Include `Model key: P makes food; Q consumes P; R consumes Q; D decomposes remains`, `Draw and label the five original arrows`, `Original represented links: ____`, `Cross out card removed: Q`, `Links removed: ____`, `Links remaining: ____`, `One possible real-world effect: ____`, `Why that effect is uncertain: ____`, and `One habitat-care action supported by this model: ____`. No-print: pupils draw four labeled circles and the before/after table `P→Q, Q→R, P→D, Q→D, R→D`.
- **Evidence and answer guidance:** Finished work shows all five arrows **from food/remains toward the receiver**, original count `5`, removes `P→Q`, `Q→R`, and `Q→D` with Q, and reports `2` remaining (`P→D`, `R→D`). Accept `R loses its represented food link` as a modeled change and `R might find another food source not shown` as the limit. Accept care actions such as protecting P's habitat or reducing litter only when pupils explain which represented relationship could be protected. Do not require real species names; these are hypothetical roles.
- **Worked example and finished example:** Arrange P left, Q center, R right and D below. Draw `P→Q`, `Q→R`, and three remains links `P→D`, `Q→D`, `R→D`; write `before = 5`. Cover Q and its three attached strings; write `after = 2; change = 3 fewer`. Finish with `R's shown food connection is gone, but this model cannot prove R dies because alternate foods and conditions are omitted`.
- **Visual example and board setup:** Post the stipulated model key and a legend `arrow means food/remains moves to receiver`; place sunlight above P as a dashed **nonliving energy input, not one of the five links**. Beside the tabletop model draw `BEFORE 5 / REMOVE Q AND ATTACHED LINKS / AFTER 2 / WHAT THE MODEL DOES NOT SHOW`. Keep all string on the desk.
- **Discussion with acceptable response, and misconception:** Ask `Which way does P→Q point and why?` (P is food for Q; arrow points to the eater), `Which three links touch Q?` (P→Q, Q→R, Q→D), `Does removing Q prove R dies?` (no; only the represented food link is removed), and `Why include D?` (to model decomposition of remains from P/Q/R). Correct predator-to-prey arrows by asking who receives food; correct `sun is an organism/link` by distinguishing nonliving input; correct guaranteed catastrophe claims by naming omitted relationships.
- **Kit contents, kit label, cleanup and reset:** Kit label: **S4 MODEL P-Q-R-D—TEAM __**. Each tray contains exactly `4 large cards P/Q/R/D, five 20 cm cotton-string pieces, and 2 markers`; each pupil receives `2 plain sheets and 1 pencil`. Reset by removing string from hands/desks without pulling, laying five pieces straight in the `5 LINKS` sleeve, returning cards in P-Q-R-D order, capping both markers, and checking `4 cards / 5 strings / 2 markers`. No live animals, soil or specimen material belongs in the tray.
- **Unfinished work:** Pupils circle the last verified link on the record, list any missing links, then fully disassemble and reset the tray. Resume from the printed key; if Q removal was not performed, mark `after count not observed` rather than copying another team's 2. File individual diagrams even when the shared model is incomplete.
- **Annotated exemplar, common error and reteach:** Annotate the exemplar `producer P`, `food-to-eater`, `remains-to-D`, `5 before`, `3 removed`, `2 after`, and `model limit`. A reversed P/Q arrow signals receiver confusion: hand the P card to Q while tracing P→Q. A count of 3 after removal usually leaves Q→D or P→Q in place; physically identify every string touching Q. A claim `R dies` needs the frame `The model shows ____; it does not show ____`.
- **Safety classification:** **Severity: low; likelihood: unlikely; Adult ratio: 1 adult to 24 pupils maximum.** Use only five short desk strings per team; no loops on bodies/fingers, tying, tugging, throwing, unknown specimens, live animals or tasting. Stop for frayed string or unsafe play. **Commercial-product hold:** Used markers. Enter the exact manufacturer/product in [Safety Data Sheet Register](../../../Review/Safety_Data_Sheet_Register.csv). Keep each listed product on **HOLD** until school approval and SDS applicability are resolved; do not infer a brand or claim SDS N/A.
- **Print/accessibility check:** Use 18-point P/Q/R/D labels with distinct shapes and textures in addition to color, thick arrowheads, high contrast and generous spacing. Offer preprinted arrows to trace, tactile string placement, oral link naming, partner placement or scribing; read the hypothetical key aloud so species knowledge is not a hidden prerequisite.
- **Technology disposition:** No technology artifact applies; evidence is the physical model and paper records.
