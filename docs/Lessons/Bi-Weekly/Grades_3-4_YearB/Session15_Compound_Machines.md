# Session 15: Compound Machines

## Lesson at a glance

Grades 3-4; Bi-Weekly B; Machines; **one 40-minute meeting**.
**Objective:** label two machine functions, test a lever/ramp transfer model
and revise a guide using repeated results. **Why:** combining tools changes
work's route/force/distance, not creates free energy.
Catholic/CST-C3 careful dignified work; CST-S3 interacting mechanisms;
CST-E1 criterion/limit; CST-E2 retest; CST-A1 labeled model.
Official alignment **VERIFICATION REQUIRED**. Technology **None**;
introductory. Prep 15; cleanup 5. No unsupported Joseph-tool history or bicycle
chain-as-pulley claim.

## Before class and exact supplies

Teams <=3 (4/5/7/9): 30 cm ruler/low broad block, smooth 20 cm cardboard ramp,
paper cup, three identical plastic counters, tray, **30 cm tape**.
Each pupil receives two sheets/pencil; the teacher demo uses a separate
pretested lever-ramp model made from the listed ruler, block, cup, cardboard,
counters, tray and masking tape, plus the board and timer.
Pretest low lever supporting cup: adult tapes cup to load end; pressing opposite
end slowly raises cup **<=1 cm** to a low ramp entrance. Pupil **slides**, never
flicks, counters down ramp into tray. Mechanism combines lever lifting platform
and inclined-plane transfer, not automatic Rube Goldberg.

## Vocabulary/background/SAFETY

Compound = combined; lever = pivoted bar; inclined plane = ramp; tradeoff =
gain with cost. Machines can change direction/distance/force; friction matters.
**Misconception:** adding parts always improves. **If asked "Is a bicycle chain
a pulley?"** Chain/sprocket drive is distinct; today's tested functions are
lever/ramp only.
Low trays/slow pressure, no launches, suspended loads, blades, disassembly or
pinch tests. Stop/reset sliding block/report damaged kit. Indoor primary.

## Meeting 1: exactly 40 minutes

1. **0-4:** "Why combine tools for useful work?" Optional careful-work prayer.
2. **4-10:** Model load/effort/pivot/ramp and slow capped lift/slide.
3. **10-22:** Each labels functions, team builds from pretested layout, rotates
   operator/recorder/checker; criterion three counters reach tray twice.
4. **22-30:** Record transfer counts 0-3, change cardboard side-guide only;
   repeat two transfers with same load/ramp/lift cap.
5. **30-35:** Each child draws interacting mechanisms, gives four counts and change/
   tradeoff reason; "What did the guide help, and what did it add?"
6. **35-40:** Remove/count counters, untape/store tools, collect dated diagrams.

## Success/access/troubleshooting

Meets: two correct functions, criterion/actual retest and labeled interaction/
work-care choice. Grade 3/support: preassembled cup/arrow labels/scribing.
Grade 4/challenge: manual-versus-automatic limitation, no mechanical ratio.
Unstable pivot: stop/reset, repeat invalid trial. No safe block/ramp: teacher
demonstration with pupil turns; absent hardware diagram only, physical transfer
not observed. Early finish: same repeat, not longer/faster launcher.

**Family:** We combined lever/ramp functions and retested a guide. Ask, "What
job did each machine do?" Optional: identify a safe tool by sight.

## Teacher artifact pack (lesson-specific release checklist)

- **Student material (print/no-print):** Prepare a two-page **Lever + Ramp Counter Transfer** sheet. Page 1 supplies a side-view drawing box and labels to place: `effort/input`, `30 cm ruler lever`, `broad block/pivot`, `paper cup/load`, `lift no more than 1 cm`, `20 cm cardboard inclined plane`, `counter slide`, and `tray/output`. It asks `Lever function ____`, `Inclined-plane function ____`, and `How the two mechanisms interact ____`. Page 2 contains `Criterion: all 3 counters reach tray on each transfer`, a table `Before guide trial 1 __/3 | trial 2 __/3 | After cardboard side-guide trial 1 __/3 | trial 2 __/3`, then `Only change made ____`, `What the guide helped ____`, `What the guide added/cost ____`, `Recommendation ____`, and `Model limit ____`. No-print pupils copy the trial table and label a plain-paper sketch from the board.
- **Evidence and answer guidance:** Require correct functions for the tested mechanisms only: slow pressure on the lever raises the taped cup/platform no more than 1 cm to the ramp entrance; the inclined plane provides the downward transfer path; the pupil slides rather than launches three identical counters into the tray. Four actual counts must be integers 0-3, with the cardboard side-guide as the sole revision and load/ramp/lift cap unchanged. Accept `the guide raised transfers from 2/3 and 1/3 to 3/3 and 3/3, but it added material and setup` or an honest no-improvement result. Do not substitute wheel-and-axle/pulley labels, claim the model is automatic, infer less total work/free energy, or invent force measurements.
- **Worked example and finished example:** Diagram the pretested arrangement with the ruler pivoting on the broad block, cup taped at the load end, ramp touching the cup exit, and tray at the lower end. Record before-guide results `2/3, 2/3`; observed issue `one counter left each side`; add only a low folded-cardboard side-guide; record `3/3, 3/3`. Write `The lever lifted the cup to the ramp entrance; the ramp carried the counters downward. The guide improved direction but added a part, friction contact, and setup. Recommend the guide for reliable transfer; this small counter model does not measure force or prove all compound machines improve when parts are added`.
- **Visual example and board setup:** On the demonstration tray label `EFFORT`, `PIVOT`, `LOAD/CUP`, `≤1 cm`, `RAMP`, `SLIDE`, and `TRAY`. Draw a system arrow `slow push → lever lift → manual slide → inclined-plane transfer → tray`, emphasizing the manual handoff so it is not described as an automatic Rube Goldberg machine. Beside it make the exact four-count table and a `CONSTANT / ONE CHANGE` T-chart: constants are three identical counters, ramp, lift cap, tray, and operator method; change is cardboard side-guide only.
- **Discussion with acceptable response, and misconception:** Ask `What job does the lever do?` (pivots to raise the cup/load slightly); `What job does the inclined plane do?` (provides a sloped path for sliding to the tray); `Where does a person still act?` (slowly presses and manually slides counters); `Did the machine create energy or free work?` (no--input and tradeoffs remain); `Does an extra part always help?` (no--it may add friction, setup, instability, or no benefit); `Why repeat twice before and twice after?` (to see whether the result is consistent); and `What did the guide cost?` (material/setup and possibly friction). Correct launch language, chain-as-pulley claims, and the assumption that `compound` means strongest/most complicated.
- **Kit contents, kit label, cleanup and reset:** Kit label: **S15 COMPOUND MACHINE--TEAM __**, applied to each tray holding exactly 1 metric 30 cm ruler, 1 low broad wooden block, 1 smooth 20 cm cardboard ramp, 1 paper cup pre-taped by an adult to the load end, 3 identical plastic counters, 1 receiving tray, and a pre-portioned 30 cm masking-tape strip. Issue two plain sheets and one pencil per pupil. Reset in this order: stop motion; count three counters into the cup; remove only teacher-approved tape without tearing the ruler/cup; flatten and inspect ramp/side-guide; nest cup, block, ruler, and ramp in the tray outline; report a sliding pivot, bent ramp, cracked ruler, or lost counter before reissue.
- **Unfinished work:** Put a slash through any trial affected by a moving pivot, launch, counter change, ramp shift, or lift over 1 cm and write `invalid--repeat`; never convert it to 0/3. If time ends, preserve the last valid raw counts and mark `DIAGRAM`, `BEFORE REPEAT`, `GUIDE BUILD`, or `AFTER REPEAT` pending. Work without safe hardware is labeled `diagram reasoning only--physical transfer not observed`; teacher-demonstration participants record their own observations but not operator evidence.
- **Annotated exemplar, common error and reteach:** Annotate `two distinct functions`, `interaction/handoff`, `four raw counts`, `one variable`, `tradeoff`, and `bounded recommendation`. Calling the ramp a lever indicates function-by-shape confusion; ask what pivots versus what remains sloped. Writing `3/3` with no trial identity indicates data organization needs a fresh demonstration; physically place counters in four labeled result boxes. Claiming `longer ramp uses less work` imports an unmeasured conclusion; rewrite to the observed transfer outcome only. Missing tradeoff prompts a material/setup/friction comparison before recommendation.
- **Safety classification:** **Severity: moderate; likelihood: unlikely** when the model stays low and slow, covering finger pinch at the pivot or a counter launched toward eyes. Adult ratio: **1 adult to no more than 12 pupils actively operating models**; other teams diagram or record until supervised. Adult tapes/pretests the cup, pupils press slowly with open hands, lift remains ≤1 cm, counters are slid one at a time into the tray, and everyone stops for a moving block or damaged part. No suspended loads, blades, disassembly, launches, or pinch experiments. **Commercial-product hold:** Used masking tape. Enter the exact manufacturer/product in [Safety Data Sheet Register](../../../Review/Safety_Data_Sheet_Register.csv). Keep each listed product on **HOLD** until school approval and SDS applicability are resolved; do not infer a brand or claim SDS N/A.
- **Print/accessibility check:** Use a large side-view diagram, 16-point labels, bold directional arrows, a four-cell count table with counter icons, and terms paired with action phrases (`lever--raises`, `inclined plane--sloped transfer`). Keep meaning independent of color. Offer a pre-labeled or partially assembled model, nonslip mat/ruler support, alternate operator/recorder/checker roles, oral count/reasoning, scribing, and raised/tactile labels that do not enter the mechanism.
- **Technology disposition:** No technology artifact applies; evidence is the physical mechanism tests and paper records.
