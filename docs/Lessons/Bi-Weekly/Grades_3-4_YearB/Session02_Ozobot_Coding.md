## Before class

1. Confirm model and loan; do not assume CSCOE inventory. Obtain its official chart from the kit or [Ozobot color-code resources](https://ozobot.com/create/color-codes/) (also keep the existing family resource **ozobot.com**).
2. **VERIFICATION REQUIRED:** code order, entry direction, line/segment widths and calibration procedure for the actual model. The previous unsourced U-turn/spin/turbo color list has been removed. Do not replace it with guessed colors.
3. Choose **one speed-change command** from that chart, copy its exact colors/label to the board and record `model / chart title / command / teacher observed output`. Test it twice. Choose a slow setting, not a race/turbo challenge. If chart/testing unavailable, **announce line-following observation only; command programming not observed**, or use the paper pathway.
4. Draw one smooth, unbranched black line on white paper, roughly 30 cm long, with rounded bends and clear start/end. Start around 5 mm width, then adjust to the model's instructions. Prepare a second line with the verified code on a straight portion. Follow the model guide for calibration; do not guess power-button timing.
5. Pair pupils, final trio if odd. Two desk testing trays; assign seven one-minute rounds at most for thirteen teams.

## Vocabulary and teacher background

**Sensor:** hardware detecting information. **Input:** sensed line/color. **Output:** movement/light response. **Command:** instruction represented by a verified color pattern. **Calibration:** adjusting sensing for the surface. **Debug:** compare expected and actual, change one thing and retest.

Ozobot does not "think" like a person; onboard programs respond to sensor readings. A line-following demonstration is **not proof that a pupil programmed it**. A pupil-created, tested, compatible color command is additional programming evidence. Branch choices are not guaranteed without verified instructions.

**Misconception:** "Any colorful stripe is code." The model expects particular patterns and spacing.
**If asked:** "Why didn't it obey?" Answer: "Check the model chart, entry direction, colors, spacing and calibration one at a time. An invented command cannot be fixed by trying harder."

## SAFETY

Use only desk trays; prevent falls at table edges. Teacher handles charging and calibration controls; dry paper/clean hands. Keep robots/small parts out of mouths and away from younger children. No racing, stairs, liquids or marker ink on the robot. Cap markers during tests; stop motion before retrieval.

## Numbered sequence: exactly 40 minutes

1. **0-4: Purpose.** Original prayer: "God, help us be truthful and patient with one another." Ask: "What does a sensor detect? Does a robot understand kindness the way a person does?"
2. **4-10: Model input/output.** Teacher shows baseline line-following, then the preverified speed-change command. Point to model chart. "What input changed? What output did we actually see?" If not verified, explicitly teach only observed line following.
3. **10-15: Paper prediction.** Partners trace a line with a token and sequence cards: follow route, read named command, change speed. Use "slow" as a **paper rule**, not a claim about an unverified color combination. Swap reader/executor.
4. **15-27: Draw/test slots.** Teams draw a smooth line and the exact verified code, with correct entry direction from the chart. In one-minute slots across two trays, one child places/starts, another observes output, then swap on a retest. Trio adds chart checker. Teacher logs each child's real operation/command evidence; waiting teams trace and label route.
5. **27-32: Debug one thing.** Teacher chooses a failed line or code. Change line width, segment spacing or calibration **one at a time**, per guide. Retest. "Which change is supported by the result?"
6. **32-35: Individual evidence.** Child names input/output, points to the documentation used, and explains one expected/actual difference. Paper-only children identify a sequence bug; do not claim real color decoding.
7. **35-40: Cleanup.** Teacher switches off/stores robots and counts kits; pupils cap markers, collect paper/tokens/rulers, clear trays and hand in slips.

## Unplugged / indoor alternative

Use the same sequence/time windows. Teacher follows named paper rule cards with a token; partners insert a wrong instruction, find the first mismatch, replace it and retest. No invented color chart. Label **CT sequencing/debugging observed; sensor/robot programming not observed**. All pathways are indoor, independent of winter and internet.

## Success, troubleshooting, support and challenge

**Success:** child predicts a sequence, identifies input/output and documentation, and explains one debug change. Actual robot programming additionally requires that child's verified command construction/test, not simply watching line following.
Score **1** guess; **2** trace with help; **3** independent prediction/debug/evidence distinction; **4** explains a hardware/model limitation.

- **No chart / unexpected response:** stop the color-code claim; use observed line following or paper sequence and report missing programming evidence.
- **Leaves line:** smooth solid width, plain white surface, gentle curves, calibration per model. No guessed "connect Ozobot" tablet requirement.
- **Support / Grade 3:** pre-drawn route and adult marker assistance; child chooses/checks command and predicts.
- **Challenge / Grade 4:** compare two trials, explain why successful sensing differs from successful paper execution; OzoBlockly is a later optional extension with separate setup.
- **25 pupils:** thirteen stationary teams, two trays, seven rounds; no multi-mission race. With one robot, use a teacher demonstration and log who actually tests.

## Teacher artifact pack (lesson-specific release checklist)

- **Student material (print/no-print):** Give each pupil an **Ozobot verified-command evidence half-sheet** with `Robot model: ____`, `Official chart title/date checked: ____`, `Board command name: ____`, `Exact colors in travel order: ____`, `Draw an entry arrow`, `Prediction: after reading the code it will ____`, `Trial 1 actual output: ____`, `One variable checked/changed: line width / color spacing / entry direction / calibration`, `Trial 2 actual output: ____`, and `Evidence route: robot / paper (circle)`. No-print: pupils copy those labels while the teacher keeps the model/chart reference visible. Paper-route pupils replace trial outputs with `token output` and label **robot sensing/programming not observed**.
- **Evidence and answer guidance:** Robot-route evidence requires the actual model, chart citation, exact board command, a predicted output, two observed runs and one controlled check; line following alone is recorded as `sensor followed black line`, not `I programmed it`. Accept the observed slow-speed change only if it matches the teacher's twice-preverified chart command. `No change`, `left the line`, or `read the pattern inconsistently` are honest acceptable results when followed by an evidence-based check. Paper-route evidence may show correct sequence/debugging but cannot earn color-sensor operation.
- **Worked example and finished example:** There is deliberately **no universal printed color sequence**: codes, spacing and calibration can differ by model, so a fixed example would be unsafe misinformation. Complete the local worked sample before class as `model ____ / chart ____ / command ____ / colors ____ / entry → / prediction: speed decreases / Trial 1 ____ / checked ____ / Trial 2 ____`. Show the tested sheet itself. A finished pupil card repeats that verified command and distinguishes expected from actual output; if verification is unavailable, the finished example is the paper rule `follow line → read SLOW card → move token slowly`, marked **simulation only**.
- **Visual example and board setup:** Mount the **model-matched official chart** beside two 30 cm routes: baseline black line and verified-code line. Write `SENSOR INPUT → DOCUMENTED COMMAND → OBSERVED OUTPUT` and fill `model / chart / command / teacher test 1 / teacher test 2`. Add a large travel arrow over the code, a `change only one` debug list, and seven numbered one-minute rounds for the two trays.
- **Discussion with acceptable response, and misconception:** Ask `What did the sensor detect?` (black line and/or the documented color segments), `What proves this was a command rather than decoration?` (model chart plus compatible pattern and observed response), `Why can two trials differ?` (line quality, spacing, direction, calibration or placement), and `What may a paper run prove?` (sequencing/debugging, not sensing). Correct `the robot understands` to programmed sensor response and `any colorful stripe is code` to exact documented representation.
- **Kit contents, kit label, cleanup and reset:** Kit label: **S2 OZOBOT—MODEL ____**. The class case contains exactly `2 numbered charged Ozobots, 2 matching chargers, 2 shallow trays, 1 laminated official model chart, 2 teacher demo routes, and the model guide`. Each team envelope contains `2 white sheets, 1 paper robot token, 1 ruler, 1 black marker, 1 red marker, 1 blue marker, 1 green marker, and 6 team-drawn command/condition cards`; each pupil gets `1 evidence half-sheet and pencil`. Reset by powering off both robots, teacher checking casing/charge, returning each to its numbered slot, clearing trays dry, capping four markers, stacking six cards/token/ruler, and recycling only marked test paper.
- **Unfinished work:** File the half-sheet under `prediction only`, `Trial 1 complete—retest needed`, `robot evidence complete`, or `paper pathway`. Add the robot number and exact next round. A child who missed a physical turn receives the next supervised slot; do not convert a peer's run, a demonstration or a paper trace into that child's operation evidence.
- **Annotated exemplar, common error and reteach:** Annotate the local teacher sheet `chart source`, `travel order`, `expected`, `actual`, and `one changed variable`. Leaving the black line suggests route width/curve/surface or calibration; rereading the code without the expected speed suggests order, spacing or model compatibility. Compare one feature to the guide, retest, and avoid declaring hardware failure until the known-good baseline line also fails.
- **Safety classification:** **Severity: moderate; likelihood: unlikely; Adult ratio: 1 adult to no more than 12 pupils around the two robot trays** (other pupils remain at paper stations). Keep units centered on dry desks; no racing, stairs, liquids, mouths, grabbing moving robots or pupil charging. Adult handles charging/calibration and isolates a hot, swollen, cracked or exposed device. **Commercial-product hold:** Used markers. Enter the exact manufacturer/product in [Safety Data Sheet Register](../../../Review/Safety_Data_Sheet_Register.csv). Keep each listed product on **HOLD** until school approval and SDS applicability are resolved; do not infer a brand or claim SDS N/A.
- **Print/accessibility check:** Print color names and ordered pattern positions as well as color swatches, use 18-point text, thick black entry arrows and grayscale-distinct hatch marks. Offer a raised-line route, chart reader, oral prediction, scribe, enlarged chart or teacher placement while preserving each pupil's own prediction/observation.
- **Device compliance:** Use only the two classroom robots, model guide and teacher-approved printed chart. No account, tablet, OzoBlockly, internet search, cloud upload, personal device or student photo is needed. Record model/operation evidence on paper; if the approved hardware/chart is absent, explicitly use the paper pathway rather than simulating a successful robot test. Paper/unplugged work is not executed-device evidence; until the approved device or hardware turn is witnessed, operation remains deferred/not observed.
