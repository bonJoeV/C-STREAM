# Session 13: Easter Creation

## Lesson at a glance

Grades 3-4; Bi-Weekly B; Faith communication; **one 40-minute meeting**.
**Objective:** compose a three-part Easter hope display, test reader
interpretation and revise one visual feature. **Why:** art communicates belief
purposefully without pretending an image proves a scientific result.
CST-C1 symbol/evidence distinction; CST-A2 composition purpose; CST-A3
critique/revision; CST-E1 audience criterion/limit.
Official alignment **VERIFICATION REQUIRED**. Technology **None**;
introductory. Prep 10; cleanup 5. No technology artifact applies; the separate
[Christmas Circuits](Session07_Christmas_Circuits.md) lesson remains unchanged.

## Before class and exact supplies

Teams <=3 (4/5/7/9): two cardstock sheets, two markers, **20 cm tape**.
Each pupil paper/pencil; the teacher demo uses a pre-folded three-panel
cardstock display, the listed markers and masking tape, board and timer. Fold one card into
three standing panels; spare for revision. Board teacher paraphrase:
"Easter celebrates Jesus' Resurrection; an empty-tomb image, sunrise and
hopeful message can communicate belief." Original retelling/convention, not
archaeological reconstruction or borrowed quotation. Criterion reader identifies
hope/Resurrection message at 1 m within ten seconds.

## Vocabulary/background/SAFETY

Composition = arrangement; emphasis = directing attention; symbol = image
with meaning; critique = specific helpful response.
**Misconception:** butterfly is a dead animal resurrecting.
**If asked "Does the display prove Easter?"** No; it expresses Christian belief,
not an experiment. Living development differs from Resurrection.
Dry paper only, no real stones/moss, batteries, flames or identifying names.
Stop/report damaged markers. Indoor primary, no spring materials required.

## Meeting 1: exactly 40 minutes

1. **0-4:** Optional Easter prayer; "What should a reader understand?"
2. **4-9:** Model three panels/visual emphasis and 1 m test/stock limits.
3. **9-21:** Each sketches/creates one purposeful panel, team joins composition.
4. **21-30:** Peer names message; record correct/not yet/comment, revise a
   word/shape placement and retest at same distance/time.
5. **30-35:** Each child explains intended symbol/form, actual feedback/revision,
   criterion and faith/science distinction.
6. **35-40:** Cap/count, save dated designs/evidence, clear; approved paper only
   may go home.

## Success/access/troubleshooting

Meets: purposeful composition, explicit criterion/test record and critique-
backed revision, not enthusiasm. Grade 3/support: three-panel frame/picture
stems/scribing. Grade 4/challenge: explain clarity-versus-detail tradeoff.
Misread symbol: add a brief original word, do not coach test. Missing card:
fold paper, note form constraint change. Early finish: new reader, equal test.

**Family:** We revised an Easter hope display for an audience. Ask, "What did
your reader understand?" Optional: discuss a hope symbol without buying materials.

## Teacher artifact pack (lesson-specific release checklist)

- **Student material (print/no-print):** Provide one **Easter Hope Three-Panel Planner and Reader Test** per pupil. It contains three adjacent boxes: `Panel 1--empty-tomb image or another teacher-approved Easter symbol; intended meaning ____`, `Panel 2--sunrise/light or another emphasis feature; why it draws attention ____`, and `Panel 3--my original short hope/Resurrection message ____`. Below are `My team composition should communicate ____`, `Reader at 1 m, within 10 seconds, said ____`, `Criterion: identified hope/Resurrection message--yes / not yet`, `Specific critique ____`, `One word/shape/placement revised ____`, `Retest at the same distance/time: ____`, and `This display expresses Christian belief; it is not scientific proof because ____`. No-print pupils fold plain paper into three planning columns and copy only the bold field labels; an adult may record dictated exact words.
- **Evidence and answer guidance:** Release evidence requires each pupil's purposeful sketch/created panel, a team display with three coherent standing panels, a stated intended message, one uncoached reader result, a specific visual revision, and a same-condition retest. Accept original messages such as `Easter brings hope because Christians celebrate Jesus' Resurrection` or `The empty tomb helps us express Easter hope`. Accept an empty tomb, sunrise/light, cross, growing seed, or butterfly only when the maker states its intended symbolic role; with a butterfly, require the clarification that living metamorphosis is not literal Resurrection. The reader need not use the artist's exact words, but must identify hope or the Resurrection meaning within ten seconds at 1 m.
- **Worked example and finished example:** Fold a cardstock strip into three standing panels. Panel 1 shows a simple open tomb with `The empty tomb is an Easter symbol`; Panel 2 uses a large outlined sunrise to lead the eye across the fold; Panel 3 reads `Christians celebrate Jesus' Resurrection with hope`. Initial reader record: `said "a sunrise after a cave"; not yet`. Critique: `Resurrection message is too small`. Revision: move the message to the top, enlarge `Easter hope`, and simplify rays. Retest: `reader said "Easter and Resurrection give hope"; yes`. Finish the record with `This artwork communicates belief by symbols and words; it is not an experiment or archaeological reconstruction`.
- **Visual example and board setup:** Pre-fold the teacher model and mark a floor or desk viewing line exactly 1 m away. On the board arrange `INTENDED MESSAGE → PANEL 1 → PANEL 2 → PANEL 3 → READER'S WORDS → ONE REVISION → RETEST`, and post a ten-second symbol next to the viewing line. Display `emphasis can use size, outline, spacing, or placement--not color alone` and the frame `For this display, ____ symbolizes/expresses ____`. Keep the teacher paraphrase about Easter visible; do not post a borrowed quotation pupils are expected to copy.
- **Discussion with acceptable response, and misconception:** Ask `What should the reader understand before the ten seconds end?` (Easter hope/Jesus' Resurrection); `How can size or placement create emphasis?` (it directs attention to the key image/words); `If the reader names something different, did the test fail as a person?` (no--the result identifies a design revision); `Can an image prove Easter scientifically?` (no--the display expresses Christian belief); `Why is a butterfly comparison limited?` (a butterfly remains alive while developing; Resurrection is not insect development); and `What makes critique useful?` (it names a specific feature and likely improvement). Correct the ideas that decoration alone guarantees communication, reader coaching is valid testing, or a religious symbol is laboratory evidence.
- **Kit contents, kit label, cleanup and reset:** Kit label: **S13 EASTER CREATION--TEAM __--2 CARDSTOCK / 2 MARKERS / 20 cm TAPE**, applied to each team bundle. Add no glue, ruler, glitter, moss, stones, batteries, or flames. Issue one plain planning sheet and pencil per pupil; the teacher holds a timer and one 20 cm tape strip per team. Reset by capping and counting both markers, measuring/collecting unused tape backing or scraps, separating the finished three-panel display from the spare/revision cardstock, and filing dated planners/test evidence. Only approved paper artwork may go home.
- **Unfinished work:** Pencil the pupil's name/date on the reverse only, keep all three team panels together, and tag the next action `PANEL NEEDED`, `MESSAGE NEEDED`, `FIRST READER NEEDED`, `REVISION NEEDED`, or `RETEST NEEDED`. An orally composed message is transcribed verbatim and marked `scribed`, not omitted. A display without a reader test remains a prototype; a first reader miss without retest remains revision evidence, not a completed criterion.
- **Annotated exemplar, common error and reteach:** Call out `three-part composition`, `intended symbol meaning`, `visual emphasis`, `actual reader words`, `specific revision`, `equal retest`, and `faith/science boundary` on the finished model. If the page has three unrelated decorations, have the pupil state one intended message and remove/reposition elements that do not support it. If the record says only `reader liked it`, repeat the test with the neutral prompt `What message do you understand?`. If the artist explains during the ten seconds, invalidate/repeat without coaching. If the claim says a seed or butterfly proves Resurrection, sort cards headed `expresses belief` and `biological observation` before rewriting.
- **Safety classification:** **Severity: low; likelihood: unlikely.** Adult ratio: **1 adult to 25 pupils** for dry paper construction and desk-level display testing. Keep the 1 m route clear, tape pre-portioned at 20 cm per team, and damaged markers removed; prohibit real stones/moss, sharp tools, batteries, flames, identifying names, and floor-mounted trip hazards. **Commercial-product hold:** Used markers and masking tape. Enter the exact manufacturer/product in [Safety Data Sheet Register](../../../Review/Safety_Data_Sheet_Register.csv). Keep each listed product on **HOLD** until school approval and SDS applicability are resolved; do not infer a brand or claim SDS N/A. Substitute paint, spray, liquid adhesive, or cleaning agent is outside this release.
- **Print/accessibility check:** Set planner labels at 16-point minimum, use heavy panel borders, ample white space, left-to-right numbering, and black outlines that reproduce in grayscale. Pair symbol images with words, permit a non-color emphasis choice, offer pre-folded cardstock, pencil grips/motor assistance, oral composition/scribing, alternate seated viewing at the measured distance, and a private critique route. Avoid requiring detailed drawing skill to demonstrate message design.
- **Technology disposition:** No technology artifact applies; evidence is the listed physical work and paper records.
