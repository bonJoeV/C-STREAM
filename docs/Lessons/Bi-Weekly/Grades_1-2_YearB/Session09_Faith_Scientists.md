# Faith Scientists

## Before class

1. Assign teams of at most three: **4 / 5 / 7 / 9** kits for **10 / 15 / 20 / 25** pupils. Keep work at tables; distribute materials, not roaming station rotations.
2. Read the supplied paraphrase, citing the University of St Andrews source below; call Agnesi a mathematician, not a DNA discoverer. Set 18 counters per kit (six per child at teams of three) and draw two part/part/whole boxes.
3. Put two plain-paper sheets per child per meeting and crayons at tables. Draw this record on the board: **part + part / whole six / clearer explanation**. Children copy boxes/icons, not paragraphs. Teacher dates and saves each child's record between meetings.
4. Read the SAFETY section, pretest the actual task with one issued kit and put a roster beside the timer. Demonstrate orally with objects; read every direction aloud. No independent reading, Year A completion or family donation is a prerequisite.

## Vocabulary and teacher background

Mathematician = studies mathematics; compose = combine parts; explanation = show why.

Agnesi's historical work is not six-counter arithmetic; our task is age-appropriate reasoning inspired by clear explanation, not reenacting advanced mathematics.

**Common misconception:** Women did not contribute to mathematics, or our arithmetic is the historical text.

**If asked:** "Was she the scientist who discovered DNA?" No such claim is made; she wrote about mathematics.

## SAFETY

Large counters only; no mouths, independent web research or personal-information sharing. Keep aisles/exits clear. Stop for damaged materials; do not improvise a hazardous replacement. No student handles loose batteries or teacher cutting tools.

## Meeting 1: Reason clearly, serve thoughtfully - 30 minutes

1. **0-3:** Read the short verified historical context and separate it from today's task.
2. **3-7:** Model six counters split 2/4 and a second split 1/5; draw readable part/whole panels.
3. **7-20:** Each child uses six counters to find two different splits, explains each total and creates a clear pictorial panel. Partner checks that all six are represented; author revises an unclear symbol.
4. **20-26:** Each child explains both decompositions and the visual revision; identify the teacher-read historical fact versus today's demonstrated result.
5. **26-30:** Count 18 counters and six cards per kit; save individual panels. Collect each child's dated evidence, count kit items, return tools and clear tables. Cleanup is part of the period, not time added afterward.

## Questions to ask

- How do both of your splits still make six?
- Which statement came from the historical source?

## Individual evidence and success

**Grade 1:** Show two different six-counter splits and explain each with objects.

**Grade 2:** Represent/check both equations and justify a clearer visual explanation, distinguishing source context from own evidence.

Teacher visits three table teams during each of the first three four-minute blocks of practice (7-19), listening to or observing each member briefly; use the remaining practice minute and 20-26 for unfinished checks and comparison. At 25, this is nine table visits, **not 25 presentations**. Where a meeting specifies waves, use its supervised wave/check schedule instead of simultaneous table visits. Children keep separate records and partners may not answer for each other. Score each stated criterion **not yet / prompted / independent**; record absent/unobserved evidence as such. A group product, attendance, attractive art or a certificate is not individual mastery. Use the grade-level criterion, not handwriting, reading speed or religious worth.

## Support and challenge

**Support / nonreader access:** Use two part boxes, adult-read history and oral counting; no biography test. Adult scribing, pointing, tactile/large pieces and a seated partner-directed action can show understanding; do not score motor speed. Record who actually operated hardware/software: a partner's operation does not establish the child's independent operation.

**Challenge:** Find all possible unordered six splits and explain how you know without speed competition. Do not add an unprepared activity or extend the native period.

## If things go wrong / indoor alternative

Counters missing: draw six large circles on allocated paper. Do not substitute unverifiable affiliations, quotes, honorary-office details or DNA anecdotes.

**Indoor:** The complete primary lesson is indoors at tables; Minnesota weather, internet access and daylight are not prerequisites.

## Family copy - optional, no routine homework

We encountered a source-checked woman mathematician, then composed six in different ways and revised clear explanations. Ask: "How do both of your splits still make six?" Optional: show two small groups making six with drawings or words. No purchase, donation, device, home teaching or required take-home project.
