## Before class

1. Assign teams of at most three: **4 / 5 / 7 / 9** kits for **10 / 15 / 20 / 25** pupils. Keep work at tables; distribute materials, not roaming station rotations.
2. Check the school's actual recycling guidance and draw twelve labeled model cards: four dry scrap-paper reuse examples, four accepted clean recycling examples, four nonaccepted trash examples. If guidance is unknown, label recycling cards EXAMPLE pending local rule; do not put real items in bins based on them.
3. Put two plain-paper sheets per child per meeting and crayons at tables. Draw this record on the board: **category counts / total twelve / modeled before-after waste**. Children copy boxes/icons, not paragraphs. Teacher dates and saves each child's record between meetings.
4. Read the SAFETY section, pretest the actual task with one issued kit and put a roster beside the timer. Demonstrate orally with objects; read every direction aloud. No independent reading, Year A completion or family donation is a prerequisite.

## Vocabulary and teacher background

Reuse = use again; recycle = process accepted material; waste = unusable remainder; data = counts.

Accepted recycling varies locally; a symbol does not guarantee school acceptance. A twelve-card model shows counts, not measured annual waste reduction.

**Common misconception:** All materials with a recycling symbol go in every recycling bin.

**If asked:** "May we pick up outdoor litter?" Not in this class; we use clean model cards and ordinary classroom reuse.

## SAFETY

No litter, broken items, dirty containers, unknown waste, chemicals or outdoor collection. Keep aisles/exits clear. Stop for damaged materials; do not improvise a hazardous replacement. No student handles loose batteries or teacher cutting tools.

## Meeting 1: Count and choose stewardship - 30 minutes

1. **0-3:** Explain actual versus example local rules and a feasible classroom paper-care need.
2. **3-7:** Model one-card/one-category sorting and three count columns.
3. **7-20:** Each child sorts/directs all twelve cards, checks the 4/4/4 allocation and represents it. Teams propose reusing four scrap-paper cards instead of treating them as trash; represent example trash count 8 -> 4 without claiming real measured savings.
4. **20-26:** Each child explains the category rule, total twelve and modeled reduction of four; state a concrete classroom reuse action and its criterion/limit.
5. **26-30:** Count twelve cards, store reusable paper and clean the normal classroom area only. Collect each child's dated evidence, count kit items, return tools and clear tables. Cleanup is part of the period, not time added afterward.

## Questions to ask

- What rule supports your sorting choice?
- What does the modeled waste reduction show and not show?

## Individual evidence and success

**Grade 1:** Sort with the supplied rules, count three groups and show a reuse action.

**Grade 2:** Explain 4 + 4 + 4 = 12 and modeled 8 - 4 = 4, distinguish example data from actual waste and propose a feasible need/limit.

Teacher visits three table teams during each of the first three four-minute blocks of practice (7-19), listening to or observing each member briefly; use the remaining practice minute and 20-26 for unfinished checks and comparison. At 25, this is nine table visits, **not 25 presentations**. Where a meeting specifies waves, use its supervised wave/check schedule instead of simultaneous table visits. Children keep separate records and partners may not answer for each other. Score each stated criterion **not yet / prompted / independent**; record absent/unobserved evidence as such. A group product, attendance, attractive art or a certificate is not individual mastery. No real annual waste reduction or official local acceptance is invented from the model.

## Support and challenge

**Support / nonreader access:** Large category icons, teacher-read local rule and picture quantities. Adult scribing, pointing, tactile/large pieces and a seated partner-directed action can show understanding; do not score motor speed. Record who actually operated hardware/software: a partner's operation does not establish the child's independent operation.

**Challenge:** Explain how a changed local acceptance rule would change counts without changing the total items. Do not add an unprepared activity or extend the native period.

## If things go wrong / indoor alternative

School recycling rule unavailable: use labeled examples for math/model evidence and keep real disposal unchanged. No printing: teacher-drawn cards on allocated paper suffice.

**Indoor:** The complete primary lesson is indoors at tables; Minnesota weather, internet access and daylight are not prerequisites.

## Family copy - optional, no routine homework

We used local-rule sorting and counted models to choose responsible classroom reuse. Ask: "What rule supports your sorting choice?" Optional: discuss one familiar reuse possibility without collecting waste. No purchase, donation, device, home teaching or required take-home project.
