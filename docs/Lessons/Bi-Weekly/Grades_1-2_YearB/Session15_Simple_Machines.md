## Before class

1. Assign teams of at most three: **4 / 5 / 7 / 9** kits for **10 / 15 / 20 / 25** pupils. Keep work at tables; distribute materials, not roaming station rotations.
2. Set one cardboard ramp using a 5 cm support and a second sloped length using both cardboard pieces overlapping. Mark the same top height, measure path lengths before class and keep difference within 20 cm. Pretest a wide stick lever over the other low support, lifting a broad block only 1 cm within the tray.
3. Put two plain-paper sheets per child per meeting and crayons at tables. Draw this record on the board: **ramp path lengths / difference / lever input-output**. Children copy boxes/icons, not paragraphs. Teacher dates and saves each child's record between meetings.
4. Read the SAFETY section, pretest the actual task with one issued kit and put a roster beside the timer. Demonstrate orally with objects; read every direction aloud. No independent reading, Year A completion or family donation is a prerequisite.

## Vocabulary and teacher background

Ramp = sloped path; lever = rigid tool turning on support; path = traveled length; tradeoff = gain/cost.

A longer, gentler ramp can reduce required effort but adds distance. We measure path lengths here, not force/energy. The lever lifts slowly; it is not a catapult.

**Common misconception:** Machines create energy or always make every part of work easier.

**If asked:** "Can we flick the lever?" No; gently press to lift the block about 1 cm, then lower it.

## SAFETY

No launches, marbles, heavy loads, high ramps or snapping rulers. Wide stick lever is contained in tray; fingers clear of support. Keep aisles/exits clear. Stop for damaged materials; do not improvise a hazardous replacement. No student handles loose batteries or teacher cutting tools.

## Meeting 1: Ramps and gentle lift - 30 minutes

1. **0-3:** State a helper need: moving/lifting a broad block safely on a tabletop.
2. **3-7:** Model measuring both ramp paths from common endpoints and gently operating the bounded lever.
3. **7-20:** Each child guides the block along both ramp paths, records lengths using the same units and tests/directs a gentle 1 cm lever lift. Partners check kept-same height and slow handling.
4. **20-26:** Each child compares the two lengths, shows their difference with drawn units and identifies ramp/lever jobs; no measured force claim is made.
5. **26-30:** Lower loads, return supports/stick/ruler, save comparison records and clear trays. Collect each child's dated evidence, count kit items, return tools and clear tables. Cleanup is part of the period, not time added afterward.

## Questions to ask

- What is the distance tradeoff between your two ramps?
- What did your gentle lever input do?

## Individual evidence and success

**Grade 1:** Compare path lengths and demonstrate/describe a safe lever lift.

**Grade 2:** Calculate/explain the path-length difference within 20 using units and distinguish measured distance from unmeasured effort.

Teacher visits three table teams during each of the first three four-minute blocks of practice (7-19), listening to or observing each member briefly; use the remaining practice minute and 20-26 for unfinished checks and comparison. At 25, this is nine table visits, **not 25 presentations**. Where a meeting specifies waves, use its supervised wave/check schedule instead of simultaneous table visits. Children keep separate records and partners may not answer for each other. Score each stated criterion **not yet / prompted / independent**; record absent/unobserved evidence as such. A group product, attendance, attractive art or a certificate is not individual mastery. Use the grade-level criterion, not handwriting, reading speed or religious worth.

## Support and challenge

**Support / nonreader access:** Equal-unit strips and adult-stabilized lever, child directs the input/output. Adult scribing, pointing, tactile/large pieces and a seated partner-directed action can show understanding; do not score motor speed. Record who actually operated hardware/software: a partner's operation does not establish the child's independent operation.

**Challenge:** Explain why a longer path alone does not prove the exact force needed. Do not add an unprepared activity or extend the native period.

## If things go wrong / indoor alternative

Ramps sag: overlap/stiffen cardboard before issue and remark actual lengths. No safe lever support/stick: compare ramps only and record lever operation unassessed; never improvise a launcher.

**Indoor:** The complete primary lesson is indoors at tables; Minnesota weather, internet access and daylight are not prerequisites.

## Family copy - optional, no routine homework

We compared ramp distances and explored a gentle lever as useful classroom tools. Ask: "What is the distance tradeoff between your two ramps?" Optional: identify a pictured ramp or lever; no construction or lifting task required. No purchase, donation, device, home teaching or required take-home project.
