## Before class

1. Assign teams of at most three: **4 / 5 / 7 / 9** kits for **10 / 15 / 20 / 25** pupils. Keep work at tables; distribute materials, not roaming station rotations.
2. Check actual robot model/app and charge; aim/heading calibration must match each lane before runs. Pretest a low-speed brief roll with Stop. Mark three clear 1 m indoor lanes with start/10 cm-wide goal zone and metric endpoint line. Test finite Repeat 2 only as an extension if the installed model supports it.
3. Put two plain-paper sheets per child per meeting and crayons at tables. Draw this record on the board: **predicted endpoint / two actual measurements / changed parameter**. Children copy boxes/icons, not paragraphs. Teacher dates and saves each child's record between meetings.
4. Read the SAFETY section, pretest the actual task with one issued kit and put a roster beside the timer. Demonstrate orally with objects; read every direction aloud. No independent reading, Year A completion or family donation is a prerequisite.

## Vocabulary and teacher background

Heading = travel direction; parameter = adjustable value; debug = test a correction.

Travel is actual measured output, not commanded duration alone. Reset start/heading and change only one parameter before comparison.

**Common misconception:** A good group endpoint proves all members programmed, or paper/hardware paths are equivalent.

**If asked:** "Did it go farther because gravity grew stronger?" No; we compare a changed code setting on the same floor.

## SAFETY

Low speed in dry lanes, no riding, added markers, collisions or crossing exits; adult handles chargers. Keep aisles/exits clear. Stop for damaged materials; do not improvise a hazardous replacement. No student handles loose batteries or teacher cutting tools.

## Meeting 1: Measured mission recheck - 30 minutes

1. **0-3:** Recheck Run/Stop and show the goal criterion; recall safe resource sharing.
2. **3-7:** Model stored Start -> low-speed Roll -> Stop, predict endpoint, measure, reset/aim and change duration only.
3. **7-20:** Two four-minute waves for 10/15 pupils or three for 20/25, three teams per wave. Each child gets about 40 seconds to change/predict/Run/Stop/identify a result; waiting teams trace cards and draw two endpoint bars. Teacher checks individual actions during waves.
4. **20-26:** Each child compares an actual first/revised endpoint record, explains the changed parameter and kept-same conditions, and returns shared equipment carefully.
5. **26-30:** Stop/park robots, retain local projects, count cards and remove lane tape. Collect each child's dated evidence, count kit items, return tools and clear tables. Cleanup is part of the period, not time added afterward.

## Questions to ask

- How did the two measured endpoints compare?
- What did you keep the same when debugging?

## Individual evidence and success

**Grade 1:** Individually Run/Stop a stored program and identify a changed block/result.

**Grade 2:** Represent two measured endpoints with the same units, explain the one-parameter correction and a kept-same condition.

Teacher visits three table teams during each of the first three four-minute blocks of practice (7-19), listening to or observing each member briefly; use the remaining practice minute and 20-26 for unfinished checks and comparison. At 25, this is nine table visits, **not 25 presentations**. Where a meeting specifies waves, use its supervised wave/check schedule instead of simultaneous table visits. Children keep separate records and partners may not answer for each other. Score each stated criterion **not yet / prompted / independent**; record absent/unobserved evidence as such. A group product, attendance, attractive art or a certificate is not individual mastery. No paper execution, group success or unsupported loop trace is called physical robot mastery.

## Support and challenge

**Support / nonreader access:** Starter script, oral directions and large block pictures; partner resets under child direction. Adult scribing, pointing, tactile/large pieces and a seated partner-directed action can show understanding; do not score motor speed. Record who actually operated hardware/software: a partner's operation does not establish the child's independent operation.

**Challenge:** Trace a supported finite Repeat 2 and compare expected/actual output, recording loop execution only after a device turn. Do not add an unprepared activity or extend the native period.

## If things go wrong / indoor alternative

Only one/two/no complete kits: do feasible individual turns and record remaining hardware evidence unassessed. App labels differ: adult matches stored Roll/Stop controls in preflight; manual joystick driving does not substitute. No safe floor space: cards only.

**Indoor:** Marked indoor lanes are the primary route; no weather/outdoor run is required.

## Family copy - optional, no routine homework

We rechecked stored Sphero code, measured endpoints and explained a debugging change. Ask: "How did the two measured endpoints compare?" Optional: compare two paper-token routes verbally. No purchase, donation, device, home teaching or required take-home project.
