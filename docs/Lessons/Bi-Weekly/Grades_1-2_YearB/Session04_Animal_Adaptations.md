## Before class

1. Assign teams of at most three: **4 / 5 / 7 / 9** kits for **10 / 15 / 20 / 25** pupils. Keep work at tables; distribute materials, not roaming station rotations.
2. Prepare equal-size silhouettes and high-contrast/matching backgrounds. Set the same viewing distance and five-second looking time. Read: "Some animals' colors/patterns can make them less visible in a habitat; this classroom model tests visibility, not survival or how traits arose." No camel-hump or species-history anecdotes are needed.
3. Put two plain-paper sheets per child per meeting and crayons at tables. Draw this record on the board: **background / number noticed out of three / model limitation**. Children copy boxes/icons, not paragraphs. Teacher dates and saves each child's record between meetings.
4. Read the SAFETY section, pretest the actual task with one issued kit and put a roster beside the timer. Demonstrate orally with objects; read every direction aloud. No independent reading, Year A completion or family donation is a prerequisite.

## Vocabulary and teacher background

Feature = characteristic; camouflage = reduced visibility against surroundings; model = representation.

Shape, background, lighting and observer affect detection. Actual adaptations develop across populations; children drawing a trait do not engineer a living species.

**Common misconception:** An animal chooses a new inherited feature instantly, or camel humps store water.

**If asked:** "Can our test prove an animal survives?" No; it compares noticing silhouettes, not feeding, predators or survival.

## SAFETY

Large dry silhouettes; no live animals, food, small beads or outdoor capture. Keep aisles/exits clear. Stop for damaged materials; do not improvise a hazardous replacement. No student handles loose batteries or teacher cutting tools.

## Meeting 1: Counted visibility model - 30 minutes

1. **0-3:** Discuss a visible feature and a labeled model, distinguishing a prediction from a result.
2. **3-7:** Model placing three same-color silhouettes on each background, giving five seconds and recording number noticed out of three.
3. **7-20:** Rotate arranger/observer/recorder; each child tries both backgrounds at the same distance/time. Record actual counts, then draw an animal representation with a purposeful blending feature and label it MODEL.
4. **20-26:** Each child compares the two count records, represents an observed model feature and explains a limit; no guaranteed camouflage result is assigned.
5. **26-30:** Count six silhouettes and two background squares; save individual count/model pages. Collect each child's dated evidence, count kit items, return tools and clear tables. Cleanup is part of the period, not time added afterward.

## Questions to ask

- How did the two visibility counts compare?
- What can this model not tell us about a living animal?

## Individual evidence and success

**Grade 1:** Count noticed silhouettes in two trials and show a model feature.

**Grade 2:** Interpret the two records, name a kept-same viewing condition and distinguish model visibility from survival or inherited change.

Teacher visits three table teams during each of the first three four-minute blocks of practice (7-19), listening to or observing each member briefly; use the remaining practice minute and 20-26 for unfinished checks and comparison. At 25, this is nine table visits, **not 25 presentations**. Where a meeting specifies waves, use its supervised wave/check schedule instead of simultaneous table visits. Children keep separate records and partners may not answer for each other. Score each stated criterion **not yet / prompted / independent**; record absent/unobserved evidence as such. A group product, attendance, attractive art or a certificate is not individual mastery. Use the grade-level criterion, not handwriting, reading speed or religious worth.

## Support and challenge

**Support / nonreader access:** Large high-contrast/tactile outlines; an adult describes actual visible features. Sensory access route may model positions without claiming visual detection evidence. Adult scribing, pointing, tactile/large pieces and a seated partner-directed action can show understanding; do not score motor speed.

**Challenge:** Predict the reversed color/background test and explain why a fair comparison keeps silhouette size fixed. Do not add an unprepared activity or extend the native period.

## If things go wrong / indoor alternative

Counts tie: report honestly, discuss contrast/observer limits, not alter the recorded result. No colored stock: crayon-shaded allocated paper works; label the actual setup.

**Indoor:** The complete primary lesson is indoors at tables; Minnesota weather, internet access and daylight are not prerequisites.

## Family copy - optional, no routine homework

We counted visibility in a camouflage model and explained its limits for living animals. Ask: "How did the two visibility counts compare?" Optional: notice blending/contrast in a picture, without animal handling. No purchase, donation, device, home teaching or required take-home project.
