## Before class / supplies
1. Confirm real inventory/loan and school permissions. Check the exact model supports chosen blocks; do not assume Mini/BOLT features identical.
2. In Blocks test light-color -> roll at heading 0 for 0.5-1 second at low speed -> stop; write model-specific working settings on board. Aim heading 0 away from children.
3. Mark dry 1 m floor bays; prepare light/roll/stop picture cards, tabletop tokens and safe stop signal.
- **Per student:** half-sheet, crayon, individual roster check.
- **Per team of 2-3:** one robot/tablet kit and picture cards/token.
- **Class:** floor tape, timer, board, storage trays.
- **Teacher-only:** chargers, installed help and model screening. No attached pens.

| Students | Teams / simultaneous kits | Papers |

| 10 | 4 | 10 |
| 15 | 5 | 15 |
| 20 | 7 | 20 |

If only three kits exist, at 25 supervise one station with three 4-minute waves of three teams; off-device teams predict cards. With one kit, record unobserved individual runs and repeat assessment later. Do not infer mastery from watching another child's robot.

## Vocabulary / background
Sequence = instructions in order; program = stored instructions; input/output = code/result; aim = set direction reference; debug = fix a mismatch.
Manual Drive mode is remote control. A connected stored sequence that executes hands-off is programming. Floor, charge and aim influence travel.
**Misconception:** every rolling robot run matches an exact path. Observe before promising accuracy.
**If asked "Are we experts now?"** We are beginning programmers; evidence shows specific skills, not expertise.

## Exact numbered sequence (30 minutes)
1. **0-4:** Show robot, purpose and care/turn rule. Optional original prayer asks for wise tool use.
2. **4-7:** Children predict color then forward movement using cards/token, orally or by pointing.
3. **7-11:** Teacher contrasts Drive with a stored Blocks sequence; demonstrate aim, Run and Stop.
4. **11-23:** Each child predicts and executes starter, changes a color or roll duration and reruns. Roles rotate after each run; teacher records each child's observed actions.
5. **23-26:** Individual exit: point to two ordered actions, name an output and one change; identify safe equipment care.
6. **26-30:** Cleanup.

## Questions / measurable evidence
What is stored before Run? Why aim again? What changed after your edit? How did you protect shared equipment?
**Grade 1:** order two action cards, personally Run/Stop under supervision and point to changed output. **Grade 2:** assemble at least two stored actions, predict, change a block and explain mismatch or change. Record **not observed / prompted / independent** for each criterion; care assessed through an action, not a saint fact.

## If things go wrong / indoor fallback
Connection failure: check selected robot/charge once, switch after two minutes. Token alternative: arrange two cards, have partner follow, fix one intentionally wrong card. Record algorithm/debug evidence only; **no real robot operation/programming mastery**. Mis-aim: stop/re-aim/shorten movement. All work indoors, no winter/outdoor condition.

## SAFETY / support / challenge
Low-speed marked bays; stop if out of area; no chasing/racing or holding running robots. Teacher handles chargers and small removable model components. Dry floor only, no art paint/markers attached to ball.
Support: picture starter, narration, large cards, adult scribing and accessible tabletop token; Grade 1 needs no independent reading. Challenge: Grade 2 adds light/wait action or compares two durations, not expert mazes.

## Cleanup / family / sources
Stop/power down according to model guide, count kits, return trays, remove lesson tape; teacher handles charging.
We contrasted driving with stored programs and tested one changed instruction. Catholic connection: stewardship and fair access. Ask: "What changed in your program?" Optional: give two directions to a paper token; no robot/tablet/purchase or routine homework.
