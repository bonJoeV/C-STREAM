## Before class

1. Assign teams of at most three: **4 / 5 / 7 / 9** kits for **10 / 15 / 20 / 25** pupils. Keep work at tables; distribute materials, not roaming station rotations.
2. Choose quiet body percussion or seated gesture: tap tabletop softly, pat hands softly, rest. Draw four beat spaces and two contrasting sample phrases; no instrument purchases or hymn reproduction.
3. Put two plain-paper sheets per child per meeting and crayons at tables. Draw this record on the board: **four-beat unit / repeated phrase / purposeful revision**. Children copy boxes/icons, not paragraphs. Teacher dates and saves each child's record between meetings.
4. Read the SAFETY section, pretest the actual task with one issued kit and put a roster beside the timer. Demonstrate orally with objects; read every direction aloud. No independent reading, Year A completion or family donation is a prerequisite.

## Vocabulary and teacher background

Beat = steady time pulse; phrase = musical/gesture idea; rest = silence; pattern = organized repeat.

A child-created rhythmic phrase is a musical composition. An Easter hope connection is expressive, not biological evidence; no copyrighted song/quotation is used.

**Common misconception:** Louder music expresses more joy or everyone must sing/clap.

**If asked:** "May my phrase be silent?" Yes, a directed gesture phrase gives pattern/communication evidence; sound performance is recorded separately.

## SAFETY

Quiet volume, no shared mouth instruments, balloons, striking peers or compulsory sound/movement. All actions are seated or child-directed. Keep aisles/exits clear. Stop for damaged materials; do not improvise a hazardous replacement. No student handles loose batteries or teacher cutting tools.

## Meeting 1: Compose hopeful rhythm - 30 minutes

1. **0-3:** Discuss a welcoming/hopeful idea and accessible tap/gesture/rest choices.
2. **3-7:** Model a steady four-beat phrase, repeat it twice and change one beat to express a calmer or livelier idea.
3. **7-20:** Each child lays four icon cards, composes a phrase, performs/directs two repeats and receives one specific audience comment. Revise one beat and record the four-position pattern.
4. **20-26:** Each child identifies the repeat unit, counts beats/rests and explains how the purposeful revision communicates hope while respecting listeners.
5. **26-30:** Count four cards per child, stop performance and save phrase drawings. Collect each child's dated evidence, count kit items, return tools and clear tables. Cleanup is part of the period, not time added afterward.

## Questions to ask

- Which four-beat unit repeats in your phrase?
- Why did you change that beat?

## Individual evidence and success

**Grade 1:** Compose/repeat four positions and identify a purposeful beat/rest choice.

**Grade 2:** Represent the repeated unit, predict its next four positions and explain a musical/gesture revision for audience meaning.

Teacher visits three table teams during each of the first three four-minute blocks of practice (7-19), listening to or observing each member briefly; use the remaining practice minute and 20-26 for unfinished checks and comparison. At 25, this is nine table visits, **not 25 presentations**. Where a meeting specifies waves, use its supervised wave/check schedule instead of simultaneous table visits. Children keep separate records and partners may not answer for each other. Score each stated criterion **not yet / prompted / independent**; record absent/unobserved evidence as such. A group product, attendance, attractive art or a certificate is not individual mastery. Silence or card direction is not claimed as physical sound production; mathematical pattern and artistic intent remain assessable.

## Support and challenge

**Support / nonreader access:** Two initial action icons plus rest; adult keeps pulse, child points/directs or uses a seated gesture. Adult scribing, pointing, tactile/large pieces and a seated partner-directed action can show understanding; do not score motor speed.

**Challenge:** Compare two phrases with the same beat count but different rest positions. Do not add an unprepared activity or extend the native period.

## If things go wrong / indoor alternative

Noise grows: pause, use silent gesture/card performance and record sound evidence accurately. Motor access: child directs a partner, preserving composition ownership.

**Indoor:** The complete primary lesson is indoors at tables; Minnesota weather, internet access and daylight are not prerequisites.

## Family copy - optional, no routine homework

We composed and revised four-beat hopeful phrases with quiet, inclusive participation. Ask: "Which four-beat unit repeats in your phrase?" Optional: point, gesture or softly tap a short phrase. No purchase, donation, device, home teaching or required take-home project.
