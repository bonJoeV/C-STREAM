## Before class

1. Assign teams of at most three: **4 / 5 / 7 / 9** kits for **10 / 15 / 20 / 25** pupils. Keep work at tables; distribute materials, not roaming station rotations.
2. Pretest a flexible ruler secured under a book at the table edge, projecting 8 cm then 14 cm into the tray space. Mark both lengths, tape book/ruler securely and confirm a very gentle fingertip pluck produces distinguishable pitches without swinging/snapping. Prepare high/low/rest beat icons.
3. Put two plain-paper sheets per child per meeting and crayons at tables. Draw this record on the board: **free length / observed pitch / four-beat composition**. Children copy boxes/icons, not paragraphs. Teacher dates and saves each child's record between meetings.
4. Read the SAFETY section, pretest the actual task with one issued kit and put a roster beside the timer. Demonstrate orally with objects; read every direction aloud. No independent reading, Year A completion or family donation is a prerequisite.

## Vocabulary and teacher background

Vibration = back-and-forth motion; pitch = high/low sound; volume = loud/soft; rhythm = timed phrase.

A shorter freely vibrating part generally has higher pitch. Change length while using similarly gentle plucks; this is a guided comparison, not precise acoustic measurement.

**Common misconception:** Louder means higher pitch, or a pupa/balloon instrument is needed for music.

**If asked:** "Why can I feel sound?" Sound involves vibration; our strip lets us see/feel a vibration safely without putting anything in mouths.

## SAFETY

No latex balloons, loose rubber bands, mouth-blown instruments or ruler snapping. Keep book/strip flat and stable; pluck minimally with fingers away from edges. Quiet volume, no compulsory loud sound; stop if strip cracks or securing fails. Keep aisles/exits clear. Stop for damaged materials; do not improvise a hazardous replacement. No student handles loose batteries or teacher cutting tools.

## Meeting 1: Two-pitch instrument and rhythm - 30 minutes

1. **0-3:** Introduce quiet high/low/rest choices and ask what will vibrate.
2. **3-7:** Model two gentle plucks at each marked length; predict and record high/low, holding material and gentle technique consistent.
3. **7-20:** Each child directs/tries both lengths and records two outcomes. Teams adjust securing if the strip slips and retest. Each child composes four high/low/rest cards and performs or directs two repetitions at quiet volume.
4. **20-26:** Each child distinguishes pitch from volume, explains a securing/test revision and identifies the repeated four-beat musical phrase; peer suggests one purposeful rhythm change.
5. **26-30:** Stop sound, remove tape, return intact ruler/book and count eight beat cards. Collect each child's dated evidence, count kit items, return tools and clear tables. Cleanup is part of the period, not time added afterward.

## Questions to ask

- Which free length made the higher pitch?
- How did your four-beat phrase express your idea?

## Individual evidence and success

**Grade 1:** Compare high/low outcomes and compose/repeat a four-beat phrase.

**Grade 2:** Name the changed length and kept-same material, distinguish pitch/volume and explain an instrument/rhythm revision.

Teacher visits three table teams during each of the first three four-minute blocks of practice (7-19), listening to or observing each member briefly; use the remaining practice minute and 20-26 for unfinished checks and comparison. At 25, this is nine table visits, **not 25 presentations**. Where a meeting specifies waves, use its supervised wave/check schedule instead of simultaneous table visits. Children keep separate records and partners may not answer for each other. Score each stated criterion **not yet / prompted / independent**; record absent/unobserved evidence as such. A group product, attendance, attractive art or a certificate is not individual mastery. A silent card phrase is rhythm evidence, not observed sound or physical instrument mastery.

## Support and challenge

**Support / nonreader access:** Teacher/adult stabilizes the book while child directs length; silent beat cards support sensory access, with sound comparison recorded separately if unobserved. Adult scribing, pointing, tactile/large pieces and a seated partner-directed action can show understanding; do not score motor speed. Record who actually operated hardware/software: a partner's operation does not establish the child's independent operation.

**Challenge:** Change one rhythm position and explain the musical effect without increasing loudness. Do not add an unprepared activity or extend the native period.

## If things go wrong / indoor alternative

No distinguishable pitch: adult rechecks projecting lengths/security before class; record unclear results rather than louder plucks. No safe flexible ruler: quiet body percussion/card composition assesses rhythm only; strip-sound/engineering evidence remains unassessed.

**Indoor:** The complete primary lesson is indoors at tables; Minnesota weather, internet access and daylight are not prerequisites.

## Family copy - optional, no routine homework

We compared safe vibrating lengths and composed quiet four-beat music with accessible participation. Ask: "Which free length made the higher pitch?" Optional: tap or point a quiet four-beat phrase. No purchase, donation, device, home teaching or required take-home project.
