## Before class

1. Assign teams of at most three: **4 / 5 / 7 / 9** kits for **10 / 15 / 20 / 25** pupils. Keep work at tables; distribute materials, not roaming station rotations.
2. Ask teacher/partner audience for a welcoming hopeful symbol, not personal faith disclosures. Precrease a fold guide and state stand-for-10-seconds/readable criteria; keep seasonal interpretation separate.
3. Put two plain-paper sheets per child per meeting and crayons at tables. Draw this record on the board: **intended message / stand test / audience revision**. Children copy boxes/icons, not paragraphs. Teacher dates and saves each child's record between meetings.
4. Read the SAFETY section, pretest the actual task with one issued kit and put a roster beside the timer. Demonstrate orally with objects; read every direction aloud. No independent reading, Year A completion or family donation is a prerequisite.

## Vocabulary and teacher background

Hope = positive expectation; stable = stands; critique = specific helpful feedback.

The standing criterion is engineering; visual meaning is artistic communication. Neither physically tests an Easter belief.

**Common misconception:** A seasonal craft proves a religious event.

**If asked:** "Is this the real Resurrection?" No; it is classroom communication of hope, not a scientific model of that event.

## SAFETY

Dry card only, no sharp punctures, flames, food symbols as edible materials or public identifiers. Keep aisles/exits clear. Stop for damaged materials; do not improvise a hazardous replacement. No student handles loose batteries or teacher cutting tools.

## Meeting 1: Hopeful sign design - 30 minutes

1. **0-3:** Name the audience and hope/welcome purpose.
2. **3-7:** Model a folded tent sign and a 10-second standing/readability check.
3. **7-20:** Each child creates a clear sign, tests standing, revises the base and receives one partner message comment. Author clarifies one visual detail.
4. **20-26:** Each child gives first/revised stand results and explains how the message welcomes its audience; no faith belief is scored.
5. **26-30:** Return unused tape/cardstock and save checked signs/evidence. Collect each child's dated evidence, count kit items, return tools and clear tables. Cleanup is part of the period, not time added afterward.

## Questions to ask

- How does your sign welcome its viewer?
- What did the standing test tell you?

## Individual evidence and success

**Grade 1:** Show a standing test and identify a clearer message feature.

**Grade 2:** Explain a base revision from the test and a separate communication revision from feedback.

Teacher visits three table teams during each of the first three four-minute blocks of practice (7-19), listening to or observing each member briefly; use the remaining practice minute and 20-26 for unfinished checks and comparison. At 25, this is nine table visits, **not 25 presentations**. Where a meeting specifies waves, use its supervised wave/check schedule instead of simultaneous table visits. Children keep separate records and partners may not answer for each other. Score each stated criterion **not yet / prompted / independent**; record absent/unobserved evidence as such. A group product, attendance, attractive art or a certificate is not individual mastery. Use the grade-level criterion, not handwriting, reading speed or religious worth.

## Support and challenge

**Support / nonreader access:** A precreased broad tent and two symbol choices; allow dictated explanation. Adult scribing, pointing, tactile/large pieces and a seated partner-directed action can show understanding; do not score motor speed. Record who actually operated hardware/software: a partner's operation does not establish the child's independent operation.

**Challenge:** Discuss whether a larger picture makes the base less stable and test one change. Do not add an unprepared activity or extend the native period.

## If things go wrong / indoor alternative

Sign falls: widen/lower the fold. Audience unavailable: teacher/partner fulfills the request; outside delivery is not required.

**Indoor:** The complete primary lesson is indoors at tables; Minnesota weather, internet access and daylight are not prerequisites.

## Family copy - optional, no routine homework

We designed hopeful welcome signs with a stable form and clear audience message. Ask: "How does your sign welcome its viewer?" Optional: say a welcoming phrase without a required project. No purchase, donation, device, home teaching or required take-home project.
