## Before class

1. Assign teams of at most three: **4 / 5 / 7 / 9** kits for **10 / 15 / 20 / 25** pupils. Keep work at tables; distribute materials, not roaming station rotations.
2. Choose pictures showing a growing plant and fallen leaf plus a washed smooth stone. Draw three category icons: living/once-living/never-living. Point out that a leaf picture is a representation, not a live sample.
3. Put two plain-paper sheets per child per meeting and crayons at tables. Draw this record on the board: **two details / category and reason / visible feature count**. Children copy boxes/icons, not paragraphs. Teacher dates and saves each child's record between meetings.
4. Read the SAFETY section, pretest the actual task with one issued kit and put a roster beside the timer. Demonstrate orally with objects; read every direction aloud. No independent reading, Year A completion or family donation is a prerequisite.

## Vocabulary and teacher background

Living = has life processes; once-living = came from a living thing; never-living = not formerly alive.

A fallen leaf came from a living plant; a stone is not a dead animal. Movement alone is not a reliable life test.

**Common misconception:** All nonmoving things are nonliving, or a fallen leaf was never alive.

**If asked:** "Is a dormant tree dead?" Not necessarily; one still moment does not establish whether it is alive.

## SAFETY

No tasting, wild collection, unknown plants or Sun-focusing magnifiers; use the screened indoor set. Keep aisles/exits clear. Stop for damaged materials; do not improvise a hazardous replacement. No student handles loose batteries or teacher cutting tools.

## Meeting 1: Notice and sort - 30 minutes

1. **0-3:** Show the three examples and discuss observing without disturbing nature.
2. **3-7:** Model two picture details and a category reason based on origin/life processes, not movement.
3. **7-20:** Each child observes/draws two details, counts visible leaves/features in the selected picture and sorts all three examples. Partner asks for a reason rather than supplies it.
4. **20-26:** Each child points to two details, compares two visible feature counts and explains one category/protective action.
5. **26-30:** Return pictures/stone, save individual drawings and clear tables. Collect each child's dated evidence, count kit items, return tools and clear tables. Cleanup is part of the period, not time added afterward.

## Questions to ask

- Was this example once living or never living?
- Which details did you actually see?

## Individual evidence and success

**Grade 1:** Represent two details, distinguish leaf from stone and count visible features once.

**Grade 2:** Explain all three categories and a count comparison without claiming a picture proves current growth.

Teacher visits three table teams during each of the first three four-minute blocks of practice (7-19), listening to or observing each member briefly; use the remaining practice minute and 20-26 for unfinished checks and comparison. At 25, this is nine table visits, **not 25 presentations**. Where a meeting specifies waves, use its supervised wave/check schedule instead of simultaneous table visits. Children keep separate records and partners may not answer for each other. Score each stated criterion **not yet / prompted / independent**; record absent/unobserved evidence as such. A group product, attendance, attractive art or a certificate is not individual mastery. Use the grade-level criterion, not handwriting, reading speed or religious worth.

## Support and challenge

**Support / nonreader access:** Large category icons and tactile stone; teacher describes pictures for access. Adult scribing, pointing, tactile/large pieces and a seated partner-directed action can show understanding; do not score motor speed.

**Challenge:** Explain why a toy that moves is still never-living. Do not add an unprepared activity or extend the native period.

## If things go wrong / indoor alternative

Plant image unclear: use a simpler drawn model with an explicit label; observation is of the representation. Outdoor cancellation: the whole primary task already works indoors.

**Indoor:** The complete primary lesson is indoors at tables; Minnesota weather, internet access and daylight are not prerequisites.

## Family copy - optional, no routine homework

We noticed nature details, compared visible counts and sorted examples with reasons. Ask: "Was this example once living or never living?" Optional: describe a nature picture without collecting specimens. No purchase, donation, device, home teaching or required take-home project.
