## Before class / supplies
1. On each table place two identical-height books 10 cm apart, measured between inside edges. Test ordinary A4/letter paper across gap; do not promise a flat sheet must fail.
   Make pairs, with one trio at odd enrollment: 5/7/10/12 active teams for 10/15/20/25 pupils. The table budgets 5/8/10/13 kits, retaining one reserve at 15/25.
2. Cut/choose identical paper sheets; prepare equal broad wooden blocks as loads, **maximum five**, one at a time. Do not use pennies or heavy weights.
3. Draw two-result chart (flat/folded). Demonstrate accordion folds; Grade 1 can use a teacher-started fold.
- **Per student:** half-sheet, crayon, individual evidence check.
- **Per pair:** two support books, two paper sheets, five identical broad blocks; ruler shared.
- **Class:** timer, class comparison board; optional bridge pictures.
- **Teacher-only:** scissors if paper needs cutting, model-preflight materials.

At 25 partners test simultaneously on low tables while teacher checks individuals; no 13-group sequential whole-class loading queue.
In a trio, rotate builder, loader and observer so every child predicts, handles a

## Vocabulary / background
Span = gap crossed; support = holds bridge at ends; load = what bridge holds; criterion = success requirement; redesign = change after testing.
Beam bridges carry loads across supports. Folding can stiffen paper, but paper type, span and placement affect results. Model behavior is not proof a real bridge is safe.
**Misconception:** the strongest student's bridge matters most. Same conditions and explaining evidence matter.
**If asked "Is a bridge a Bible verse?"** No. Our relationship metaphor is a teacher illustration; the removed "bridges, not walls" quotation was not Scripture. Optional service reference: Galatians 5:13 in school-approved Bible, wording VERIFICATION REQUIRED.

## Exact numbered sequence (30 minutes)
1. **0-4:** Show a bridge purpose; ask who needs safe access and how a design includes them. Optional original prayer asks for kind cooperation.
2. **4-8:** Demonstrate fixed 10 cm span and loading one equal block at center for five seconds, stopping at five or failure. Clear hands before loading.
3. **8-14:** Partners test flat paper; each predicts and counts/points to result. Record actual supported load before failure, not total dropped blocks.
4. **14-21:** Fold second sheet, keep supports/load alike, test again; swap loader/observer roles. If needed redesign one fold and retest within block.
5. **21-26:** Each child draws/shows a changed feature and compares two results; Grade 2 names what stayed the same. Partner explains who a real bridge serves.
6. **26-30:** Cleanup.

## Questions / success
What stayed the same? Which shape held more, or were they equal? What would you change next? Who benefits from crossing safely?
- **Grade 1:** count/point to two loads, show one change and orally explain a user need; drawing replaces writing.
- **Grade 2:** record two counts, identify same span/load, explain improvement from evidence, not appearance.
- Record **not yet / prompted / independent** per child for comparison, change and criteria. Failed models can still demonstrate learning.

## If things go wrong / indoor fallback
Both hold five: record ceiling "at least five"; narrow paper equally or widen span slightly for a second comparison, labeling changed conditions. Neither holds one: reduce span equally. Missing books: identical boxes. All work indoors with no devices; no extended curing/glue time.

## SAFETY / support / challenge
Models on tables only, no student weight, floor crossings, climbing, heavy loads or loose coins. Broad loads maximum five; hands out during collapse. Never apply model capacity to a real bridge.
Support: prestarted folds, four picture steps, adult scribing and oral count. Challenge: Grade 2 compare two fold patterns under identical criteria, not an unlimited load contest.

## Cleanup / family / sources
Count loads/books, flatten/recycle usable paper, return ruler, retain comparison sketch.
We tested two paper bridge shapes and compared honest load counts. Catholic connection: designs that help neighbors cross safely. Ask: "What changed, and how did you know it helped?" Optional: notice a bridge in a picture or fold scrap paper with an adult; no supplies purchase or routine homework.
