## VOCABULARY / TEACHER BACKGROUND

**Cell:** basic unit of living organisms. **Membrane:** boundary regulating
movement. **Nucleus:** compartment containing much DNA in typical plant/animal
cells. **Base pair:** complementary DNA letters.
**Common misconception:** every cell has a nucleus/DNA.
Bacteria lack membrane-bound nuclei; mature human red blood cells lack nuclei
and DNA. **If asked, "Is DNA a tiny ladder?"** Ladder is a flat model; DNA has
three-dimensional structure and chemistry omitted here.
Complete reference: typical animal cell has membrane, cytoplasm, nucleus,
mitochondria; typical plant cell additionally wall/chloroplasts/large central
vacuole (not every plant cell has chloroplasts). DNA pairing A-T/C-G;
ATCGTAAC -> TAGCATTG, eight pairs. Model letters do not measure actual DNA.

## EXACT LESSON SEQUENCE

1. **0-4 (4 min):** Ask "What does a model leave out?" Introduce honest life/faith distinction.
2. **4-11 (7 min):** Model cell compartments/exceptions and A-T/C-G key.
3. **11-25 (14 min):** Each draws typical cell, labels four shared parts, marks plant differences; pairs eight-letter strand on second sheet.
4. **25-35 (10 min):** Peer checks eight pairs and diagram against reference, not aesthetics; revise one mistaken label and explain exception.
5. **35-41 (6 min):** Individual function of membrane/nucleus, 8-base complement, model limit and natural-process versus faith-symbol statement.
6. **41-45 (4 min):** Save models, cap/count tools, sort paper and tidy.

## QUESTIONS TO ASK STUDENTS / WHAT SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE

"Does every plant cell photosynthesize? What pairs with C? Is this observation?"
Each has four correct shared labels, at least 7/8 pairs with correction,
one accurate exception/limit and faith distinction. 1 unsupported; 2 prompted;
3 independent; 4 explains another omission; NE for missing.

## IF THINGS GO WRONG / SAFETY

Pairing reversed -> point to key, preserve correction; confusing cell detail ->
four shared parts first. No cardstock -> write letters on paper. No biological
sampling, chemicals, unknown soil/water, food/tasting or microscope claims.
Stop/report unsafe substitute equipment or disclosure.

## SUPPORT / CHALLENGE / INDOOR FALLBACK

Grade 5/support: preoutlined cells, base key, tactile letters and oral response.
Grade 6/challenge: explain exceptions/compartments and 2D model limits.
Indoor dry-model plan; ecosystem fieldwork not added as unsafe choice.
