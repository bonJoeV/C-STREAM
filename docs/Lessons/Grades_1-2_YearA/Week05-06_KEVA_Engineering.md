## Before class

1. Assign teams of at most three: **4 / 5 / 7 / 9** kits for **10 / 15 / 20 / 25** pupils. Keep work at tables; distribute materials, not roaming station rotations.
2. Inspect equal planks for splinters. Mark a 10 cm bridge gap between two supports. Pretest a low tower and span using three broad blocks; model a 30 cm height ceiling.
3. Put two plain-paper sheets per child per meeting and crayons at tables. Draw this record on the board: **plan or prediction / actual result / reason or revision**. Children copy boxes/icons, not paragraphs. Teacher dates and saves each child's record between meetings.
4. Read the SAFETY section, pretest the actual task with one issued kit and put a roster beside the timer. Demonstrate orally with objects; read every direction aloud. No independent reading, Year A completion or family donation is a prerequisite.

## Vocabulary and teacher background

Base = bottom support; stable = stays standing; span = gap crossed; redesign = change after a test.

A broad base often helps stability, but overlap and alignment also matter. Equal loads and an unchanged span make comparisons interpretable.

**Common misconception:** Taller always means better, or a failure means the builder failed.

**If asked:** "Was Noah mocked while building?" No story detail is needed; today we study tested structures and responsible teamwork, not an unsourced Bible anecdote.

## SAFETY

Tabletop builds under 30 cm; no standing on furniture, heavy loads or hands under collapsing structures. Keep aisles/exits clear. Stop for damaged materials; do not improvise a hazardous replacement. No student handles loose batteries or teacher cutting tools.

## Meeting 1: Stable towers - 30 minutes

1. **0-3:** Show a stable low tower; state criterion: stand 10 seconds below 30 cm.
2. **3-7:** Model counting equal plank layers and measuring from tabletop; compare narrow and broad bases.
3. **7-20:** Teams build with 24 planks, test 10 seconds, record height and change one base feature. Every child adds or tests a part.
4. **20-26:** Retest at the same height where possible; each child points to the changed feature and compares pass/fall records.
5. **26-30:** Remove loads first, then dismantle from the top and count 24 planks and three loads. Collect each child's dated evidence, count kit items, return tools and clear tables. Cleanup is part of the period, not time added afterward.

## Meeting 2: Supported crossing - 30 minutes

1. **0-3:** Recall the base test; show the fixed 10 cm gap.
2. **3-7:** Model overlap on both supports and adding one broad block at a time, maximum three.
3. **7-20:** Teams build a plank bridge, test three blocks for 5 seconds, change overlap/support arrangement and retest with the same blocks. Each child counts one trial.
4. **20-26:** Each child states two load results and a support change; distinguish span from tower height.
5. **26-30:** Remove loads first, then dismantle from the top and count 24 planks and three loads. Collect each child's dated evidence, count kit items, return tools and clear tables. Cleanup is part of the period, not time added afterward.

## Questions to ask

- Which change helped your structure in the test?
- What did we keep the same?

## Individual evidence and success

**Grade 1:** Show a tested feature, count layers or supported loads and identify a change.

**Grade 2:** Give two comparable counts/measurements with units and explain how keeping height/span/load conditions steady supports the comparison.

Teacher visits three table teams during each of the first three four-minute blocks of practice (7-19), listening to or observing each member briefly; use the remaining practice minute and 20-26 for unfinished checks and comparison. At 25, this is nine table visits, **not 25 presentations**. Where a meeting specifies waves, use its supervised wave/check schedule instead of simultaneous table visits. Children keep separate records and partners may not answer for each other. Score each stated criterion **not yet / prompted / independent**; record absent/unobserved evidence as such. A group product, attendance, attractive art or a certificate is not individual mastery. Use the grade-level criterion, not handwriting, reading speed or religious worth.

## Support and challenge

**Support / nonreader access:** Use a demonstrated low base and large blocks; partner steadies the ruler while the child directs measurement. Adult scribing, pointing, tactile/large pieces and a seated partner-directed action can show understanding; do not score motor speed.

**Challenge:** Try the same-height tower with fewer planks and discuss the resource tradeoff. Do not add an unprepared activity or extend the native period.

## If things go wrong / indoor alternative

No branded planks: use equal screened wooden blocks and adjust the demonstrated low span before class; record the actual span. Immediate collapse: align bases and reduce height, not add heavy ballast.

**Indoor:** The complete primary lesson is indoors at tables; Minnesota weather, internet access and daylight are not prerequisites.

## Family copy - optional, no routine homework

We tested towers and bridges, compared counts and reused materials responsibly. Ask: "Which change helped your structure in the test?" Optional: point to a support in a picture of a building. No purchase, donation, device, home teaching or required take-home project.
