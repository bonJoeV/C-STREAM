## Before class

1. Assign teams of at most three: **4 / 5 / 7 / 9** kits for **10 / 15 / 20 / 25** pupils. Keep work at tables; distribute materials, not roaming station rotations.
2. Open ScratchJr offline, create one local practice project per team and test Home > New (+), character selection, yellow Green Flag start, blue Move Right, red End, flag Run and Stop. Prepare six picture block cards. Use a single scene, not two text-heavy scenes.
3. Put two plain-paper sheets per child per meeting and crayons at tables. Draw this record on the board: **intended message / predicted movement / run result and changed block**. Children copy boxes/icons, not paragraphs. Teacher dates and saves each child's record between meetings.
4. Read the SAFETY section, pretest the actual task with one issued kit and put a roster beside the timer. Demonstrate orally with objects; read every direction aloud. No independent reading, Year A completion or family donation is a prerequisite.

## Vocabulary and teacher background

Sequence = ordered blocks; run = execute; debug = correct a mismatch; audience = viewer.

A character follows stored blocks, not the coder's spoken wishes. A single scene leaves time for each child's hands-on turn.

**Common misconception:** Manual dragging the character is the same as running code.

**If asked:** "Must I type thank you?" No; draw a thank-you symbol and tell its meaning. No identifying text is required.

## SAFETY

Tablets stay on dry tables; no photos, names or uploads. Keep volume muted. Keep aisles/exits clear. Stop for damaged materials; do not improvise a hazardous replacement. No student handles loose batteries or teacher cutting tools.

## Meeting 1: One-scene thank-you - 30 minutes

1. **0-3:** Choose a school helper and a visual thank-you idea; predict a character's motion.
2. **3-7:** Model a connected Green Flag -> Move Right 2 -> End script. Run, reset character to the same start, then change 2 to 1 and rerun.
3. **7-20:** Each team member gets a four-minute tablet turn: choose a character, connect the three blocks, predict, flag-run, reset and change one motion number. Other members draw/predict on cards.
4. **20-26:** Each child shows the executed change and explains a visual choice. Partner offers one specific comment and the author revises the message or placement. Teacher checks during turns, not only this final phase.
5. **26-30:** Return to project Home to retain local work, lock tablets, count cards and clear tables. Collect each child's dated evidence, count kit items, return tools and clear tables. Cleanup is part of the period, not time added afterward.

## Questions to ask

- Which block did you change and what happened?
- How does your picture thank its audience?

## Individual evidence and success

**Grade 1:** Individually connect/run a three-block sequence, point to the changed motion and tell the visual message.

**Grade 2:** Predict the direction/step-count difference, explain the rerun and revise a purposeful visual choice after feedback.

Teacher visits three table teams during each of the first three four-minute blocks of practice (7-19), listening to or observing each member briefly; use the remaining practice minute and 20-26 for unfinished checks and comparison. At 25, this is nine table visits, **not 25 presentations**. Where a meeting specifies waves, use its supervised wave/check schedule instead of simultaneous table visits. Children keep separate records and partners may not answer for each other. Score each stated criterion **not yet / prompted / independent**; record absent/unobserved evidence as such. A group product, attendance, attractive art or a certificate is not individual mastery. Gratitude alone is not evidence for a Catholic competency; a paper plan is not executed code.

## Support and challenge

**Support / nonreader access:** Provide a starter script with one missing block, large picture cards and oral message choices. Adult scribing, pointing, tactile/large pieces and a seated partner-directed action can show understanding; do not score motor speed. Record who actually operated hardware/software: a partner's operation does not establish the child's independent operation.

**Challenge:** Add a Wait block only after explaining the original result; test the timing change. Do not add an unprepared activity or extend the native period.

## If things go wrong / indoor alternative

Missing app or incompatible tablet: use three drawn story panels and block cards; record app execution unassessed. Disconnected blocks: drag until they snap; unintended motion: reset position before rerun.

**Indoor:** The complete primary lesson is indoors at tables; Minnesota weather, internet access and daylight are not prerequisites.

## Family copy - optional, no routine homework

We used a short stored animation to communicate thanks and checked a changed instruction. Ask: "Which block did you change and what happened?" Optional: give two spoken directions to a paper character. No purchase, donation, device, home teaching or required take-home project.
