## Before class

1. Assign teams of at most three: **4 / 5 / 7 / 9** kits for **10 / 15 / 20 / 25** pupils. Keep work at tables; distribute materials, not roaming station rotations.
2. Check secured light battery covers and mirrors. Mark lamp, object and white paper screen locations at 10 cm intervals. Pretest shadow visibility in normal safe room light; never darken exits.
3. Put two plain-paper sheets per child per meeting and crayons at tables. Draw this record on the board: **prediction / two observed positions / explanation**. Children copy boxes/icons, not paragraphs. Teacher dates and saves each child's record between meetings.
4. Read the SAFETY section, pretest the actual task with one issued kit and put a roster beside the timer. Demonstrate orally with objects; read every direction aloud. No independent reading, Year A completion or family donation is a prerequisite.

## Vocabulary and teacher background

Source = makes light; shadow = blocked light region; reflect = redirect light; symbol = represents meaning.

Moving the object changes shadow size when lamp and screen remain fixed. A mirror redirects incoming light; it does not create more light.

**Common misconception:** A mirror or a shadow makes its own light.

**If asked:** "Is being kind measurable like brightness?" We can observe a kind action, but spiritual worth is not measured by a flashlight.

## SAFETY

No flames, lasers, glass, sunlight focusing or beams aimed at eyes. Keep classroom safely lit; teacher alone handles cells. Keep aisles/exits clear. Stop for damaged materials; do not improvise a hazardous replacement. No student handles loose batteries or teacher cutting tools.

## Meeting 1: Shadow size - 30 minutes

1. **0-3:** Identify flashlight as a source and cardboard as an object that blocks light.
2. **3-7:** Model fixed lamp/screen; put cardboard at near and far marked positions and trace shadow edges.
3. **7-20:** Teams test the two positions with the same source/screen. Each child predicts and traces or points to one actual outline; rotate lamp/object/recorder roles.
4. **20-26:** Each child compares near/far shadow outlines and names the kept-same positions; distinguish observation from the hope symbol.
5. **26-30:** Switch off lights, count mirrors/cardboard, store drawings and remove tape from tables. Collect each child's dated evidence, count kit items, return tools and clear tables. Cleanup is part of the period, not time added afterward.

## Meeting 2: Reflection - 30 minutes

1. **0-3:** Retrieve outlines; recall source versus reflected light.
2. **3-7:** Aim light at a mirror toward a paper screen, keeping beams away from faces; tilt slightly to move the spot.
3. **7-20:** Teams compare two mirror angles with lamp fixed. Each child directs an angle and sketches arrows from source to mirror to screen.
4. **20-26:** Each child explains which part redirected light and one model limitation; name a concrete kind action without calling it a light measurement.
5. **26-30:** Switch off lights, count mirrors/cardboard, store drawings and remove tape from tables. Collect each child's dated evidence, count kit items, return tools and clear tables. Cleanup is part of the period, not time added afterward.

## Questions to ask

- What stayed the same in your light test?
- Does the mirror make light or redirect it?

## Individual evidence and success

**Grade 1:** Show blocked light and identify source/mirror using observed results.

**Grade 2:** Compare two shadow conditions, name a kept-same condition and trace source -> mirror -> screen without calling a symbol data.

Teacher visits three table teams during each of the first three four-minute blocks of practice (7-19), listening to or observing each member briefly; use the remaining practice minute and 20-26 for unfinished checks and comparison. At 25, this is nine table visits, **not 25 presentations**. Where a meeting specifies waves, use its supervised wave/check schedule instead of simultaneous table visits. Children keep separate records and partners may not answer for each other. Score each stated criterion **not yet / prompted / independent**; record absent/unobserved evidence as such. A group product, attendance, attractive art or a certificate is not individual mastery. Use the grade-level criterion, not handwriting, reading speed or religious worth.

## Support and challenge

**Support / nonreader access:** Use large cardboard and adult-stabilized light; child directs position and points to outlines. Adult scribing, pointing, tactile/large pieces and a seated partner-directed action can show understanding; do not score motor speed. Record who actually operated hardware/software: a partner's operation does not establish the child's independent operation.

**Challenge:** Repeat a position and discuss why trace outlines may differ. Do not add an unprepared activity or extend the native period.

## If things go wrong / indoor alternative

Weak shadow: teacher adjusts screen position before testing and marks the new fixed setup. No mirrors: repeat shadow evidence; reflection remains unassessed, never use glass.

**Indoor:** The complete primary lesson is indoors at tables; Minnesota weather, internet access and daylight are not prerequisites.

## Family copy - optional, no routine homework

We compared shadows and reflected light, separating observations from a hope symbol. Ask: "What stayed the same in your light test?" Optional: notice a shadow indoors without aiming a light into eyes. No purchase, donation, device, home teaching or required take-home project.
