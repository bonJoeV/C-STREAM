# Weeks 19-22: STREAM Exploration Stations

## Before class
1. Choose one workshop kit per meeting; do not put water/paint/robots out simultaneously.
2. Prepare a picture instruction card showing **plan -> try -> compare -> change**.
3. Assign table teams of 2-3 but give each child a separate evidence page. Grade 1 tells/draws; Grade 2 adds units/reasons.
4. Keep prior science sink/float and robot ideas as **optional future extensions needing separate safety and assessment planning**, not hidden required stations.

## Supplies / scaling
- **Per student per meeting:** half-sheet and crayon.
- **Per team meeting 1:** 20 wooden planks or cardboard blocks, ruler/equal cubes, soft eraser as optional load.
- **Per team meeting 2:** folded paper and crayons (no wet paint required).
- **Per team meeting 3:** 12 pattern blocks or teacher-cut colored paper shapes.
- **Per team meeting 4:** ruler or equal cubes and three accessible classroom objects.
- **Class:** timer, board results chart, reusable trays; tape only if using cardboard.
- **Teacher-only:** roster, kit screening and storage labels.

## Vocabulary / background
Redesign = change after a test; stable = stays standing; symmetry = matching across a line; repeating unit = the part repeated; unit = same-size measuring amount; data = recorded results.
Different hand spans are not interchangeable units. Drawings can show design decisions without writing.
**Misconception:** finishing a passport proves learning. Only the demonstrated workshop criterion does.
**If asked "Why no robots today?"** Tools match the question; we already have separate lessons for actual programming.

## Four exact 30-minute meetings
**Meeting 1: Stable structure**
1. **0-4:** Show challenge: tabletop structure at least five equal blocks tall, stands 10 seconds, height below 30 cm; discuss reusing planks.
2. **4-8:** Model a narrow/wide base and height measurement; each child sketches a possible base.
3. **8-20:** Teams build, test for 10 seconds, change one feature and retest; each child helps one test/measurement.
4. **20-24:** Record two results and show changed base.
5. **24-26:** Individual: explain a change using test evidence.
6. **26-30:** Cleanup.

**Meeting 2: Symmetry studio**
1. **0-4:** Show matching versus nonmatching designs.
2. **4-8:** Fold paper; teacher marks matching points across fold.
3. **8-20:** Each child makes a purposeful symmetric design; partner checks two matching features.
4. **20-24:** Revise one mismatch or explain intentional asymmetry; never equate beauty with perfection.
5. **24-26:** Individual points to line and two matching features.
6. **26-30:** Cleanup.

**Meeting 3: Pattern communication**
1. **0-4:** Recall a two-color AB example.
2. **4-8:** Model repeating unit and intentional broken pattern.
3. **8-20:** Children make two AB units (Grade 1) or three ABB/AAB units (Grade 2), trade predictions and repair one broken pattern.
4. **20-24:** Draw pattern and explain what comes next.
5. **24-26:** Individual identifies unit and predicts next two shapes.
6. **26-30:** Cleanup.

**Meeting 4: Measurement gallery**
1. **0-4:** Choose one object to compare; recall equal units.
2. **4-8:** Teacher models no gaps/overlaps from endpoint.
3. **8-20:** Children measure three objects with same units; Grade 2 nearest whole cm if ready; record/draw lengths.
4. **20-24:** Compare longest/shortest and represent results as aligned strips.
5. **24-26:** Individual demonstrates measuring start and justifies one comparison.
6. **26-30:** Cleanup.

## Questions / success
What did your test show? Which part repeats? Did units have gaps? Why did you save usable materials?
Grade 1 uses oral explanations and sketches, two matching features, AB prediction, correct endpoint and longer/shorter comparisons. Grade 2 gives measured two-trial structure results, ABB/AAB rule and three labeled comparable lengths with reasoning.
Teacher records **not yet / prompted / independent** for each meeting's stated criterion and care action; do not assign science/robot mastery from unrelated products.

## If things go wrong / indoor fallback
No planks: fold cardboard blocks or make a low paper structure; same standing criterion. Limited rulers: use equal paper strips. No pattern blocks: colored paper shapes. All four workshops work indoors with no devices; no hidden loan requirement. Early finishers revise/explain rather than visit unprepared stations.

## SAFETY / support / challenge
Structures below 30 cm; hands out before collapse test; no heavy top loads or furniture climbing. Screen recycled materials; no glass, exposed staples or wet materials. Keep aisles clear and tools age appropriate.
Support: prebuilt base, thick blocks, picture steps, partner stabilization and adult scribing. Challenge: Grade 2 defend a design tradeoff or compare repeated measurements; maintain individual evidence.

## Cleanup / family / sources
Count and sort kit parts; return tools, recycle scraps, store each child's labeled evidence. Four-minute cleanup is already counted.
**Family snippet:** Over four workshops we tested structures, revised symmetry and patterns, and measured with equal units. Catholic connection: responsible reuse and respect. Ask: "What did you change after checking your work?" Optional: find a pattern or compare two familiar objects orally; no purchase/device or routine homework.
