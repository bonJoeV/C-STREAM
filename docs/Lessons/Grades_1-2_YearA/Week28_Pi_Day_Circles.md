## Before class / supplies
1. Choose two smooth round lids about 8 cm and 12 cm across (neither wider than 15 cm), so their boundaries fit the 60 cm yarn. Remove sharp metal edges. Trace a square to compare.
2. Cut 60 cm lengths of yarn; prepare one equal-size block chain per team. Demonstrate wrapping yarn once with no overlap or stretching.
3. Draw AB and AAB circle patterns on the board. Do not use food rewards.
- **Per student:** one sheet and crayon.
- **Per team of 2-3:** two round lids, two yarn lengths, 20 equal blocks about 2 cm wide (or 20 equal 2 cm paper squares) and scrap paper for comparing laid-out yarn. Check that the unit chain spans the longer boundary before class.
- **Class:** tape and board, circle/square examples; reusable lids/yarn.
- **Teacher-only:** scissors for precutting yarn; optional ruler to show approximation.

## Vocabulary / teacher background
Circle = flat round shape; circumference = distance around its boundary; diameter = distance across through the center; approximate = close, not exact; repeating unit = part that repeats.
Pi is a **finite** number about 3.14, the ratio of circumference to diameter. Its decimal representation does not terminate or repeat. Endless digits do not mean infinite magnitude. No ratio calculation is required of Grades 1-2.
**Misconception:** every round-looking object is a flat circle. A ball is a sphere; its flat outline may look circular.
**If asked "Is pi as big as God?"** No. Mathematics describes a finite quantity; a devotional analogy is not a mathematical or theological measurement.

## Exact numbered sequence (30 minutes)
1. **0-4:** Find a circle versus square. Explain Pi Day's 3/14 date briefly without digit recitation or theology claims.
2. **4-8:** Model wrapping yarn around one lid, marking the join, laying it flat and comparing to the same equal blocks.
3. **8-18:** Team members each help measure both lids. Align yarn starts; predict then compare. Grade 2 counts equal blocks, including "a little more than" if needed; Grade 1 points to longer/shorter. Repeat once to notice small measurement differences.
4. **18-23:** Each child traces circles in an intentional AB (Grade 1) or AAB/ABB (Grade 2) pattern. Partner extends it and explains the repeating unit.
5. **23-26:** Individual exit: identify the longer boundary, explain one measurement step and point to the repeating unit; say why a measure may be approximate.
6. **26-30:** Cleanup.

## Questions / success
Which yarn is longer when starts line up? What made our two attempts differ? What part of your pattern repeats?
- **Grade 1:** correctly compare two boundaries; demonstrate lining up endpoints; create two complete AB units and predict the next circle.
- **Grade 2:** record two approximate counts in the same units and justify comparison; create/extend three units of AAB or ABB; identify one uncertainty.
- Teacher records **not yet / prompted / independent** for comparison, measurement method, pattern and honest uncertainty. No coding credit from circle artwork.

## If things go wrong / indoor fallback
Slipping yarn: tape the starting end or teacher models a paper strip. No lids: use two teacher-drawn circles and paper strips; describe these as drawings. No equal blocks: use identical paper squares. Entire lesson works indoors without devices. Early finishers compare a third circle, not speed.

## SAFETY
No food/treats, loose coins, sharp can lids, neck loops or strings tied to students. Precut yarn; teacher manages scissors. Accessible tabletop work only.

## Support / challenge / cleanup
Support: pretraced circles, oral prompts and partner stabilization without taking the child's decision. Grade 1 responds by pointing/drawing.
Challenge: Grade 2 compare around/across lengths informally; no required division.
Cleanup: count reusable lids/blocks, coil yarn into bags, recycle scraps and retain each child's pattern.
