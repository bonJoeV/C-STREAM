## Before class

1. Assign teams of at most three: **4 / 5 / 7 / 9** kits for **10 / 15 / 20 / 25** pupils. Keep work at tables; distribute materials, not roaming station rotations.
2. Precut one strip per pupil; ask a classroom reader/helper for an encouraging bookmark with no raised decorations. Draw page-marking and flatness icons.
3. Put two plain-paper sheets per child per meeting and crayons at tables. Draw this record on the board: **plan or prediction / actual result / reason or revision**. Children copy boxes/icons, not paragraphs. Teacher dates and saves each child's record between meetings.
4. Read the SAFETY section, pretest the actual task with one issued kit and put a roster beside the timer. Demonstrate orally with objects; read every direction aloud. No independent reading, Year A completion or family donation is a prerequisite.

## Vocabulary and teacher background

Recipient = intended user; function = job; feedback = specific helpful comment.

A bookmark must identify a page without damaging it; flatness and visible contrast matter.

**Common misconception:** More glued decorations always make a better gift.

**If asked:** "Do I have to give it away?" No; a classroom reader can test it and you may keep your example.

## SAFETY

Teacher precuts; no beads, glitter, staples or personal/private messages. Keep aisles/exits clear. Stop for damaged materials; do not improvise a hazardous replacement. No student handles loose batteries or teacher cutting tools.

## Meeting 1: Functional encouragement - 30 minutes

1. **0-3:** State recipient request and kindness purpose.
2. **3-7:** Demonstrate placing the strip halfway into a book, closing gently and finding the same page.
3. **7-20:** Each child plans a clear symbol/message, colors the flat strip and tests in the shared book. Partners give one comment about readability; each author revises one detail.
4. **20-26:** Each child shows the page-marking criterion and explains how a design choice fits the recipient, without ranking gifts.
5. **26-30:** Return books and unused cardstock; retain the individual drawing/check page. Collect each child's dated evidence, count kit items, return tools and clear tables. Cleanup is part of the period, not time added afterward.

## Questions to ask

- How does your bookmark help its reader?
- What did you revise after feedback?

## Individual evidence and success

**Grade 1:** Show a successful page mark and tell one purposeful picture choice.

**Grade 2:** Explain both the functional constraint and audience choice; show a revision following specific feedback.

Teacher visits three table teams during each of the first three four-minute blocks of practice (7-19), listening to or observing each member briefly; use the remaining practice minute and 20-26 for unfinished checks and comparison. At 25, this is nine table visits, **not 25 presentations**. Where a meeting specifies waves, use its supervised wave/check schedule instead of simultaneous table visits. Children keep separate records and partners may not answer for each other. Score each stated criterion **not yet / prompted / independent**; record absent/unobserved evidence as such. A group product, attendance, attractive art or a certificate is not individual mastery. Use the grade-level criterion, not handwriting, reading speed or religious worth.

## Support and challenge

**Support / nonreader access:** Offer two symbol choices, a broad strip and oral dictation; use partner book handling. Adult scribing, pointing, tactile/large pieces and a seated partner-directed action can show understanding; do not score motor speed.

**Challenge:** Compare two contrast choices against the same readability criterion. Do not add an unprepared activity or extend the native period.

## If things go wrong / indoor alternative

Strip too short for a book: use a smaller issued book rather than add craft decorations. Recipient unavailable: teacher plays a reader using the stated request.

**Indoor:** The complete primary lesson is indoors at tables; Minnesota weather, internet access and daylight are not prerequisites.

## Family copy - optional, no routine homework

We designed flat bookmarks with a recipient in mind and revised clear messages. Ask: "How does your bookmark help its reader?" Optional: discuss a helpful message you could say to a reader. No purchase, donation, device, home teaching or required take-home project.
