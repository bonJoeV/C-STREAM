## Before class

1. Assign teams of at most three: **4 / 5 / 7 / 9** kits for **10 / 15 / 20 / 25** pupils. Keep work at tables; distribute materials, not roaming station rotations.
2. Draw a six-dot pattern on each card, labeled MODEL, not a named official constellation. Display a classroom lamp as an example of a nearby source, without pointing it toward eyes.
3. Put two plain-paper sheets per child per meeting and crayons at tables. Draw this record on the board: **plan or prediction / actual result / reason or revision**. Children copy boxes/icons, not paragraphs. Teacher dates and saves each child's record between meetings.
4. Read the SAFETY section, pretest the actual task with one issued kit and put a roster beside the timer. Demonstrate orally with objects; read every direction aloud. No independent reading, Year A completion or family donation is a prerequisite.

## Vocabulary and teacher background

Star = a distant light-producing object; pattern = arrangement; model = representation.

The Sun is a star. Dots on paper do not show actual star size or distance. We do not need Magi numbers, professions, journey dates or historical navigation claims.

**Common misconception:** All stars in a picture are close together or equally distant.

**If asked:** "Did the Christmas star have six dots?" This classroom pattern is not a reconstruction of the biblical event.

## SAFETY

No sharp puncturing, hole-punch stations, dark-room movement or Sun viewing. Keep aisles/exits clear. Stop for damaged materials; do not improvise a hazardous replacement. No student handles loose batteries or teacher cutting tools.

## Meeting 1: Sky pattern model - 30 minutes

1. **0-3:** Show the MODEL label and distinguish a classroom drawing from the actual sky.
2. **3-7:** Demonstrate counting each of six dots once and copying relative positions using a starting corner.
3. **7-20:** Each child copies the pattern with six large dots, counts and checks placement against the card. Partner identifies one moved/missing dot; author revises.
4. **20-26:** Each child points to two matching position details, gives the count and says the paper does not show real distances or sizes.
5. **26-30:** Return pattern cards and save the checked model pages. Collect each child's dated evidence, count kit items, return tools and clear tables. Cleanup is part of the period, not time added afterward.

## Questions to ask

- What does your star model leave out?
- How did you check every dot once?

## Individual evidence and success

**Grade 1:** Count six dots and point to two position details matching the reference.

**Grade 2:** Represent the arrangement accurately enough to compare and explain one real-sky/model difference.

Teacher visits three table teams during each of the first three four-minute blocks of practice (7-19), listening to or observing each member briefly; use the remaining practice minute and 20-26 for unfinished checks and comparison. At 25, this is nine table visits, **not 25 presentations**. Where a meeting specifies waves, use its supervised wave/check schedule instead of simultaneous table visits. Children keep separate records and partners may not answer for each other. Score each stated criterion **not yet / prompted / independent**; record absent/unobserved evidence as such. A group product, attendance, attractive art or a certificate is not individual mastery. No official constellation, navigation or biblical history mastery is claimed.

## Support and challenge

**Support / nonreader access:** Use tactile raised dots on teacher-prepared cards if needed; point/direct the drawing. Adult scribing, pointing, tactile/large pieces and a seated partner-directed action can show understanding; do not score motor speed.

**Challenge:** Draw a second view after rotating the card and explain why the count does not change. Do not add an unprepared activity or extend the native period.

## If things go wrong / indoor alternative

Pattern too complex: use four reference dots for support and record that count, not the six-dot criterion. No printer or dark room: teacher drawings work in daylight.

**Indoor:** The complete primary lesson is indoors at tables; Minnesota weather, internet access and daylight are not prerequisites.

## Family copy - optional, no routine homework

We counted and represented a star-pattern model and named its limits. Ask: "What does your star model leave out?" Optional: compare a drawing of an object with the real object. No purchase, donation, device, home teaching or required take-home project.
