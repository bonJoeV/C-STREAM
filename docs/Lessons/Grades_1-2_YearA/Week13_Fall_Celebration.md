## Before class

1. Assign teams of at most three: **4 / 5 / 7 / 9** kits for **10 / 15 / 20 / 25** pupils. Keep work at tables; distribute materials, not roaming station rotations.
2. Pull two records per child from fall lessons; if absent supply two fresh comparison sketches made today, clearly labeled fresh. Draw three experience columns: observe/build/code, one card per child.
3. Put two plain-paper sheets per child per meeting and crayons at tables. Draw this record on the board: **earlier work / changed detail / audience comment**. Children copy boxes/icons, not paragraphs. Teacher dates and saves each child's record between meetings.
4. Read the SAFETY section, pretest the actual task with one issued kit and put a roster beside the timer. Demonstrate orally with objects; read every direction aloud. No independent reading, Year A completion or family donation is a prerequisite.

## Vocabulary and teacher background

Artifact = saved work; evidence = what supports a claim; graph = arranged counts.

A favorite-experience graph measures preferences, not mastered domains. Saved work may show a revision, not prove learning growth unless the task is comparable.

**Common misconception:** Certificates establish mastery.

**If asked:** "Who is best?" We celebrate a specific contribution by each person; the graph is not a ranking.

## SAFETY

Use table galleries, no live robot replay, food prizes or crowding exits. Keep pupil identifiers off public displays. Keep aisles/exits clear. Stop for damaged materials; do not improvise a hazardous replacement. No student handles loose batteries or teacher cutting tools.

## Meeting 1: Evidence gallery - 30 minutes

1. **0-3:** Name the difference between a favorite memory and demonstrated evidence.
2. **3-7:** Model showing two records, saying what changed and giving a specific kind comment.
3. **7-20:** At tables each child explains one before/after record; partner gives a clarity comment and author redraws one unclear detail. Each places one anonymous card in a preferred-experience column.
4. **20-26:** Children count column cards, compare two counts and point to a purposeful visual revision. Teacher records the actual criterion, not all-domain mastery.
5. **26-30:** Return private artifacts to folders, count cards and clear the gallery. Collect each child's dated evidence, count kit items, return tools and clear tables. Cleanup is part of the period, not time added afterward.

## Questions to ask

- What changed in the example you showed?
- Which graph column has more cards?

## Individual evidence and success

**Grade 1:** Point to an actual change and compare two card counts.

**Grade 2:** Explain why the visual change makes the explanation clearer and distinguish a preference count from a skill score.

Teacher visits three table teams during each of the first three four-minute blocks of practice (7-19), listening to or observing each member briefly; use the remaining practice minute and 20-26 for unfinished checks and comparison. At 25, this is nine table visits, **not 25 presentations**. Where a meeting specifies waves, use its supervised wave/check schedule instead of simultaneous table visits. Children keep separate records and partners may not answer for each other. Score each stated criterion **not yet / prompted / independent**; record absent/unobserved evidence as such. A group product, attendance, attractive art or a certificate is not individual mastery. This OPTIONAL meeting is not a replacement for missed core instruction.

## Support and challenge

**Support / nonreader access:** Use two pictured samples, partner narration and a quiet table audience. Adult scribing, pointing, tactile/large pieces and a seated partner-directed action can show understanding; do not score motor speed. Record who actually operated hardware/software: a partner's operation does not establish the child's independent operation.

**Challenge:** Explain why different tasks cannot by themselves prove measured improvement. Do not add an unprepared activity or extend the native period.

## If things go wrong / indoor alternative

No saved work: make two versions of a quick observation sketch and assess today's revision only. Short time: keep seated pair sharing rather than open stations.

**Indoor:** The complete primary lesson is indoors at tables; Minnesota weather, internet access and daylight are not prerequisites.

## Family copy - optional, no routine homework

Our fall celebration used specific work samples and an anonymous preference graph. Ask: "What changed in the example you showed?" Optional: ask for one change your child can describe. No purchase, donation, device, home teaching or required take-home project.
