## Before class

1. Assign teams of at most three: **4 / 5 / 7 / 9** kits for **10 / 15 / 20 / 25** pupils. Keep work at tables; distribute materials, not roaming station rotations.
2. Ask a teacher/classroom helper to request moving a 5 cm paper ball 20 cm across a tray without touching it directly, with no lifting above the tray. Precut cardboard and tape. Draw start/end marks.
3. Put two plain-paper sheets per child per meeting and crayons at tables. Draw this record on the board: **plan or prediction / actual result / reason or revision**. Children copy boxes/icons, not paragraphs. Teacher dates and saves each child's record between meetings.
4. Read the SAFETY section, pretest the actual task with one issued kit and put a roster beside the timer. Demonstrate orally with objects; read every direction aloud. No independent reading, Year A completion or family donation is a prerequisite.

## Vocabulary and teacher background

User = person with a need; criterion = must do; constraint = limit; prototype = test model.

A tabletop scoop/pusher is a model for this request, not an assistive device. Successful redesign may reduce spills even if it looks less attractive.

**Common misconception:** We know another person needs a gadget because of an assumed disability.

**If asked:** "Can we use it on a real person?" No; we test a paper ball on a tray and ask for feedback, not medical or mobility use.

## SAFETY

No high shelves, grabbing bodies, sharp reaches, mouth-held tools or lifting people. Use screened cardboard; teacher precuts. Tape stays off skin. Keep aisles/exits clear. Stop for damaged materials; do not improvise a hazardous replacement. No student handles loose batteries or teacher cutting tools.

## Meeting 1: Listen and plan - 30 minutes

1. **0-3:** Teacher states the helper request; children repeat who it helps and what it must do.
2. **3-7:** Model the 20 cm tabletop route and material limit. Draw a pusher and scoop as alternative ideas.
3. **7-20:** Each child sketches two options, chooses one and directs a small cardboard mock-up using one square and two sticks. Partner acts as user and checks the request.
4. **20-26:** Each child points to a criterion and a limit; revise a sketch after specific user feedback.
5. **26-30:** Remove loose tape and return sticks; store labeled prototypes between meetings and save usable cardboard. Collect each child's dated evidence, count kit items, return tools and clear tables. Cleanup is part of the period, not time added afterward.

## Meeting 2: Build and first test - 30 minutes

1. **0-3:** Retrieve sketches and repeat the user request.
2. **3-7:** Model attaching a broad cardboard edge to sticks with two strips, hands remaining over tray.
3. **7-20:** Teams choose one plan, build using the remaining materials and run two 20 cm tests. Rotate mover, counter and observer; record success/spill for each.
4. **20-26:** Each child gives a test result and proposes one change tied to it; mark the proposed feature on a separate page.
5. **26-30:** Remove loose tape and return sticks; store labeled prototypes between meetings and save usable cardboard. Collect each child's dated evidence, count kit items, return tools and clear tables. Cleanup is part of the period, not time added afterward.

## Meeting 3: Improve for the user - 30 minutes

1. **0-3:** Recall recorded results, including spills.
2. **3-7:** Model changing only edge height or handle placement, not the route or ball.
3. **7-20:** Teams make their selected change and repeat two trials. Each child conducts or directs a test and records the outcomes.
4. **20-26:** Compare before/after successes out of two; partner-user gives feedback. Each child explains whether the request was better met or still needs work.
5. **26-30:** Remove loose tape and return sticks; store labeled prototypes between meetings and save usable cardboard. Collect each child's dated evidence, count kit items, return tools and clear tables. Cleanup is part of the period, not time added afterward.

## Questions to ask

- What did the user ask your design to do?
- Did the same test show improvement?

## Individual evidence and success

**Grade 1:** Name the user need, show a safe test and identify a change.

**Grade 2:** Distinguish criterion from material limit and compare successes out of two before and after a one-feature revision.

Teacher visits three table teams during each of the first three four-minute blocks of practice (7-19), listening to or observing each member briefly; use the remaining practice minute and 20-26 for unfinished checks and comparison. At 25, this is nine table visits, **not 25 presentations**. Where a meeting specifies waves, use its supervised wave/check schedule instead of simultaneous table visits. Children keep separate records and partners may not answer for each other. Score each stated criterion **not yet / prompted / independent**; record absent/unobserved evidence as such. A group product, attendance, attractive art or a certificate is not individual mastery. Use the grade-level criterion, not handwriting, reading speed or religious worth.

## Support and challenge

**Support / nonreader access:** Use two pictured design choices; accept a child-directed partner action for reaching/motor needs. Adult scribing, pointing, tactile/large pieces and a seated partner-directed action can show understanding; do not score motor speed. Record who actually operated hardware/software: a partner's operation does not establish the child's independent operation.

**Challenge:** Compare scoop versus pusher against the same route and explain one tradeoff. Do not add an unprepared activity or extend the native period.

## If things go wrong / indoor alternative

Ball rolls away: keep the test over a wide tray and add a stop at its edge. No improvement: keep the honest result and identify the next possible change rather than declare success.

**Indoor:** The complete primary lesson is indoors at tables; Minnesota weather, internet access and daylight are not prerequisites.

## Family copy - optional, no routine homework

We listened to a classroom user, tested a tabletop model and revised using evidence. Ask: "What did the user ask your design to do?" Optional: ask someone which ordinary classroom task is awkward; no home device construction. No purchase, donation, device, home teaching or required take-home project.
