## Before class

1. Assign teams of at most three: **4 / 5 / 7 / 9** kits for **10 / 15 / 20 / 25** pupils. Keep work at tables; distribute materials, not roaming station rotations.
2. Retrieve Week 1 goals if available. Prepare picture choices: share a turn, put tools away, invite a partner. Draw a three-space school tracker; do not use private family/faith disclosures.
3. Put two plain-paper sheets per child per meeting and crayons at tables. Draw this record on the board: **first step / next steps / actual school attempt**. Children copy boxes/icons, not paragraphs. Teacher dates and saves each child's record between meetings.
4. Read the SAFETY section, pretest the actual task with one issued kit and put a roster beside the timer. Demonstrate orally with objects; read every direction aloud. No independent reading, Year A completion or family donation is a prerequisite.

## Vocabulary and teacher background

Goal = action to practice; sequence = order; tracker = record of attempts.

A tick records that a practice occurred, not a person's goodness. An action sequence can be checked immediately.

**Common misconception:** Setting a goal proves it was achieved.

**If asked:** "What if I miss a day?" Record honestly and choose the next useful attempt; no punishment or public comparison.

## SAFETY

Seated role-play, no physical helping/lifting; keep personal trackers private. Keep aisles/exits clear. Stop for damaged materials; do not improvise a hazardous replacement. No student handles loose batteries or teacher cutting tools.

## Meeting 1: Plan and practice - 30 minutes

1. **0-3:** Recall one earlier goal or choose a school-helping picture.
2. **3-7:** Model invite -> listen -> share a turn on three cards; intentionally swap the first two and discuss the problem.
3. **7-20:** Each child draws/orders three action cards, practices with a partner, repairs a sequence mismatch and marks one actual attempt on the tracker.
4. **20-26:** Each child explains a first step and tracker mark; teacher saves tracker for a brief opening check in the next existing lesson, not a new meeting.
5. **26-30:** Store cards/trackers in private folders; return supplies. Collect each child's dated evidence, count kit items, return tools and clear tables. Cleanup is part of the period, not time added afterward.

## Questions to ask

- What is the first action in your goal?
- What does your tracker mark actually show?

## Individual evidence and success

**Grade 1:** Order two or three concrete actions and identify one actual practice.

**Grade 2:** Repair an order problem, explain how the action includes someone and distinguish an attempt from an outcome.

Teacher visits three table teams during each of the first three four-minute blocks of practice (7-19), listening to or observing each member briefly; use the remaining practice minute and 20-26 for unfinished checks and comparison. At 25, this is nine table visits, **not 25 presentations**. Where a meeting specifies waves, use its supervised wave/check schedule instead of simultaneous table visits. Children keep separate records and partners may not answer for each other. Score each stated criterion **not yet / prompted / independent**; record absent/unobserved evidence as such. A group product, attendance, attractive art or a certificate is not individual mastery. Use the grade-level criterion, not handwriting, reading speed or religious worth.

## Support and challenge

**Support / nonreader access:** Offer two pictured steps first, then add the third if ready. Adult scribing, pointing, tactile/large pieces and a seated partner-directed action can show understanding; do not score motor speed. Record who actually operated hardware/software: a partner's operation does not establish the child's independent operation.

**Challenge:** Describe an if/then response if the invited partner says no; respect the answer. Do not add an unprepared activity or extend the native period.

## If things go wrong / indoor alternative

Broad goal like "be good": narrow to an observable school action. Prior goals missing: start fresh without inventing progress. At next meeting ask one actual school example before the usual hook.

**Indoor:** The complete primary lesson is indoors at tables; Minnesota weather, internet access and daylight are not prerequisites.

## Family copy - optional, no routine homework

We planned a helpful classroom action and recorded one real practice, not a promise of achievement. Ask: "What is the first action in your goal?" Optional: talk about a small helpful action; no required home chart. No purchase, donation, device, home teaching or required take-home project.
