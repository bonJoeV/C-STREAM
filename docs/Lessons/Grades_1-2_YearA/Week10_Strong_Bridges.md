## Before class

1. Assign teams of at most three: **4 / 5 / 7 / 9** kits for **10 / 15 / 20 / 25** pupils. Keep work at tables; distribute materials, not roaming station rotations.
2. Mark a 15 cm span; pretest overlap using 12 equal planks and five broad blocks. Draw two five-box load bars for each child.
3. Put two plain-paper sheets per child per meeting and crayons at tables. Draw this record on the board: **plan or prediction / actual result / reason or revision**. Children copy boxes/icons, not paragraphs. Teacher dates and saves each child's record between meetings.
4. Read the SAFETY section, pretest the actual task with one issued kit and put a roster beside the timer. Demonstrate orally with objects; read every direction aloud. No independent reading, Year A completion or family donation is a prerequisite.

## Vocabulary and teacher background

Load = supported object; equal = same size/mass for this kit; result = observed count.

These are model bridge comparisons, not predictions of full-size safety. Load counts compare only the same kit/span.

**Common misconception:** A high load count proves every bridge of that shape is strongest.

**If asked:** "Can we walk on it?" No; this is a tabletop model for broad blocks only.

## SAFETY

Supports stay low and stable; no coins, heavy weights, hand pressure or standing on models. Keep aisles/exits clear. Stop for damaged materials; do not improvise a hazardous replacement. No student handles loose batteries or teacher cutting tools.

## Meeting 1: Same-span load comparison - 30 minutes

1. **0-3:** Recall earlier bridge supports and state the fixed-span comparison.
2. **3-7:** Model ruler endpoints and place loads singly at the center, stopping at collapse or five.
3. **7-20:** Teams build, test, record the supported count, change plank overlap/arrangement and retest the same five loads. Every child counts a trial or directs loading.
4. **20-26:** Each child draws aligned bars for both counts, identifies the change and explains a resource-care decision. No strongest-team competition.
5. **26-30:** Remove loads first, dismantle and return all 12 planks/five loads. Collect each child's dated evidence, count kit items, return tools and clear tables. Cleanup is part of the period, not time added afterward.

## Questions to ask

- How do your two load bars compare?
- Was the gap the same in both tests?

## Individual evidence and success

**Grade 1:** Count supported loads and represent two results with one square per load.

**Grade 2:** Label both results, explain the fixed span/load condition and justify a changed arrangement using counts.

Teacher visits three table teams during each of the first three four-minute blocks of practice (7-19), listening to or observing each member briefly; use the remaining practice minute and 20-26 for unfinished checks and comparison. At 25, this is nine table visits, **not 25 presentations**. Where a meeting specifies waves, use its supervised wave/check schedule instead of simultaneous table visits. Children keep separate records and partners may not answer for each other. Score each stated criterion **not yet / prompted / independent**; record absent/unobserved evidence as such. A group product, attendance, attractive art or a certificate is not individual mastery. Use the grade-level criterion, not handwriting, reading speed or religious worth.

## Support and challenge

**Support / nonreader access:** Provide five drawn boxes and a prebuilt first bridge; children still predict/count. Adult scribing, pointing, tactile/large pieces and a seated partner-directed action can show understanding; do not score motor speed.

**Challenge:** Repeat a result and explain any difference instead of adding unsafe loads. Do not add an unprepared activity or extend the native period.

## If things go wrong / indoor alternative

Both support five: record a tie and compare overlap/material use; do not add unlisted weights. No planks: fold paper from the allocated sheets and pretest the same safe load criterion before class.

**Indoor:** The complete primary lesson is indoors at tables; Minnesota weather, internet access and daylight are not prerequisites.

## Family copy - optional, no routine homework

We compared bridges with the same gap and loads and represented real results. Ask: "How do your two load bars compare?" Optional: compare two counted groups of familiar objects verbally. No purchase, donation, device, home teaching or required take-home project.

### Error interpretation and reteaching

- **Common error interpretation:** Compare the pupil's plan, action, and actual result. A mismatch means the prediction, sequence, condition, measurement, or explanation needs another check; it does not establish low ability, poor effort, or weak faith. Specifically guard against: **A high load count proves every bridge of that shape is strongest.**
- **Reteaching option within existing time:** Use the lesson's own fallback/support, not a new activity: **Both support five: record a tie and compare overlap/material use; do not add unlisted weights. No planks: fold paper from the allocated sheets and pretest the same safe load criterion before class.** Then apply **Support / nonreader access: Provide five drawn boxes and a prebuilt first bridge; children still predict/count. Adult scribing, pointing, tactile/large pieces and a seated partner-directed action can show understanding; do not score motor speed. ** Re-model only **3-7: Model ruler endpoints and place loads singly at the center, stopping at collapse or five.**, let the pupil repeat one smallest safe step, and collect a new independent result in a different color or on a fresh line so the first evidence remains visible.
