## BEFORE CLASS

1. Use teams of at most three (4/5/7/9). Copy the complete dataset below once/team; identify it as **fictional Minnesota winter practice data**, never as observed school weather.
2. Draw graph axes: five dates on horizontal axis, temperature -15 to 5 degrees C vertically. Make high-contrast large copies available.
3. If using thermometers, adult checks non-glass instruments and places two side-by-side indoors for 10 minutes before class. They measure room conditions, not outdoor weather.
4. Read aloud: "We will not go outside, touch unknown water or trash, open an electrical meter, or infer drinking-water safety from pH."
5. Prepare action brief: "The school wants a classroom reminder to shut exterior doors promptly **without obstructing access or exits**. Test clarity, not actual energy savings."

## SELF-CONTAINED DATA

All readings in this **invented** example are at 12:00 noon using comparable shielded instruments at the same height; site A is a fictional paved area, B a fictional grassy area. Snow/cloud cover and location could still confound interpretation.

| Date | A air temperature (degrees C) | B air temperature (degrees C) | Practice context |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Jan 6 | -10 | -11 | Cloudy |
| Jan 7 | -8 | -9 | Cloudy |
| Jan 8 | -4 | -6 | Sunny |
| Jan 9 | -12 | -12 | Cloudy |
| Jan 10 | -6 | -7 | Sunny |

Teacher key: A range = 8 degrees C; B range = 6 degrees C. A mean = -8; B mean = -9. A-B differences = 1, 1, 2, 0, 1 degrees C; mean difference = 1. These five points cannot establish climate change, an urban heat island, or a causal surface effect.

## VOCABULARY

**Weather:** conditions over a short time. **Climate:** long-term patterns. **Variable:** a factor that may change. **Range:** highest minus lowest value. **Uncertainty:** limits on what is known. **Stewardship:** responsible care and use of gifts/resources.

## TEACHER BACKGROUND

Surface temperature and air temperature differ. Comparable air measurements need consistent height, shielding, time, and instrument. A small difference may be instrument uncertainty. Graphs communicate evidence; they do not create new evidence. Never erase an inconvenient value.

**Common misconception:** one cold week disproves climate change. Climate requires long-term records, not five invented days.

**If asked, "Did grass make it colder?"** Answer: We can describe this dataset's difference, but cannot isolate cause without more controls and repeated observations.

## EXACT LESSON SEQUENCE

### Meeting 1 - measurement protocol

1. **0-5 (5 min):** Introduce common-home stewardship and ask why accuracy is a responsibility to neighbors.
2. **5-12 (7 min):** Distinguish weather/climate, air/surface; label practice data fictional.
3. **12-20 (8 min):** Each student reads two negative values, computes their difference, and identifies time/height/shield controls.
4. **20-30 (10 min):** Teams compare five A/B pairs. Optional indoor instruments: record both readings at 20 and 25 minutes without moving them; distinguish real room readings from invented outdoor data.
5. **30-38 (8 min):** Draft a repeatable outdoor protocol on paper, including adult weather permission, instrument checks, same time, and missing-data notation. Do not execute outdoors.
6. **38-41 (3 min):** Individual exit: state two controls and one reason the comparison cannot prove cause.
7. **41-45 (4 min):** Store data and tools.

### Meeting 2 - graph and interpret

1. **0-5 (5 min):** Check controls; keep the raw data intact.
2. **5-12 (7 min):** Model an axis and one plotted pair; use symbols plus labels, not color alone.
3. **12-22 (10 min):** Everyone independently plots all ten points, titles graph, labels degrees C, and keys A/B.
4. **22-32 (10 min):** Compare ranges and differences. Grade 5 uses a number line. Grade 6 computes means using the worked key only after trying.
5. **32-38 (6 min):** Partners critique graph accuracy and clarity; each revises one label/scale or explains why no revision is needed.
6. **38-41 (3 min):** Individual claim: cite two actual numbers and a limitation; no climate trend claims.
7. **41-45 (4 min):** File individual graphs and clean up.

### Meeting 3 - communicate a bounded action

1. **0-5 (5 min):** Read action brief. This action is a stewardship choice, **not an energy saving proven by the weather data**.
2. **5-12 (7 min):** Set criteria: readable at 1 m; no blame; explicitly keep entrances accessible.
3. **12-22 (10 min):** Teams make one purposeful visual sign and include a small truthful data graph/caption.
4. **22-32 (10 min):** Exchange with a neighboring team. Each tester states the action without coaching; record number interpreting correctly out of testers.
5. **32-38 (6 min):** Revise and retest. For nine teams use paired exchanges, not nine full-class talks.
6. **38-41 (3 min):** Individual exit: explain the stewardship tradeoff, one visual revision, and a proposed real measurement needed before claiming energy savings.
7. **41-45 (4 min):** Classroom display only with teacher permission; clean up.

## QUESTIONS TO ASK STUDENTS

Which values are real observations here? How would a sunlit thermometer bias a reading? What does a range tell us that a mean does not? Who might be excluded by a rushed door-closing rule? What data would test energy savings?

## WHAT SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE

Each student names two controls and one uncertainty, plots at least 9 of 10 points correctly with units, gives a supported comparison, and explains an accessible action. Art evidence is a purposeful visual choice and a recorded revision, not neatness. Grade 6 additionally computes both means and identifies the limited sample.

## IF THINGS GO WRONG

Thermometers disagree: report both and uncertainty; do not average away the discrepancy. No tools/printing: board-copy dataset and draw graph. Negative numbers overwhelm: provide number line and begin with differences. Sign misread: enlarge/action-label it, test again. Never invent "measured savings."

## SAFETY

No outdoor requirement; no -10 F exposure, road/parking-lot sampling, unknown water, trash handling, or electrical access. Adult manages instruments and spills; no glass/mercury thermometers. A pH strip cannot determine potability. Student plans are classroom proposals only, not permission to alter doors, thermostats, wiring, or habitat.

## SUPPORT / CHALLENGE - GRADES 5 AND 6

Grade 5: premarked axis and number line; dictate limitation. Challenge: compare ranges. Grade 6: scaffold signed sums; challenge redesign the measurement protocol to reduce one confound and explain why more data may still not establish cause. Provide seated work and large symbols.

## INDOOR FALLBACK

The dataset, protocol, graphs, and sign tests are the primary complete indoor path. Live indoor readings practice tool use only; neither path proves real outdoor monitoring.

## CLEANUP

Keep original datasets, collect rulers/instruments, cap markers, remove test signs from exits, recycle drafts, and retain individual graphs for the portfolio.
