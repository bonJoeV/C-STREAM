# Week 1: Welcome Back Scientists

## Lesson at a glance

Grades 3-4; Weekly B; Foundations; **one 40-minute meeting**.
**Objective:** observe/draw three details and pose a testable question, separating
what was seen from a guess. **Why:** Year B begins with evidence, not assumed
Year A attendance. Catholic/CST-C1: integrity in inquiry; CST-S1: detail/question;
CST-A1: labeled observation drawing. Official alignment **VERIFICATION REQUIRED**.
Technology **None**; introductory. Prep 10; cleanup 5 included. Preview flight,
architecture, marine systems and weather, but do not claim mastery of them.

## Before class / supplies

Teams <=3 (4/5/7/9); each gets a folded cardstock "mystery structure" and ruler.
Per pupil: two paper sheets/pencil (observation page and journal cover).
Teacher board/timer; demo uses issued card. Make different folds, no hidden
hazardous objects. Board `observed / predicted / question`. Reuse card/ruler;
save pages as Year B journal, no notebook purchase.

## SAFETY

**Background, vocabulary, and troubleshooting:**

Observe = inspect evidence; infer = explain from evidence; testable = can
investigate; journal = dated record. **Misconception:** recognizing a name is an
observation of function. **If asked "What if I never did STREAM?"** Today teaches
the needed routines; no prerequisite course. Faith values curiosity and honesty,
not a claim that every scientist/history episode lacked conflict.
Dry desk materials only; no tasting, blindfold mystery or sharp objects.
Stop/report damaged tools; indoor primary.

## Meeting 1: exactly 40 minutes

1. **0-4:** Welcome novices; optional original prayer for honest curiosity.
2. **4-10:** Demo visible fold/height versus "it is strongest"; model ruler zero.
3. **10-22:** Each handles/measures/draws card, labels three visible details,
   asks an investigable question. Rotate tools.
4. **22-30:** Peer asks "Where can I see that?" Revise one unsupported label;
   agree norms: record truth, safe hands, fair turns. Preview units briefly.
5. **30-35:** Each child shows three details, question and corrected drawing; teacher
   records novice checkpoint, not only participation.
6. **35-40:** Date/file pages, count cards/rulers, clear desks.

## What success looks like

Meets: three verifiable details, testable question and faithful visual revision.
Grade 3/support: stem/picture choices, dictation. Grade 4/challenge: two
possible explanations and evidence needed to distinguish them. No card:
teacher folds ordinary paper, label substitution. Unsupported guess: keep in
prediction column, do not erase uncertainty. Early finish: refine a question's
changed/measured features. No forced journal decoration.

**Family:** We began Year B with careful observations. Ask, "Which detail did
you actually see?" Optional: notice an everyday object together.

## Teacher artifact pack

- **Student observation record (print/no-print):** Give each pupil a page headed `Mystery structure evidence` with fields `Name`, `Date`, `Card ID`, `Measured height: ___ cm`, `Detail 1/2/3 (draw and label only what is visible)`, `My prediction (not observed)`, `Testable question: What happens to ___ when we change ___?`, and `Revision after "Where can I see that?": ___`. Without printing, pupils copy those exact labels from the board onto one sheet; the second sheet remains the journal cover.
- **Expected evidence and acceptable answers:** Three details must be independently checkable, such as “two triangular folds,” “the left edge is 8 cm,” or “one flap touches the desk.” A question is acceptable when a class test could change or measure one feature, for example, “Does widening the base change how long it stands?” “It is strong,” “it is a church,” and “why is it pretty?” belong under prediction or are not yet testable. Measurement within one ruler division of the teacher check is acceptable when the zero mark was aligned.
- **Finished worked example:** `Card ID B; height 9 cm; details: two upright panels, one 4 cm base flap, right panel leans left; prediction: it will fall if tapped; question: Does a 2 cm wider base change the number of seconds it stands?` Annotate “9 cm” and the three visible labels as **observed**, circle the prediction as **not tested**, and underline the changed feature and measured outcome in the question.
- **Visual/board setup:** Draw three columns titled `OBSERVED | PREDICTED | TESTABLE QUESTION`. Place “two folds” and “height 9 cm” in the first, “strongest” in the second, and the worked question in the third. Add a ruler sketch with the card base at zero and a small drawing labeled with three leader lines.
- **Discussion guidance:** Ask “Which words tell us this was measured?” (a number and unit), “Could two observers verify it?” (yes, by looking/measuring), “What would we change and measure in your question?” (answers must identify both), and “May two predictions differ?” (yes, until evidence distinguishes them). Correct the misconception that naming an object or intended function is a visible detail; move that statement to prediction rather than erasing it.
- **Kit contents:** Each team envelope contains `1 folded cardstock mystery structure + 1 metric ruler`; each pupil receives `2 plain-paper sheets + 1 pencil`.
- **Kit label:** Kit label: mark each team envelope `W01`, the school-assigned team identifier, `1 CARD + 1 RULER`; mark the class crate `G3-4 Year B | W01 OBSERVATION`.
- **Cleanup and reset:** Match the card ID to its envelope, flatten without a teacher fold, return the ruler, sharpen/return pencils per school routine, and count 4/5/7/9 complete team sets for 10/15/20/25 pupils.
- **Unfinished work:** Clip the dated observation record to the journal cover and place it in `W01 | IN PROGRESS`; add one checked status: `needs third detail`, `needs measurable question`, or `ready for checkpoint`. At the next class opening, allow three minutes with the same Card ID and ruler; do not send the structure or work home.
- **Annotated exemplar, error reading, and reteach:** Mark `It has a roof` with `I = inference unless a visible part is described`; mark a height written without `cm` with `unit missing, measurement may still be valid`; mark “What is it?” with `interesting, not a changed/measured test`. Reteach by revealing one fold at a time: pupils point, say “I see ___,” draw it, and add a leader line. Then jointly convert “Is it strong?” to “Does base width change standing time?”
- **Safety and supervision:** Severity **minor** (paper edge, pencil, or damaged-ruler scratch); likelihood **unlikely** with intact dry materials. Adult ratio is **1 adult to no more than 9 teams/25 pupils**, with teams of at most 3 in direct view. Stop and replace cracked rulers or sharp cardstock. No commercial mixture/article is used; no SDS hold applies.
- **Print accessibility:** Produce a black-and-white master with 14-point minimum text, bold field labels, 1.5 cm writing lines, and patterns/words rather than color categories. Offer a large-print copy, tactile access to the same card, oral description, dictation, pointing, or a scribe. Print one sample at 100% and confirm the ruler-record field, leader lines, and column headings remain distinct; the board-copy version uses the same reading order.
- **Finished example:** Finished example: The observation card is complete with three visible details, one measured value, and a testable question; the prediction remains separate and not treated as evidence.
- **Common error:** Common error: A student labels a name or function as a visible detail; correct by moving it to the prediction column and asking, "Where can I see that?"
- **Safety classification with Severity:** Safety classification with Severity **minor** and likelihood **unlikely**: paper edges, pencil points, and damaged rulers are the main hazards; keep teams in direct view and replace any damaged tool before use.
