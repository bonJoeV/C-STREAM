# Week 28: Pi Day Activities

## Lesson at a glance

Grades 3-4; Weekly B; Mathematics; **one 40-minute meeting**.
**Objective:** compare around with three across-lengths on two circles and
compose a four-part radial pattern with an explained visual choice.
**Why:** mathematical relationships can support purposeful art and honest wonder.
CST-C1 approximation/faith distinction; CST-M1 measurements; CST-M3 three equal
lengths/remainder; CST-A2 radial pattern purpose. Official alignment
**VERIFICATION REQUIRED**. Technology **None**; developing. Prep 10; cleanup 5.
No digit memorization contest, forced decimal division or unsupported sacred
geometry/biography claims.

## Before class / supplies

Teams <=3 (4/5/7/9): two smooth plastic circles under 15 cm diameter, ruler,
four 50 cm strings, two markers. Per pupil: two paper sheets/pencil.
Teacher board/timer, demo from kit; check strings reach around circles.
Model folding a traced circle into four sections for repeated motif.
Tools/string reusable; retain measurements/art.

## SAFETY

**Background, vocabulary, and troubleshooting:**

Diameter = across center; circumference = around; radial = arranged about
center; motif = repeated design. Pi is finite between 3 and 4, nonterminating/
nonperiodic decimal representation. **Misconception:** endless digits mean
infinite size. **If asked "Does pattern prove God?"** Mathematical evidence
and faith reflection are distinct; accurate reasoning can inspire wonder.
Short desk strings only, no sharp lids, compasses, beads, food or neck loops.
Stop/report damaged tools. Indoor primary.

## Meeting 1: exactly 40 minutes

1. **0-4:** "How can around/across help us organize a design?"
2. **4-9:** Model center/across, string around, three equal diameter portions.
3. **9-21:** Each measures a circle; team records two whole-cm pairs and compares
   three across-lengths plus remainder.
4. **21-30:** Each traces/folds four-section circle, repeats chosen motif for
   balance or emphasis; peer identifies intention, revise one section.
5. **30-35:** Individual pairs/remainder demonstration and explained visual
   purpose/finite-pi distinction.
6. **35-40:** Count/coil strings, cap markers, save dated data/art, clear.

## What success looks like

Meets: measurements/comparison and intentional repeated design, not polish.
Grade 3/support: center mark/premarked portions/pre-folded sheet/dictation.
Grade 4/challenge: explain measurement error and radial balance tradeoff.
Wrong result: recheck zero/center/string stretch and record correction.
No circles: traced teacher-selected circular object; no arbitrary oval.
Early finish: change motif while keeping four-section constraint.

**Family:** We compared circles and composed radial art. Ask, "Why did you
repeat that shape?" Optional: notice a circular pattern in a picture.

## Teacher artifact pack

- **Student measurement/design pages (print/no-print):** Page 1 fields: `Name/team/date`, table `Circle A/B | diameter across center ___ cm | circumference string ___ cm | three diameter lengths ___ cm | remainder ___ cm | measurement issue`, plus `Around is about ___ across-lengths`. Page 2: traced circle, four folded sections, `motif`, `repeated feature`, `visual purpose: balance/emphasis/rhythm`, `peer interpretation`, and `revision`. If unprinted, pupils copy this table and the art prompts onto two plain sheets.
- **Evidence and acceptable answers:** Whole-centimeter approximations are acceptable. For each true circle, the circumference should be a little more than three diameters; modest discrepancies are interpreted through center placement, ruler zero, string overlap/stretch, and rounding. Accept any four-part radial motif when repetition and visual intent are explained. Do not require decimal division or memorized pi digits.
- **Finished worked example:** `Circle A: diameter 8 cm, circumference 25 cm, three diameters 24 cm, remainder 1 cm. Circle B: diameter 12 cm, circumference 38 cm, three diameters 36 cm, remainder 2 cm.` Radial design repeats a leaf in four sections, with tips toward the center “to emphasize the center.” Annotate both approximations and note that the second remainder can reflect whole-centimeter rounding.
- **Board/visual setup:** Draw a circle with center, diameter, and string circumference; beneath it place three diameter-length strips plus a small remainder. Add a comparison table for two circles and a four-sector radial template labeled `same motif / rotate around center / purpose`.
- **Discussion and misconceptions:** “Why must the line cross the center?” (that is the diameter), “Does an endless decimal mean an infinite-size circle?” (no), “Why might our remainder differ?” (measurement/rounding/string placement), and “Does the pattern prove a faith claim?” (no; mathematical reasoning and reflection remain distinct). Correct measurements taken across an off-center chord.
- **Kit, labels, reset:** Per team: `2 smooth plastic circular objects <15 cm, 1 metric ruler, 4 cotton strings each 50 cm, 2 markers`; per pupil: `2 plain-paper sheets, 1 pencil`. Label pouch `W28 | assigned team ID | CIRCLES A/B | 4 STRINGS | 1 RULER | 2 MARKERS`. Coil each string separately, return both circles/ruler, cap markers, discard frayed string, and count 4/5/7/9 complete pouches; keep measurement/art pages flat.
- **Unfinished work:** File both pages in `W28 | assigned student ID | DATA + RADIAL DESIGN` and check `Circle A`, `Circle B`, `four-part motif`, or `purpose/revision`. Resume with the same labeled circles and string; do not substitute an oval or copy another team's measurements.
- **Annotated error/reteach:** Mark an off-center “diameter” `chord—find center`; a loose string gap `circumference not fully traced`; `pi is infinite` `decimal digits continue; value is finite between 3 and 4`; four unrelated images `motif not repeated yet`. Reteach by folding a traced circle to locate center, laying one diameter strip three times along a measured string, then stamping/drawing one motif in each sector.
- **Safety classification:** Severity **minor** (damaged plastic edge, string misuse, marker contact), likelihood **unlikely** with inspected circles and 50 cm desk strings. Adult ratio **1:25 maximum**, teams at most 3; strings remain on desks and never around bodies. No sharp lids, compasses, beads, or food. The markers are a commercial marker/ink product; record the exact manufacturer/product in [Safety_Data_Sheet_Register.csv](../../Review/Safety_Data_Sheet_Register.csv). Keep that product **ON HOLD** until school approval and SDS applicability are resolved.
- **Print accessibility:** Use 14-point minimum, bold center marks, thick table rules, tactile string/circle access, and motif sectors distinguishable without color. Offer large-print templates, pretraced/prefolded circles, oral measurements, scribing, or tactile/raised motif placement. Confirm a grayscale sample preserves sector boundaries and leaves room for two-digit centimeter values.
- **Student material:** Student material: give each pupil the two-page circle record for A/B diameter, string circumference, three diameter lengths, remainder, measurement issue, four-sector motif, visual purpose, peer interpretation, and revision.
- **Finished example:** Finished example: Circle A records diameter 8 cm, circumference 25 cm, three diameters 24 cm, remainder 1 cm; Circle B records 12 cm, 38 cm, 36 cm, and 2 cm, with a four-leaf radial motif emphasizing the center.
- **Visual example:** Visual example: draw a circle with center, diameter, and string circumference, then place three diameter-length strips plus a remainder beside a four-sector radial template.
- **Board setup:** Board setup: display the A/B diameter-circumference comparison table and the four-sector guide labeled `same motif | rotate around center | purpose`.
- **Kit label:** Kit label: mark each pouch `W28`, the school-assigned team identifier, and `CIRCLES A/B | 4 STRINGS | 1 RULER | 2 MARKERS`.
- **Annotated exemplar:** Annotated exemplar: mark both center-crossing diameters, circumference string paths, three-diameter placements, 1 cm and 2 cm remainders, repeated leaf motif, and stated emphasis purpose.
- **Common error:** Common error: an off-center chord, loose string gap, claim that pi itself is infinite, or four unrelated images must be annotated before remeasurement or motif revision.
- **Print/accessibility check:** Print/accessibility check: print the black-and-white measurement and radial-design pages at 100% with 14-point text, bold center marks, thick table rules, clear sector boundaries, and large writing spaces.
