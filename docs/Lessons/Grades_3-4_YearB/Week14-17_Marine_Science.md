# Weeks 14-17: Marine Science Lab

## Lesson at a glance

Grades 3-4; Weekly B; Marine systems; **four 40-minute meetings**.
**Objective:** label ocean-light zones, explain a food-to-eater model and improve
a collection tool using equal ten-token tests. **Why:** ocean stewardship needs
system knowledge, evidence and honest limits.
Catholic/CST-C3 creation care; CST-S3 zone/feeding models; CST-E1 criteria/limits;
CST-E2 actual retest; CST-M2 labeled before/after count display.
Official alignment **VERIFICATION REQUIRED**. Technology **None**; developing.
Prep 20 minutes; cleanup 5 each. No voyage legends, unverified statistics,
soap/oil/cocoa experiments or universal energy-transfer percentages.

## Before class and exact supplies

Teams <=3 (4/5/7/9). Per unit kit: three cardstock sheets (zone strip/tool),
ruler, tray, plastic spoon, **40 cm tape**, twenty **3 cm paper debris squares**
(ten per test). Per pupil: four record sheets/pencil. Teacher board/timer;
adult pours **100 mL fresh water per test**, **200 mL/team for two tests**, in
meeting 4 only; demo from issued kit. Class: one graduated plastic pouring cup
and one roll of paper towels for drying/spills.
Pretest spoon/card scoop and paper pickup within **30 seconds**, use a fresh
ten-square set each test; soaking/sinking is recorded, not replaced silently.
Reuse tools, retain charts, dispose wet paper after drying.

Copy these **ready cards to board**, no separate research required:
[NOAA light zones](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/light_travel.html),
retrieved October 4, 2026: sunlight 0-200 m; twilight 200-1,000 m; below
1,000 m no sunlight. Deeper subdivisions: midnight 1,000-4,000 m, abyss
4,000-6,000 m, hadal 6,000 m and deeper. Boundaries are teaching divisions,
not equal-width physical layers. Draw a not-to-scale strip.
Food-model cards: **hypothetical** photosynthetic producer P; small consumer Q
eats P; larger R eats Q; decomposer D breaks down remains. These stipulated
relationships teach arrow direction, not unverified species diets.

## Vocabulary/background/SAFETY

Zone = depth/light region; producer = makes food; consumer = eats organisms;
decomposer = breaks down dead matter; model = limited representation.
**Misconception:** removing one card proves all organisms will die.
**If asked "Are all producers sun-powered?"** No; some use chemical energy;
this simplified model uses photosynthesis. Religious creation care motivates
responsibility, not predicted cleanup success.
No actual pollution, animals, chemicals, glass, tasting or stagnant water.
Adult pours/empties, dry spills immediately, stop/report unsafe setups.
Indoor paper/water work, no winter outing or unseen home research.

## Meeting 1: exactly 40 minutes

1. **0-4:** Wonder/care question: "What changes with depth?"
2. **4-10:** Read NOAA cards, model five labeled depth bands, not stacked liquids.
3. **10-22:** Each labels five zones/depth bounds on not-to-scale strip and
   marks which regions permit sunlight; team compares cards.
4. **22-30:** Peer checks boundaries/model scale; revise one misleading spacing
   or caption. "Does equal paper spacing mean equal ocean depth?"
5. **30-35:** Individual three light regions, five labels and scale limitation.
6. **35-40:** Save dated strips/count/clear.

## Meeting 2: exactly 40 minutes

1. **0-4:** Recall light/producer relationship.
2. **4-9:** Model P -> Q -> R, arrows food-to-eater; D receives remains.
3. **9-21:** Each draws stipulated feeding web, labels roles/arrows and states
   what is hypothetical; add light as energy source, not an organism.
4. **21-30:** Remove Q card on paper; identify represented links lost and an
   uncertain possible effect, not a guaranteed cascade.
5. **30-35:** Each explains arrow/role and a model limitation; "What source
   would we need to name real species?"
6. **35-40:** File diagrams/count tools/clear.

## Meeting 3: exactly 40 minutes

1. **0-4:** "How can collecting debris help people and creation?"
2. **4-9:** Criterion >=8 of ten paper squares collected in 30 seconds;
   limit one spoon/card tool/40 cm tape, no claim about dissolved contaminants.
3. **9-21:** Design two scoop shapes and build dry prototype from cardstock.
4. **21-30:** Dry-tray ten-square practice; every pupil operates/records,
   choose one change for wet test.
5. **30-35:** Individual criterion/constraint, practice count and design reason.
6. **35-40:** Save tools/remaining squares, count/clear.

## Meeting 4: exactly 40 minutes

1. **0-4:** Retrieve dry-test plan; distinguish paper debris from real marine waste.
2. **4-9:** Adult water setup/safety; fixed ten squares, 30 seconds, spoon/tool.
3. **9-21:** Test version 1, record collected 0-10 and sinking; rotate operator/
   counter/recorder. Revise one scoop edge, not water volume.
4. **21-30:** Adult empties/resets with fresh 100 mL, fresh ten-square set,
   same setup/time; retest. Each draws
   two bars and makes whole-number comparison.
5. **30-35:** Individual results/limit and stewardship choice; "Does clear
   water mean safe drinking water?" No.
6. **35-40:** Adult empties clean water, collect wet paper, dry trays/count/store.

## Success/access/troubleshooting

Meets: zone/food models with limits, criterion and real repeated counts/chart.
Grade 3/support: read-aloud depth labels/0-10 graph. Grade 4/challenge:
zone scale error and collection-versus-contamination tradeoff. Sinking paper:
record limitation, no replacement during a timed test. Water not approved:
same ten-token dry tray/time tests, clearly **dry debris model**, no flotation
evidence. No tray: dry desk boundary, same criterion. Early finish: explain
what a real ocean trial would need. Creation-care source: paraphrased
[Laudato Si'](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/encyclicals/documents/papa-francesco_20150524_enciclica-laudato-si.html).

**Family:** We studied marine models and retested debris collection. Ask,
"What did your model leave out?" Optional: discuss reducing waste.
