# Weeks 29-31: Weather Station

## Lesson at a glance

| Field | Teacher information |
|---|---|
| Grade / unit / title | Grades 3-4 / Weather data / Weather Station |
| Time | **Three 40-minute meetings**, including cleanup |
| Domains | Catholic care for neighbors, Earth science, tool use, mathematics |
| Big idea | A measurement needs a suitable tool, units and context; a few observations do not establish climate or a certain forecast. |
| Student objective | "I can read a tool, label the source of data, and make an evidence-based comparison." |
| Why | Meteorologists use instruments and many observations; communities use forecasts to protect people. |
| Catholic connection | Plan safe help for people affected by weather; do not suggest storms punish victims. |
| Local standards | **Local, not official:** CST-C2 (safe/inclusive weather response); CST-S1 (observations/evidence); CST-S2 (test a measurement method); CST-T1 (tool use/troubleshooting); CST-M2 (labeled graph/data comparison). |

| Difficulty / prerequisites | Moderate; count/read whole millimeters and whole-number Celsius values; no negative-number arithmetic, mean, percentage or pie chart required. |
| Prep / cleanup | 20 minutes before meeting 1; 10 minutes before meetings 2/3 with reusable kit; 5 minutes each meeting |

## Before class

1. Seat pairs, one final trio if needed. Keep water trays away from outlets/books. Set a class **room thermometer** at a readable height, away from heater/sunlight; label its reading "indoor air."
2. Use **uncut, clear, flat-bottomed straight-sided plastic cups**, at least 5 cm tall and **inside diameter at most 4.5 cm**. This keeps each 20 mm-deep fill under 32 mL and the thirteen-cup plus demonstration water budget under 500 mL. Tape a ruler with zero at the inside bottom. If the cup tapers, use it only as a water-container model, **not** a rainfall gauge.
3. Cut 20 cm tissue strips for wind indicators; no pins, balloons, glass or cut bottles. Put a tray, cup, ruler and paper in each team's kit.
4. Copy the supplied dataset onto the board. Label it **SIMULATED PRACTICE DATA, not observed local weather**. It makes the core unit independent of weather and unscheduled between-class monitoring.
5. Outdoor observations are optional only with school approval, supervision, accessible route and safe conditions. Do not require outdoor access to meet the objective.

## Exact supplies

Tables budget ceil(students / 2) kits. A final trio gives **5/7/10/12 active teams** at 10/15/20/25 pupils; unused eighth/thirteenth tray is spare. Thirteen-tray capacity is conservative.

- **Per student:** pencil, 1 evidence half-sheet per meeting.
- **Per team:** 1 clear straight-sided cup (inside diameter <=4.5 cm, height >=5 cm), 1 shallow tray, 1 ruler, 1 tissue strip (20 cm), 1 scrap-cardboard handle (15 cm), 20 cm tape, 3 recording/graph paper sheets across unit.

- **Teacher only:** scissors for strips, water dispensing jug and towel. Optional printed actual weather report must include source, station, date/time and units.

## Vocabulary and background

**Weather:** atmospheric conditions over a short time at a place. **Climate:** patterns over many years, not this week's graph. **Precipitation:** rain/snow/sleet/hail. **Gauge:** a measurement tool. **Source:** where data came from. **Forecast:** evidence-based estimate of future conditions.

Rainfall depth is read in a straight-sided collector with matching collection/storage cross-section; an uncalibrated tapered bottle is not a reliable rain gauge. Our cup test is **indoor water-depth practice**, not rainfall or snow measurement. A tissue strip shows air movement, not wind speed in miles per hour. Snowfall depth is not the same as melted-water depth.

**Misconception:** "A spinning homemade cup device tells exact wind speed." It needs calibration; this unit does not claim that.
**If asked:** "Will rain come tomorrow?" Answer: "Our five practice days cannot tell us for sure. A forecast uses wider, current information; follow school alerts and official forecasts, not this model."

### Supplied dataset

| Practice day | Temperature (degrees C) | Rain (mm) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Monday | 6 | 0 |
| Tuesday | 8 | 4 |
| Wednesday | 7 | 0 |
| Thursday | 10 | 6 |
| Friday | 9 | 2 |

These are **invented values for learning**, not a source claim. Total rain = 12 mm; warmest = Thursday; largest temperature difference = 4 degrees C. They describe neither Minnesota climate nor a real forecast.

## SAFETY

No glass, latex balloons, exposed pins, cut bottle edges, outdoor storms/ice or unsupervised movement. Teacher pours water, maximum **20 mm depth per cup**; students report spills immediately and stop movement until dried. No tasting. Tissue strips are handheld by the cardboard, not tied to wrists/necks or placed at faces. No electrical fan needed; teacher may move a card gently to create airflow. Outdoor work stops under school weather restrictions; **-10 degrees F means use the indoor pathway**.

## Meeting 1: observations and model tools, exactly 40 minutes

1. **0-5: Weather or climate?** Original prayer: "God, help us care for neighbors and observe carefully." Ask: "Does one snowy day describe the whole climate?" Teach the time-scale distinction.
2. **5-11: Observe safely.** Look through a window; record date/time, sky and visible precipitation. If the view is blocked, write "not visible," not a guess. Read the room thermometer as **indoor**, not outdoor weather.
3. **11-17: Tool demonstration.** Show cup/ruler zero alignment. Pour to 10 mm; read at eye level at the bottom of the curved water surface. Ask: "Where is zero? Which unit are we using?" Show how a tapered cup would mislead.
4. **17-27: Build/test indicator.** Teams tape strip to cardboard handle, predict its direction and gently wave a card nearby. Record still/moving and direction relative to their hand. No numeric wind speed. Teacher checks one kept-same condition in each team.
5. **27-32: Compare observations.** "Which observation was direct? Which came from a tool?" Draw the tool and identify one limitation.
6. **32-35: Individual evidence.** Each child reads the demonstrated 10 mm cup, labels the room temperature location, and explains weather versus climate orally or in writing.
7. **35-40: Cleanup.** Teacher empties water; children dry cups/trays, store strips/rulers, collect slips, clear floor.

## Meeting 2: measurement consistency, exactly 40 minutes

1. **0-5: Recall.** "Why can't room temperature be called today's outdoor temperature?"
2. **5-10: Demonstrate fair reading.** Teacher prepares two levels, about 5 and 15 mm. Read first with ruler zero misplaced, then aligned. Ask: "Was the water changed, or the measuring method?"
3. **10-22: Team depth trials.** Teacher pours team cups to a depth below 20 mm; both partners independently read and record to nearest whole mm, then compare. Change the water depth once and repeat. A trio adds a third reader. Record disagreements instead of forcing identical numbers.
4. **22-28: Investigate error.** Reset ruler alignment/eye height; reread one cup. "What adjustment made our reading more dependable?"
5. **28-32: Label datasets.** Introduce the practice table above. Contrast measured classroom water with simulated rainfall; they are different kinds of evidence.
6. **32-35: Individual evidence.** Draw zero alignment, record own two readings with units, name one cause of differing measurements.
7. **35-40: Cleanup.** Teacher drains cups into sink, students dry trays and store supplies; collect measurement slips.

## Meeting 3: graph and communicate, exactly 40 minutes

1. **0-5: Source check.** "Did we collect Monday's data outdoors?" Expected: no, these are simulated.
2. **5-11: Model a graph.** Draw rain bar graph, days along bottom, mm scale **0-6 by ones**. Tuesday's bar reaches 4. Label title and "simulated practice."
3. **11-23: Make graph.** Grade 3 draws five rain bars and adds 0+4+0+6+2 = 12 mm. Grade 4 also draws a temperature graph, 0-10 by ones; connecting points describes sequence, not measurements between days. No average, percentages or pie chart.
4. **23-28: Compare evidence.** "Which day had most rain? What data justify that? Does Thursday's warmest temperature cause its rain?" Expected: coincidence here cannot establish causation.
5. **28-32: Neighbor-care design.** Plan a safe indoor message for school weather alerts: readable text, no color-only warning, tell an adult rather than venture into storms. Peers check clarity. This is an ethical communication decision, not forecasting certification.
6. **32-35: Individual evidence.** Child reads two graph values, states one comparison and labels the source as simulated. Grade 4 also states a limitation of forecasting from five days.
7. **35-40: Cleanup/share.** Two volunteers explain comparisons; collect graphs and slips, return tools, recycle spare paper, wipe trays. No ongoing home log required.

## Success, troubleshooting, support and challenge

**Success:** every child reads a tool with units, distinguishes indoor/simulated/actual data, creates an accurate labeled graph, and gives a supported comparison. Score **1** guessed data; **2** correct values with help but missing source/units; **3** accurate values, source and comparison; **4** level 3 plus explains a method or forecast limitation.

- **No thermometer:** teacher displays a clearly labeled picture scale with 18 degrees C as **practice**, not a room observation. Tool-reading is observed; actual temperature measurement is not.
- **Cup tapered or zero inaccessible:** do not certify rain measurement; use a teacher's straight-sided cup for the class depth demo.
- **Spill:** stop; adult dries it. Return to dry graph work if repeated.
- **Support / Grade 3:** pre-draw axes, read orally, allow finger-point/oral response, pair for handling but collect separate interpretations.
- **Challenge / Grade 4:** compare two readers' values and differences; explain why a weather rhyme or this small table cannot guarantee a forecast.
- **Indoor alternative / winter:** all three core meetings already work indoors; no between-meeting weather, growth, network or family labor required.
- **25 pupils:** thirteen stationary trays; teacher makes small pours during the twelve-minute work window; no four-station tool-building queue.

## Family snippet

We used safe tools, labeled where our data came from, and graphed a practice weather week. Each child explained a comparison and a limit of the evidence. Our Catholic connection was caring for neighbors in severe weather. **Ask:** "Was your graph observed or simulated, and how can you tell?" **Optional at home:** discuss an adult's forecast and a safe plan. No outdoor exposure or homework.
