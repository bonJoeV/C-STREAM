# Week 33: Easter Engineering

## Lesson at a glance

Grades 3-4; Weekly B; Easter design; **one 40-minute meeting**.
**Objective:** make a paper scene whose movable stone reveals an Easter message,
test twice, revise and distinguish model mechanics from religious belief.
**Why:** a communication model can be purposeful without proving Resurrection.
CST-C1 symbol/evidence distinction; CST-E1 reveal criterion/limits;
CST-E2 actual retest; CST-A3 critique/message revision.
Official alignment **VERIFICATION REQUIRED**. Technology **None**;
introductory. Prep 10; cleanup 5. No raw eggs, drop devices or spring launchers.

## Before class / supplies

Teams <=3 (4/5/7/9): cardstock scene base, two ordinary paper sheets (stone/
message), ruler, two markers, 40 cm tape. Per pupil: evidence paper/pencil.
Teacher board/timer; draw empty-tomb illustration as religious retelling,
fold a paper slider behind taped guide from issued kit. Adult pre-cuts any slot;
no live specimens. Reuse tools, retain scene/records.

## SAFETY

**Background, vocabulary, and troubleshooting:**

Mechanism = moving parts doing a job; slider = guided movable piece; symbol =
meaningful representation; retelling = adapted narrative.
**Misconception:** model opening proves a miracle. **If asked "Is a butterfly
the same as Jesus rising?"** No; metamorphosis develops while alive, Easter is
belief about Resurrection. Scene is not an archaeological reconstruction.
No stones, moss, eggs, springs, bands, flames or sharp cutting. Adult cuts,
children move low desk paper. Stop/report torn guides; indoor primary.

## Meeting 1: exactly 40 minutes

1. **0-4:** Easter hope/prayer optional; "What can our mechanism actually test?"
2. **4-9:** Model slider/reveal; criterion full message visible, mechanism
   operates two cycles without detached parts; limits supplied paper/tape.
3. **9-21:** Each sketches purpose; team builds scene/reveal, rotating roles.
4. **21-30:** Two open/close cycles, peer reads message; record, change guide/
   letter layout, repeat two cycles/reader test.
5. **30-35:** Each child explains criterion, actual before/after and message revision/
   symbol distinction. "What caused sticking?"
6. **35-40:** Count tools, flatten scraps, save dated scene/evidence.

## What success looks like

Meets: functional criterion/test record, critique-backed revision and honest
faith/model boundary. Grade 3/support: prepared guide/large message/dictation.
Grade 4/challenge: readability-versus-hidden-space tradeoff. Sticking slider:
loosen one guide, record retest; no sharp tool workaround. No card: folded
paper base, label material change. Early finish: new reader, same cycle test.
Celebration/enthusiasm not mastery.

**Family:** We retested an Easter communication model. Ask, "What did the
mechanism show, and what was a faith symbol?" Optional: discuss hope.

## Teacher artifact pack

- **Student mechanism/test record (print/no-print):** Fields: `Name/team/date`, `Message to reveal`, labeled sketch `base / guide / slider-stone / hidden message`, `Limits: issued paper + 40 cm tape`, and a table `before revision: cycle 1/2 attached? full message visible? reader words`, `cause of sticking/unclear message`, `one guide or lettering change`, `after revision: cycle 1/2`, plus `The mechanism demonstrates ___; it does not prove ___`. Without printing, pupils copy these prompts onto evidence paper.
- **Expected evidence and acceptable answers:** A pass means two full open/close cycles with no detached part and a peer can read the complete message. Accept guide-width, tape placement, slider shape, letter size, spacing, or contrast revisions supported by the observed problem. The final distinction should state that the paper mechanism tests movement/communication and retells or symbolizes Easter; it does not scientifically prove Resurrection or reconstruct archaeology.
- **Worked finished example:** `Before: cycle 1 stuck/only "He is" visible; cycle 2 guide detached. Observation: top tape crosses slider path. Revision: move tape 1 cm above path and enlarge "He is risen." After: two attached cycles, full message read both times.` Annotate the mechanical cause, unchanged religious message, and the limited engineering claim.
- **Board/visual setup:** Draw an exploded side view of base, two taped guide rails, and slider path; post a two-cycle pass chart and a reader box. Beside it create separate headings `TESTABLE MECHANICS` and `EASTER BELIEF/SYMBOL` with examples under each.
- **Discussion/misconception prompts:** “What caused sticking?” (evidence may show tight guide, torn edge, or tape in path), “What must a reader see?” (the whole message), “Does a working slider prove a miracle?” (no), and “Is butterfly development the same event as Resurrection?” (no). Correct the claim that enthusiasm or decoration substitutes for two completed cycles.
- **Kit, labeling, reset:** Per team: `1 cardstock scene base, 2 plain-paper sheets, 1 ruler, 2 markers, 40 cm masking tape`; per pupil: `1 evidence paper, 1 pencil`. Label storage `W33 | assigned team ID | SLIDER SCENE + TEST RECORD`; label tool pouch `1 RULER + 2 MARKERS`. Return/cap tools, secure loose slider inside the scene without testing it further, flatten reusable offcuts, place all adult-cut slots face down, and count 4/5/7/9 complete team sets.
- **Unfinished work:** Store the scene flat with one exact next step: `assemble guide`, `run baseline cycles`, `revise`, or `run retest/reader check`. Resume at school with the remaining issued tape; do not use home stones, eggs, springs, or extra adhesives.
- **Annotated errors and reteach:** Mark a detached guide `criterion not yet met`; reader coached with the hidden words `reader test invalid`; “model proves Easter” `mechanics/belief boundary`; sticking blamed on “bad design” `locate contact point`. Reteach with a plain rectangular slider through two widely spaced paper rails, gradually narrow to a free-moving fit, then add one large two-word message and retest.
- **Safety classification:** Severity **minor** (paper edge, marker, or loose taped part), likelihood **unlikely**; adult cutting would raise severity and is therefore adult-only before class. Adult ratio **1:25 maximum**, teams no larger than 3, all mechanisms operated low on desks. No stones, moss, eggs, springs, bands, flames, or pupil cutting. The markers and masking tape are commercial marker/ink and tape products; record each exact manufacturer/product in [Safety_Data_Sheet_Register.csv](../../Review/Safety_Data_Sheet_Register.csv). Keep both products **ON HOLD** until school approval and SDS applicability are resolved.
- **Print accessibility:** Use 14-point record text and at least 24-point model message letters, high-contrast outlines, tactile guide/slider parts, and no color-only message. Offer a prepared guide, large-grip slider tab, oral reader response, dictation, or scribing. Check the printed/test message from the normal peer viewing position in grayscale.
- **Student material:** Student material: give each pupil the mechanism/test record for the message, base/guide/slider sketch, issued-material limits, two before cycles, cause, sole guide or lettering change, two after cycles, reader response, and mechanics/belief distinction.
- **Board setup:** Board setup: display the exploded base-guide-slider view, two-cycle pass chart, reader box, and separate `TESTABLE MECHANICS` and `EASTER BELIEF/SYMBOL` headings.
- **Kit label:** Kit label: mark each storage folder `W33`, the school-assigned team identifier, and `SLIDER SCENE + TEST RECORD`; mark the tool pouch `1 RULER + 2 MARKERS`.
- **Annotated exemplar:** Annotated exemplar: mark the tape crossing the slider path, first partial-message/stuck cycle, detached guide, tape moved 1 cm above the path, enlarged `He is risen`, and two successful attached reader cycles.
- **Common error:** Common error: a detached guide, coached reader, claim that the model proves Easter, or nonspecific `bad design` diagnosis must be annotated before locating the contact point and retesting.
- **Print/accessibility check:** Print/accessibility check: print the black-and-white mechanism record at 100% with 14-point text, at least 24-point model-message letters, high-contrast outlines, and large drawing/dictation boxes.
