# Week 12: Making Gifts for Others

## Lesson at a glance

| Field | Plan |

| Domains / big idea | C, A; visual choices help a recipient understand a message |

## Before class and supplies

Read [K routines](../../Resources/Kindergarten_Reference_Routines.md).
Teacher offers a card for a school helper or child-chosen trusted person.
Arrange school-approved delivery first; if acceptance is unknown retain it
as a communication model. Precut four paper shapes at least 5 cm per child.
Fold one sheet into a card; a second sheet is the plan.

## Vocabulary and teacher background

**Recipient:** person the message is for. **Plan:** picture of the idea.
**Choice:** feature selected for a reason. **Revise:** make meaning clearer.
Misconception: more decorations make the best gift. Judge communicated meaning,
not polish or expense. If asked whether this is engineering, say today's target
is art/service; no functional engineering test is claimed.

## Exact sequence: 25 minutes

1. **0-4:** Discuss caring without assuming every recipient likes the same gift.
   Ask "Who is it for, and what message will it carry?"
2. **4-8:** Show a simple unclear drawing, add one meaningful feature and explain
   why. Model a plan with no required words.
3. **8-17:** Each child plans and makes a card; partners point to the main idea
   and give one respectful suggestion. Revise a feature; begin checks.
4. **17-21:** Each child identifies recipient and a purposeful shape/color/
   picture choice, then indicates a change making the message clearer.
5. **21-25:** Retain evidence/arrange approved delivery, count shapes, cap glue,
   collect crayons and clear.

## Success, SAFETY, access and troubleshooting

Record recipient awareness, choice/reason and communication revision. Do not
grade handwriting, expense, artistic realism or personal piety.
Support: large ready-folded card, two picture choices, adult scribing.
Challenge: explain how spacing/contrast helps the recipient.
No sequins, glitter, ribbon, food, unsafe tiny stickers or public names/images.
No recipient permission: keep the card in class; no external visit.
No glue: draw shapes directly and assess the same design choices.
All work indoors; no family donation or required home delivery.

**Family:** We made and revised a thoughtful visual message.
Ask, "Why did you choose that picture, and what did you change?" No routine homework.
