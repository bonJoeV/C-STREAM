# Weeks 7-9: Engineering Helpers

## Lesson at a glance

| Field | Plan |
|---|---|
| Grade / unit / time | K / Service design / three 25-minute meetings |
| Domains / big idea | C, E, A, M; a useful design must meet a user's stated need and a test |
| Student objective / why | "I can plan a tray, carry two empty cups, change a part and retest." Engineers use evidence to serve people. |
| Catholic connection | Listen to a requested need; respect people rather than assuming incapacity. |
| Local standards | CST-C2: need/inclusion; CST-E1: carrying criterion; CST-E2: test/redesign; CST-A1: plan; CST-M1: count two cups. Official alignment: VERIFICATION REQUIRED. |

## Before class and exact materials

Read [K routines](../../Resources/Kindergarten_Reference_Routines.md).
Use 5/7/10/12 active teams; budget 5/8/10/13 kits. On each desk mark a 30 cm
path. Tell this complete fictional request: "Our classroom helper asks for a
tray to move two empty cups from one mark to the other without either falling."
Precut smooth cardboard and tape; pretest a flat tray at slow walking-hand speed.

| Kit/student allocation | 10 | 15 | 20 | 25 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Kit: two cups; two 20 cm cardboard squares | 10 each | 16 each | 20 each | 26 each |
| Kit: ten craft sticks; ten 10 cm tape strips | 50 each | 80 each | 100 each | 130 each |
| Kit: storage tray | 5 | 8 | 10 | 13 |
| Student: plan/evidence sheet; crayon, per meeting | 10 each | 15 each | 20 each | 25 each |

Teacher: ruler, timer, roster, pencil; adult-only scissors. Demonstrate with a
budgeted kit. Store work by kit number; no family materials or purchased unit.

## Vocabulary, background and SAFETY

**Need:** a requested problem. **Criterion:** what the model must do.
**Plan:** drawing before making. **Redesign:** a change informed by a test.
A rim may retain cups but can use extra material; test rather than promise.
Misconception: decoration proves a helper works. Ask about the same two cups
and path. If asked about helping a real person, say this is a desk model only.
No liquid, loaded glasses, high shelves, climbing, wearables, pets, infants,
disability simulation or trial of an actual assistive device.

## Meeting 1: ask, plan and try, 25 minutes

1. **0-4:** Read the fictional request; each child names who asks and what they need.
2. **4-8:** Demonstrate two cups/path; draw a flat-tray plan and the pass condition.
3. **8-17:** Children draw a plan, count two cups and try moving them on a flat
   piece. Swap chooser/mover/checker roles; begin individual checks.
4. **17-21:** Child points to their plan and explains/indicates the criterion,
   not just appearance. Record whether both cups remained aboard.
5. **21-25:** Date drawings; store dry labeled kits; count and clear.

## Meeting 2: make and collect a first result, 25 minutes

1. **0-4:** Retrieve plans and re-read the request.
2. **4-8:** Model folding a low edge and attaching it with precut tape.
3. **8-17:** Build from the two cardboard pieces, at most ten sticks and ten strips.
   Each child handles an appropriate construction step and observes a slow test.
4. **17-21:** Record cups aboard, 0/1/2, and where a cup moved. Child shows a
   result on the plan; drawing/pointing is sufficient.
5. **21-25:** Retain the first-result sheets and dry models; reset trays.

## Meeting 3: evidence-led redesign, 25 minutes

1. **0-4:** Retrieve results. A missing result is collected freshly, not invented.
2. **4-8:** Model changing one edge, keeping the same cups/path and slow movement.
3. **8-17:** Partners propose one change, retest and swap roles. Count cups
   remaining and begin each child's evidence check.
4. **17-21:** Each child points to a changed part, compares two results and
   explains whether it met the requested need. Keep unsuccessful results too.
5. **21-25:** Save evidence, remove reusable sticks, sort dry scraps and clear.

## Success, questions, troubleshooting and access

Check requested need, plan, two-cup count and tested change separately.
Ask "What stayed the same? Which part changed first? Who requested this?"
Support: adult-started folds, two choices, dictated explanation. Challenge:
explain a material tradeoff without claiming real-device safety.
Insufficient cardboard: fold stiff school cardstock to the same size and
record the material used. No stored model: rebuild a simple first version
and take a fresh result. All work is indoors; never turn a missing kit into
an unsafe real-person challenge.

**Family:** We planned a desk model, moved two empty cups and retested one
change. Ask, "What did your test show?" Models are not real assistive devices;
no homework, donation or home testing.
