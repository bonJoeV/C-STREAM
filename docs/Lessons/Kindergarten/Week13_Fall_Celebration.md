## Vocabulary and background

**Evidence:** work/result supporting a claim. **Demonstrate:** show a skill.
**Favorite:** something enjoyed. **Practice:** work on a skill not yet secure.
Misconception: attending means mastery. A partner's result does not prove this
child's understanding. If asked about a missing portfolio, collect a new dated
demonstration and label it today, not as a past event.

## Exact sequence: 25 minutes

1. **0-4:** Celebrate effort; contrast "I liked robots" with "I predicted this path."
2. **4-8:** Model explaining a dated result. For fresh evidence demonstrate
   RIGHT, UP, STOP from the lower-left square and reset.
3. **8-17:** Each child chooses existing evidence and rehearses its explanation,
   or predicts/follows the fresh route. Swap turns; begin individual checks.
4. **17-21:** Child shows one actual result, explains it and changes a drawing/
   gesture if the listener cannot understand. Record any prompted evidence.
5. **21-25:** Neighboring partners share concurrently; retain dated sheets,
   return cards/crayons and clear. No 25-child presentation queue.

## Success, access, SAFETY and troubleshooting

Check claim matches actual evidence and communicates a meaning. Favorite
activity, certificate and attractive drawing are not skill evidence.
Support: two sample explanations and pointing. Challenge: name a limit or next
practice step. Desk-only large paper pieces; no snack/allergy or body-maze activity.
Child cannot recall: use the fresh route, not a leading invented memory.
No prior work: the supplied paper test is the complete indoor pathway.
Optional camera documents evidence but does not substitute for observing it.

**Family:** We celebrated an actual discovery and named something to practice.
Ask, "What can you show me, and what does it show?" Home work is optional.
