# Week 16: Star of Wonder

| Field | Plan |
|---|---|
| Grade / unit / time | K / Stars and models / one 25-minute meeting |
| Domains / big idea | S, A, M; a drawn pattern represents stars but does not make light or show actual distances |
| Objective / why | "I can count five star dots and explain that my picture is a model." Models help communicate ideas while leaving things out. |

## Before class and materials

Read [K routines](../../Resources/Kindergarten_Reference_Routines.md).
Draw five separated dots, numbered for the teacher only, in a simple zigzag
on one kit card. It is an invented pattern, not an official constellation.
Budget 5/8/10/13 cards for active 5/7/10/12 pair/trio teams.

| Allocation | 10 | 15 | 20 | 25 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Kit: five-dot model card | 5 | 8 | 10 | 13 |
| Student: sheet; crayon | 10 each | 15 each | 20 each | 25 each |

Teacher: timer/roster, demonstration from one card. No stickers, glitter, glue,
night visit, Sun viewing or device required.

## Vocabulary, background and source

**Star:** makes its own light. **Pattern:** arrangement we recognize.
**Model:** representation with limits. The Sun is a star; stars remain present
in daytime even when difficult to see. Our dots are not stars at equal real
distances; a crayon does not produce light. Source inspected October 4:
[NASA stars](https://science.nasa.gov/universe/stars/), teacher paraphrase.
Misconception: stars physically have five points. Say pointed paper shapes
are symbols; real stars are not paper cutouts.
If asked about Magi, use only the school's approved religion material;
number, travel time and astronomical explanation are not needed or asserted here.

## Exact sequence: 25 minutes

1. **0-4:** Invite wonder; compare a real light-producing star with a picture.
2. **4-8:** Model one-to-one counting of five dots and drawing their arrangement.
   Explain the picture does not show actual size/distance.
3. **8-17:** Partners point/count and each make a model with 3-5 dots. Swap
   roles; child chooses spacing to help a partner recognize it. Begin checks.
4. **17-21:** Each child counts their dots and explains/points to a model limit:
   not actual stars, not producing light, or not real distances.
5. **21-25:** Neighbor partners interpret the pictures; retain dated work,
   return cards/crayons and clear.

## Success, questions, SAFETY and troubleshooting

Check count matches dots, recognizable representation and one limit separately.
Ask "What is real in the sky? What can this picture not tell us?"
Support: three large dots, tactile raised paper marks/adult scribing. Challenge:
same five dots drawn differently, explaining that lines are our drawing choice.
No Sun/night observations or dark room. Child draws pointed stars: accept the
symbol, then explicitly distinguish shape from the real object.
No dark paper: ordinary paper works; no supply purchase is necessary.

**Family:** We counted a star-pattern model and explained what it cannot show.
Ask, "Does your picture make light like a star?" No required evening outing.
