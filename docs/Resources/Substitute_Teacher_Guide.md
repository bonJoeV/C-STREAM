# C-STREAM substitute teacher guide

C = Catholic identity, R = religion/faith integration, A = arts including music,
M = mathematics. Thank you for leading hands-on learning. **October 2026.**
Use the actual scheduled lesson, not a generic assurance that every old lesson
is ready. [Readiness review](../Review/Substitute_Readiness.md).

## SAFETY: stop rather than improvise

No button cells, latex balloons, projectile activities or neodymium magnets in
K. No unknown chemicals, food experiments, glass, hot surfaces, unsafe cold
exposure or electrical repair. Teacher controls batteries/clip leads/small loads.
Do not use unfamiliar electric/magnetic equipment without a verified pretest.
Stop, isolate unsafe materials, move to a seated safe task and notify the
responsible school adult. Injury, suspected ingestion of a battery/magnet or
other urgent danger requires the school's emergency escalation immediately.
Do not send pupils hunting for missing dangerous parts.

## Fallback A: paper structure comparison

**Objective:** Compare flat and folded paper under the same span and load.
**Big idea:** Shape can affect a structure; a fair comparison keeps test
conditions the same. **Catholic connection:** Reliable design serves people;
shared resources are used responsibly. No "God is an engineer" comparison.
**Technology Requirement:** None. **Difficulty:** introductory comparison.
**Prep:** 10 minutes if counted stock/supports exist; cleanup included below.
**Vocabulary:** span = gap between supports; load = what a structure supports;
compare = notice how results are alike/different.

**Background/misconception:** Folded and flat paper may respond differently.
The result can be the same under a small load; report that honestly.
A collapse is information, not failure of the student. If asked why a fold
matters, show the changed shape; do not promise that every fold always improves it.

| Scope/material | Rate | 10 pupils | 15 | 20 | 25 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Student response paper (or oral response) | 1/child | 10 | 15 | 20 | 25 |
| Team paper | 2/team | 8 | 10 | 14 | 18 |
| Team index-card test loads | 10/team | 40 | 50 | 70 | 90 |
| Team ruler | 1/team | 4 | 5 | 7 | 9 |
| Stable support books/blocks | 2/team | 8 | 10 | 14 | 18 |
| Teacher | 2 demonstration sheets, 10 cards, timer, evidence roster | One class set in addition to team issue quantities | | | |

Pencils/crayons are school basics. Total paper at 25 = 25 + 18 + 2 = 45 sheets;
total cards = 90 + 10 = 100. **No scissors, coins, tape, devices or donations.**
Books/supports must be confirmed; without them use fallback B, not an unsafe stack.

**Before class:** Set stable supports 10 cm apart on each desk; lay one sheet
flat across. Fold the second lengthwise into an accordion with adult modeling.
Try adding cards one by one at the center in both tests; reset with the same
gap and count. Test before class; reduce the gap if neither supports even one
card and record that shared change. No escalating to heavy falling weights.
K adult places loads; child still predicts/points/compares.

### Exact native timing (choose the grade column)

| Step | K 25 | 1-2 30 | 3-4 40 | 5-6 45 | What to do/say |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hook/purpose | 0-3 | 0-3 | 0-4 | 0-4 | "What might a fold change?" Explain objective and stewardship |
| Model/vocabulary/safety | 3-7 | 3-8 | 4-10 | 4-11 | Align supports, show flat/folded examples, demonstrate one load placement |
| Build/test first comparison | 7-14 | 8-18 | 10-24 | 11-27 | Students take turns placing paper/cards; count or point to result |
| Improve/retest | 14-19 | 18-23 | 24-32 | 27-36 | Change one paper shape; keep gap/load placement. "What stayed the same?" |
| Individual check | 19-22 | 23-27 | 32-37 | 36-41 | "Show the result. What stayed the same in the test?" |
| Cleanup/close | 22-25 | 27-30 | 37-40 | 41-45 | Count cards/rulers/supports, retain dry paper, clear desks, brief thanks |

**Meets:** Each child identifies the observed comparison (including equal
results) and shows one common condition. For K, adult reads/models and accepts
pointing/gesture. Use [Show, Try, Tell](../Student_Resources/Show_Try_Tell_Card.md).
**Support:** Pre-fold paper, steady supports, scribe or allow a large gesture.
**Challenge:** Repeat the comparison and explain whether it supports the first
result; older pupils can compare equal-material versions without untaught ratios.
**At 25:** Nine seated teams, rotating turns; check children during tests plus
simultaneous show/draw exit. Mark unobserved evidence NE and leave a follow-up.
**Troubleshoot:** Sliding supports -> stabilize or stop that test; equal results
-> record them, not "wrong"; time short -> stop new construction, keep check/cleanup.
**Family copy:** "We compared paper structures using the same gap and loads.
Ask: 'What stayed the same, and what changed?' Optional: discuss a folded object
already nearby. No home project needed."

## Fallback B: no devices, no consumable supplies, or unsafe weather

**Objective:** Make and distinguish a direct observation from a guess.
**Materials:** A familiar safe classroom object/view already present; no handling
of hazardous objects. **Technology:** None. **Prep:** 2 minutes.
**Vocabulary/background:** Observe = notice directly; guess/inference = an idea
that needs more evidence. "Looks wooden" does not prove material composition.

Follow the same native timing columns above:
1. Hook: "What can we actually notice about this desk/door/window view?"
2. Model one observation and one inference; safety: seated, no climbing/touching.
3. Each child notices/points/tells one feature; partners compare.
4. Identify which claim can be checked by looking; refine it.
5. Each child shows one observable feature (older pupils distinguish a guess).
6. Restore room, finish the evidence notes and close.

**Meets:** Child points/names an observable feature; for a stated older-grade
inference objective also explains why a guess needs evidence. Read aloud, model,
allow gesture/gaze/home-language response. Challenge: propose a safe way to check
a guess. Record objective change; no claim of engineering/programming mastery.
Cleanup: no materials to collect; reset seating and leave evidence/NE.
Family: "We practiced observing carefully. Ask: 'What did you notice directly?'"
At -10 F remain indoors; if there is no safe view, use an ordinary indoor object.

## Device/supply contingency and return note

Paper algorithms preserve sequence/debugging concepts, **not real programming or
robot-operation mastery**. Defer those objectives if devices fail; do not invent
accounts or app permissions. [Technology Plan](../Review/Technology_Plan.md).

Leave lesson/steps completed, actual individual evidence or NE, objectives
changed/deferred, restock counts, broken parts OUT OF SERVICE, safety/source
questions and the next check. Named evidence stays under school privacy practice.
See [handoff template](../Templates/Substitute_Lesson_Template.md) and
[Local Standards](../Review/Local_Standards.md); official alignment and unverified
Church/supplier claims are VERIFICATION REQUIRED.
