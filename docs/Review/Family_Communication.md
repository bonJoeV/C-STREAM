# Family communication system

**October 2026.** C-STREAM is Catholic identity + science + technology +
religion/faith integration + engineering + arts (including music) + mathematics.
C shapes purpose/dignity/stewardship; R is explicit faith teaching/reflection.
Music belongs within A; M means mathematics. Art involves observation,
composition, form, performance or communication, not decoration alone.

## One copy system, not competing guides

1. At unit launch, complete the [newsletter block](../Templates/Family_Newsletter_Template.md).
2. At completion, change planned verbs to what students actually did and include
   a specific result/skill. Send an additional update when objectives change.
3. Keep the teacher-only trace: lesson path, objective, selected
   [local code](./Local_Standards.md), individual prompt and evidence threshold.
   Verify official/Church source claims separately. **VERIFICATION REQUIRED**
   for unverified quotations, diocesan requirements or biographies.
4. Copy through the school's **actual** approved newsletter/email/paper route.
   Provide translation/read-aloud support as locally arranged; no invented platform.
5. Keep learning at school. No routine homework, purchases, donations, accounts,
   outdoor exposure, home device, photo submission or event attendance required.
   All home activities below are optional, ungraded and can be skipped.

Existing entry points [Weekly Parent Sheet](../Resources/Weekly_Parent_Sheet_Template.md)
and [Parent Communication Guide](../Resources/Parent_Communication_Guide.md)
use the same block. Do not send two contradictory versions.
If an exceptional required home task is proposed, document why it cannot
reasonably happen in class, obtain school approval and provide an equally
accessible school alternative; the default remains no routine homework.

## Ready-to-adapt unit examples for every grade

These are **example copy**, not claims that these units have already been taught
or an independent scope/sequence. Adapt to the actual band lesson and assessed
objective. Do not claim device programming if the class used paper.

### Kindergarten - Notice and make patterns

**What we explored:** Repeating patterns and careful observation.
**What students did:** Sorted large classroom objects, built a two-part pattern
and drew or pointed to its next part.
**What they learned:** To notice a repeating unit and show what comes next.
**Our Catholic connection:** We practiced caring for shared materials and giving
each classmate a turn as we explored creation with gratitude.
**Ask your child:** "Can you show the two parts of your pattern and what comes next?"
**Try this at home, optional:** Make a clap-pause-clap-pause pattern together
for one minute; use silent hand motions if sound is uncomfortable. No supplies.

Teacher trace: each child completes/explains a pattern orally or by pointing;
adult modeling/read-aloud replaces independent reading. Select the matching
arts/math code from [Local Standards](./Local_Standards.md).

### Grade 1 - Sound and rhythmic phrases

**What we explored:** How a pattern of sound and silence makes a rhythmic phrase.
**What students did:** Compared gentle tapping sounds and composed a four-beat
phrase using claps and pauses.
**What they learned:** To repeat a phrase and explain one intentional sound/silence
choice. Music was an artistic activity, not just background noise.
**Our Catholic connection:** We used our gifts to listen to and include one
another; a silent pattern was an equally welcome response.
**Ask your child:** "Can you perform your four beats and show where the pause is?"
**Try this at home, optional:** Echo one four-beat phrase with hands or silent
motions for a minute. No instruments or recording required.

Teacher trace: individual phrase/repetition, not participation or singing talent;
select the taught A/math/science competency only where evidenced.

### Grade 2 - Directions and debugging

**What we explored:** Algorithms: ordered instructions, and how to fix an error.
**What students did:** Used large arrow cards to move a paper pointer on a desk
grid, tested the route, and changed an incorrect turn.
**What they learned:** To explain how the changed instruction affected the route.
This was unplugged computing, not a claim that software was programmed.
**Our Catholic connection:** We considered how clear, respectful directions help
other people and why responsibility matters when using tools.
**Ask your child:** "Which arrow did you change, and where did the pointer go after that?"
**Try this at home, optional:** Give a seated partner two safe hand-motion
instructions, then reverse them and compare. No blindfold, walking maze or food.

Teacher trace: individual sequence trace and correction; actual device execution
is assessed later if it is a separate objective.

### Grade 3 - Paper bridges and fair comparisons

**What we explored:** How changing a paper structure can change its performance.
**What students did:** Planned, built, tested and improved paper bridges under
common test conditions.
**What they learned:** To name an observed result, keep test conditions the same,
and explain a change using evidence, even when a bridge collapsed.
**Our Catholic connection:** We considered how reliable structures serve people
and why using shared materials responsibly matters.
**Ask your child:** "What stayed the same in your two tests, and what did you change?"
**Try this at home, optional:** Look at the shape of an ordinary book spine or
folded paper already nearby and talk about what a fold might change. No building,
weights, purchase or homework submission required.

Teacher trace: use the [rebuilt bridge lesson](../Lessons/Grades_3-4_YearA/Week10_Bridge_Engineering.md)
and its exact individual check; this copy does not impose a different span/load.

### Grade 4 - Indoor insulation and evidence

**What we explored:** Comparing how materials affect temperature change.
**What students did:** Used a teacher-prepared indoor comparison, measured at
the same planned times and represented results on paper.
**What they learned:** To compare temperature changes with units and describe
what was held constant. Conclusions were limited to the classroom test.
**Our Catholic connection:** We considered responsible resource use and how
good evidence can inform care for people and creation.
**Ask your child:** "Which comparison result supported your claim, and what stayed the same?"
**Try this at home, optional:** Discuss why a familiar blanket may help keep a
person warm. No heating, outdoor experiment or materials needed.

Teacher trace: publish this only after the planned safe indoor investigation;
no claim about outdoor testing, hot liquids or guaranteed material performance.

### Grade 5 - Closed electrical paths

**What we explored:** What happens when a protected low-voltage circuit path is
closed or opened.
**What students did:** Took supervised turns connecting a protected AA pack and
current-limited light module, then opened the path and compared outputs.
**What they learned:** To identify a complete path and explain the observed
change. Drawing a circuit alone was not the practical test.
**Our Catholic connection:** We discussed using knowledge responsibly and caring
for equipment and people rather than wasting energy or taking unsafe shortcuts.
**Ask your child:** "What changed when the path opened, and which observation showed that?"
**Try this at home, optional:** Point to a familiar room-light switch and discuss
its purpose without opening it, handling batteries or touching wiring.

Teacher trace: each child's actual supervised circuit action and explanation;
record any missing hands-on evidence as NE.

### Grade 6 - Design choices for people

**What we explored:** How criteria, cost and user needs influence a design.
**What students did:** Compared classroom paper/model prototypes and communicated
a design choice using measured evidence and a clear visual explanation.
**What they learned:** To defend a choice and name a tradeoff or limitation.
Our models were not certified devices or real-world solutions.
**Our Catholic connection:** Human dignity and stewardship informed whom the
design included and how resources were used.
**Ask your child:** "Which result supported your choice, and who might need a different design?"
**Try this at home, optional:** Discuss one familiar door handle or container:
who might find it easy or difficult to use? No disability simulation, purchase
or alteration of household equipment.

Teacher trace: individual evidence/tradeoff explanation; select only practiced
engineering, arts, math and Catholic competencies.

## Project/events/donations and privacy

The school provides required materials and class work time. An optional event
celebrates the same evidence, not family-funded polish. Invitations include a
non-attendance route (in-class sharing or a generic newsletter summary).
Requests for clean recyclables say explicitly: "Donations are optional; every
student will have school-provided supplies." Staff screen all donated items.

Use anonymous/general class descriptions by default. Student names, images,
audio, scores and identifiable work require the school's verified consent/
privacy process; no public upload or personal account is assumed.

## Parent simulation

A teacher can answer "What is my child learning?" with: **a specific concept,
student action, individual success evidence, Catholic decision and next
learning step**. A bare "fun craft" or "used an iPad" is insufficient.
Progression/official alignment belongs to the shared review; local codes are
not state or diocesan accreditation claims.
