# Local K-6 C-STREAM standards

**Local program framework. Planning date: 2026-10-04. School year: 2026-27.**
These are original school-program competencies, **not official MDE or
Archdiocesan standards and not approved by either body**. They integrate Catholic
identity with science, technology, engineering, art and mathematics without
replacing the school's religion or primary subject curricula.

The fixed contract is **18 codes: three per domain**. Keep codes and meanings
stable when projects, materials, schedules or rotations change. Religion/faith
is integrated in the Catholic domain and across practice; there is no additional
`CST-R*` domain. Grade-specific performance targets are in
[Learning Progression](Learning_Progression.md), not new standard codes.

## Competency catalog

Evidence examples are possible individual checks, not mandatory projects or
claims that existing lessons already assess these skills.

| Code | Competency and observable intent | Examples of individual evidence |
|---|---|---|
| CST-C1 | **Faith/reason and honest evidence.** Use reason to study creation; distinguish an observation, prediction, explanation and taught faith connection; report results truthfully, including uncertainty. | K-1: tell what was actually seen rather than hoped for. 2-3: revise a prediction using a recorded observation. 4-6: defend a claim, identify a limit, and explain why honest inquiry is compatible with the taught Catholic view. |
| CST-C2 | **Dignity/service/inclusive ethical design.** Respect every person's worth; identify a real need, include others, and consider who benefits or might be excluded or harmed. | K-1: offer an accessible role or adapt a shared tool. 2-3: explain how a design helps its user. 4-6: evaluate an access/privacy concern and justify a safer, more inclusive design. |
| CST-C3 | **Stewardship/resources/work.** Care for creation and shared resources; use materials responsibly; value careful work and gifts used in service. | K-1: sort reusable scraps and explain their care. 2-3: choose less wasteful materials without losing function. 4-6: compare resource use, durability and effects on people; explain a work/service connection. |
| CST-S1 | **Observations/questions/evidence.** Ask investigable questions; observe, record and use evidence to explain a phenomenon without overstating a conclusion. | K-1: point out an observed change. 2-3: explain a pattern with recorded observations. 4-6: support a claim with data, uncertainty and a useful follow-up question. |
| CST-S2 | **Investigations/testing/variables.** Predict, plan and conduct safe tests; increasingly identify what changes, what is measured and what stays consistent; repeat and compare results. | K-1: show a prediction and follow a teacher-set test. 2-3: identify the one changed feature. 4-6: present a repeatable investigation, controlled variables and a limitation. |
| CST-S3 | **Physical/life/Earth systems/models.** Observe and model age-appropriate physical, living and Earth/space systems; use models to explain relationships and identify their limits. | K-1: depict an observed object, living thing or weather change. 2-3: label interacting parts. 4-6: use a model to explain a change and describe what it omits. Collect physical, life and Earth/space examples across the year. |
| CST-T1 | **Tools/input-output/troubleshooting.** Select and use appropriate tools safely; describe a system's input and output; diagnose problems using evidence rather than random changes. | K-1: select a ruler or safe tool and describe its job. 2-3: trace an input/action/output. 4-6: explain a systematic troubleshooting sequence. A non-digital tool can support local evidence but not an external device-use claim. |
| CST-T2 | **Algorithms/loops/conditions/debugging.** Create, follow, represent and improve instructions; progress from sequence to repetition and conditions; locate and correct errors. | K-1: give and fix movement-card instructions. 2-3: trace a loop and an if/then choice. 4-6: test an algorithm on contrasting inputs and explain a correction. Devices are optional for this local competency unless the chosen objective requires execution on one. |
| CST-T3 | **Data/digital citizenship/technology impacts.** Represent and question data; protect privacy, credit ideas and communicate responsibly; evaluate technology's benefits, limitations and effects on people. | K-1: sort anonymous objects and recognize private information in a scenario. 2-3: explain a data display and a safe sharing choice. 4-6: evaluate data quality, access, privacy and a technology tradeoff. |
| CST-E1 | **Problems/criteria/constraints.** Define a user-centered problem, observable success criteria and real limits before choosing a solution. | K-1: name what a model must do and one limit. 2-3: distinguish a success requirement from a material limit. 4-6: write and justify measurable criteria and constraints for a user. |
| CST-E2 | **Build/test/redesign.** Make a prototype, test against criteria, record what happened, and improve deliberately using evidence. | K-1: identify a weak part and make a change. 2-3: compare before/after tests. 4-6: document iterations and distinguish measured improvement from an attractive but untested change. |
| CST-E3 | **Tradeoffs/communicate solutions.** Compare alternatives, explain design choices and limitations, and communicate an evidence-supported recommendation to an audience. | K-1: show a choice and give a reason. 2-3: compare two options against a criterion. 4-6: defend a recommendation using performance, cost/resources, access and tradeoffs. |
| CST-A1 | **Observational sketches/models.** Observe closely and represent form, parts, relationships and details in drawings or made models that help understanding. | K-1: show a noticed detail. 2-3: label a drawing/model so someone else can interpret it. 4-6: use multiple views, relative sizes or annotations for an accurate visual explanation. Artistic polish is not the criterion. |
| CST-A2 | **Purposeful pattern/form/design.** Use pattern, shape, form, composition, color or texture intentionally for an aesthetic or functional purpose; explain choices. | K-1: create and describe a repeat or form. 2-3: choose visual features to direct attention or support function. 4-6: compare alternatives and justify choices for purpose and audience. Decoration alone is not evidence. |
| CST-A3 | **Artistic communication/critique/revision.** Convey ideas visually or artistically; discuss meaning and intent, give respectful specific feedback, and revise while preserving or clarifying purpose. | K-1: explain an idea and respond to a helpful comment. 2-3: describe how a visual choice communicates meaning. 4-6: give criterion-based critique and show a reasoned revision. |
| CST-M1 | **Count/compare/measure.** Count or compare relevant quantities; estimate and measure with appropriate tools and units; consider precision and repeated measurements as appropriate. | K-1: count materials and compare lengths. 2-3: record a measurement with units. 4-6: choose a tool/unit, repeat a measurement and explain precision appropriate to the task. |
| CST-M2 | **Data/pattern representation.** Organize quantities and patterns in objects, drawings, tables or suitable graphs; interpret a representation without distorting its message. | K-1: make a tally or object graph. 2-3: label a table/bar display and explain a pattern. 4-6: choose a defensible representation, identify an unusual value and avoid unsupported conclusions. |
| CST-M3 | **Grade-appropriate quantities/operations/reasoning.** Use quantities, operations and relationships already taught or explicitly scaffolded in classroom mathematics; explain strategies, units and reasonableness in practical problems. | K-1: show a quantity or joining/separating strategy. 2-3: justify a count, equal-group or measurement calculation. 4-6: solve and check an appropriate multi-step problem; use fractions, decimals or ratios only when their prerequisites are taught. |

## What counts as demonstrated learning?

Use a short observation, explanation, drawing, model, data record or performance.
Offer oral, pointing, tactile, large-format, scribed or assistive responses where
they preserve the skill being assessed. Reading stamina, handwriting, speech
fluency, fine-motor speed and expensive supplies are not proxies for competence.

| Local assessment level | Decision rule |
|---|---|
| Beginning | Student attempts the task but cannot yet demonstrate the stated grade target, even with the offered support. Record the next support. |
| Developing | Student demonstrates part of the target, or needs a conceptual prompt/model to complete it. |
| Meets expectation | Student demonstrates the full stated grade target and explains/shows the relevant reasoning, using permitted access supports but without an adult or peer supplying the answer. |
| Extends learning | Student meets the target and transfers, critiques or improves it in a new appropriate situation. More complicated or prettier work alone is not extension. |

**Mastery rule:** A group product, exposure, attendance, participation, or a
single lucky result cannot establish individual mastery. At an expected-mastery
checkpoint, collect **at least two dated observations on separate occasions,
including an individually attributable demonstration and a changed context or
follow-up explanation**. Both must meet the grade target. This is a local
assessment policy, not an external agency requirement. If evidence is missing,
record **not yet assessed**; if it is partial, record **developing** and reteach.

Assess Catholic competencies through understanding of taught ideas and
observable truthful, respectful, responsible choices. Do not grade prayer
intensity, private belief, a child's perceived holiness, family religious
practice, or willingness to disclose personal circumstances. Distinguish a
student's design critique from the worth of the student.

## Traceability contract for integration

A future lesson/assessment record should identify:

1. **Local code and grade target** from this catalog/progression.
2. **Grade, schedule and rotation, unit and lesson**; combined grades retain
   separate success criteria.
3. **Student action and evidence:** what each learner will do, how the teacher
   will check it, and what meets expectation.
4. **Instructional stage:** introduced, developing, reinforced or
   expected-mastery checkpoint, separate from the student's assessed result.
5. **External mapping, if claimed:** source/edition, exact verified number and
   grade/band, paraphrased requirement, matching task, individual assessment
   and evidence location. Label unresolved rows **VERIFICATION REQUIRED**.

Until lesson tasks and assessments are inspected, a source connection is only
a **candidate**, not a verified alignment. Do not infer official science
alignment from `CST-S*`, CSTA alignment from `CST-T*`, or OMCE approval from
`CST-C*`. See [Standards Sources](Standards_Sources.md).

## Scope limits

- Eighteen competencies are a durable integrated-skills framework, not all
  religion, science, CS, engineering, arts, mathematics, ELA or social studies
  standards. They do not certify complete subject coverage.
- `CST-S3` requires physical, life and Earth/space contexts over time; one
  bridge or robotics unit cannot stand for the entire science program.
- Local art emphasizes observation, visual design and communication; it does
  not represent full dance, music, theater or media arts provision.
- Unplugged computing can assess algorithms. It cannot by itself prove actual
  device operation, executable programming, online research or digital-tool
  use when an external benchmark specifically requires those actions.
- Mathematical sophistication follows the primary math curriculum. Extension
  for an older student must not become an unsupported prerequisite for a
  younger student in a combined-grade class.
- The [planned progression](Learning_Progression.md) is a design target,
  not a finding that every existing lesson or rotation already supplies it.
