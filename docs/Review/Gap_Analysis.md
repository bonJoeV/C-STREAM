# Curriculum gap analysis

A gap can be **missing content**, **insufficient depth**, **missing individual
evidence**, **an unsuitable prerequisite**, or **an unusable teaching procedure**.
The existence of a file or tag does not close it. The detailed band reviews
and audit rationale fields provide the lesson-level evidence.

**Autonomous K closure:** all 37 K documents are complete references, with
dated weather comparison, hypothetical privacy/adult-help scenarios, requested
book criteria/tradeoffs and formerly audit-only individual checks implemented.
Every K primary-material term is cataloged; twelve arts candidates have exact
source-verified identifiers. This closes those document-level gaps, not actual
school stock, official full-subject coverage or observed mastery.

**All-grade closeout:** the other 214 lesson documents are also complete
conditional references. Fair retests, individual checks, grade-specific
support/challenge, complete timed sequences and explicit material/pathway
boundaries replace the earlier package queues. Baseline weaknesses remain
historical evidence, not a claim that every old defect is still active.
Full external subject coverage and actual mastery remain separate checks.

## Program-wide gaps and controls

| Area to investigate | Baseline concern or evidence limit | Improvement and closure evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Official standards | No structured, verified benchmark-to-task-to-assessment map in the baseline | Source register and local map added; official benchmark alignment remains VERIFICATION REQUIRED until reviewed against the applicable version. |
| Grade progression | Combined-grade activities do not automatically define seven distinct expectations | Local K-6 performance progression; collect individual evidence at the younger/older expectation rather than awarding mastery for exposure. |
| Pacing | Legacy planner/menu totals double-count time and extend beyond the claimed year | Corrected calendar and document-level meeting records; compare the chosen track with the actual school timetable. |
| Substitute usability | General guide refers to quick-reference packets without proving they exist | Revised reference lessons and common template; complete remaining scheduled P1 lessons and conduct actual kit walkthroughs. |
| Early readers | Written journals, vocabulary and independent directions can hide a reading prerequisite | Model, point, draw, tell and manipulate; verify an emerging reader can meet the objective without another child doing the thinking. |
| Devices and computing | Equipment/app use can be mistaken for computational thinking | Require an algorithm trace/debugging explanation; distinguish that from real digital execution and hardware operation. |
| Indoor access | Nature, weather and growth topics do not guarantee an indoor pathway | Specify the indoor procedure and objective; prepared data/models must not be described as a child's firsthand measurement. |
| Materials/cost | Specialty kits and assumed school stocks obscure a minimum budget | Normalized core inventory, budget ceilings and kits; verify local stock and distinguish optional legacy equipment from core purchases. |
| Assessment | Group products and participation can hide individual understanding | A named oral/drawn/demonstrated evidence check and objective-linked rubric for each scheduled lesson. |
| Catholic identity | Prayers, metaphors, saint references and technological enthusiasm are not enough | Students give an age-appropriate reason about truth, dignity, stewardship or service; verify historical/scriptural attributions. |
| Arts | Digital tools and decoration can replace genuine artistic learning | Observational sketches, purposeful form/pattern, communication, feedback and artistic revision; assess the stated art objective, not polish. |
| Homework/equity | Take-home continuation can make household support a hidden prerequisite | No routine homework; class evidence and optional no-purchase family questions. |
| Safety | "Safe materials," unspecified samples and small electrical parts are not sufficient controls | Exact approved supplies, adult-only steps, stop rules and local policy checks; hold unresolved P0 activities. |
| Year A/B continuity | A returning student's changed project does not prove more sophisticated reasoning | Reestablish prerequisites in either entry year; preserve a small portfolio and increase independence/evidence, not simply product size. |

## Content questions that must not disappear

The repository has named activities in physical, life and Earth science,
structures, weather, light/circuits, robots/coding, art and service. This is
evidence of **available contexts**, not complete subject coverage. Use the
lesson map to locate intended evidence, then check the following across the
selected schedule and both rotation entry years:

| Competency | Minimum closure evidence |
|---|---|
| Electricity before middle school | A safe, age-appropriate complete/open-circuit investigation with an explanation; neither a craft light nor a robot alone proves understanding. |
| Data interpretation | Each student interprets a table, graph or organized comparison appropriate to the grade. |
| Measurement progression | Count/compare -> consistent unit -> record/compare -> repeated measurements -> justified precision and limitations. |
| Engineering redesign | An actual second test after a justified change, not only a drawing of an imagined improvement. |
| Life science | Observations or an explicitly labeled model explain living things, change or interdependence; plants need follow-up time. |
| Earth/space science | Evidence/modeling of weather, seasonal change or systems, with a scientifically accurate explanation. |
| Physical science | Students investigate an effect such as force, motion, light, sound, energy or circuits rather than only name it. |
| Unplugged computing | Students trace, predict, debug or revise instructions; later grades encounter loops/conditions/data as appropriate. |
| Technology ethics | A reasoned decision about users, accessibility, resources, privacy or impacts; not just a device rule list. |
| Catholic scientific heritage | A verified, relevant example with a distinction between biography, Church teaching and scientific evidence. |
| Stewardship | A concrete decision about reuse, waste, care or service and a student explanation. |
| Hands-on mathematics | Quantities/measurements/data used to reason about the activity, not decorative numbers. |
| Student choice | A choice within understandable criteria/constraints, followed by a reason. |
| Independent and collaborative work | Team activity plus an individual check; roles do not permanently assign one child all reasoning or tool use. |
| Communication | A drawing, model, oral explanation or display communicates evidence to an audience and receives feedback. |
| Learning from failure | Students describe what a test showed, change an appropriate feature and try again without blaming the student. |

**Do not "fill a gap" by adding another topic title.** Identify the exact
grade, rotation, meeting, student action and evidence; reduce competing
activities if necessary. The [scope and sequence](Scope_and_Sequence.md)
prioritizes fewer, better investigations.
