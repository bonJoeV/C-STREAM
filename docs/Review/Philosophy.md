# K-6 Catholic C-STREAM philosophy

**Local program philosophy. Planning date: 2026-10-04. School year: 2026-27.**
Not an MDE- or Archdiocese-approved curriculum.

## Purpose

C-STREAM joins Catholic identity and faith with science, technology, engineering,
art and mathematics. Students study creation carefully, ask honest questions,
make and improve things, reason with quantities, communicate creatively and
consider the dignity of people affected by their choices. Their growing skills
can serve God, neighbor and the common good in everyday life and many vocations,
not only future technical careers.

Catholic identity is more than an opening prayer. It shapes truthful reporting,
stewardship, respectful collaboration, ethical technology use, purposeful work
and service. Faith and reason strengthen inquiry rather than competing with it.
Use an authentic connection when it deepens learning; do not attach a forced
theological metaphor to every fact. Distinguish Church teaching, devotional
tradition, an educator's analogy and scientific evidence. See the verified
teaching and guidance in [Standards Sources](Standards_Sources.md).

## Classroom commitments

- **Hands-on first; technology for a reason.** Begin with observation, objects,
  sketches, paper algorithms, cardboard models, rulers and reusable materials.
  Use devices when they materially enable programming, research, measurement,
  accessibility or another objective. Device consumption is not computer science.
  State **None / Optional / Recommended / Required**, explain Required, and
  provide a valid low-tech fallback where possible. Never claim the fallback
  proves a digital operation it does not perform.
- **Real disciplinary learning.** Science asks for evidence; engineering tests
  solutions against needs and limits; mathematics supports reasoning; art
  involves intentional observation, form, meaning, critique and revision, not
  just decorating a finished product. Lessons need not give equal time to every
  domain, but the year should intentionally revisit all six local domains.
- **Failure is information.** A weak prototype or unexpected result is a reason
  to investigate, not a judgment on the student. Reward evidence-based revision,
  intellectual honesty and thoughtful choices rather than winning or polish.
- **Safe and inclusive by design.** Use school-approved materials and age-appropriate
  tools; make adult-only actions explicit. Avoid unknown substances, unsafe
  projectiles, mains electricity, loose button batteries and strong small magnets.
  Address allergies, choking hazards, water, sharp/hot tools and outdoor exposure.
  Use fictional or anonymous data rather than sensitive student/family information.
  Do not use classmates as experimental subjects for risky activities.
- **Every child can show learning.** Model short oral/picture directions for K-1.
  Offer pointing, building, acting, drawing, oral explanation, scribing and
  assistive tools. Preserve the target while reducing irrelevant reading,
  writing, sensory or motor demands. Rotate roles; do not let a confident partner
  supply all the reasoning. Challenge adds depth, not compulsory expense.
- **Practical, reusable and weather-resilient.** Prefer low-cost multipurpose kits
  and light preparation. Plan for 10, 15, 20 or 25 students with explicit quantities,
  team roles and cleanup. Outdoor learning has an indoor alternative; January
  cold, limited daylight or a cancelled outing must not erase an essential target.
- **No routine homework.** Teach and assess core learning at school. Family
  conversations or observations are optional, ungraded invitations with an
  in-class equivalent. Participation never depends on home devices, purchases,
  donations, transport or an adult teaching the lesson.

## Fit the school's periods

These are example single-period budgets, **including safety, setup and cleanup**.
Adjust for the actual activity; do not squeeze a full investigation into a
shorter period by removing evidence or safety. Use a second meeting when needed.

| Period | Hook, objective, safety and demonstration | Explore/build/test, including setup | Individual check and explanation | Cleanup |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 25 minutes | 4 | 13 | 4 | 4 |
| 30 minutes | 5 | 16 | 5 | 4 |
| 40 minutes | 6 | 23 | 6 | 5 |
| 45 minutes | 7 | 26 | 7 | 5 |

In short periods, use one meaningful test or design choice. In longer periods,
add repeated testing or revision, not filler. Some processes need several
meetings; store labelled work and resume rather than assigning completion at home.

## Evidence, continuity and honest claims

The [eighteen local standards](Local_Standards.md) and
[seven-grade progression](Learning_Progression.md) survive lesson changes.
Combined-grade classes share a context but use separate grade-level success
criteria. Weekly/bi-weekly schedules and A/B rotations need their own checked
opportunities to learn and demonstrate those targets.

Keep assessment light but real: observation, an individual explanation, a
sketch, a tested model or a data record. Participation and attractive group
products are not mastery. Apply the individual evidence rule at planned
checkpoints, and record what still needs teaching or assessment. Evaluate
understanding of taught Catholic ideas and ethical choices, never private belief,
perceived holiness or family religious practice.

External standards claims require a verified edition, grade/benchmark, matching
lesson task and individual assessment. These local competencies do **not**
certify full science, mathematics, arts, ELA, social studies, CS or religion
coverage. The program complements primary subjects and OMCE guidance; local
school/diocesan review remains necessary.

**Teacher design test:** Can a capable teacher or substitute explain the purpose,
prepare the kit, teach safely, include every learner, finish cleanup and identify
each child's learning within the available period? If not, simplify the activity,
improve its supports or allow another meeting without weakening the target.
