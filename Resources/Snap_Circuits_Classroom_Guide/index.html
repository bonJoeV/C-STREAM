    OLP Snap Circuits classroom guide¶

    Reported stock: 8 individual Snap Circuits STEM Classroom Activity Kits, item OHM-135, reported 2026-10-04. This means eight student activity kits, not eight six-kit classroom packs. See the holdings record. Physical inspection and classroom approval are still required.

    Before teaching: release the hardware pathway¶

    Allow 30 minutes for first inspection/pretesting, plus time to obtain any missing instructions or parts; allow 15 minutes for repeat preparation. Leave a prepared kit, approved diagram, component list and this guide for a substitute. A substitute must not invent a circuit or approve unfamiliar parts.

    1. Label the individual kits SC01-SC08. Record the actual manufacturer model, instruction/workbook edition, contents, condition, location and checker. OHM-135 is the supplier item number, not a verified manufacturer model.
    2. Read the matching in-box manufacturer instructions. Select one documented battery/slide-switch/lamp project with a complete return path. Record its project/page number and component IDs on the teacher's setup card. Use the same project in every station; do not mix modules from different systems.
    3. Verify manufacturer age guidance for the actual pupils, battery access, holder protection and the school's electrical-safety requirements. The supplied holder is not assumed equivalent to a screw-secured, current-protected AA holder. If it does not meet school requirements, use the protected-AA alternative or paper route, not improvised covers or protection.
    4. Adult installs only the battery type/count specified for that project. Record b = fitted cells per station and the chemistry/voltage. Count fitted cells as stations * b; reserve complete kits need their own checked cells. Keep replacement cells separately in adult-controlled storage. Do not assume the kit uses the earlier lessons' two-AA allocation.
    5. Pretest the documented switch-OFF/light-off and switch-ON/light-on states twice. An LED project is allowed only if the matching instructions document polarity and required current limiting; never substitute a bare LED for a lamp.
    6. Teach power isolation, not just switch OFF: before changing snap connections, the adult disconnects the battery holder from the build as directed by the manufacturer. Pupils do not handle cells or holder contacts. Adult checks the complete approved layout before reconnecting power.
    7. Issue only the approved project's parts, grid, component list and diagram. Leave motors/fan blades, sound modules, extra loads and unused holders stored. No free wiring, shorts, series/parallel extensions or assumed sensors.

    Stop conditions: missing matching instructions, unsuitable age rating, unapproved holder/protection, missing parts, failed pretest, warmth, smell, damage or leakage. Mark the kit OUT OF SERVICE, tell the responsible adult and use a named alternative. A lit lamp alone does not certify safe wiring.

    The supplier description lists switches, lamps, LEDs and battery holders and recommends upper elementary. It describes a six-kit classroom pack, not OLP's eight individual kits. It does not verify OLP's exact model, battery allocation or school approval. This guide provides original teaching routines, not a replacement for that model's manufacturer instructions or a claim of official standards alignment.

    Station capacity: no group larger than three¶

    Use balanced pairs/trios, not a singleton. Each kit is one hardware station. The teacher demonstration uses a station kit before pupil work, not a ninth kit.

    Pupils Teams (size distribution) Hardware stations Hardware waves Unissued kits available as reserves
    10 4 (two trios, two pairs) 4 1 4
    15 5 (five trios) 5 1 3
    20 7 (six trios, one pair) 7 1 1
    25 9 (seven trios, two pairs) 5 2 3

    These numbers assume all eight kits pass preflight. Reserves are not automatically complete or powered. Recalculate when a kit is out of service; do not increase team size or mark a paper turn as electrical operation. One wave cannot serve more than 24 pupils with eight kits.

    The 25-pupil rotation¶

    Assign teams 1-5 to wave A (15 pupils) and teams 6-9 to wave B (10 pupils). Provide nine team work areas but only five dry hardware stations. Waiting teams work on their own prediction, diagram and audience/design task. The timed lessons include one minute for transfer/reset; supplies move, not live circuits. Adult isolates power and checks stations before the second wave.

    Each hardware wave gives a team 12 minutes in Grades 3-4 or 14 minutes in Grades 5-6: follow the approved build, adult inspection, individual switch tests, and one teacher-managed open-path repair/retest. Every child contributes a designated snap connection to the isolated team build and then operates the switch for two OFF/ON cycles. Rotate connector/tester/recorder; a role label alone is not evidence. The teacher checks each team before energizing and again after a repair. Never create a live short as the fault.

    At smaller enrollments, all teams use wave A; wave B is extra retesting, feedback and individual checking time, not an additional meeting. If inspections or motor/access needs take longer, record unfinished physical evidence and schedule a supervised completion turn. Do not rush a safety check or report that every child operated a kit unless observed.

    Individual evidence sheet: draw or copy onto paper¶

    No printer, account, app or additional workbook purchase is required. Each pupil records:

    Check Pupil response / teacher observation
    Full path Label battery, switch, light and conducting return path
    Prediction OFF -> expected output; ON -> expected output
    Test OFF/ON observations for cycle 1 and cycle 2
    Open-path repair Gap, one approved correction and observed retest result
    Design/message Intended user/reader; one choice and specific feedback
    Evidence level Physical contribution and switch test observed / prompted / not yet; paper reasoning recorded separately

    Teacher key for the approved simple switched light: OFF opens the path and the light is off; ON closes the path and the light is on. If observation differs, report the actual result. Disconnect power; adult checks the diagram, contacts and cells. Do not substitute parts or bypass protection.

    Protected-AA alternative: separate equipment, not a Snap conversion¶

    Keep the original protected circuit option if already verified and available. Do not attach Snap parts to its supply or LittleBits modules.

    • Grades 3-4: screw-secured covered 2-AA holder with documented protection, matching cells, switch, enclosed current-limited 3 V LED module and three insulated leads. Pretest holder + -> switch -> module +; module - -> holder -.
    • Grades 5-6: enclosed, current/short-circuit-protected switched 2-AA holder, push switch, adult-prepared sleeved red LED/220-ohm ¼-watt series-resistor assembly and three insulated leads. Pretest the complete series return path. Supply OFF before reconnection; adult checks before ON.

    Use the same station/wave counts above. Add one complete spare: ⅚/8/6 kits at 10/15/20/25 pupils, 10/12/16/12 fitted AA cells, and 15/18/24/18 leads. This is a selected-path allocation, not a change to the shared purchasing BOM. For Grades 5-6 retain the original four-state evidence: supply OFF with button released/pressed -> off/off; supply ON with button released/pressed -> off/on. Snap's slide switch instead uses two OFF/ON states repeated twice; do not claim push/release behavior or four independently controlled states from that route.

    Safety and return¶

    Adult supervision, dry seated stations, counted small parts and teacher-managed power throughout. No button/coin cells, household/mains/USB power, soldering, cutting wires, exposed fan blades, battery-terminal shorts or take-home circuits. Never mix cell types/old and new cells or recharge disposable cells. Use manufacturer-directed isolation and adult handling of damaged/hot cells.

    Reserve the lesson's final cleanup block: isolate power, count issued parts and cells against each station list, return parts to SC01-SC08, flag discrepancies, and store in a dry adult-controlled location away from water. Adult removes cells for long storage as directed. Only diagrams and paper art go home. Kindergarten remains adult-demonstration only after preflight; no new student circuit-building objective is introduced.
