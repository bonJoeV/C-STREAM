    K-6 core unit plans¶

    These local units organize the lesson library into a coherent year. Teachers select grade/rotation lessons from the lesson map; they do not teach every alternative. The instructional time baseline controls the calendar.

    Common unit requirements¶

    • Grade: use the K, 1-2, 3-4, or 5-6 target in the learning progression.
    • Weekly duration: 24 core meetings across Units 1-8 plus four flex/recovery meetings. Bi-weekly duration: 13 core plus two flex/recovery meetings.
    • Native minutes: K 25; 1-2 30; 3-4 40; 5-6 45 per meeting.
    • Assessment: an individually attributable show/tell/draw/build/record check; expected-mastery claims require two dated observations in changed contexts.
    • Kit: label C-STREAM | grade/rotation | unit | lesson | team count | issue count | return count | consumables to replace | teacher-only items | checked by/date.
    • Unfinished work: place named or coded work in the unit bin; the lesson must say whether it resumes next meeting or is documented/recycled.
    • Safety/access: complete the risk protocol and access check before release.
    • Family communication: send one plain-language launch/completion summary; no required home device, purchase, printing, or graded homework.

    The order matters: students first establish truthful observation and routines, then criteria/testing, then patterns/systems, and finally increasingly independent redesign, ethical tradeoffs, and communication.

    Unit 1: Wonder, evidence and tools¶

    • Enduring understanding: careful observation and honest questions begin scientific, artistic, and design work; tools extend human action.
    • Essential questions: What did we actually observe? What do we still wonder? When does a tool help?
    • Prerequisites: follow one modeled safety direction; use an oral, pointing, drawing, or written response.
    • Sequence: distinguish observation/question -> compare and measure -> use a simple tool or directional sequence -> explain evidence. Reversing the sequence invites tool play without a question.
    • End performance: each student identifies an observed detail, asks a checkable question, and communicates how a tool or representation helped.
    • Vocabulary: introduce observe, question, evidence, tool; reinforce detail, compare, care.
    • Standards: CST-C1, CST-S1, CST-T1, CST-A3, CST-M1 as actually evidenced.
    • Materials/kit: familiar objects/pictures, plain paper, crayons/pencils, ruler or comparison cards, roster; one observation tray per team.
    • Catholic through-line: creation is worthy of truthful, patient study; wonder does not replace evidence.
    • Culminating evidence: new-object observation and question without copying the modeled example.
    • Differentiation/family: model orally and allow pointing/dictation; older students separate inference from observation. Family asks, “What did you see, and what do you still wonder?”

    Unit 2: Structures and forces¶

    • Enduring understanding: criteria, constraints, fair tests, and redesign make structures more useful; collapse supplies information.
    • Essential questions: What must the design do? What must stay the same in a fair comparison? Who needs safe access?
    • Prerequisites: Unit 1 evidence language; counting/comparison appropriate to grade; safe handling of paper, blocks, and loads.
    • Sequence: user need/criterion -> sketch -> build -> controlled test -> record -> change one feature -> retest. The redesign depends on the first test.
    • End performance: each student uses test evidence to explain one structural change under a stated criterion and constraint.
    • Vocabulary: introduce criterion, constraint, span, load, stable, redesign; reinforce measure, evidence, compare.
    • Standards: CST-C2, CST-E1, CST-E2, CST-M1, CST-M3, CST-A1.
    • Materials/kit: paper/card, tape allowance, rulers, stable supports, identical safe counters, trays; label load cap and return counts.
    • Catholic through-line: design for people and report limitations honestly; models are not certified human structures.
    • Culminating evidence: two-version fair test and individual before/after explanation; older students name a limitation or tradeoff.
    • Differentiation/family: pre-fold/pre-cut as access support without changing the criterion; challenge with repeated trials, not heavier unsafe loads.

    Unit 3: Patterns, creation and community¶

    • Enduring understanding: patterns help people predict, organize, create, and communicate; purposeful design differs from decoration.
    • Essential questions: What repeats or changes? What rule supports the next element? How can a pattern serve or communicate?
    • Prerequisites: sort by one feature; continue a modeled simple pattern.
    • Sequence: notice/classify -> state rule -> predict/repair -> make a visual or rhythmic choice -> explain purpose.
    • End performance: each student identifies and extends a grade-appropriate pattern and explains one artistic or community-use choice.
    • Vocabulary: introduce unit, repeat, sequence, rhythm, composition; reinforce pattern, predict, represent.
    • Standards: CST-S2, CST-T2, CST-A2, CST-A3, CST-M2, CST-C3.
    • Materials/kit: large shape/pattern cards, paper, school art tools, optional body/hand percussion; no device required.
    • Catholic through-line: creativity is a gift used with respect and stewardship; do not claim a mathematical pattern proves theology.
    • Culminating evidence: repair an unfamiliar error and communicate a purposeful visual/rhythmic sequence.
    • Differentiation/family: tactile/high-contrast pieces and spoken rule; family notices a pattern without a purchase or worksheet.

    Unit 4: Light, sound and sequences¶

    • Enduring understanding: systems transform inputs into outputs; sequences can be predicted, traced, tested, and debugged.
    • Essential questions: What enters the system? What changes? How do we know where a sequence or path failed?
    • Prerequisites: Unit 3 sequence language; stop signal; protected component handling where approved.
    • Sequence: human/body or paper input-output model -> trace sequence/path -> predict output -> test -> debug -> communicate with light/sound/art.
    • End performance: each student traces and corrects a sequence or closed path and distinguishes prediction from observed output.
    • Vocabulary: introduce input, output, algorithm, debug, circuit/vibration only where taught; reinforce sequence, evidence, pattern.
    • Standards: CST-T1, CST-T2, CST-S2, CST-S3, CST-A2, CST-M2.
    • Materials/kit: arrow cards, grid, rhythm cards; optional approved protected circuit/robot kit. Batteries remain adult controlled.
    • Catholic through-line: technology carries human choices; use volume, light, energy, and shared tools considerately.
    • Culminating evidence: unfamiliar sequence/path correction; hardware operation is credited only when actually performed.
    • Differentiation/family: large floor/desk cards, seated trace, silent rhythm; no home app or internet.

    Unit 5: Evidence, faith and reason¶

    • Enduring understanding: scientific evidence, historical sources, Catholic teaching, and classroom metaphors answer different kinds of questions and should not be confused.
    • Essential questions: What supports this claim? What is measured, sourced, believed, or imagined? How should uncertainty be reported?
    • Prerequisites: distinguish observation/result from prediction; listen to a short sourced account.
    • Sequence: examine claim -> identify source/evidence type -> compare with record/model data -> qualify uncertainty -> communicate honestly.
    • End performance: each student supports a claim with appropriate evidence and labels a limit or question requiring verification.
    • Vocabulary: introduce claim, source, uncertainty, belief, model; reinforce evidence, result, question.
    • Standards: CST-C1, CST-S1, CST-S2, CST-A3, CST-M2.
    • Materials/kit: teacher-prepared source/data cards, paper, board; no live weather or biography claim required.
    • Catholic through-line: faith and reason are complementary; neither false quotation nor experimental overclaim serves truth.
    • Culminating evidence: sort and explain new statements as observation, scientific explanation, sourced faith teaching, or metaphor.
    • Differentiation/family: icons/oral sorting for younger students; older students identify source scope. Family receives qualified, sourced language.

    Unit 6: Life, stewardship and service¶

    • Enduring understanding: living systems have needs and change over time; stewardship uses evidence, resources, and care routines.
    • Essential questions: What does this living system need? What change can we observe? How can a design serve without making unsupported promises?
    • Prerequisites: observation records; hygiene and no-tasting rules.
    • Sequence: identify needs -> plan/model or set up approved observation -> record change -> compare -> design a stewardship/service response -> evaluate.
    • End performance: each student uses an observation/model to explain a need, change, or resource choice and who benefits.
    • Vocabulary: introduce habitat, organism, resource, stewardship, system; reinforce observe, change, evidence, criterion.
    • Standards: CST-C2, CST-C3, CST-S3, CST-E1, CST-M2, CST-A1.
    • Materials/kit: indoor model cards/cups/paper; exact approved growth materials only with care, allergy, spill, and disposal plan.
    • Catholic through-line: care for creation and human dignity shape resource choices; classroom models do not prove real-world impact.
    • Culminating evidence: recommend one evidence-based change under a resource constraint and name a limitation.
    • Differentiation/family: prepared image/data path when live growth is inaccessible; optional family observation is ungraded.

    Unit 7: Systems, redesign and responsible technology¶

    • Enduring understanding: designs are systems with users, criteria, constraints, tradeoffs, and ethical effects.
    • Essential questions: Who is included or excluded? Which evidence supports improvement? Is added technology worth its cost and burden?
    • Prerequisites: fair test/redesign from Unit 2; sequence/system language from Unit 4; evidence boundaries from Unit 5.
    • Sequence: user need -> criteria/constraints -> alternatives -> prototype or algorithm -> test -> stakeholder feedback -> optimize/tradeoff -> communicate.
    • End performance: each student defends a revision using performance evidence and one cost, resource, access, privacy, or dignity consideration.
    • Vocabulary: introduce optimize, tradeoff, stakeholder, privacy, accessibility; reinforce system, criterion, constraint, redesign.
    • Standards: CST-C2, CST-C3, CST-T2, CST-T3, CST-E1, CST-E2, CST-E3, CST-M3.
    • Materials/kit: commodity prototyping supplies and fictional user cards; device only after the external-service release record passes.
    • Catholic through-line: technological capability does not by itself make a choice wise; human dignity and common good inform constraints.
    • Culminating evidence: compare two alternatives and justify a tested, inclusive revision without claiming deployment certification.
    • Differentiation/family: assign genuine tool/test turns and multiple communication modes; family copy names the decision, evidence, and limits.

    Unit 8: Communicate, reflect and transfer¶

    • Enduring understanding: explaining evidence and revising communication strengthen learning; a team display does not replace individual mastery.
    • Essential questions: What can I now do that I could not do before? Which evidence best supports that claim? What should I revise?
    • Prerequisites: retained unit artifacts and feedback routines.
    • Sequence: select evidence -> explain objective/result/limit -> receive feedback -> revise -> transfer to a small new prompt -> reflect on service.
    • End performance: each student communicates one evidence-based learning claim and applies the method in a changed context.
    • Vocabulary: introduce portfolio, audience, feedback, revision, transfer; reinforce claim, evidence, limitation.
    • Standards: CST-A3, CST-E3, CST-C1, CST-C3 plus the demonstrated domain code.
    • Materials/kit: portfolio artifacts, simple labels, reflection/recording tools; display technology optional.
    • Catholic through-line: share gifts humbly, credit collaborators, report limits truthfully, and consider how learning can serve.
    • Culminating evidence: individual explanation and changed-context check; exhibition polish, confidence, and family attendance are not mastery.
    • Differentiation/family: private/recorded/teacher-conference options; family summary states skills and evidence, not guaranteed outcomes.
