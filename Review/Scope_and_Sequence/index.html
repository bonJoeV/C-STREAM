    K-6 scope and sequence¶

    Choose a feasible year¶

    Retain the existing periods: K 25 minutes, Grades 1-2 30 minutes, Grades 3-4 40 minutes, Grades 5-6 45 minutes.

    • Bi-weekly: Select one existing 17-session track.
    • Weekly: Select one band/rotation. The common Year A scaffold covers 34 calendar slots, including two breaks, for 32 teaching meetings. Check Year B against its grade review; fewer documents can cover longer units and do not prove identical pacing.
    • Do not combine weekly and bi-weekly tracks or add all thematic menu entries to the selected year.
    • Controlling baseline: until the school verifies its actual calendar, plan only 28 weekly meetings (24 core + 4 flex) or 15 bi-weekly meetings (13 core
    • 2 flex). See the instructional time baseline.
    • Core: Preserve inquiry, measurement/data, purposeful art, design/test/ redesign, unplugged computing and Catholic ethical reflection.
    • Recommended: Additional investigations or retesting that deepen an existing objective.
    • Optional/enrichment: Specialty robotics, advanced software, elaborate exhibitions, field trips and extra build variants when they add learning.

    The legacy index's single-week/multi-week/long-arc quotas double-counted meetings. The corrected planner and downloadable audits replace that arithmetic. No grade should be scheduled simply by counting filenames.

    | Band | 15-meeting baseline | 28-meeting baseline | Nominal 17/32 maximum | |---|---:|---:| | K | 375 | 700 | 425 / 800 minutes | | 1-2 | 450 | 840 | 510 / 960 minutes | | 3-4 | 600 | 1,120 | 680 / 1,280 minutes | | 5-6 | 675 | 1,260 | 765 / 1,440 minutes |

    Baseline totals include distribution, transitions, assessment and cleanup and reserve realistic interruptions. Nominal totals are library/calendar capacity, not guaranteed instructional time. The weekly program has more practice time; equal mastery in half the time is not assumed.

    The audited weekly Year B tracks for Grades 3-4 and 5-6 represent 33 meetings, or 1,320 and 1,485 minutes, respectively. Their units include the nominal winter-break slot; reconcile this with the actual school calendar rather than treating it as an extra guaranteed teaching meeting.

    Program unit spine¶

    This is a local planning organization of recurring concepts, not a claim that every legacy lesson already has this title or alignment. The actual lesson-level unit, rotation, evidence and disposition are in the lesson map. Use the band reviews to select ready lessons and adapt themes to the real school/liturgical calendar.

    Unit and bi-weekly slots Concepts and competencies Principal local standards Main evidence/project Weekly depth
    1. Wonder, observation and tools; 1-2 Notice, ask, predict; tools carry out human purposes; truthful observation CST-C1, CST-S1, CST-T1, CST-M1 Oral/drawn observation; directional or tool demonstration Repeat observations and introduce team routines
    2. Structures and forces; 3-4 Problem, criterion, constraints, stable forms, fair comparison CST-E1, CST-E2, CST-M1, CST-A1 Bridge/tower prototype, measured test and revision Compare alternatives and retest under the same load
    3. Creation, patterns and community; 5-6 Living/physical systems as appropriate; purposeful pattern; service CST-S3, CST-C2, CST-A2, CST-M2 Model or useful design with an explanation of who benefits Observe/change one feature and collect individual explanations
    4. Light, sound and sequences; 7-8 Physical systems, input/output, algorithms and visual/audio communication CST-S3, CST-T2, CST-A3 Light/sound model or paper algorithm with a trace Safe circuit investigation or actual digital execution when justified
    5. Evidence, faith/reason and local conditions; 9-10 Honest evidence, biography/source checks, weather/measurement/data CST-C1, CST-S1, CST-S2, CST-M2 Investigation record with evidence and uncertainty Compare observations across time; revise an explanation
    6. Life, stewardship and service; 11-13 Change over time, systems, resource care, inclusive design CST-C3, CST-S3, CST-E1, CST-C2 Indoor growth/habitat model or service prototype Repeated observations, testing and feedback
    7. Systems, redesign and responsible technology; 14-16 Algorithms, mechanisms, criteria, tradeoffs and ethical impacts CST-T2, CST-T3, CST-E2, CST-E3 Debugged plan or tested mechanism with a justified improvement Evaluate alternate designs; digital/hardware extensions only when meaningful
    8. Communicate and reflect; 17 Explain learning, compare earlier/later work, revise communication CST-A3, CST-E3, CST-C3 Individual explanation plus a team display/model Practice feedback, revise display and collect final evidence

    Codes are local program competencies, not official benchmarks. Each individual lesson claims only what students actually practice and demonstrate. Not every unit needs every domain. Arts must include purposeful choices and communication; counting decoration as art or a tablet as computing is insufficient.

    Grade-specific progression and memorable experiences¶

    Grade Entry/prerequisite to establish End-of-grade performance to seek Signature experience and next step
    K Follow one modeled oral instruction; compare visible objects Observe, build, point/draw/tell one change; count/compare a small set Structures that connect/help; next explain a problem and simple instruction sequence
    1 Reestablish safe tool use, oral sequencing and counting Build for a purpose, trace a short sequence, describe a result Paper bridge; next explain choices and represent results
    2 Repeat Grade 1 demonstrations rather than assume they were taught in the other rotation Explain design choice, compare measured results and revise an algorithm/design A service structure/mechanism with a before/after explanation; next conduct fair tests
    3 Model criteria, test procedure, measurement units and a simple table Test a design under fixed conditions and use evidence to suggest a revision Measured bridge; next use repeated measures and clearer evidence
    4 Recheck fair testing, recording and distinguishing prediction from result Use measurements and evidence to improve a design or explanation Structures, indoor weather inquiry or safe circuits; next compare against multiple criteria
    5 Establish criteria/constraints, repeated observations and respectful feedback Compare designs using evidence, criteria, cost/resources and an ethical reason Service design or environmental model; next defend a tradeoff and identify limitations
    6 Reestablish data quality, computational trace and explanation of users' needs Defend design choices with evidence, tradeoffs, cost, ethics and performance Inclusive design exhibition or fictional evidence investigation; next transfer methods to independent inquiry

    These are desired local outcomes, not automatic promotions based on exposure. The learning progression gives competency-level introduced/developing/reinforced/expected indicators and individual evidence.

    Rotations and prerequisites¶

    Year A and B are alternative entry years, not beginner/advanced levels. Every rotation must reintroduce the design cycle, safe tool routines, relevant vocabulary, measurement method and computing representations. Younger students receive modeling and accessible responses; older students extend evidence, independence and justification.

    Repeat the skill intentionally, not an identical worksheet or unexamined product. Keep a small portfolio so a returning student can compare earlier and later reasoning. When a project recurs, increase only an appropriate dimension: independence, precision, data, number of criteria or ethical tradeoffs.

    Seasonal and workload controls¶

    • No required objective depends on outdoor access in a Minnesota winter. Use room-temperature materials, safe indoor observations and prepared data when appropriate; do not claim a paper model supplies real weather data.
    • Growing investigations require observations over time. Schedule follow-up checks explicitly; a single period cannot demonstrate a life cycle.
    • Start with reusable kits and common classroom materials. Specialist kit reservations and digital accounts are VERIFICATION REQUIRED.
    • Keep projects small enough to test and assess in the native period. If a longer project is selected, label the meetings and storage/reset steps.
    • Use the audit's prep/cleanup estimates as planning estimates, not measured teacher timings. Time a first run and record improvements.
    • No routine homework. Family questions and activities are optional and do not supply evidence required for participation or mastery.
